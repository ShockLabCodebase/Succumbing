## Summary
An experimental survey of how two screeching underexpanded round jets (10 mm converging nozzles from a shared plenum) couple, at nozzle spacings S/D = 3 and 6 over NPR ≈ 2.3–5.5. Two microphones on either side of the pair give the peak screech frequency and, through Welch coherence and phase, the relative phase of the two plumes; high-resolution (1 Hz) schlieren in two orthogonal views gives two-point intensity correlations that identify four coupling modes: antisymmetric flapping, counter-rotating helical (~180° offset), symmetric flapping and corotating helical. Spacing changes which modes appear, not only where transitions occur, and some phase discontinuities occur with no jump in screech frequency, so frequency alone cannot identify twin-jet mode switches. Convective velocity from x–t diagrams of high-speed schlieren stays at U_c/U_j ≈ 0.57–0.60 across all modes, so it cannot explain the staging; the ratio of standing wavelength to shock-cell spacing instead changes from one coupling mode to the next, as reported earlier for single jets. Single-jet frequency models (Powell, Gao & Li) do not capture the twin-jet staging. There is no modelling or simulation beyond these empirical frequency formulas.

## Key findings
- **Staging and spacing (Fig. 4–6, p. 3–4):**
  - The twin jet suppresses staging over NPR 2.9–4.35 relative to the single jet, in line with Wlezien (1989) (p. 4).
  - At low NPR (S/D = 3) the high-St tones have nearly the same frequency as the single jet, so coupling seems to matter little there; a distinct drop in St at NPR ≈ 2.3 marks a change of plume oscillation (p. 4).
  - A second jump to higher St occurs between NPR 4.35 and 4.4 at S/D = 3, but between 4.7 and 4.8 at S/D = 6; S/D = 6 has an extra jump at NPR 2.8 (p. 4).
  - The single jet's last mode switch is delayed to NPR 5.0, after which its screech is weak and intermittent (p. 4).
  - Even at S/D = 6, coupling dominates the frequency behaviour (p. 4).
  - Caveat by the authors: this facility (short nozzles, large reflective flange just upstream of the exits) gives single-jet staging different from other nozzles on the same rig, so the coupling details are likely facility-specific (p. 3–4).
- **Gao & Li mode-number model (eq. 5; Fig. 5–6, p. 4):** before the drop, tones match m = 6 (axisymmetric A-type modes); after the drop at NPR ≈ 2.3, m = 5 (B or D) fits both spacings. The model predicts much larger frequency changes than the twin jet shows, suggesting a different mode-selection mechanism in twin jets (p. 4).
- **Acoustic phase regions (Fig. 7–8, p. 5):**
  - S/D = 3: region 1 (to NPR ≈ 3.4) ≈ 0° phase (antisymmetric); region 2 (3.4–4.35) phase scattered between 0 and 120° with no frequency jump at its start; region 3 (after the 4.35 jump) ≈ 180° (symmetric) (p. 5).
  - S/D = 6: a much wider scattered region centred on 180°, with 0° regions above and below it (p. 5).
  - The scatter is attributed to temporal unsteadiness of the coupling (switching between modes or brief uncoupling) smeared by time averaging; this is a hypothesis, not resolved by the data (p. 5, 8).
  - Conclusion: larger spacing changes mode selection, not just the transition NPR (p. 5).
- **Mean field (Fig. 10, p. 5–6):** mean schlieren shows only steady shock-cell lengthening with NPR, so the coupling modes are not tied to mean-flow changes (p. 5).
- **Four coupling modes from two-point correlation (Table 2, p. 6–9):**
  - *Antisymmetric flapping* (S/D = 3, NPR 3.0, 3.3; Fig. 11): front view antisymmetric about each plume and about y/D = 0; side view symmetric with half the front-view wavelength, attributed to acoustic waves emitted at both extremes of the flap (Humphrey & Edgington-Mitchell 2016). The correlation alone cannot tell this from counter-rotating helices at 0°; instantaneous images (less side-view deflection) favour flapping (p. 6–7).
  - *Counter-rotating helical, ~180° offset* (S/D = 3, NPR 3.6, 4.0; Fig. 13–14): front view symmetric about the midline with lobes spanning the internozzle gap; side view antisymmetric with similar lobe length. The side-view lobes above and below the jet are slightly misaligned, more so at 3.6 than at 4.0 (closer to 180°), read as varying phase from unsteady coupling (p. 7–8).
  - *Symmetric flapping* (S/D = 3, NPR 4.6, 5.0; Fig. 15–16; and S/D = 6, NPR 3.2, 3.8, 4.2; Fig. 17): front view like region 2, side view weak and symmetric with similar wavelength, put down to high pressure between the jets when both structures are nearest the centreline. The S/D = 6 version has a wider phase spread, read as less stable coupling (p. 8).
  - *Corotating helical, 0° phase* (S/D = 6 only, NPR 2.5; Fig. 18–19): antisymmetric patterns in both views (p. 9).
  - The technique favours the strongest correlation and cannot resolve multiple modes or mode switching (p. 6).
- **Convective velocity (Table 3, p. 10):** U_c = 253–320 m/s for NPR 3.0–5.0, U_c/U_j = 0.57–0.60 with no discrete change across modes. U_c scales with U_j, so (as for single jets, Mercier et al. 2017) U_c does not explain staging (p. 10–11). Regions 1 and 3 agree with the single-jet U_c/U_j = 0.615 of Gao & Li; region 2 deviates more, but correcting for that barely changes Fig. 5–6 (p. 11).
- **Length scales (Table 4, p. 11):**
  - Region 1 (NPR 3.0, 3.3): λ_SW ≈ shock spacing s (ratio ≈ 0.99–1.02).
  - Region 2, helical (3.6, 4.0): λ_SW < s (ratio ≈ 0.96).
  - Region 3, symmetric flapping (4.6, 5.0): λ_SW > s (ratio ≈ 1.04–1.05).
  - Shock spacing agrees with Gao & Li's modified Prandtl–Pack formula to within 1% (p. 11).
  - So the λ_SW/s ratio changes with coupling mode, as in single jets (Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2015; Mercier et al. 2017), but the mechanism is unclear (p. 11). Ratios are my arithmetic from Table 4.
- **Standing-wave details (Fig. 21, p. 10–11):** the first standing-wave lobe next to the nozzle is much longer and is excluded; the authors suggest it comes from upstream waves and reflections off the nozzle flange where the shear layer is still weak. A second standing-wave pattern beyond |y/D| ≈ 3 in the front view is put down to reflections from the base plate (p. 11).
- **Conclusion (p. 11):** phase discontinuities without frequency jumps show twin-jet staging is more complex than single-jet staging, and single-jet frequency models fail for the twin system.

## Setup / method
- **Facility:** Shock Lab group facility. The paper names the Laboratory for Turbulence Research in Aerospace and Combustion (LTRAC), Monash University, not the lab space (p. 2). Continuous high-pressure air supply through a regulator into a plenum; stagnation pressure from an RS-461 transducer (0–10 bar, 0.25% accuracy) (p. 2). The rig is not anechoic (p. 3).
- **Nozzles (Fig. 2b–c, p. 2–3):** purpose-built modular twin-nozzle attachment. Two converging round nozzles, exit D = 10 mm, elliptical internal contraction profile, fed from one plenum so NPR is matched. The nozzles slide in a track and are held by interchangeable sealing plates of fixed separation. Short nozzles with a large reflective flange (base plate) a short distance upstream of the exits (p. 4). The same nozzle plate was later used by Stavropoulos et al. 2023 and (single-jet version) by the Monash data in Wong et al. 2023.
- **Conditions:** S/D = 3 and 6 (centre-to-centre), plus a single jet from the same nozzle; acoustic NPR range stated as 2.5–5.5 (p. 3), although Table 2 regions start at NPR 2.3 (p. 9).

U_j at NPR 3.6 and 4.0 (562, 584) breaks the monotonic trend; 462 and 484 would give the U_c/U_j of Table 3 (270/462 = 0.58, 277/484 = 0.57), so these are probably typos in the paper or the extraction. The total temperature is not given.
- **Acoustics (p. 3):**
  - Two G.R.A.S. Type 46BE 1/4-in. microphones (4 Hz–80 kHz, 35–160 dB), at 8D either side of the pair along the shared nozzle plane (measured from the closer nozzle), at lip height, pointing at the nozzle.
  - 5 × 10⁵ samples at 200 kHz per recording (2.5 s, my arithmetic); five recording sets per case.
  - Screech peak defined as a point 7 dB above the local average of the PSD (p. 3).
  - Phase method: Welch cross-spectral density → coherence and phase; frequencies with coherence > 0.7 sustained over ≥ 120 Hz are "coherent peaks"; peaks overlapping across the five sets are grouped and their phase ensemble-averaged; the group must contain the screech peak (p. 3–4). Welch block length, overlap and window are not stated.
  - Because the mics sit on opposite sides, the measured phase is 180° off the physical plume phase; reported phases are corrected to the jet frame (p. 4).
  - Only fundamental frequency and phase are used; amplitude is not, because the room is not anechoic (p. 3).
- **Schlieren (p. 2–3):**
  - Toepler Z-type, two mirrors f = 2032 mm; knife edge for the streamwise gradient ∂ρ/∂x only.
  - High-speed: Shimadzu HPV-1, 320 × 260 px, 102 frames per run at up to 1 Mfps, 0.25 µs exposure, Metz Mecablitz flash illumination. Frame rate used is not stated.
  - High-resolution: PCO4000, 4008 × 2672 px at 1 Hz (not time-resolved), 1.6 µs exposure, pulsed LED (Willert et al. 2012).
  - Two views: "side" = line of sight in the plane of the two nozzles (plumes overlap); "front" = perpendicular to it (both plumes side by side) (p. 5).
- **Two-point correlation (eq. 4, p. 3, 5–6):** on ≥ 900 PCO4000 images per case. The reference point was chosen for strong global correlation; its position changes only the strength, not the pattern (p. 5–6). Contours span −1 to 1. Path-integrated, single-point statistics: dominant mode only, no switching resolved. Mode identification relies on comparing front/side patterns with sketches of expected patterns (Fig. 12, 14, 16, 19) plus inspection of instantaneous deflections.
- **Convective velocity (Fig. 20, p. 10):** 20 sets of 102 time-resolved HPV-1 images per case; a pixel row at fixed y/D is stacked in time to make x–t diagrams; five y/D locations in each of the four shear layers give 20 x–t plots per case; a local extremum filter isolates the structure traces (text says "minimum", caption says "maximum"); a Hough transform gives the trace slopes; ensemble average over ≥ 100 traces with 95% confidence intervals.
- **Length scales (p. 10–11):** λ_SW from the standard-deviation (rms) image of > 900 schlieren images, reading band peaks along a vertical line in the internozzle region; average of the three nodes after the first, per plume. Shock-cell length s = distance between shock reflection points, averaged over the first five cells.
- **Phase-mean images (Fig. 1, p. 2):** shown for the two modes at S/D = 3; how the phase averaging was done is not described.

## Key equations
- Powell's screech frequency with shock spacing s (eq. 1, p. 1):
  $$ f = \frac{U_c}{s\,(1 + M_c)} $$
- Panda's standing-wave version (eq. 2–3, p. 2):
  $$ f = \frac{U_c}{\lambda_{SW}(1 + M_c)}, \qquad \frac{1}{\lambda_{SW}} = \frac{1}{\lambda_A} + \frac{1}{\lambda_L} $$
  λ_A acoustic (upstream) wavelength, λ_L hydrodynamic wavelength.
- Two-point spatial correlation of schlieren intensity (eq. 4, p. 3):
  $$ R(x_0+\Delta x, y_0+\Delta y) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} I_i(x_0,y_0)\, I_i(x_0+\Delta x, y_0+\Delta y)}{\sigma(x_0,y_0)\,\sigma(x_0+\Delta x, y_0+\Delta y)} $$
  > Rebuilt from garbled extraction. Check against the PDF whether I is the fluctuating intensity and whether there is a 1/N factor (needed for R to lie in [−1, 1]).
- Gao & Li (2010) multi-mode screech formula (eq. 5, p. 4), reinforcement region of n = 5 shock cells holding m combined hydrodynamic + acoustic wavelengths:
  $$ \lambda_s = \frac{n\,s\,(1 + M_c)}{m\,M_c} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad f = \frac{m\,U_c}{n\,s\,(1+M_c)} $$
  m = 5 for A1, B, D; m = 6 for A0, A2, C. Gao & Li's shock-cell (modified Prandtl–Pack) and U_c models are used.
  > Rebuilt from garbled extraction and checked for consistency with eq. 2–3 (n s = m λ_SW). Confirm the exact printed form in the PDF.

## Notation
D nozzle exit diameter (10 mm); D_j fully expanded diameter; S centre-to-centre spacing; NPR = P_plenum/P_atm; M_j, U_j fully expanded Mach number and velocity; St = f D_j/U_j; U_c, M_c convective velocity and Mach number; s shock-cell spacing; λ_SW, λ_A, λ_L standing, acoustic and hydrodynamic wavelengths; n number of shock cells in the reinforcement region (5); m number of combined wavelengths (Gao & Li); I pixel intensity; σ its standard deviation; R two-point correlation. "Antisymmetric" coupling = 0° phase between plumes, "symmetric" = 180°, both defined about the midplane between the jets (p. 2). x downstream, y along the common nozzle axis (the line joining the nozzles).

## Relevance to this project
An early Shock Lab group twin-jet paper on resonance and feedback, with acoustics and schlieren but no modal decomposition and no model beyond empirical frequency formulas. It is the baseline that later group twin-jet work builds on.

- **Method and data dependence:**
  - The mode identification uses **non-time-resolved** 1 Hz schlieren (≥ 900 images) and single-point two-point correlation. That suits non-TR data but gives only the dominant pattern and, as the authors note, cannot separate helical from flapping without extra views and instantaneous images. Snapshot POD with mode pairs on the same images (as in [wong-2023](wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), [nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa](nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md)) would give phase and symmetry more directly, especially with symmetry imposition about the midplane (cf. the D2 split in [yeung-2022](yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md)).
  - The x–t / Hough-transform U_c needs time-resolved bursts (102 frames) but not long records. Acoustic phase uses long, time-resolved mic records but is averaged with Welch, so intermittent coupling shows up only as phase scatter (regions 2 and 5).
  - **Intermittency:** the scattered-phase regions are the paper's main open question, and the explanation (switching between coupling modes or brief uncoupling) is a hypothesis it cannot test. Later group work resolved this with time-resolved schlieren, wavelets and conditional POD ([wong-2023](wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md); Bell et al. 2021, not indexed). For the SPOD → reconstruction → Hilbert POD pipeline, these NPRs (S/D = 3, NPR 3.45–4.35; S/D = 6, NPR 2.8–4.45) are natural targets: reconstruct the screech-frequency SPOD modes and track the instantaneous inter-plume phase with Hilbert POD ([raiola-2025](raiola-2025-hilbert-pod.md)), or apply SPOD frequency–time analysis ([nekkanti-2021](nekkanti-2021-spod-reconstruction-denoising-frequency-time.md)) or wavelet coherence ([torrence-1998](torrence-1998-practical-guide-wavelet-analysis.md); used for twin rectangular jets in [esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets](esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md)). A Hilbert phase difference between plumes is what [jeun-2024](jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md) used in LES.
- **Modelling side:** only Powell, Panda and Gao & Li frequency formulas are tested, and none captures twin-jet staging (p. 4, 11). The later theory explains the gaps:
  - Eq. 3 uses a free-stream acoustic wave for closure. [edgington-mitchell-2018](edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) and [edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) replace it with the guided jet mode and show that the loop closes on k_kh − k_s1 or on a suboptimal shock peak. On that reading, λ_SW ≈ s in region 1 would be closure on the primary shock wavenumber, and the λ_SW ≠ s of regions 2 and 3 closure on a suboptimal peak. This is my interpretation, not the paper's.
  - Twin-jet linear stability ([rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets](rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), [stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa](stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), [stavropoulos-2024-upstream-waves-merging-twin-jets](stavropoulos-2024-upstream-waves-merging-twin-jets.md), [nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa](nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md)) splits the m = 1 KH and guided-jet eigenvalues into symmetric/antisymmetric pairs that depend on S/D, which is a candidate explanation for why spacing changes mode selection here. The resonance-model approach of [mancinelli-2021](mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) could be extended to twin-jet branches to predict the region boundaries.
  - Flapping vs helical in round jets: [nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets](nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) offers a mechanism (weak |m| = 2 streaks splitting m = ±1) for single round jets that flap; whether the second jet plays that role here is open.
- **Agreement with and differences from related notes:**
  - [wong-2023](wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md): same group and nozzle family (D = 10 mm). Wong reports twin and single-jet A1/A2/B staging NPRs nearly equal up to NPR ≈ 2.36 and cites Knast as agreeing; this matches Knast's finding that coupling barely changes the low-NPR tones and the St drop at NPR ≈ 2.3 (m = 6 → m = 5, i.e. A → B). Wong resolves the intermittency Knast inferred. Coupling-symmetry conventions differ (Knast's antisymmetric/symmetric about the midplane vs Wong's SS/SA labels), so map them carefully before comparing.
  - [esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets](esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md) cites this paper for the standing wavelength growing with shock spacing; Table 4 supports that, but with a mode-dependent offset.
  - [stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa](stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) uses this nozzle plate and cites Knast for coupling persisting at large spacing; consistent with S/D = 6 still being coupling-dominated here.
  - [raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review](raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md): Raman's review discusses twin rectangular jet coupling; this paper argues round twin jets have richer interactions because rectangular jets are constrained to major-axis oscillation (p. 2).
- **PyFR staging work:** the single-jet A1/A2 staging target in `Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/` sits below the NPR range of the twin-jet modes here. The facility caveat (short nozzles and a reflective flange change single-jet staging, p. 3–4) is a reminder that nozzle-exterior geometry should match in any comparison with Monash data.
