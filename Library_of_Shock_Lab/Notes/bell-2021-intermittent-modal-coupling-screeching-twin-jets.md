## Summary
Bell et al. explain the "phase anomaly" of round twin jets at s/D = 3, the NPR range in which [Knast 2018](knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) found high coherence between opposing microphones but an erratic phase. The range is NPR 3.4–4.4 (M_j ≈ 1.45–1.62), between antisymmetric coupling below and symmetric coupling above (p. 3–4, 9). Re-analysing Knast's opposing-microphone records with coherence at all frequencies, they find two or three coherent tones of smoothly varying frequency ("processes") inside the anomaly (p. 8–9). A 200 Hz band-pass filter and a Hilbert transform per process then give instantaneous amplitude and inter-microphone phase. Where the phase spreads, the tones show "interruptions", taken as dips more than 5 dB below the mean, for about 20% of the time; these last about 5–15 screech cycles, and the tones of the different processes are uncorrelated rather than mutually exclusive (p. 10–16). Non-time-resolved PIV at NPR 4.6 and 5.0 from a second Monash rig, decomposed by snapshot POD of the transverse velocity and conditioned on the POD amplitude of each jet, shows three states at NPR 4.6: one jet oscillating alone; both jets coupled symmetrically; both coupled antisymmetrically. The two coupled states never occur together, and their POD wavelengths (3.1D and 2.8D) with u_c = 0.6u_j reproduce the two measured tones (St 0.22 and 0.23) (p. 18–27). An OSU anechoic facility with different nozzles shows the same sequence (antisymmetric, indeterminate, symmetric) at different NPRs (p. 31–36). The authors read the intermittency as competition between two global modes; they do not compute any model (p. 29).

## Key findings
- **Coherent "processes" in the phase anomaly** (§3, p. 7–10; Figs 3–5):
  - Facility one PSD waterfall (Knast 2018 data): a single fundamental over most of the range, with discontinuities in the tone and the general spectrum at NPR 3.4 and 4.4 that suggest mode switches (p. 7).
  - Coherence at all frequencies shows extra highly coherent but low-amplitude tones, concentrated in the anomaly (p. 8). Three non-harmonic tones vary continuously with NPR (Fig. 5, p. 9):
    - *Process 1:* from NPR ≈ 2.3 to the end of the range; 180° opposing-microphone phase at low NPR. Fig. 5 reading: St ≈ 0.46 at NPR 2.3, ≈ 0.32 at 3.0, ≈ 0.27 at 4.0, ≈ 0.25 at 5.0.
    - *Process 2:* from NPR ≈ 3.4–3.5 to the end; "not associated with a particular phase" (p. 9), but "centred on 180 degrees" on p. 11 and in Table 1. Fig. 5 reading: just below process 1, St ≈ 0.28 at 3.4, ≈ 0.25 at 4.0, ≈ 0.22 at 5.0.
    - *Process 3:* from NPR 4.4 with 0° phase. Fig. 5 reading: St ≈ 0.21 at 4.4, ≈ 0.20 at 5.0.
  - Within the anomaly two processes with strongly varying phase coexist, so a CPSD phase read at the single peak frequency is "likely to return spurious values despite the high coherence" (p. 9–10).
  - A "process" is defined as one feedback loop whose tone varies continuously with operating condition: same azimuthal modes, same coupling, same effective source (p. 9).
  - Three candidate causes of discrete staging are listed, none tested (p. 9): a change in the most amplified azimuthal mode or, in twin jets, in the coupling (Rodríguez et al. 2018); a change in the number of structures and waves in the loop (Gao & Li 2010), perhaps through the source location ([Mercier 2017](mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md)); a change of upstream wave from free-stream sound to guided jet mode (Shen & Tam 2002).
- **Instantaneous amplitude and phase statistics** (§4.1, p. 11–12; Fig. 6, Table 1, Appendix C Fig. 27):
  - "Wide" means a standard deviation above 20° in phase or 3 dB in amplitude (p. 11).
  - Process 1:
    - NPR 2.3–3.0: wide, centred on 180°.
    - NPR 3.0–3.5: narrow in phase and amplitude, centred on 180°. This band has the highest acoustic intensity, Fig. 6(b) peaking near 172 dB at NPR ≈ 3.2 (read). It matches the strongest coupling found by Knast (NPR 3.0–3.4) and Seiner et al.'s (1986) observation that strong coupling raises amplitude above the sum of two single jets (p. 11–12).
    - NPR 3.5–3.9: wide and uncentred. From NPR ≈ 3.9 the amplitude is lower (Fig. 6b: about 150 dB) and the phase very wide (p. 11).
  - Process 2: wide phase centred on 180°, and a broad amplitude distribution (about 143–155 dB in Fig. 6d). Its phase narrows somewhat above NPR 4.1, with rising amplitude (p. 11).
  - Process 3 (NPR ≥ 4.4): narrow phase centred on 0°, and narrow, high amplitude (about 165–170 dB, Fig. 6f) (p. 11).
  - Narrow phase and narrow amplitude occur together (p. 11).
  - Fig. 27 standard deviations (read; p. 34):
    - process 1 phase: 50–63° at NPR 2.3–2.75; 2–6° at 2.9–3.4; 30–45° above 3.9. Process 1 amplitude: 5–7.5 dB at 2.3–2.45; ≈ 1 dB at 3.0–3.4; 5.5–5.8 dB above 4.0.
    - process 2: 32–49° in phase, 5.5–6.0 dB in amplitude everywhere;
    - process 3: 4–9° in phase and 1.3–2.5 dB in amplitude, with spikes of 16–19° and 4.5–5 dB at NPR 4.5 and 4.65.
- **Interruptions** (§4.2, p. 12–14; Figs 7–8, Appendix A Figs 22–23):
  - An interruption is a dip of the Hilbert envelope below μ − 5 dB, where μ is the ensemble mean. The 5 dB was chosen as the normal variation of a steady signal (p. 12, 30).
  - Interruption fraction (Fig. 7, read):
    - Process 1: ≈ 22–27% at NPR 2.3–2.45, falling through ≈ 5–15% to 0% at NPR 3.0–3.45; mostly 0–6% at 3.5–3.9, with outliers of 11% (3.8); 18–21% from NPR ≈ 3.85.
    - Process 2: 19–21% throughout (NPR 3.4–5.4).
    - Process 3: 0–4%, except 7% (4.5) and 13% (4.65).
  - The authors take 20% as a high rate. Wide distributions generally go with high rates, except process 1 at NPR 3.5–3.9: wide phase but only ≈ 2% interruption (p. 13).
  - Durations, in screech periods (Fig. 8; p. 13–14):
    - at NPR ≥ 3.5, interruptions last 5–15 periods and coupled intervals cluster at 20–30 periods, with a tail to 150;
    - at NPR 2.5–3.0 the interruption durations are much more widely spread, and the shortest coupled intervals sit randomly above about 20 periods.
  - The screech is ≈ 10 kHz, so the loop period and the interruption time scale are "within an order of magnitude" (p. 13).
  - Threshold sensitivity (Appendix A, p. 30–31, Fig. 23): μ − 4, −5 and −6 dB change the interrupted fraction (process 2: ≈ 24, 20 and 16%, read) but, the authors say, not the detection of events or the shape of the plateau.
- **Interruptions in one jet or both** (§4.3, p. 14–16; Fig. 9):
  - This assumes each microphone hears essentially only its closer jet, with the far jet "sufficiently shielded" (p. 14).
  - Narrow joint distributions: process 1 at NPR 2.75 and 3.20, process 3 at 4.60.
  - Process 1 at NPR 3.85: the joint distribution sits in the upper-right corner, with horizontal and vertical tails. When one jet is interrupted the other is not: the interruptions are "essentially anti-correlated" (p. 14–16).
  - Processes 1 and 2 at NPR 4.30 and 4.60, and process 2 at 3.85: fan-shaped joint distributions, i.e. correlated interruptions with both jets quiet together (p. 16).
- **Between processes in one jet** (§4.4, p. 16; Fig. 10):
  - At NPR 4.0, processes 1 and 2 have no clear relationship: at times both are active, at times one, at times neither.
  - At NPR 5.0, process 3 never drops out while processes 1 and 2 appear intermittently.
  - The tones are therefore not mutually exclusive, in contrast with the A1/A2 switching on time scales of seconds in [Mancinelli 2019](mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) (p. 16).
  - Interim summary: one to three processes; interruptions in one or both jets; tones "generally uncorrelated" (p. 16–17).
- **Facility two acoustics** (p. 17–19; Figs 11–12):
  - A lower-frequency tone (process 3) also appears at higher NPR, but at a different NPR, which the authors put down to acoustic boundary conditions (Weightman et al. 2019; p. 17).
  - Process 2 cannot be confirmed with a single microphone (p. 17–18).
  - At NPR 4.6 this rig shows two moderate tones, like facility one at NPR 3.9–4.4, whereas facility one has a steady process 3 at NPR 4.6 (p. 18).
  - Process 1 and 3 envelopes at NPR 4.6 are uncorrelated (Fig. 12a), with interruption fractions of 21.4% and 18.5% and means of 136.7 and 136.4 dB (Fig. 12b,c, p. 19).
  - Only processes 1 and 3 appear in all three facilities, so the PIV study concentrates on them (p. 18).
- **POD at NPR 5.0, steady symmetric coupling** (§5.2, p. 20; Fig. 13):
  - Two leading modes hold ≈ 9% of the u_y energy (≈ 5.0% and 3.9%, read; p. 21) and form a 90°-shifted pair: a travelling KH wavepacket.
  - u_y is mirrored about the symmetry plane (symmetric coupling), consistent with the 0° phase of process 3 (p. 20).
- **POD at NPR 4.6, unsteady coupling** (§5.3, p. 21–23; Figs 14–16):
  - Four leading modes, with no clear pairing and unequal levels in the two jets. Together they hold ≈ 6% (the dots in Fig. 14a sum to about 4.9%, read) against 9% at NPR 5.0 (p. 21).
  - All four look like flapping or helical motion of each jet; the coupling is unclear (p. 21–22).
  - Sub-domain POD of each jet, cut at y/D = 0 and excluding the first shock cell (whose Mach-disk motion correlates weakly with the downstream structures), gives one clear leading pair per jet, ≈ 2.5% each against ≈ 1.2% per mode for the full field (p. 22). Taken alone, each jet "oscillate[s] in the manner of an isolated screeching jet" (p. 22).
  - Spatial correlation matches full-field modes 1–2 to the lower-jet pair and modes 3–4 to the upper-jet pair (p. 22).
- **Conditional POD** (p. 23–27; Figs 17–21):
  - Per-jet oscillation amplitude: |a_{1,2}| = (a₁² + a₂²)^{1/2} from the sub-domain pair (p. 23). The upper-jet and lower-jet amplitudes are only weakly correlated, and many snapshots have one jet strong and the other weak (Fig. 17a, p. 23).
  - *Upper quartile of one jet only.* POD of these snapshots gives a separate pair for each jet. Strong oscillation of the selected jet goes with weak fluctuations of the other: the jets influence each other but are not necessarily coupled. Selecting on the upper jet gives the same result with the mode order reversed (p. 23).
  - *Upper quartile of both jets* (≈ 650 snapshots; jack-knife convergence after ≈ 500; p. 24–25):
    - five leading modes, the fifth a shear-layer-variance mode (Weightman et al. 2018) that is set aside (p. 25);
    - modes 1–2 are a symmetric coupled pair, modes 3–4 an antisymmetric coupled pair, both with equal strength in the two jets (Fig. 20, p. 25–26);
    - so whenever both jets oscillate strongly, they are coupled one way or the other (p. 25).
  - *Mutual exclusivity* (Fig. 21, p. 27–28): |a_{1,2}| (symmetric) against |a_{3,4}| (antisymmetric). The upper-right quarter is empty (no simultaneous coupling in both forms), and so is the lower-left (strong oscillation of both jets requires coupling). Snapshots of moderate strength in both may be transitions but "more likely" reflect turbulence (p. 27).
  - Supplementary movie 1: snapshots reconstructed from the first 20 POD modes, assigned a phase from the mode-pair coefficients and averaged in 10° bins, for NPR 5.0 and for each NPR 4.6 subset (p. 28).
- **Linking POD pairs to tones** (Table 2, p. 25–27):
  - λ_D is read from the POD modes, and u_c = 0.6u_j is taken from Knast's schlieren x–t measurements. Then St_Est matches the facility-two acoustic tones to within 0.01 (Data tables).
  - The symmetric pair (λ_D = 3.1D) is linked to the lower tone (process 3) and the antisymmetric pair (2.8D) to the higher tone (process 1).
  - The authors call the link strong evidence, not proof; the same method was validated against schlieren in Weightman et al. (2019) (p. 25–27).
- **Overall picture at NPR 4.6** (p. 27):
  - Neither jet, one jet or both jets may be oscillating. When both oscillate strongly they couple symmetrically or antisymmetrically, and taken alone each jet shows a single wavelength.
  - A non-oscillating jet probably produces the acoustic interruptions, and a jet oscillating alone produces a tone "generally independent from the other jet".
- **Schlieren check** (p. 28–29, Appendix D Figs 28–29): Knast's 102-frame HPV-1 clips were revisited. Within NPR 3.9–4.4 they show the left jet alone, the right jet alone, both jets and neither jet oscillating. At low and high NPR they show clear antisymmetric and symmetric coupling (p. 28–29).
- **Facility three, OSU** (Appendix B, p. 31–36; Figs 24–26):
  - Staging differs noticeably, especially at NPR 3.0–4.0 (p. 35). There are four coherent processes (Fig. 25d):
    - process 1: St ≈ 0.65 → 0.55 at NPR ≈ 2.3–2.6, read;
    - process 2: St ≈ 0.40 → 0.30 at 2.5–3.3;
    - process 3: St ≈ 0.32 → 0.25 at 3.3–4.2;
    - process 4: St ≈ 0.24 → 0.17 at 4.2–6.
  - Processes 1 and 2 have wide phase before a narrow-phase range. Process 3 is very wide in phase and amplitude. Process 4 moves from wide to steady coupling for NPR ≥ 5.0 (p. 36).
  - The qualitative sequence holds in all three facilities: antisymmetric coupling at low NPR, an indeterminate-phase range, symmetric coupling at high NPR (p. 18, 35–36).
  - The facilities differ as follows. The two Monash rigs use the same nozzles, but the PIV enclosure has hard Perspex walls and the plenum face sits flush with its base (p. 6). OSU uses an anechoic room and different nozzle contours and lips (p. 18, 33).
- **Conclusion** (p. 29):
  - Antisymmetric, steady, high-amplitude coupling occurs at NPR ≤ 3.5 and symmetric coupling at NPR ≥ 4.4 (facility one).
  - In between, several weaker tones are highly intermittent, and the jets couple either way or oscillate alone.
  - That this happens between two operating points with different coupling modes "strongly suggests" competition between two global modes.
  - Jets with one wavelength each can produce different tones by coupling in different symmetries.
  - The coupled modes depend on s/D and NPR, so other spacings must be recharacterised.

## Setup / method
- **Facility one** (acoustics and schlieren; p. 4–5, Fig. 1):
  - A Shock Lab group facility, which the paper calls the LTRAC schlieren jet facility at Monash. Compressed air at 298 K feeds a plenum with honeycomb and screens.
  - Twin converging round nozzles: D = 10 mm, lip thickness 1.5 mm (t/D = 0.15), centre spacing s/D = 3.0. Choked exit with U_e ≈ 310 m s⁻¹.
  - Re ≈ 7.8 × 10⁵ at NPR 4.6 and 8.5 × 10⁵ at NPR 5.0 (p. 5). Elsewhere 3.12 × 10⁵ and 8.46 × 10⁵ are given for NPR 2.0 and 5.0 (p. 33).
  - These are the Knast 2018 data. That note records a non-anechoic room and a large reflective flange just upstream of the exits.
- **Microphones** (p. 5):
  - Two opposing GRAS 46BE 1/4-inch microphones (20 Hz–100 kHz), calibrated with a GRAS 42AB, 8D radially from the closer nozzle lip. Recorded on an NI DAQ at 250 kHz and 16 bit.
  - Millimetre positioning is stated to give about ±5° phase error at 15 kHz (see check item 5).
  - NPR 2.0–5.0 in steps of 0.05, i.e. M_j 1.05–1.71 (γ = 1.4); the M_j step is 2.04 × 10⁻² at the low end and 6.58 × 10⁻³ at the high end. Figures and Table 1 run to NPR 5.4–5.5.
  - 500 k samples per record on both microphones; five records ensemble-averaged per NPR.
- **Schlieren** (p. 5): Toepler Z-type with two f = 2032 mm mirrors, ∂ρ/∂x knife edge. Shimadzu HPV-1, 320 × 260 px, 102 frames at up to 1 Mfps, 0.25 µs exposure. Not simultaneous with the acoustics; used only qualitatively (supplementary movie 2).
- **Facility two** (PIV; p. 6, 17–18, Fig. 2):
  - A Shock Lab group facility, which the paper calls the LTRAC supersonic PIV jet facility, with the same nozzles and plenum design. Air at ≈ 298 K passes through a mixing chamber seeded by a Viscount 1300 smoke generator, which seeds both the jets and the ambient.
  - The jets exhaust into an enclosure of 60 × 60 × 200 D with hard Perspex walls, which are strong acoustic reflectors. The plenum face sits nearly flush with the enclosure base, so the upstream reflection condition also differs (p. 6).
  - One microphone only, so no phase or coherence can be measured; its position is not given (p. 18).
  - PIV at ≈ 0.5 Hz, so the snapshots are statistically independent; ≈ 9500 fields per NPR, NPR 4.6 and 5.0 only (p. 17–18). Camera, interrogation windows and field of view are in Bell et al. (2018), not here. The figures show x/D ≈ 0–12, |y/D| ≲ 4.
- **Facility three, OSU** (Appendix B, p. 31–35, Fig. 24):
  - GDTL anechoic jet chamber, 6.2 × 5.6 × 3.4 m, fibreglass wedges with a 160 Hz cut-off. Air from 16 MPa tanks.
  - Converging nozzles, D = 19.05 mm, s/D = 3, mounted on the end of the plenum with acoustic foam (Fig. 24). Different internal contour and lip thickness from the Monash nozzles (p. 18). Re = 5.95 × 10⁵ (NPR 2.0) to 1.61 × 10⁶ (NPR 5.0).
  - Opposing B&K 4939 1/4-inch microphones, 9D from the respective nozzle centrelines, through a Nexus 2690 conditioner (20 Hz–100 kHz). NI PXI-6133 at 200 kHz; 819,200 points split into 8192-point segments, Δf = 24.4 Hz.
- **Ensemble spectra** (time-resolved data, block-averaged; p. 7–8):
  - CPSD over 2¹³-sample sub-records (eq. 3.1), coherence (eq. 3.2) and phase (eq. 3.3). That is Δf ≈ 30.5 Hz at 250 kHz (my arithmetic).
  - Phase is plotted as a magnitude folded into 0–180° wherever the coherence is ≥ 0.7 (p. 8).
  - Window and overlap are not stated.
  - The phase is the raw opposing-microphone phase: 0° goes with symmetric (mirror-image) coupling and 180° with antisymmetric coupling (p. 9, 20). Knast reports the same physics with a 180° jet-frame correction ("antisymmetric" = 0°), so the numbers are swapped between the two papers.
- **Band-pass Hilbert analysis** (needs time-resolved, single-component signals; p. 10–11):
  - The STFT was rejected as too coarse in time. The Hilbert route was preferred to wavelets after Huang (2014), although wavelets are noted to work for resonant jets (Mancinelli 2019) (p. 10).
  - Each process frequency is band-passed with a 200 Hz-wide filter, chosen so that tones do not overlap; filter type and order are not given. Then the analytic signal gives A(t) and θ(t) per microphone, and Δθ(t) = θ₁ − θ₂ (eqs 4.1–4.6).
  - The authors stress that the Hilbert transform needs a single statistical signal, which is why each process is isolated first (p. 10–11).
  - Outputs: joint PDFs of Δθ and A against NPR, each normalised per NPR (Fig. 6); interruption fraction and durations (Figs 7–8); joint amplitude histograms between the two microphones (Fig. 9) and between processes at one microphone (Fig. 10).
- **POD** (does not need time resolution; p. 18–20):
  - Snapshot POD (eqs 5.1–5.5) on the mean-subtracted **transverse velocity u_y only** (eq. 5.6). This keeps the shear-thickness mode (Weightman et al. 2018) out of the leading modes (p. 19–20).
  - No weighting or masking of the full field is described.
  - Sub-domain POD truncates at y/D = 0 and excludes the first shock cell (p. 22).
  - Conditional sampling keeps snapshots above the 75th percentile of |a_{1,2}| for one jet or for both, then repeats the POD (p. 23–24). Jack-knife convergence is reached at ≈ 500 snapshots (p. 25).
  - Phase animations: reconstruction from 20 modes, phase from the leading pair, 10° bins (p. 28).
- **Mode-to-tone link** (p. 25–27): St_Est = u_c/λ_D (normalised by D_j/u_j), with u_c = 0.6u_j taken from Knast et al. (2018) for these conditions. This is a frequency assignment by convective wavelength, not a measured frequency of the POD modes.
- **Modelling side.** There is no simulation or stability analysis. The paper uses:
  - twin-jet stability theory, cited only qualitatively: vortex-sheet and finite-thickness models (Morris 1990; Du 1993, 2003) show that twin jets tend to couple but leave out the upstream part of the loop (p. 3). Panickar et al. (2004) matched coupling margins with stability analysis (p. 3). PSE wavepackets for subsonic twin jets (Rodríguez et al. 2018) are cited for coupling changes as a staging mechanism (p. 9);
  - numerical work on twin jets, cited as motivation with the warning that resonance is sensitive to boundary conditions (Brès et al. 2013 LES; Goparaju & Gaitonde 2018; Weightman et al. 2019) (p. 3);
  - the conclusion's statement that coupling forms are "well predicted by stability theory" (p. 29), and the global-mode competition reading, neither of which is computed;
  - the only quantitative model, St_Est = u_c/λ_D with a fixed u_c/u_j.
- **Earlier literature summarised** (p. 3–4):
  - Seiner et al. (1986): 1/40-scale F-15 nozzles (D = 1.57 cm, s/D 1.9, NPR 1.89–7.8). They found fewer, wider and much louder stages than a single jet, with coupling about the symmetry plane.
  - Raman (1998): twin rectangular jets (NPR 1.89–4.6, s/D 5.5–15). An antisymmetric mode was followed by a symmetric one, with a "weak complex interaction" region that would not phase-lock.
  - Panickar et al. (2005) and Srinivasan et al. (2009) counted cross-bicoherence peaks as precursors of mode jumps.
  - Bell et al. (2018) PIV correlations: clear antisymmetry with a strong near-field standing wave at the steady condition, and no clear mode shape or standing wave in the anomaly.

## Key equations
- **CPSD** (eq. 3.1, p. 7): $\hat G_{xy}(f)=\dfrac{2}{n_dT}\sum_{i=1}^{n_d}X_i^*(f)\,Y_i(f)$, with n_d sub-records of length T.
- **Coherence** (eq. 3.2, p. 7): $\hat C_{xy}(f)=\dfrac{|G_{xy}(f)|^2}{G_{xx}(f)\,G_{yy}(f)}$.
- **CPSD phase** (eq. 3.3, p. 8): $\hat\theta_{xy}(f)=\tan^{-1}\!\big[\mathrm{Im}\,G_{xy}/\mathrm{Re}\,G_{xy}\big]$.
- **Hilbert transform and analytic signal** (eqs 4.1–4.5, p. 10): $\tilde x(t)=x(t)*(1/\pi t)$, $z(t)=x(t)+j\tilde x(t)=A(t)\,e^{j\theta(t)}$, $A=[x^2+\tilde x^2]^{1/2}$, $\theta=\tan^{-1}(\tilde x/x)$.
- **Inter-microphone phase** (eq. 4.6, p. 10): $\Delta\theta(t)=\theta_1(t)-\theta_2(t)$.
- **Interruption criterion** (p. 12, 30): interrupted when $A_{\rm dB}(t)<\mu-5$ dB, with μ the mean.
- **Snapshot POD** (eqs 5.1–5.5, p. 18–19): $X=[\hat x(t_1),\dots,\hat x(t_n)]$ (written ∈ ℝ^{n×m} in eq. 5.1, but m × n in eq. 5.6), $R=X^{T}X$, $R\Psi_j=\lambda_j\Psi_j$, $\phi_j=X\Psi_j/\sqrt{\lambda_j}$, $a_j(t)=X\cdot\phi_j$. Eq. 5.6 fills X with u_y only.
- **Pair amplitude** (p. 23): $|a_{1,2}|=\sqrt{a_1^2(t)+a_2^2(t)}$.
- **Tone estimate from a POD pair** (p. 27): $St_{Est}=u_c/\lambda_D$, i.e. $St=f D_j/u_j=(u_c/u_j)(D_j/\lambda_D)$ with u_c = 0.6u_j.
  > Checked (my arithmetic, isentropic D_j for a convergent nozzle): D_j/D = 1.138 at NPR 4.6 and 1.160 at NPR 5.0 give 0.6 × 1.160/3.5 = 0.199, 0.6 × 1.138/3.1 = 0.220 and 0.6 × 1.138/2.8 = 0.244, the tabulated 0.20, 0.22 and 0.24. So λ_D is in nozzle diameters D and St is based on D_j.
- **Null model for the envelope statistics** (my derivation, not in the paper). For a narrow-band Gaussian random signal (no deterministic tone), A is Rayleigh distributed, with $P(A<A_t)=1-\exp(-A_t^2/\langle A^2\rangle)$ and $\sigma(20\log_{10}A)=(10/\ln 10)\,\pi/\sqrt6=5.57$ dB.
  - Fraction of time below μ − 4, −5 and −6 dB: 20.0%, 16.3% and 13.2% with μ the mean of the dB envelope; 26.8%, 22.0% and 17.9% with μ = 20 log₁₀ of the mean linear envelope (Monte Carlo, 2 × 10⁶ samples).
  - Folded |Δθ| standard deviation for two jointly Gaussian signals: 42°, 35° and 24° at magnitude-squared coherence 0.5, 0.7 and 0.9; 52° for a uniform phase.
- **Inter-jet phase and the coupled pairs** (my derivation, idealised). Write the lower- and upper-jet pair coefficients as complex numbers L and U, and the symmetric and antisymmetric coupled coefficients as S = (L + U)/√2 and A = (L − U)/√2, with the sign convention absorbing the mirror. Then $|S|^2+|A|^2=|L|^2+|U|^2$ and $|S|^2-|A|^2=2|L||U|\cos\Delta\phi$, where Δφ is the inter-jet oscillation phase. So selecting snapshots with both |L| and |U| large confines (|S|, |A|) to an arc. Its two ends are locked symmetric or antisymmetric coupling; a wandering phase spreads the points along it.

"Antisymmetric" gives 180° raw phase, and corresponds to SA in the plane of the jets.

## Relevance to this project
This is the Shock Lab group's main paper on intermittent coupling of round twin jets in the m = 1 (B/C-type) screech range, and the bridge between [Knast 2018](knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) (Welch phase only, intermittency hypothesised) and [Wong 2023](wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md) (time-resolved schlieren at A1/A2). Its tools fit both halves of the CLAUDE.md workflow. Hilbert envelopes of time-resolved microphone signals give the "instantaneous amplitude and frequency" step. Conditional POD of non-time-resolved PIV gives the PIV step. Its weak points (see below) are the ones the SPOD → reconstruction → Hilbert POD pipeline is designed to avoid.

**How it fits the twin-jet literature**
- *Confirms Knast's hypothesis and reinterprets region 2.* Knast called NPR 3.45–4.35 "counter-rotating helical" from two-point correlations (that note, Table 2). Here the same NPR range in the same facility is a mixture: symmetric coupling, antisymmetric coupling, and one jet oscillating alone (p. 23–29). Bell et al. also narrow Knast's steady antisymmetric region 1 (NPR 2.3–3.4). Process 1 is narrow in phase (σ 2–6°) and amplitude (σ ≈ 1 dB) only over NPR ≈ 2.9–3.4; at 2.3–2.75 its phase is nearly uniform (σ 50–63°, against 52° for a uniform folded phase) (Fig. 27; my comparison).
- *Same three states as Wong 2023, at a different stage and spacing.* Wong found steady coupling, SS↔SA competition and uncoupling at A1/A2 (NPR 2.02–2.41), with uncoupling only at s/D = 6 (that note, p. 9–19). Here all three appear at s/D = 3 in the m = 1 range. Both papers put the intermittency at the boundary between two coupling states, and Wong cites this paper for facility dependence (that note, p. 4, 26).
- *Symmetric tone below antisymmetric tone.* At NPR 4.6 in facility two, the symmetric pair gives St 0.22 and the antisymmetric pair 0.23 (Table 2). In facility one, process 3 (0°) lies below process 1 (180°) by about 0.04 in St (Fig. 5 reading). Wong's SS also sits below SA, but by only 0.3–0.6% ([Wong 2023](wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 19), as twin-jet stability models predict ([Stavropoulos 2023](stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 20, 22). The split here is 5–15%, about ten times larger, and comes with a 10% POD wavelength difference (3.1D against 2.8D). That suggests a different loop closure per symmetry, not merely a phase-speed change. The four m = 1 KH symmetries share one phase speed in [Nogueira 2021](nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md) (p. 15–17), so a fixed u_c with different λ requires the resonance condition itself to differ. This is my inference.
- *Global-mode competition.* The conclusion (p. 29) is the origin of the "competing global modes" reading used by [Wong 2023](wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md) (p. 26, 39–41) and [Stavropoulos 2023](stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) (higher-gain loop wins). [Stavropoulos 2023](stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) note that their coupled-jet models cannot reproduce the uncoupled single-jet state seen here. [Rodríguez 2023](rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md) cite it for intermittency between uncorrelated and coupled states (that note, p. 3). The LES analogue in rectangular twins is AA/AS competition ([Jeun 2024](jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), p. 9–10).
- *Same PIV data as Nogueira 2021.* At NPR 5.0 the steady symmetric pair here is the 1SS pair there, and [Nogueira 2021](nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md) explain it by the 1SS guided-mode band (p. 18–21).
  - The obvious next model test is the NPR 4.6 condition: the same azimuthal-Floquet LSA on the NPR 4.6 mean flow, checking whether the 1SS and 1SA guided-mode bands both reach the two tones (0.22, 0.23).
  - If both do, that would support competition between two closable loops. If only one does, the other tone needs another explanation (for example process 2 rather than process 1; see check item 9).
  - This is a suggestion; nobody has run it.

**Reading it critically (my analysis)**
- **The "wide" acoustic states look statistically like narrow-band Gaussian noise.**
  - A Rayleigh envelope has σ = 5.57 dB in dB. Process 2 has σ ≈ 5.5–6.0 dB at every NPR, and process 1 has σ ≈ 5.5–5.8 dB above NPR 4.0 and 5–7.5 dB at NPR 2.3–2.45 (Fig. 27).
  - The ≈ 20% interruption plateau, and its change from ≈ 24% to 20% to 16% for thresholds of 4, 5 and 6 dB (Fig. 23), fall inside the Rayleigh range for a μ − 5 dB threshold: 16.3% to 22.0% depending on how μ is defined, falling by about 3–5 points per dB.
  - The phase spreads of 32–49° match two partially coherent Gaussian signals with γ² ≈ 0.4–0.8, consistent with the coherence ≥ 0.7 the processes were picked by.
  - So these "interruptions" may be the ordinary fading of a stochastically driven, damped resonance. They need not be on/off switching of a limit cycle; in a limit cycle with dropouts, the fraction would not track the threshold this way. Only process 1 at NPR 2.9–3.4 and process 3 (σ ≈ 1–2.5 dB) look like saturated limit cycles. The spikes at NPR 3.8 (process 1) and 4.5–4.65 (process 3) look like genuine intermittency between states.
  - This does not overturn the global-mode reading. It refines it: in the anomaly, neither coupled mode seems to saturate, which fits two weakly damped or marginal modes excited by turbulence.
  - The amplitude-threshold method needs this null hypothesis, as wavelet power needs a χ² background ([Torrence 1998](torrence-1998-practical-guide-wavelet-analysis.md), p. 69–71).
- **Filter time resolution against event durations.**
  - A 200 Hz pass band implies an envelope time scale of order 1/(200 Hz) = 5 ms, about 50 cycles at 10 kHz.
  - The reported interruptions (5–15 cycles, 0.5–1.5 ms) and coupled intervals (20–30 cycles) are shorter than that. Either the filter's effective band is much wider (type and order are not given), or the durations are threshold-crossing times of slow fades.
  - If the band is wider, processes 1 and 2, about 0.02 apart in St (≈ 800 Hz at NPR 4), would leak into each other and beat with a period of about 12 screech cycles. That would also produce 5–15-cycle dips.
  - A strong process 1 leaking into the weak process 2 band at NPR 3.5–3.9 would explain why process 2 shows 20% while process 1 shows ≈ 2% there.
  - No beat test (envelope period = 1/Δf; [Wong 2023](wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md) p. 18, 36; [Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md) p. 10–11) is reported.
- **One microphone per jet is assumed, not shown.** The far jet is only about 3D farther away (a spreading loss of roughly 2–3 dB at 8D, my estimate) and partly shielded. With cross-talk c, a wandering inter-jet phase modulates each microphone by up to 20 log₁₀[(1 + c)/(1 − c)] dB, which is about 10 dB for c = 0.5. The two microphones then vary together or in opposition depending on the propagation phase difference between the jets.
  - So the corner and fan shapes in Fig. 9 cannot by themselves say which jet stopped.
  - The schlieren clips and the conditional POD are independent evidence that single-jet episodes exist (p. 23, 28–29), but they give no durations.
- **The empty corners of Fig. 21 follow largely from the selection.**
  - Once both jets are in their upper quartile, |S|² + |A|² is bounded below, so the lower-left corner must be empty. And |S|² − |A|² = 2|L||U| cos Δφ, so both coefficients cannot be large together (Key equations).
  - The physical information is the density of points along the arc. Clustering at its ends means locked coupling; uniform spreading means a random inter-jet phase. Fig. 21 shows points spread along the arc with some concentration towards both ends, which is suggestive, not decisive.
  - The different POD wavelengths of the two coupled pairs (3.1D against 2.8D) are better evidence that they are distinct structures and not rotations of the same two single-jet pairs.
  - The statement that each jet alone has "only a single wavelength" (p. 27, 29) then means only that per-jet POD did not separate the two. A 10% wavelength difference is easily merged into one POD pair.
- **The selection statistics confirm weak coupling.** If the two jets' amplitudes were independent, 0.25 × 0.25 × 9500 ≈ 594 snapshots would fall in both upper quartiles, against ≈ 650 reported (p. 24). That is only 9% above independence (my arithmetic), consistent with "weak correlation" (p. 23).
- **u_y-only POD hides the amplitude marker.** The shear-thickness mode that [Weightman 2019](weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md) (p. 47) and [Nogueira 2021](nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md) (p. 11–13; same NPR 5 data) use to track amplitude modulation without time resolution is removed by design (p. 19–20). It reappears only as the discarded fifth mode of the conditioned set (p. 25). Keeping u and v, or symmetry-imposed POD, would give a per-jet amplitude label independent of the pair magnitude.
- **The tones are not exclusive, unlike A1/A2.** At NPR 5.0, process 3 is steady while processes 1 and 2 come and go (p. 16). This differs from the exclusive A1/A2 switching of [Mancinelli 2019](mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) (p. 6–7) and [Mazharmanesh 2026](mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) (ρ = −0.82, p. 11–12). If processes 1 and 2 are noise-like (see the first point), "coexistence" may mean a saturated loop plus weakly excited damped ones, not two active loops.

**Methods and data for the lab workflow (CLAUDE.md)**
- **Time-resolved data** (microphones here; schlieren and LES in the lab):
  - CPSD coherence at all frequencies, not just at the peak, is a cheap way to find candidate processes (p. 8–9).
  - Band-pass Hilbert envelopes and phases give per-process JPDFs and event statistics (p. 10–14). They need single-component bands, a stated filter, a Rayleigh or χ² null, and a beat test.
  - In the SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD pipeline:
    1. Reconstruct each process band from SPOD ([Nekkanti 2021](nekkanti-2021-spod-reconstruction-denoising-frequency-time.md)) separately for the even and odd fields about the midplane, and for each half-domain (as in [Wong 2023](wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md)).
    2. Run HPOD_t ([Raiola 2025](raiola-2025-hilbert-pod.md)). This gives per-jet and per-symmetry instantaneous amplitude and phase directly, removes the microphone-shielding assumption, and gives the inter-jet phase Δφ(t) whose distribution is what Fig. 21 tries to infer.
  - The time–frequency trade-off is set by the band width. Here ΔSt ≈ 0.01–0.04 between processes, i.e. a 4–15% separation (much easier than Wong's 0.3–0.6%), so bands of a few hundred Hz resolve events of tens of cycles at best.
  - Target conditions for the group's twin-jet schlieren: s/D = 3, NPR 3.4–4.4 (facility one) or about 4.6 (facility two).
- **Non-time-resolved PIV:**
  - Snapshot POD, sub-domain POD and conditional POD on pair amplitudes work at 0.5 Hz (p. 17–27). About 650 conditioned snapshots converged, in line with the ≈ 500-snapshot budget for a leading pair in [Weightman 2019](weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md) (p. 56–57).
  - The PIV cannot give durations, frequencies or the order of states.
  - The missing link is a microphone sampled at each PIV snapshot. Conditioning snapshots on the instantaneous Hilbert amplitude and phase of each process would tie the two halves of this paper together (suggestion).
  - HPOD_x or Davey's pairing test ([Davey 2025](davey-2025-hpod-sphere-wake.md)) could pair the four unpaired NPR 4.6 modes objectively.
- **Facility sensitivity:** identical nozzles on two Monash rigs give different process-3 onset NPRs because of enclosure reflections and a flush plenum face (p. 6, 17–18). Record the acoustic environment and upstream surfaces for any comparison, as for single-jet staging.

**Modelling side and the PyFR staging LES**
- The paper offers no model beyond St = u_c/λ. Its claims set three targets for models:
  - two coupled global modes with close frequencies (5–15% apart) whose loops close in different symmetries;
  - a range of NPR where neither saturates;
  - an uncoupled single-jet state between them.
- Linear models: twin-jet vortex-sheet and finite-thickness resonance models give SS/SA bands but assume coupled jets ([Stavropoulos 2023](stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md); [Stavropoulos 2024](stavropoulos-2024-upstream-waves-merging-twin-jets.md)), and Floquet LSA picks 1SS at NPR 5 ([Nogueira 2021](nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md)). A complex-valued, gain-bearing resonance model per symmetry, or a global analysis of the twin-jet mean flow at NPR 4.6, would test the competition reading.
- A stochastically forced amplitude equation (Landau or Stuart–Landau with noise) for two coupled modes would test the Rayleigh-like statistics. This is my suggestion; nothing in the library does it yet.
- `Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/` is a single round jet at A1/A2, not twin jets, but the paper's tools carry over to LES probe data:
  - band-pass Hilbert envelopes, a mean − 5 dB interruption statistic, and durations in cycles. Compare them with the Rayleigh null, since short LES records give noisy envelope PDFs;
  - JPDFs of two tones' envelopes to test exclusivity against coexistence.
- The "process" definition (a continuous frequency branch = one loop) is a useful classifier for continuation: a jump between branches marks a staging event, and a widening σ of a branch's envelope (Fig. 27) is an early warning near the A1/A2 boundary. That is analogous to process 1 being noisy just after its onset at NPR 2.3–2.6 and locking only at 2.9–3.4.
- Near a saddle-node, the 1/t_switch fit in the continuation driver assumes a deterministic transition. Rayleigh-like statistics would indicate noise-driven switching, where dwell times follow escape-rate (Kramers-type) statistics instead (my suggestion).

**Rocket noise.** The introduction motivates the work with twin-jet resonance fatigue on the B-1B and F-15 (p. 1, 3). Full-scale screech is absent in rocket data ([Lubert 2022](lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md)). For clustered nozzles, the transferable points are the facility sensitivity of coupling and the need to separate per-plume from coupled sources.
