## Summary
The authors measure an underexpanded round jet from a 15 mm convergent nozzle at NPR 3.4 (M_j 1.45). It screeches at St 0.30 in what they identify as the helical C mode. The data are 8000 non-time-resolved planar PIV fields from two stitched cameras (p. 825–827, 829). Snapshot POD of the transverse velocity gives a leading pair (15.3% + 13.7% of transverse energy) whose coefficients lie on a ring. The two-mode reconstruction is used for a Hussain–Reynolds triple decomposition into mean, coherent and random parts (p. 833–835). The shocks strongly modulate the coherent axial stress, which peaks just upstream of each shock–boundary reflection point. They modulate the random stress only weakly, and its maxima sit just downstream. Spatial EMD of the radially integrated coherent stress separates this modulation from underlying growth, which saturates near x/D ≈ 7.5–8, about the 6th cell (p. 835–837, 844). Coherent transverse motion of the shock cells reaches the centreline, which the authors take to show a single helix rather than Umeda & Ishii's double helix (p. 838). Peaks of u′ kurtosis with strong negative skewness at the 2nd–4th reflection points, and a band of strongest coherent vorticity fluctuation on the high-speed side of the shear layer over cells 2–4, lead to the paper's proposal (p. 839–845): the dominant screech sources are where the vortices most weaken the local vorticity at the sonic line, letting the shock leak in the Suzuki & Lele (2003) sense, not where the vortices are most energetic. The mechanism is argued from published theory; no model or simulation is run.

## Key findings
- **Tone** (p. 829, Fig. 4): a single fundamental at St = f D_j/U_j = 0.30 plus harmonics. The peak is about 162 dB at St 0.30, with the second harmonic about 133 dB at St 0.6 (read from figure). The room is not anechoic; the spectrum serves only to show that one tone is present (p. 829).
  - At T₀ ≈ 288 K this is f ≈ 7.7 kHz, with U_j ≈ 413 m s⁻¹ and D_j ≈ 1.07D = 16.0 mm (my isentropic arithmetic; the paper gives no frequency in Hz).
- **Mean and RMS fields** (§3.2, p. 830–832; Figs 6–7):
  - Centreline v′ shows broad peaks and troughs at the shock spacing. They are far too broad to come from particle lag, since an artificial peak extends at most about 0.2D even for a strong normal shock (Mitchell et al. 2013). They are read as radial displacement and tilting of the shock cells: small at the 2nd cell, stronger from the 3rd onwards (p. 830–832).
  - Shear-layer u′ is not growing monotonically. It rises just before the shock meets the shear layer and drops sharply after the reflection point, as Raman & Rice (1994) found. The modulation of v′ in the shear layer is much weaker (p. 832).
  - Centreline mean U/U_e reaches about 1.68 in the first cell; shear-layer u′/U_e is about 0.15–0.23 (Fig. 7, read from figure).
- **POD** (§4.2, p. 834–835; Figs 8–9):
  - POD of v′ alone separates the first two modes from the rest "much" more clearly than POD of both components, and leaves the mode shapes essentially unchanged (p. 834).
  - Mode 1: 15.3%; mode 2: 13.7% (together "almost 30%" of transverse fluctuating energy); mode 3: 2.5% (Fig. 8). Modes 1 and 2 have the same spatial wavelength. Modes 1 and 3 show no correlation (p. 834).
  - The (a₁, a₂)/⟨r_m⟩ phase portrait is a ring, so the pair is one oscillation. (a₁, a₃) is a vertical smear (Fig. 9).
  - Mode shapes (Fig. 8): u is antisymmetric about the axis and v symmetric (the whole jet moves sideways). This is the planar signature of an |m| = 1 mode. The v structures alternate at about 2.4D from x/D ≈ 4 onwards (read from figure). [edgington-mitchell-2020](edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) gives k_kh D = 2.6 (λ ≈ 2.4D) for this dataset.
- **Triple decomposition: axial stresses** (p. 835–837; Figs 10–11):
  - The total axial stress is confined to the shear layer and strongly modulated by the shock cells (p. 835–836).
  - The coherent axial stress has a cellular structure. Its local maximum lies just before the shock reflection point of each cell (p. 836).
  - The random axial stress has its local maxima in the shear layer just downstream of the reflection points, almost exactly where the coherent stress has its minima. Its modulation is "far less clear", except at the 1st reflection point, where the shock modulates random stress more than coherent stress. No modes 1–10 show a short-lived coherent structure there (p. 837).
  - Colour scales: total up to 0.05U_e², coherent up to 0.015U_e², random up to 0.04U_e² (Fig. 10). The coherent part is thus about 30% of the total at its peak (my reading).
  - Integrated coherent axial stress π∫₀^y (u^c u^c/U_e²) y dy (Fig. 11a) grows with x, with a superposed shock modulation. EMD (Huang, Shen & Long 1999) splits it as follows (p. 836–837):
    - The first IMF (modulation) grows to a maximum at the 5th shock cell and then decays.
    - The second IMF (growth) rises until x/D ≈ 7.5 and then decays: the coherent structures saturate at about the 6th cell.
- **Triple decomposition: transverse stresses** (p. 837–838; Fig. 12):
  - There is little coherent transverse stress in the shear layer until the 2nd or 3rd cell, and even there it is small compared with the random part.
  - Coherent v′v′ is concentrated *inside* the shock cells from the 3rd onwards, with its maximum in the 6th. These pockets are bounded upstream by the oblique shocks, so they come from tilting and translation of the shock cells rather than directly from the shear-layer vortices. Alkislar et al. (2003) saw the same in a flapping rectangular jet.
  - **Single helix:** the coherent shock motion moves the centreline radially. By continuity, a double helix would leave the centreline (and so the shocks) radially still. The authors conclude that at this NPR the instability is a single helix and Umeda & Ishii's (2002a) double helix "does not seem to exist" (p. 838, 845).
  - The random transverse stress lies entirely in the shear layer, with maxima just downstream of the reflection points (p. 838).
- **Higher-order statistics of u′** (§5.1.1, p. 839–841; Figs 13–14):
  - *Skewness* changes from negative on the high-speed side of the shear layer (intrusions of fast core fluid) to positive on the low-speed side (intrusions of slow ambient fluid) (p. 839).
    - The low-speed-side skewness is largest before each reflection point and smaller after it, matching the coherent-stress modulation.
    - The high-speed-side negative skewness is unaffected by the 1st reflection point. It is strongly modulated by the 2nd, peaking a short distance downstream of it, and the cycle repeats with diminishing strength (p. 840).
    - The two high-speed sides meet by x/D ≥ 6.0, which marks the end of the potential core (p. 840).
  - *Excess kurtosis* K (0 for a Gaussian) is platykurtic in the shear-layer centre and leptokurtic at both edges, read as intermittent intrusion of core or ambient fluid (p. 840).
    - At the 2nd cell there is a strongly leptokurtic spot just upstream of the reflection point on the low-speed side, and a local maximum just downstream of it on the high-speed side. These repeat at the 3rd and 4th cells (p. 840).
    - The radial profiles (Fig. 14) put the highest excess kurtosis at the 2nd–4th reflection points, strongest at the 3rd. The peak moves towards the axis with downstream distance (p. 840).
    - Read from Fig. 14 (see Data tables): K peaks of about 1.5, 4.5, 7.1, 6.3 and 4.8 at the 1st–5th reflection points, at y/D ≈ 0.48, 0.41, 0.30, 0.20 and 0.15. The 5th is therefore about as high as the 2nd.
- **Coherent vorticity** (§5.1.2, p. 841–842; Figs 15–16):
  - Near the nozzle the high- and low-speed sides have similar coherent vorticity fluctuation. By the 2nd cell the high-speed side is "significantly" higher.
  - The strongest fluctuation runs from upstream of the 2nd reflection point to downstream of the 4th, peaks near the 3rd, and is highly localised on the high-speed side. Total vorticity fluctuation is spread more evenly (p. 841–842).
  - Read from Fig. 16(b): ω_z^c D/U_e peaks of about 0.37 at the 1st reflection point (low-speed side), 0.53, 0.60, 0.55 and 0.45 at the 2nd–5th, moving inwards from y/d ≈ 0.41 to 0.30.
  - The coherent energy keeps growing to about the 6th cell (x/D ≈ 8), but the coherent vorticity fluctuation falls from the 4th cell onwards (p. 844).
- **Proposed source-selection mechanism** (§5.2, p. 842–845):
  - *Kurtosis as a proxy.* The DNS/LES of Manning & Lele (2000) and Mitchell's (2013) high-speed schlieren show the shock tip protruding rapidly past its usual reflection point into the high-speed side as a vortex passes. That intermittent, cyclic protrusion should leave locally low velocity behind the shock, giving high kurtosis with strong negative skewness. The kurtosis peaks match the 2nd–4th cells named as dominant sources in the literature (p. 842–843).
    - The authors stress that this is correlation, not causation (p. 843).
    - The radial drift of the kurtosis peak towards the axis is put down to the shear layer eating into the core, which moves the sonic line and so the shock tip's oscillation centre inwards (p. 842).
  - *Why cells 2–4.*
    - Raman (1997) showed that shock strength barely affects screech amplitude once it exceeds a minimum.
    - Tam (1995) argued that sources sit where the instability has grown enough.
    - But here the coherent energy is still growing at cells 5–6, and the 5th cell is only slightly weaker than the 4th in the mean (Fig. 7). So neither shock strength nor instability amplitude explains the selection (p. 843).
  - *Proposal.* Suzuki & Lele (2003) showed that an unperturbed shear layer totally reflects the shock (eqs. 5.1–5.2), and that the wavefront normal rotates with the local vorticity (eq. 5.3), so leakage needs locally weakened vorticity. The dominant sources should therefore be the reflection points where the passing vortices produce the largest coherent vorticity fluctuation on the high-speed side, i.e. cells 2–4, not the 6th cell where the vortices are most energetic (p. 843–844).
  - *Caveats the authors give.*
    - The shock–vortex interaction is two-way: the shocks modulate the instability amplitude and convective velocity, so cause and effect are hard to separate (p. 844).
    - Kurtosis and coherent vorticity are not the same phenomenon. At the 3rd reflection point (Fig. 17) the coherent vorticity is only about 10% higher than half a cell downstream, and it is high along the whole 2nd–4th band, whereas the excess kurtosis at the shock is "more than double" its upstream and downstream values and is confined to a small region around each reflection point (p. 844–845).
- **Literature on source cells cited** (p. 823, 838–839):
  - Umeda & Ishii (2001): the effective C-mode source rotates around the axis at the rear of the 3rd shock cell (p. 823); six sources at the trailing edges of the first six cells, dominant at the 3rd (p. 838).
  - Umeda & Ishii (2002b): six rotating sources, strongest just downstream of the 2nd and 3rd cells (p. 823).
  - Umeda & Ishii (2002a): a Mach cone from the moving sources, and an apparent double helix (p. 823–824).
  - Imamoglu, Baysal & Balakumar (2007) LES: 2nd and 3rd cells.
  - Gao & Li (2010) CAA: first five cells, strongest 2nd–4th (p. 838–839).
  - The authors call the 2nd–4th reflection points the "general consensus" for the dominant sources, whose superposition drives the feedback (p. 839).

## Setup / method
- **Facility** (p. 825, Fig. 2): LTRAC Supersonic Jet Facility, a Shock Lab group facility; the paper does not name a lab space.
  - Monash compressed air at about 288 K through a 40 mm line to a mild-steel mixing chamber with the smoke inlet, then 600 mm of 50 mm wire-wound hose and a conical diffuser with meshes to the plenum.
  - The plenum holds a honeycomb and four meshes of decreasing cell size.
  - A Fairchild 100 regulator holds the delivery pressure to within about 1%. Stagnation pressure is read by an RS-461 transducer (0–10 bar, ±0.25%).
- **Nozzle** (p. 825): purely converging, M_e = 1, CNC-machined from one block of mild steel. Radius of curvature 67.15 mm, a parallel section at the exit, D = 15 mm, roughness R_a ≈ 0.02 µm by tactile comparison. The lip is sharp-edged and 5 mm thick (t/D = 0.33). The contraction ratio and exit boundary-layer state are not given here.
- **Condition** (p. 825): NPR = p₀/p_a = 3.4, M_j = 1.45.
  - Re = 6.8 × 10⁵, at the jet exit with isentropic temperature and pressure. I reproduce it with sonic-exit density, U_e = 310 m s⁻¹, D and μ at about 288 K (6.9 × 10⁵, my arithmetic).
  - The later papers' 8.6 × 10⁵ (2020) and 8.1 × 10⁵ (2021) for the same dataset match ideally expanded ρ_j U_j D_j/μ_j at T₀ = 288 K and 298 K (my arithmetic). So the three values are different definitions, not different data.
- **Schlieren** (p. 826, 830; Fig. 5):
  - Toepler Z-type with mirrors of 2032 mm focal length (Mitchell, Honnery & Soria 2012), PCO Dimax 2048 × 2048 px, pulsed LED (Willert, Mitchell & Soria 2012). Knife edge for ∂ρ/∂x, background-subtracted.
  - Only one instantaneous image is shown (Fig. 5), though the overview mentions high-resolution and ultrahigh-speed images (p. 825). It shows upstream screech fronts, downstream radiation labelled "Mach wave radiation", and a sinuous bright band from the 2nd–3rd cell "indicative (although not conclusively so)" of a helical mode (p. 830).
- **Acoustics** (p. 826–827, 829):
  - G.R.A.S. 46BE 1/4" microphone (20 Hz–100 kHz) sampled at 250 kHz by an NI DAQ, calibrated with a G.R.A.S. 42AB.
  - Placed upstream at β = 30° from the centreline. The distance is given as 6D from the effective source at the 3rd shock cell, 4D downstream of the nozzle (p. 826), but the Fig. 4 caption says 6D from the nozzle exit (p. 829). Either way it is a near-field position in a reflective room (p. 829).
  - Spectra by sliding-window FFT with 50% overlap and a Hann window. Block length and record length are not stated (p. 826–827).
- **PIV** (§2.3, p. 827–829; Table 1):
  - Two PCO 4000 cameras (4008 × 2760 px) side by side with 105 mm Micro-Nikkor lenses at f/5.6, after Herpin et al. (2008). Combined field 10D (x, nozzle exit at the image edge) × 2.2D (y, centred on the axis).
  - New Wave Solo Nd:YAG (532 nm, 120 mJ), sheet 1.5 mm thick (p. 827).
  - Multigrid cross-correlation (MCCDPIV, Soria 1996) from 64 px to 16 px windows (0.10D → 0.026D) on an 8 px (0.013D) grid. Maximum displacement 12 px = 0.02D. Depth of field 2.4 mm (Table 1).
  - The two fields are stitched by convolution with an adaptive Gaussian window over a 7.5% overlap (Agüí & Jiménez) (p. 827).
  - **8000 fields at 0.5 Hz** (p. 827). That is about 4.4 h of running if acquired continuously (my arithmetic), over which the regulator allows about 1% drift in NPR (p. 825). Mode stability over the acquisition is not checked.
  - Seeding: Vicount 1300 smoke (nominal 300 nm) in both jet and ambient. Particle images are diffraction-limited at about 1.2 px; the velocity histogram shows minimal peak locking (p. 827).
  - Validation, outlier removal and Δt are not stated. Taking the 12 px maximum displacement at about 1.7U_e gives Δt ≈ 0.6 µs (my estimate).
- **Particle response** (p. 828–829; Fig. 3, Table 2):
  - Melling's method gives τ_p = 0.48 µs for 300 nm. The measured value across a Mach disk at NPR 4.2 (Mitchell et al. 2013) is **τ_p = 2.0 µs**, equivalent to 600 nm particles, which is comparable with the best tracers in the literature (Ragni et al. 2011).
  - Stokes number Stk = τ_p/τ_f ≈ 0.04, with τ_f = D/U_e (eq. 2.1). At the screech frequency 2πfτ_p ≈ 0.1 (my arithmetic, f ≈ 7.7 kHz).
  - Shock-induced artificial fluctuation follows Soria's (2006) noiseless correlation, applied to shock flows by Mitchell et al. (2011). For a normal shock at the first cell of an elliptic jet at the same NPR it is 4% (monodisperse) or 7% (log-normal), against 7% measured. Here no normal shock is resolvable, so the artificial fluctuation is "significantly lower": 3% measured above the local level (Table 2, p. 829).
  - The relaxation length U τ_p ≈ 1 mm (≈ 0.07D) is larger than the 0.39 mm final window and Δt < τ_p, so the set-up sits inside Ragni et al.'s no-bias criteria (my arithmetic; [ragni-2011](ragni-2011-particle-tracer-response-shocks-piv.md)).
- **POD and triple decomposition** (§4, p. 832–835):
  - Snapshot POD (Sirovich 1987; Kostas, Soria & Chong 2005) of the fluctuations about the 8000-field mean. The autocorrelation matrix is built from v′ only (p. 834).
  - Phase from the leading pair, â e^{iφ} = a₁ + i a₂ (eq. 4.11), following Oberleithner et al. (2011). The authors note that POD needs a "clear phase-coherent component", which holds only for self-excited or forced flows (p. 834).
  - The coherent field u^c is rebuilt from the first two **spatial** modes. The temporal coefficients serve only to show that the pair is coupled (phase portrait) (p. 835). The text allows phase-bin averaging of snapshots as an alternative (p. 834–835) but does not say which was used, how many phase bins there were, or how the axial components of the modes (Fig. 8) were obtained from a v-only POD (presumably by projecting u′ onto a_i).
  - The random part is u″ = u′ − u^c, so everything outside the pair is "random" (p. 835).
  - The authors preferred POD phase to microphone phase-locking (Alkislar et al. 2003; Henderson et al. 2005) because it uses the whole field and is less sensitive to turbulence jitter (p. 833).
  - **Data needs:** none of this needs time resolution. It needs one dominant periodic oscillation and enough uncorrelated snapshots. Amplitude modulation, other tones and switching all go into u″.
- **Higher-order statistics:** single-point skewness and excess kurtosis of u′ from the 8000 independent samples (Figs 13–14). No time resolution is needed. Convergence and outlier treatment are not discussed. For Gaussian data and N = 8000 the standard errors are about 0.03 (skewness) and 0.05 (kurtosis) (my arithmetic), so the peaks are not sampling noise, but spurious vectors near shocks would inflate the fourth moment.
- **EMD** (p. 836–837): applied to a *spatial* signal, the radial integral of the coherent axial stress against x. The first IMF is taken as the shock modulation and the second as the growth. Sifting and stopping criteria, and end-effect handling, are not given. The signal holds only about seven shock periods.
- **Modelling and simulation side.** No model, stability analysis or simulation is computed. The argument uses:
  - Manning (1999) DNS: an instability wave makes the shock oscillate, and the compression front of a cylindrical acoustic wave forms during the upstream phase of the oscillation, between vortices, with the shock deforming near its boundary reflection point (p. 824).
  - Manning & Lele (2000) linearised Euler simulations.
  - Suzuki & Lele (2003) DNS and eikonal theory: an undisturbed shear layer totally reflects the shock; leakage needs weakened local vorticity; eqs. 5.1–5.3 (p. 824, 843–844).
  - Berland, Bogey & Bailly (2007) planar-jet LES: shocks leak through the saddle point between vortices (p. 824).
  - Imamoglu et al. (2007) LES and Gao & Li (2010) CAA for source cells (p. 838–839).
  - How well models match this experiment is not tested. The leakage geometry was later computed on this dataset by ray tracing ([edgington-mitchell-2021-generation](edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md), p. 19–20).

## Key equations
All checked against rendered pages.

- **Stokes number** (eq. 2.1, p. 828): $Stk=\tau_p/\tau_f\approx 0.04$, with $\tau_f=D/U_e$.
- **Reynolds and triple decompositions** (eqs. 4.1–4.3, p. 832–833): $\mathbf u=\mathbf U+\mathbf u'$; $\mathbf u(\mathbf x,t)=\mathbf U(\mathbf x)+\mathbf u^c(\mathbf x,t)+\mathbf u''(\mathbf x,t)$; phase-averaged field $\mathbf u^p=\mathbf U+\mathbf u^c$.
- **Snapshot POD** (eqs. 4.4–4.10, p. 833–834):
  - $\mathbf u'(\mathbf x,t_k)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}a_i(t_k)\boldsymbol\psi_i(\mathbf x)+\mathbf u_{res}$ (eq. 4.5).
  - $\mathbf R=\mathbf U^{\mathsf T}\mathbf U$ (eq. 4.6), where $\mathbf U$ stacks u′ and v′ at all l × m grid points column by column (eq. 4.7). In practice only v′ was used (p. 834).
  - $\mathbf R\mathbf a_i=\lambda_i\mathbf a_i$ (eq. 4.8), with $\lambda_1>\lambda_2>\dots>\lambda_N>0$.
  - Normalisation $\overline{a_ia_j}=\lambda\delta_{ij}$ (eq. 4.9).
  - $\boldsymbol\psi_i(\mathbf x)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{N\lambda_i}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_i(t_k)\mathbf u'(\mathbf x,t_k)$ (eq. 4.10). It is printed with summation index j over a t_k summand, a typo.
  - λ is called "twice the amount of fluctuating kinetic energy" in each mode (p. 834); with v′ only, it is transverse energy only.
- **POD phase** (eq. 4.11, p. 834): $\hat a_k e^{\mathrm i\phi_k}=a_1^k+\mathrm i a_2^k$. Phase-portrait radius $r_m=\sqrt{a_1^2+a_2^2}$ (Fig. 9).
- **Critical angle for total internal reflection** (eq. 5.1, p. 843; from Suzuki & Lele 2003): $\theta_1^{cr}\equiv\arccos\!\left(\dfrac{-1}{a_2/a_1+M_1}\right)$, with a₁, a₂ the sound speeds on the two sides of the jet boundary.
- **Mach angle of the incident wave** (eq. 5.2, p. 843): $\theta_1^{M}\equiv\arccos(-1/M_1)$.
  - Since $a_2/a_1>0$, $\theta_1^{M}>\theta_1^{cr}$ for any M₁, which is why an undisturbed shear layer always totally reflects the shock (p. 843). For example, $\theta^{M}\approx134°$ and $\theta^{cr}\approx113°$ for M₁ = 1.45 and a₂/a₁ ≈ 1.15 (my arithmetic, assuming a₂/a₁ ≈ 1.15 for a cold jet).
  - The angle convention is not defined here; check it against Suzuki & Lele 2003.
- **Vorticity rotation of the wavefront normal** (eq. 5.3, p. 844; eikonal result of Suzuki & Lele): $\mathbf R_v=\tfrac12\begin{bmatrix}0&-\omega\\ \omega&0\end{bmatrix}$. Strong local vorticity rotates the ray into a closed loop (reflection); weakened vorticity lets it through (p. 844).

## Notation
- D = 15 mm nozzle diameter; D_j, U_j: ideally expanded diameter and velocity; U_e = 310 m s⁻¹: sonic exit velocity, used for all velocity scaling; NPR = p₀/p_a; M_j; M_e = 1; St = f D_j/U_j.
- x, y: axial and transverse coordinates (Fig. 16 labels y/d); the shear-layer line in Fig. 7 is the locus of maximum mean vorticity.
- u, v: axial and transverse velocity; U, u′, u^c, u″: mean, total fluctuation, coherent (POD pair) and random parts; u^p = U + u^c.
- ψ_i, a_i, λ_i: spatial POD mode, temporal coefficient and eigenvalue; N = 8000 snapshots; r_m: pair radius; φ_k: snapshot phase.
- S(u′), K(u′): skewness and excess kurtosis (K = 0 for a Gaussian).
- ω_z: through-plane vorticity; ω′_z, ω_z^c, ω″_z: total, coherent and random fluctuation.
- τ_p, τ_f, Stk: particle relaxation time, flow time scale D/U_e and Stokes number.
- θ₁^cr, θ₁^M, a₁, a₂, M₁: critical angle, incident Mach angle, sound speeds and Mach number in Suzuki & Lele's model; R_v: rotation tensor.
- Shock cell: the roughly conical region from the centreline shock to where it reflects from the jet boundary as an expansion (p. 823). The reflection points number the cells.
- Modes A1, A2 (axisymmetric), B (sinuous/lateral), C (helical), D (lateral) after Powell (1953) and Davies & Oldfield (1962) (p. 823).

## Relevance to this project
This is the first paper of the group's screech-PIV thread and the origin of the NPR 3.4 C-mode dataset. The leakage note has to be read alongside it:
- [Edgington-Mitchell 2014, Mach disk](edgington-mitchell-2014-underexpanded-jet-mach-disk-shear-layer.md) (NPR 4.2, same rig) reuses its v-only POD and triple decomposition.
- [Edgington-Mitchell 2018](edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) and [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves](edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) turned the same POD pair into wavenumber-filtered KH and G-JM waves (k_kh D = 2.6, k_s D = 4.6 at NPR 3.4).
- [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) ray-traced this dataset and partly revised this paper's selection rule.
- For the user's analysis it is a template for the CLAUDE.md step-2 route for non-time-resolved PIV: POD pair, then phase, then triple decomposition.

**Reading it critically**
- **Source location agrees across methods at M_j 1.45.** The strongest kurtosis and coherent vorticity at the 3rd reflection point (x/D ≈ 4.35) match four independent results at this Mach number:
  - first leakage at the 3rd cell, x/D ≈ 4.3, by ray tracing on this very dataset (2021 leakage note, p. 19);
  - Raman's 3rd-shock source at M_j 1.45 ([raman-1998](raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) p. 71–73);
  - [Mercier 2017](mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md)'s 3rd shock tip at M_j 1.45 (a flapping case, p. 221);
  - Umeda & Ishii's rotating C-mode source at the 3rd cell (p. 823).

  All contradict a source at the instability-wave peak (6th cell here; [Tam 1995](tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md); [Tam 1986](tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md) p. 4). That is the paper's strongest point: the source is not where the wavepacket is largest.
- **The selection rule has since been qualified.** "Leak where coherent vorticity fluctuation is largest" holds at NPR 3.4, where the first leak coincides with the maximum. It fails at NPR 2.25 (A2), where leakage occurs well downstream of the maximum. Both incidence angle and vorticity strength matter, and leakage happens at saddle points between vortices (2021 leakage note, p. 26–28). A consistent reading: the RMS coherent vorticity measures the vortex–saddle swing, so its maximum marks where the deepest vorticity minimum passes the sonic line; it is necessary but not sufficient.
- **Kurtosis does not single out cells 2–4 as cleanly as the text says.** From Fig. 14, the 5th reflection point (K ≈ 4.8) matches the 2nd (≈ 4.5), and the ranking 3 > 4 > 5 ≈ 2 ≫ 1 looks more like a smooth rise and fall than a 2–4 band. A shock oscillating across a PIV point gives a heavy-tailed or bimodal velocity PDF, partly real (shock motion) and partly artificial (particle lag, about 3% here; Table 2). So high kurtosis marks shock motion of any kind, not leakage specifically. The authors concede only correlation (p. 843).
- **Single helix or flapping?** Centreline motion rules out an m = 2 double helix (p. 838). A planar cut cannot tell a spinning m = 1 helix from a standing (flapping) m = ±1 mode, and both give a POD pair with a ring portrait. [Edgington-Mitchell 2023](edgington-mitchell-2023-screeching-jet-seen-from-all-sides.md) (p. 3–4) says this study claimed helicity but could not show it rigorously. Léon et al. (2022) later proved m = ±1 with frequent helicity reversals, and [Nogueira 2026](nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) shows that weak streaks can lock |m| = 1 screech into flapping. With 8000 fields at 0.5 Hz (hours of running), helicity reversals or flapping episodes would be invisible to this analysis.
- **Shock modulation or wave interference?** The coherent axial stress peaks just before each reflection point, which the authors attribute to shocks modulating the coherent structures (Raman & Rice 1994). [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves](edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) later attributed most near-field and core modulation to interference between the KH wave and the upstream G-JM. For screech the triad sets k_up = k_kh − k_s, so interference beats at exactly the shock spacing (2π/(k_kh − k_up) = 2π/k_s). Wavelength cannot separate the two pictures, and the EMD split into "modulation" and "growth" is neutral between them. The POD pair contains both k > 0 and k < 0 parts; a k_x-sign split of the coherent stress would decide. The same ambiguity is noted for Mercier's lobes (Mercier note). [Nogueira 2022, wavepacket modulation](nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md) models the shock-modulation part alone.
- **Random stress peaks after the reflection point.** The random-stress maxima just downstream of each reflection point, co-located with coherent minima, could be an energy transfer from coherent to stochastic motion at the shock, or shock-induced artificial fluctuation, which is strongest exactly there. The paper does not separate the two.
- **"Coherent" means "the leading pair".** Harmonics (Fig. 4 shows St 0.6), amplitude modulation of the pair and the G-JM/duct waves outside the pair's span all go into u″. [Weightman 2019](weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md) later showed that v-only POD hides an unpaired shear-thickness mode tied to pair amplitude.
- **Dataset inconsistencies to keep in mind.** St 0.30 here and in the 2021 paper against 0.26 in the 2020 paper. Re 6.8, 8.6 and 8.1 × 10⁵, explained by definitions (see Setup). PIV rate 0.5 Hz here against 1 Hz in the 2021 Table 2. T₀ 288 K here against 298 K in 2021.
  - With λ_KH ≈ 2.4D, St 0.30 gives U_c ≈ 0.68U_j and St 0.26 gives 0.59U_j. Both are plausible, and Powell's formula with L_s = 1.37D gives 7.5 kHz against 7.7 kHz, or 6.9 against 6.7 kHz. Both fit to about 3%, and k_kh + ω/c∞ ≈ k_s holds to about 3% in both cases (my arithmetic, c∞ = 340 m s⁻¹ assumed). So the C-mode tone cannot be fixed from the kinematics; the acoustic record decides.

**Methods and data for the lab workflow (CLAUDE.md)**
- **Non-time-resolved PIV (step 2).** Every analysis here works on uncorrelated snapshots:
  - mean, RMS, skewness, kurtosis;
  - v-only snapshot POD → pair → phase (eq. 4.11) → two-mode coherent field → triple decomposition;
  - integrated stresses with spatial EMD.

  This is the group's standard route, from [Oberleithner 2011](oberleithner-2011-swirling-jet-vortex-breakdown-stability.md), whose pair energies (15.0/14.9%) are nearly the same as here (15.3/13.7%). Adding a k_x-sign split of the complex pair (2018 and 2020 notes) separates KH from G-JM before computing coherent stresses.
- **Statistics as a source finder.** Kurtosis and skewness maps need no time resolution, are cheap, and point at intermittent shock motion. In time-resolved schlieren or LES they could become a real test: condition k_x < 0-filtered upstream fronts on the local kurtosis or shock-tip excursion, and check whether high-K events are leakage events (2021 leakage note, p. 10, 19).
- **Spatial EMD** is a convenient way to split growth from shock modulation along x. It suffers the usual end effects and mode mixing ([Huang 1998](huang-1998-empirical-mode-decomposition-hilbert-spectrum.md)), worse with about seven periods. A spatial Hilbert envelope or a fit with the shock wavenumber would be more reproducible.
- **SPOD → reconstruction → Hilbert POD.** The paper's picture is static: one averaged source set, one averaged coherent amplitude. With time-resolved schlieren of a C-mode jet:
  1. SPOD at f_s;
  2. reconstruct the screech band, separately for k_x > 0 (KH) and k_x < 0 (G-JM and leakage);
  3. Hilbert/HPOD for the instantaneous KH amplitude.

  Then test whether the cell of strongest leakage moves with the cycle-to-cycle vortex strength, as the r̄ ± 2σ POD rescaling implies (2021 leakage note, p. 28–30). Azimuthal microphones or two views are also needed to track helicity sign over time (Edgington-Mitchell 2023).

**Modelling side and the PyFR staging LES**
- The LES (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`, A1 at NPR 2.10 and A2 at 2.25) can compute exactly what this paper had to infer:
  - the sonic line;
  - the coherent vorticity along it from the screech-band reconstruction;
  - u kurtosis at each reflection point;
  - leakage directly from k_x < 0 pressure.
- Doing this for converged A1 and A2 states would test the selection rule where the 2021 paper found it fails (A2). It would also test whether the dominant source cell shifts on staging; Mercier's fixed 4th-shock source is the alternative.
- A C-mode run at NPR 3.4 would, in addition, resolve the m = 1 helicity question that planar PIV cannot.
- The model inputs this paper offers are Suzuki & Lele's critical-angle and vorticity-rotation criteria (eqs. 5.1–5.3). They need the local Mach number and sound-speed ratio at the shear layer, which the LES supplies and PIV could not (the 2021 ray model had to assume constant c).
- Nozzle differences: this nozzle is 15 mm with a 5 mm lip (t/D 0.33); the PyFR model has a 16 mm nozzle with a 1 mm lip ([staging page](../Topics/screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md)).

**Rocket noise.** Screech itself is absent at full scale ([lubert-2022](lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md)). The shock–vortex interaction mechanism behind leakage is also behind BBSAN, which does matter for launch vehicles. Locating interaction sites from statistics or coherent vorticity is a lab-scale idea that would need time-resolved or simulated data to carry over.
