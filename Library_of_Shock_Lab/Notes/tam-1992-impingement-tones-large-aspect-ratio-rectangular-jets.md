# Impingement Tones of Large Aspect Ratio Supersonic Rectangular Jets

- **Authors:** Christopher K. W. Tam (Department of Mathematics, Florida State University, Tallahassee) and Thomas D. Norum (Acoustics and Noise Division, NASA Langley Research Center, Hampton) (p. 304). Tam was funded by NASA Langley grant NAG 1-421 (p. 311). Not Shock Lab group work. The experimental data are Norum's (AIAA Paper 89-1138, "including many sets of unpublished data", p. 305); the rig is not described beyond a convergent nozzle, a near-nozzle microphone and a 0.6 kHz facility tone (p. 305–306).

## Summary
Norum's measurements of supersonic rectangular jets of aspect ratio above 4 hitting a wall show 20 or more tones, most not harmonics of each other (p. 304–305). The authors show that every tone is a sum or difference of two basic tones and their harmonics. The lower basic tone (s) belongs to a symmetric (varicose) feedback oscillation of the jet and the higher (a) to an antisymmetric (flapping) one, and stroboscopic schlieren shows both running in the same jet at the same plate distance (p. 305–308). To explain why a > s and why both fall as M_j rises, they adopt the Tam & Ahuja (1990) closure: the upstream leg is carried by upstream-propagating neutral acoustic modes of the jet itself. They derive these modes for a planar jet bounded by two vortex sheets (eqs. 2–20, p. 308–309). Each family (symmetric, antisymmetric) has infinitely many modes, each confined to a band between a lower limit on the sonic line k = −ω/a∞ (closed form, eqs. 21–23) and an upper limit at the maximum of its dispersion curve (p. 309–310). Only the first symmetric and first antisymmetric bands are low enough to meet the jet's instability waves, and the first symmetric band lies wholly below the first antisymmetric one, which the authors offer as the reason for a > s. Norum's two basic tones at seven Mach numbers (M_j 1.16–1.70, read from the figure) fall within or very close to the computed bands (Fig. 11, p. 310). Theory is one vortex-sheet calculation; no instability-wave growth, wall or nozzle is modelled, and the loop integer is left free.

## Key findings
- **Many tones, two basic frequencies** (p. 305–307; Figs 2–4).
  - All tones in Norum's spectra are combinations m·a ± n·s of two basic frequencies; the basic tones are usually the two loudest (p. 305). Strong amplitudes make "strong nonlinear interaction" between the two unstable modes plausible (p. 305).
  - Fig. 2 (M_j 1.29, nozzle 2.013 × 0.475 in, AR 4.24, L = 5.25 in): s = 2.5 kHz, a = 6.4 kHz and 25 combination tones up to 6a = 38.4 kHz, e.g. tone 1 = a − s, 2 = 2s, 4 = a + s (p. 305–306). Read from the figure: s ≈ 154.5 dB, a ≈ 153.5 dB, a − s ≈ 146.5 dB, 2a ≈ 141 dB, against a broadband floor near 125 dB at low frequency.
  - Fig. 3 (same jet, L = 3.15 in): s = 5.3 kHz, a = 6.7 kHz, 20 combination tones. Here a − s is smaller than s, so the low-frequency end is filled by difference tones (a − s = 1.4 kHz, 2s − a = 3.9 kHz) (p. 305–306). Read from the figure: a ≈ 156.5 dB, s ≈ 152.5 dB, 2s − a ≈ 147 dB.
  - Fig. 4 (same jet, L = 2.55 in): s = 2.9 kHz, a = 6.4 kHz, plus a facility tone e = 0.6 kHz that appears in many spectra independent of M_j and L. Through nonlinearity it adds sidebands (a ± e, 2a ± e, …) to the harmonics and combination tones; 28 tones besides e, s and a are labelled (p. 306–307). Read from the figure: a ≈ 162 dB, s ≈ 149 dB, 2a ≈ 148 dB.
  - My arithmetic: the Fig. 2 and Fig. 3 legends are in ascending frequency order; in Fig. 4, tone 16 = 4a − s (22.7 kHz) is listed below tone 17 = 3a + s (22.1 kHz), so one of the two labels is probably misassigned.
- **Symmetric and antisymmetric feedback modes coexist** (p. 305, 307–308; Figs 5–7). Strobe-lit schlieren and shadowgraph locked to the filtered microphone signal show the M_j 1.29 jet at L = 3.45 in oscillating symmetrically at 3.4 kHz (Fig. 5) and antisymmetrically at 6.3 kHz (Fig. 6). "The jet undergoes both symmetric and antisymmetric instabilities simultaneously" (p. 305). Norum is credited with the first clear evidence of the two modes coexisting in impinging rectangular jets (p. 305). At a combination frequency (9.7 kHz, Fig. 7) the motion is "highly complicated" (p. 307). By my arithmetic 9.7 kHz = s + a for the Fig. 5–6 tones (3.4 + 6.3).
- **Combination tones are nonlinear products, not instability waves** (p. 307). They come from the nonlinear motion of the jet and its interaction with the wall; even where a combination frequency falls in the jet's unstable range, the observed motion is "driven principally by flow nonlinearities" (p. 307).
- **Two facts to explain** (p. 307): the antisymmetric basic tone is always higher than the symmetric one, and both fall monotonically with M_j apart from staging jumps.
- **Planar vortex-sheet upstream modes** (Section III, p. 308–310).
  - A 2-D jet of thickness H, velocity u_j and Mach number M_j, bounded by two vortex sheets, with linearised inviscid compressible equations, gives dispersion relations for antisymmetric (eq. 19) and symmetric (eq. 20) waves (p. 308–309). These are "completely general" and contain the KH waves too; the paper follows Tam & Hu (1989), who found in the round-jet vortex sheet a family of neutral waves (real k and ω) of which those with negative group velocity travel upstream "by attaching themselves to the supersonic jet", and Tam & Ahuja (1990), who proposed them as the feedback waves (p. 309).
  - At M_j 1.29 (cold, T0 = T∞) the symmetric (Fig. 9) and antisymmetric (Fig. 10) modes each form a sequence of humped curves in the (k, ω) plane, k < 0. The upstream part (dω/dk < 0) runs from the maximum of each curve to the straight line k² − ω²/a∞² = 0 (eq. 21), where the outside decay rate vanishes (p. 309).
  - **Band limits.** The upper limit of each band is the maximum of the curve; the lower limit lies on eq. 21, where the first term of eqs. 19–20 is infinite, so the tangent argument must equal (n − ½)π for antisymmetric modes (eq. 22) or (n − 1)π for symmetric modes (eq. 23), n = 1, 2, … (p. 309–310). Eqs. 21–22 or 21–23 together give the lower limit at a given M_j and temperature ratio (p. 310).
  - My recomputation (Data tables) reproduces Figs 9–11 within a few per cent: at M_j 1.29 the first symmetric band is 0 < ωH/u_j < 0.877 and the first antisymmetric band 1.260–1.984. Fig. 9 shows the first symmetric curve ending at the origin; with n = 1 eq. 23 forces ω = 0, so **the first symmetric band has no lower limit above zero** (my derivation). Higher modes give narrower bands (S2 2.52–3.15, A2 3.78–4.34 at M_j 1.29).
- **Why a > s** (p. 310). The lowest symmetric band lies below the lowest antisymmetric band. Instability waves "exist only at low Strouhal number", so they cannot match the higher-order bands, and only the first band of each family can close a loop; hence the antisymmetric tone must be higher. No KH calculation or Strouhal limit is given for this step.
  - My check: over M_j 1.0–1.8 (cold) the first antisymmetric lower limit is 1.38–1.44 times the first symmetric upper limit. So two first-band tones must satisfy a/s > ≈ 1.4 whatever the normalisation. Fig. 11 (mid-bar ratios ≈ 1.7–2.2) and Figs 2, 4 and 5–6 (a/s = 2.56, 2.21, 1.85) satisfy it; **Fig. 3 (6.7/5.3 = 1.26) does not**, so at least one of its two basic tones cannot be a first-band tone of this model.
- **Bands against Norum's data** (Fig. 11, p. 310).
  - The tone ranges seen while L/H is swept at each of seven M_j are drawn as black bars, with occasional isolated points; "all of the measured tone frequencies lie within (or almost within)" the calculated first bands. The authors take this as "strong evidence" that the feedback waves are the upstream acoustic modes of the jet, and propose the first bands as a first estimate of the tone frequencies (p. 310).
  - The band-averaged frequency falls with M_j, as the tones do (p. 310). The band "is large enough to allow an abrupt change" in the loop integer n of eq. 1, i.e. staging inside a band (p. 310). My check: the A1 band ratio ω_max/ω_low is 1.57–1.60 over M_j 1.0–1.8, larger than (n + 1)/n for every n ≥ 2, so at least one integer stage always fits.
  - My reading of the bars (Data tables): antisymmetric tones occupy the lower half of the A1 band (from its lower limit up to about 40 % of the width). Symmetric tones sit in the top third of the S1 band and from M_j 1.49 press against its upper limit, overshooting it by up to 5 % (isolated point at M_j 1.64: 11 %). The isolated antisymmetric point at M_j 1.57 is about 1.5 % above the A1 upper limit.
  - Fig. 11 does not say whether H is the nozzle or the fully expanded height, or what T0 was. Converting the M_j 1.29 tones of Figs 2–6 with the nozzle H and T0 = 293 K (my assumption) gives s = 0.49–0.67 and a = 1.25–1.32 in ωH/u_j, mostly just below the M_j 1.29 bars (0.59–0.81 and 1.34–1.58). With the fully expanded height (×1.062) the a tones and two s tones fall on the bars, but s = 2.5 kHz (0.53) stays below and s = 5.3 kHz (Fig. 3, 1.05–1.11) sits in the gap between S1 (≤ 0.88) and A1 (≥ 1.26) under either choice.
- **Mach-number trend of the upstream modes** (p. 305, Tam & Ahuja recap). The allowable bands of the helical/flapping upstream modes are too high to meet the instability waves in subsonic jets and fall as M_j rises, so supersonic jets can sustain helical or flapping impingement loops while subsonic ones are axisymmetric (p. 305). This is the same argument as in [Tam & Ahuja 1990](tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md) (p. 81–82 there), here carried to the planar geometry.

## Setup / method
- **Experiments (Norum 1989, AIAA 89-1138; p. 305–307).** Supersonic rectangular jets of aspect ratio above 4 directed normal to a wall.
  - Nozzle for Figs 2–7: convergent (stated for Fig. 5, p. 305), breadth 2.013 in and width 0.475 in (AR 4.24), M_j = 1.29 (NPR ≈ 2.73 by my isentropic calculation, so underexpanded).
  - Nozzle-to-wall distances L = 2.55, 3.15, 3.45 and 5.25 in (L/H = 5.37, 6.63, 7.26, 11.05 on the 0.475 in width; my arithmetic).
  - Fig. 11 data: seven M_j, read as 1.16, 1.29, 1.40, 1.49, 1.57, 1.64 and 1.70 from the figure (NPR 2.30–4.94, my calculation), each with L increased gradually; the L range, the nozzle(s) and the plate size are not given (p. 310).
  - Stagnation temperature, Reynolds number, lip thickness, plate size and room treatment are not stated. A facility tone at 0.6 kHz is present (p. 306).
- **Acoustics** (p. 305). One microphone "near the nozzle slightly upstream of the nozzle exit plane". SPL spectra 0–40 kHz (Figs 2–4). Sampling rate, bandwidth, averaging and record length are not stated.
- **Imaging** (p. 305; Figs 5–7). Schlieren and shadowgraph with a stroboscopic light source triggered by the microphone signal band-filtered around one tone; multiple exposures on one film frame enhance the motion at the strobe frequency. This is an optical phase average locked to a single tone: it needs no high-speed camera, only a clean tonal trigger, and it isolates one loop even when both run at once. The view shows the thin (H) dimension, so the flapping is seen across the minor axis (my reading of Figs 5–7).
- **Tone identification** (p. 305–307). Combinations m·a ± n·s (± e) were matched to every labelled peak by hand; no bispectrum or phase coupling test was used.
- **Model** (Section III, p. 308–310).
  - Geometry: infinitely wide 2-D jet of thickness H (Fig. 8), uniform velocity u_j, Mach number M_j, density ρ_j and sound speed a_j inside; ambient at rest with ρ∞, a∞. Pressure matched across the sheets (ideally expanded, no shocks), infinitely thin shear layers, parallel flow, inviscid, linear. The major dimension is ignored (AR → ∞), and neither the wall nor the nozzle enters.
  - Temperature: calculations are for cold jets, T0 = T∞ (p. 309). Then ρ∞/ρ_j = T_j/T∞ = (1 + 0.2M_j²)⁻¹ and u_j/a∞ = M_j(1 + 0.2M_j²)^(−1/2) (my statement of the standard relations, used in my recomputation).
  - Unknowns: pressures p₀ outside and p_i inside and the sheet displacement ζ(x, t) (p. 308). Normal modes ∝ exp[i(kx − ωt)], ω > 0 (eq. 10). Eliminating ζ gives an eigenvalue problem in y (eqs. 11–16), solved in closed form (eqs. 17–18).
  - Waves kept: neutral (real k, ω), with k² > ω²/a∞² so that the outside solution decays (subsonic phase speed relative to the ambient), and with negative group velocity (p. 309).
  - Loop model: eq. 1 with c_i and c_a the phase speeds of the downstream instability wave and upstream acoustic wave and reflection/excitation delays neglected (p. 304). The integer n is not predicted; the bands bound the frequency (p. 305, 310).
- **Modelling scope.** No KH growth rates, eigenfunctions, finite-thickness profiles, heated cases or shock cells are computed. The comparison is frequency-band membership only.

## Key equations
All read from rendered pages (p. 304, 308–310); notation below.

- **Feedback condition** (eq. 1, p. 304): $f_n=\dfrac{n}{L\,(1/c_i+1/c_a)},\quad n=1,2,3,\dots$, with c_i and c_a the phase velocities of the downstream instability and upstream acoustic waves and L the nozzle-to-wall distance. Staging is an abrupt change of n as L increases.
- **Governing equations** (eqs. 2–3, p. 308):
  $$\frac{\partial^2p_0}{\partial t^2}-a_\infty^2\nabla^2p_0=0\quad(|y|\ge H/2),\qquad \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+u_j\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^2p_i-a_j^2\nabla^2p_i=0\quad(|y|\le H/2)$$
- **Sheet conditions at y = H/2** (eqs. 4–6): $p_0=p_i$; $\dfrac{\partial^2\zeta}{\partial t^2}=-\dfrac{1}{\rho_\infty}\dfrac{\partial p_0}{\partial y}$; $\left(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}+u_j\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^2\zeta=-\dfrac{1}{\rho_j}\dfrac{\partial p_i}{\partial y}$.
- **Centreline conditions** (eqs. 7–8): antisymmetric $p_i=0$, symmetric $\partial p_i/\partial y=0$ at y = 0; p₀ outgoing or bounded as y → ∞ (eq. 9).
- **Normal modes** (eq. 10): $[p_0,p_i,\zeta]=[\hat p_0(y),\hat p_i(y),\hat\zeta]\,e^{i(kx-\omega t)}$, ω > 0.
- **Eigenvalue problem** (eqs. 11–16, p. 309):
  $$\frac{d^2\hat p_0}{dy^2}-\left(k^2-\frac{\omega^2}{a_\infty^2}\right)\hat p_0=0,\qquad \frac{d^2\hat p_i}{dy^2}+\left[\frac{(\omega-u_jk)^2}{a_j^2}-k^2\right]\hat p_i=0$$
  with $\hat p_i=\hat p_0$ and $\dfrac{1}{\rho_j(\omega-u_jk)^2}\dfrac{d\hat p_i}{dy}=\dfrac{1}{\rho_\infty\omega^2}\dfrac{d\hat p_0}{dy}$ at y = H/2 (eqs. 13–14), and $\hat p_i=0$ (antisymmetric, eq. 15) or $d\hat p_i/dy=0$ (symmetric, eq. 16) at y = 0.
- **Solutions** (eqs. 17–18): $\hat p_i=A\sin\{[(\omega-u_jk)^2/a_j^2-k^2]^{1/2}y\}$ (antisymmetric) and $\hat p_0=B\exp[-(k^2-\omega^2/a_\infty^2)^{1/2}y]$.
  > Eq. 18 is printed with a₀ in place of a∞; with eq. 11 it must be a∞ (my reading).
- **Dispersion relations** (eqs. 19–20, p. 309), with $\eta=[(\omega-u_jk)^2/a_j^2-k^2]^{1/2}$ and $\mu=(k^2-\omega^2/a_\infty^2)^{1/2}$:
  $$\text{antisymmetric: }\ \frac{\eta\,\rho_\infty\omega^2}{\mu\,\rho_j(\omega-u_jk)^2}+\tan\!\left(\frac{\eta H}{2}\right)=0,\qquad \text{symmetric: }\ \frac{\eta\,\rho_\infty\omega^2}{\mu\,\rho_j(\omega-u_jk)^2}-\cot\!\left(\frac{\eta H}{2}\right)=0$$
  > Rederived from eqs. 13–18 (the signs follow from p_i = A sin ηy or A cos ηy and p₀ = B e^{−μy}). Same form as eqs. 10–11 of [Gojon 2019](gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md) (that note, p. 24 there), who use h_j for H.
- **Lower band limits** (eqs. 21–23, p. 309–310): on the line $k^2-\omega^2/a_\infty^2=0$ (eq. 21; μ → 0),
  $$\text{antisymmetric: }\ \eta\frac{H}{2}=\left(n-\tfrac12\right)\pi\ \ (22),\qquad \text{symmetric: }\ \eta\frac{H}{2}=(n-1)\pi\ \ (23),\qquad n=1,2,\dots$$
  These are the hard-wall duct conditions (∂p/∂y = 0 at the sheets) for the two parities (my observation; cf. [Nogueira 2024](nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md), whose branch points sit on hard-wall duct curves).
- **Closed form of the lower limit** (my rearrangement of eqs. 21–23, with k = −ω/a∞): writing $M_a=u_j/a_\infty$,
  $$\left.\frac{\omega H}{u_j}\right|_{\rm low}=\frac{(2n-1)\pi\ \text{(antisym.)}\ \ \text{or}\ \ 2(n-1)\pi\ \text{(sym.)}}{\sqrt{M_j^2(1+M_a)^2-M_a^2}},\qquad St_{\rm low}=\frac{\omega H}{2\pi u_j}$$
  This equals eqs. 15–16 of Gojon 2019 and eqs. 4.7–4.8 of Nogueira 2024 (with T = M_a²/M_j² and their n shifted by one). Check: M_j 1.29 cold (M_a = 1.117) gives 1.260 for A1, matching Fig. 11.
- **Upper band limit:** the maximum of ω along each branch, dω/dk = 0 (p. 309). No closed form; found numerically (Data tables). In later terminology this is the saddle point and the lower limit the branch point.

## Notation
- H: jet thickness (planar model; nozzle width 0.475 in in the experiments); L: nozzle-to-wall distance; x, y: streamwise and transverse coordinates (y = 0 on the jet centreline).
- u_j, M_j, a_j, ρ_j: fully expanded jet velocity, Mach number, sound speed and density; a∞, ρ∞: ambient sound speed and density; T0: stagnation temperature. M_a = u_j/a∞ (my notation).
- p₀, p_i: pressure disturbance outside and inside the jet; ζ: vertical displacement of the vortex sheet; k: streamwise wavenumber (k < 0 for the upstream waves); ω: angular frequency; η, μ: transverse wavenumber inside and decay rate outside (my shorthand for the brackets in eqs. 17–20).
- c_i, c_a: phase velocities of the downstream instability wave and the upstream acoustic wave; f_n: tone frequency of stage n (eq. 1); n: loop integer (eq. 1) and, separately, mode number in eqs. 22–23.
- s, a: symmetric and antisymmetric basic tone frequencies; e: facility tone (0.6 kHz).
- S1, A1, S2, …: first symmetric, first antisymmetric, … upstream-mode bands (labels as in Gojon 2019 and Nogueira 2024; not the A1/A2 screech stages).

## Relevance to this project
This is the planar half of the Tam guided-mode programme: [Tam & Hu 1989](tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md) found the upstream neutral waves in the round vortex sheet, [Tam & Ahuja 1990](tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md) made them the return leg of round impinging tones, and this paper carries the idea to large-AR rectangular jets, where both a varicose and a flapping loop run at once. It is the source of the planar band formulas later used for rectangular screech ([Gojon 2019](gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), §VIII) and re-derived as branch points of the double vortex sheet ([Nogueira 2024](nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md), eqs. 4.7–4.8). It bears on the CLAUDE.md research area through impinging-jet feedback and non-axisymmetric jets. Later papers cite it for three things: the planar bands, the idea that the band structure explains which symmetry resonates (also used for round screech modes by [Gojon 2018](gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md), p. 2920 there), and combination tones from two coexisting loops (the group's round impinging jets, [Weightman et al. 2019](weightman-2019-nozzle-external-geometry-azimuthal-mode-impinging-jet.md), p. 430, 433, and [Weightman 2019](weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md), p. 6). The modern guided-jet-mode closure of screech ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md)) and of subsonic trapped-wave resonance ([Towne 2017](towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md)) rests on the same upstream neutral waves.

**How it fits, and what later work changes**
- **Band test strengths and limits.** The headline agreement (Fig. 11) is weaker than it looks for the symmetric tones: the S1 band runs from zero, so only its upper limit tests anything, and from M_j 1.49 the symmetric tones sit at or just above it. That is the same pattern as the round m = 0 tones dying at the S(0,1) upper limit in [Jaunet 2019](jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md) (p. 3, 7 there). The antisymmetric tones sit in the lower part of A1, near the branch point on the sonic line, where [Gojon 2019](gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md) later found the LES upstream energy (p. 26–27 there).
- **Tones between the bands.** The Fig. 3 basic tones (a/s = 1.26) cannot both be first-band tones (my check above), and s = 5.3 kHz falls in the S1–A1 gap under any reasonable normalisation. Gojon 2019 met the same gap with hot rectangular screech tones and many cold points above M_j ≈ 1.45 (that note, p. 26), and the topic pages already note that band membership is a weak closure test. The paper does not comment on the Fig. 3 case.
- **Ordering of varicose and flapping tones.** Here a > s, explained by S1 lying below A1. In free AR 2 rectangular screech the varicose tone lies above the flapping one (St 0.337 against 0.265 at NPR 4.0, [Nogueira 2025](nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4215), so the ordering is not universal; a varicose loop closing on S2, or screech closure through the shock-cell wavenumber, would reverse it (my reading). The pre-2000 picture of rectangular screech, with weak symmetric modes ([Raman 1998](raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md), p. 54–55), contrasts with the strong symmetric impingement tone here (s ≈ 149–155 dB, comparable to a), so the plate makes a varicose loop viable in a large-AR jet.
- **Flapping tone near free-jet screech** (my estimate). For M_j 1.29 and this nozzle, Tam's (1988) shock-cell spacing with u_c = 0.5–0.7u_j (0.5u_j measured in an AR 4 jet by [Alkislar 2003](alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 143–144) gives a free-jet screech St = fH/u_j ≈ 0.19–0.23 ([Tam 1988](tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md); ignoring the AR correction, under 3 % at AR > 4). The flapping basic tones (St 0.198–0.211) lie in that range. Henderson & Powell likewise note that some impingement stages in Norum's 1989 rectangular data sit around the screech frequency ([Henderson 1993](henderson-1993-tones-choked-jet-impinging-flat-plates.md), p. 312). Whether the a tone is a plate-modified screech loop is untested, as for the round helical tone on [impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](../Topics/impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md).
- **Which wave closes the loop.** The paper assumes guided closure and tests only frequency bands. [Edgington-Mitchell 2025](edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md) (which includes AR 2 rectangular nozzles) and [Nogueira 2024](nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) argue that guided closure is required only for sources inside the jet core; external waves from the wall jet or stand-off shock can close the loop directly. At supersonic M_j the A1 lower limit has phase speed −a∞, so free sound travelling upstream at a∞ and a near-branch-point guided wave give nearly the same eq. 1 frequency (my arithmetic; the [Bogey 2017](bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md) note makes the same point for round jets at M_j 1.5). The antisymmetric tones' position near the branch point therefore does not separate the closures.
- **Model assumptions to keep in mind.** Infinite span (the major dimension is ignored, so wagging or diagonal modes cannot be represented), cold ideally expanded flow (the experiments are underexpanded, and T0 is not given), infinitely thin shear layers (Tam & Ahuja's finite-thickness check moves the (0, 1) band by under 5 % but lowers higher modes by about 10 %; [linear-models](../Topics/linear-models.md)), and no statement of whether H is the nozzle or fully expanded height. The KH side of the loop is never computed; the claim that instability waves exist only at low Strouhal number is qualitative, since a vortex sheet has no shear-layer thickness and hence no KH upper cut-off of its own.

**Methods and data for the lab workflow (CLAUDE.md)**
- **Time-resolved data (microphones, high-speed schlieren).** The tone analysis is block-averaged spectra plus manual sum/difference matching. Combination tones, harmonics and facility sidebands are exactly what BMD and the bispectrum detect; a bicoherence map of a near-nozzle microphone would show which pairs (s, a), (a, a), (a, e) are phase-coupled and remove the guesswork in labels such as Fig. 4's 16 and 17. Loud difference tones (a − s at ≈ 146 dB, Fig. 2) mean that s and a are present at the same time for much of the record, but a long-average spectrum cannot say whether they also alternate; band-pass Hilbert envelopes of s and a, or SPOD → reconstruction of each band → Hilbert POD on time-resolved schlieren, would show coexistence against switching and how staging jumps in n happen in time. The e = 0.6 kHz sidebands imply amplitude modulation of the tones at the facility frequency, which Hilbert envelopes would show directly.
- **Non-time-resolved data (PIV, single-shot schlieren).** The strobe-locked imaging is a tone-conditioned phase average; with PIV the equivalent is microphone-triggered phase locking or POD-pair phase recovery per tone. As Figs 5–6 show, locking to one tone isolates one loop's shape even when two loops coexist, but it averages out their interaction (Fig. 7).
- **Using the model.** The closed-form branch points and the numerically found saddles need only M_j, the temperature ratio and H, so they can be overlaid on St–NPR maps of the group's AR 2 rectangular impinging jets (Edgington-Mitchell 2025) or free rectangular screech, with the caveat that AR 2 is far from the planar limit. The a/s > ≈ 1.4 test is a normalisation-free check for two coexisting first-band tones.

**PyFR staging LES and screech.** The model is planar, while the staging LES (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`) is a round jet. Two transferable points: (1) the first axisymmetric (0, 1) band of round jets also starts at zero frequency (St 0–0.22 at M_j 1.42 in the [Tam & Ahuja 1990](tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md) note), so only its upper limit constrains A-mode closure, as here; (2) the branch-point/saddle bracket and the phase speeds along it (−a∞ to about −0.7a∞) are the inputs a dispersive version of eq. 1 needs, as in [Mancinelli 2019](mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) and [Jordan 2018](jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md).

**Rocket noise.** Impingement tones are absent at launch-vehicle scale (topic page). The paper's point that two loops and their nonlinear products can fill a spectrum with tones bears on subscale pad tests with rectangular or clustered nozzles only qualitatively.
