## Summary
Part 1 solved the sound radiated by instability waves of a 2-D compressible mixing layer by matched asymptotic expansions. This paper applies the same method to a round supersonic jet and tests it against experiment (p. 273–274). The inner solution is a WKB/multiple-scales instability wave in a jet that spreads slowly at rate ε (slow variable s = εx). At order one it is the local inviscid compressible eigenproblem. At order ε the matching condition gives an amplitude equation, which supplies the non-parallel correction in amplitude and phase (p. 276–282). The outer solution is a Fourier-transform solution of the linearised Euler equations outside the jet in r̄ = εr, which carries the field to the near and far acoustic field (p. 278–279). The cylindrical geometry and the entrainment flow outside the jet bring logarithmic gauge functions (δ₁ = ε ln ε) into the inner expansion (p. 274, 280). The far-field directivity is the axial wavenumber spectrum of the wave's pressure at the jet edge, evaluated at the acoustic wavenumber times cos χ. Only components with supersonic phase speed radiate, so Mach-wave radiation follows from acoustic matching (eqs. 5.7–5.8, p. 283). Calculations on fitted mean flows of Troutt & McLaughlin's cold M 2.1 jet, excited at St 0.2 and 0.4 by a glow discharge, reproduce the shape of the measured centreline (ρu)_rms distribution and the measured near-field SPL contours. One amplitude constant is fitted in each case, and the whole St 0.2 field is shifted downstream (p. 287–293).

## Key findings
- **A uniformly valid linear solution from the jet axis to the far field** (§§2–5, p. 275–283). Locally parallel and multiple-scales instability-wave solutions are not valid far from the shear layer, which is why earlier theories could not give the radiation (p. 274; Tam & Morris 1980; see also the dispute recorded in [Michalke 1984](michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), p. 192). Here the inner wave solution is matched to an outer acoustic solution by Van Dyke's intermediate matching principle (p. 279).
- **Logarithmic gauge functions.** The inner expansion is in δ₀ = 1, δ₁ = ε ln ε, δ₂ = ε, found as part of the matching, not assumed as powers of ε (eq. 4.9, p. 276, 280). They come from the cylindrical geometry (p. 274).
  - In eq. 4.5 the ε ln ε term is proportional to v̄_∞, the entrainment velocity outside the jet (p. 280). The O(ε ln ε) inner solution (eq. 4.10, p. 281) only renormalises the O(1) eigenfunction by −iρ̄_∞M²ωv̄_∞A₀ (my reading: with no entrainment, v̄_∞ = 0, the log terms would vanish).
- **Order-one matching** gives B₀ = 0, so only the outgoing Hankel function H_n^(1) survives outside the jet. Boundedness at r = 0 then makes α(s) an eigenvalue: the local inviscid instability wave (eqs. 4.7–4.8, p. 280). Only the eigenvalue of the instability wave is followed (p. 280).
- **Order-ε matching gives the amplitude equation** (eqs. 4.15–4.18, p. 282). The coefficient D of the incoming H_n^(2) wave in the order-ε inner solution must vanish (eq. 4.16). Written out, this is I₁ dA₀/ds + I₂A₀ = 0, so A₀(s) = Â₀ exp(−∫₀^s I₂/I₁ ds). The slow variation of A₀ is the non-parallel correction in both amplitude and phase (p. 287).
  - D is an integral of (ζ₁^p Q₂ − ζ₁^v R₂) weighted by r/ω̂ over the jet (p. 281). It plays the part of the solvability (adjoint) condition of Crighton & Gaster-type multiple-scales theory, with the matching supplying the condition (my reading; the paper does not make the comparison).
- **Sound power from the near-field wavenumber spectrum** (§5, p. 282–283). To lowest order the field outside the jet is a superposition of Hankel functions weighted by g(η), the axial Fourier transform of the instability-wave amplitude and phase (eqs. 5.1–5.2). Stationary phase at η = ρ̄_∞^½Mω cos χ gives the far field. The sound power per unit solid angle is D(χ) = 2|g(ρ̄_∞^½Mω cos χ)|² (eq. 5.8). It is the far-field formula of Tam 1975 (p. 283).
- **Only supersonic-phase-speed components radiate** (Figs 9–10, p. 289–290). The branch point η_c = ρ̄_∞^½Mω splits the spectrum into supersonic components (η < η_c), which radiate, and subsonic ones (η > η_c), which stay in the near field. At both St and for both modes the spectrum peaks well inside the supersonic region, so a "sizeable fraction" of the near-field pressure radiates. The radiation is strongly directional, as is typical of supersonic jet noise (p. 289).
  - My check: for a cold jet with T₀ = T_∞, ρ̄_∞^½M = u_j/a_∞ = 2.1 × 0.729 = 1.53. With ω = πSt (St on D_j), η_c = 1.92 at St 0.4 and 0.96 at St 0.2. That matches the marked η_c in Figs 9–10 (≈ 1.88–1.9 and ≈ 0.93–0.95, read from the figures), which confirms St = fD_j/u_j; the paper never defines St.
  - Read from the figures: the n = 0 peaks sit at η ≈ 1.38 (St 0.4) and 0.69 (St 0.2), and the n = 1 peaks at ≈ 1.50 and 0.79. Hence (my calculation) phase speeds of about 0.91u_j (n = 0) and 0.80–0.84u_j (n = 1), i.e. U/a_∞ ≈ 1.39 and 1.22–1.28. The radiation angles cos⁻¹(η/η_c) are ≈ 44° for n = 0 and ≈ 39° (St 0.4) and 35° (St 0.2) for n = 1.
- **Directivity of single modes** (Figs 11–12, p. 290–292). At St 0.2 the n = 0 near field is lobed, with strong radiation about 40° from the jet axis. The n = 1 field is less lobed and radiates fairly uniformly within a 35° cone about the axis. Both agree with my angle estimates above.
- **Centreline instability-wave amplitude against hot-wire data** (Figs 7–8, p. 287–288). The measured data are relative only, so the computed peak is scaled to the measured peak.
  - St 0.4: the calculated peak (≈ 0.092 at x/R_j ≈ 12) lies at "nearly the same location" as the measured one, and the decay "matches very well" (p. 287).
    - My reading of Fig. 7: the calculated growth is steeper and later. At x/R_j = 6–10 it is 2–5 times below the data (≈ 0.005–0.04 against 0.025–0.082), and at x/R_j = 24 it is ≈ 0.017 against 0.028.
  - St 0.2: qualitative agreement only. The computed curve peaks about four jet radii upstream of the data (x/R_j ≈ 12.5 against ≈ 16), so the computed noise source is also upstream (p. 287). The computed decay is slower (≈ 0.05 against 0.036 at x/R_j = 24, read from Fig. 8). The cause is not identified, and Troutt & McLaughlin give no error estimates (p. 287–288).
  - The authors stress that the calculation is "practically free from any adjustable parameter" (p. 288). In fact the amplitude constant is fitted, and the mean flow is measured with the forcing on.
- **Near-field SPL contours against microphone data** (Figs 13–14, p. 291–293). The complex pressures of n = 0 and n = ±1 are summed with relative amplitudes and phases from Troutt & McLaughlin's microphone ring. The absolute level is set by matching 148 dB at one marked point, (x, r)/R_j ≈ (30, 20) (read from the figures).
  - St 0.4: "excellent agreement". The 150 and 148 dB contours agree "nearly perfect[ly]", and the lobe, its direction and the contour spacing are right (p. 292–293).
  - St 0.2: the whole calculated field is moved "about four jet diameters downstream" to offset the upstream peak of the computed wave. After that, the lobe direction, field shape and contour spacing agree "almost totally" (p. 293).
    - > §6.1 gives the source offset as about four jet **radii** (p. 287), but §6.2 shifts the field by about four jet **diameters** (p. 293). One of the two is probably a slip; check the PDF.
- **Nonlinearity is treated in two parts** (p. 274–275).
  - Mean-flow distortion by the forced wave is included, because the measured mean flow of the excited jet is the base flow.
  - Self-interaction (harmonics) is neglected. Harmonics measured near the nozzle decay quickly while the fundamental is still growing, so the authors attribute them to the excitation process, and they are small where the wave peaks and radiates most.
  - Growth, peaking and decay are set by the local linear properties of the wave on the diverging mean flow, not by nonlinear saturation as in Taylor vortices or Bénard cells (p. 275). This is the "divergence caps the gain" point in [Michalke 1984](michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md) (p. 190–191).
- **Conclusions as stated** (p. 293–294). The matched-asymptotic method is valid, and the results give "concrete support" to the view that instability waves are important noise sources of supersonic jets, "at least" at low to moderately high Reynolds numbers. Predicting the near field is harder than the far field: the far field needs only a moderately accurate source model, but the near field also needs the non-propagating hydrodynamic part. The authors say no other method could then compute the unsteady near field of a supersonic jet.
- **No far-field test.** The anechoic room was too small for far-field data, which would need microphones 150–200 diameters away (p. 274). Eq. 5.8 is therefore never compared with measurements.

**Mean-flow model** (eqs. 6.1–6.3, Figs 4–6, p. 283–285). The jet is split into three regions (Fig. 1).
- *Core region*, the first five diameters: a uniform core of radius h(x) inside a half-Gaussian mixing layer of half-width b(x) (eq. 6.1). b(x) is Troutt's measurement (from McLaughlin, Seiner & Liu 1980), interpolated by a cubic spline. h(x) follows from conservation of axial momentum flux, which Troutt's data satisfy closely (p. 283).
- *Developed region*, from about eight diameters: a Gaussian with centreline velocity u_c(x) and half-width b(x) (eq. 6.2). u_c follows from momentum-flux conservation and agrees with Troutt's centreline data (Fig. 6, p. 284–285).
- *Transition region*: a three-parameter profile (eq. 6.3) with u_c, b and h as cubic splines in x, joined so that the parameters and their first x-derivatives are continuous. This smoothness is needed "to guarantee a genuine slowly varying mean flow" (p. 284).
- Read from Fig. 5: b/R_j ≈ 0.1 at x/R_j = 2, 0.5 at 6, 1.35 at 12, 1.7 at 18, 2.25 at 26, 2.5 at 30 and 2.9 at 40. Read from Fig. 6: u_c/u_j = 1 to x/R_j ≈ 10, then 0.98 (11.5), 0.89 (17.5), 0.74 (24) and 0.63 (30).
- > The text says Fig. 5 covers "the first twenty diameters" and the axis runs to x/R_j = 40, which is consistent. Fig. 4(a)'s label reads ū = u_c exp[−(ln 2)((r − b)/h)²], with b and h swapped relative to eq. 6.1 and the sketch; eq. 6.1 is the one used.

**The experiment (data reused, not new)** (p. 273–274, 287). Troutt (1978, PhD thesis, Oklahoma State) and Troutt & McLaughlin (1982, JFM 116).
- Cold, nearly perfectly expanded M 2.1 round jet at moderately high Reynolds number (value not given here).
- Instability waves are excited gently by a single glow discharge flush near the nozzle exit, at St 0.2 and 0.4. A point source cannot excite single azimuthal modes; the measured motion is dominated by n = 0 and the two helical modes n = ±1 (p. 287).
- The relative amplitudes and phases of the three modes come from near-field microphones on a circle of radius 3D, centred on the jet axis 12D downstream of the exit (p. 287). The calculation uses them to set the relative initial amplitudes Â₀ of the three waves, which leaves one overall constant.
- The modes' phase difference makes the forced motion asymmetric. All measurements were made in the half-plane on the glow-discharge side, and the calculations are evaluated in that same half-plane (p. 287).
- Hot-wire: centreline (ρu)_rms, given only relative to the value at one point (p. 287). Microphones: near-field SPL contours (Figs 13b, 14b). The room is anechoic but small, so there are no far-field data (p. 274).
- Data dependence: everything compared is **forced, single-frequency** data; the analysis needs no time resolution beyond extracting the amplitude at the forcing frequency. How Troutt extracted it (phase-locking or narrow-band filtering) is not stated here.

**Numerics** (§6.1–6.2, p. 286–290)
- *Eigenvalue α(s)* by shooting. Integrate eq. 2.5 (m = 0) inward from r_m, starting from the Hankel solution (eq. 2.7a), to a point near Re(r_c), where r_c is the critical point ω − αū(r_c) = 0. Integrate outward from near r = 0, starting from the one bounded Frobenius series. The difference of the two solutions at Re(r_c) is driven to zero by Newton iteration. The estimated eigenvalue error is at most 0.5%, "the upper error bound in all our numerical calculations" (p. 286).
- *Damped waves downstream* need two changes (p. 286). First, the inviscid damped solution is the analytic continuation of the growing one, so the integration path is deformed around the critical point in the complex r-plane (a rectangular detour; Tam 1975, Tam & Morris 1980). Second, the waves keep supersonic phase speeds relative to a_∞ even where they decay ("damped supersonic waves"). Following Part 1 §7, λ(α) in the starting condition is then evaluated on the second Riemann sheet of the α-plane.
- *Amplitude.* The eigenfunction gives I₁ and I₂ (Appendix B), integrated along the same deformed contour for damped waves. This is repeated at preselected, suitably spaced x stations, and I₂/I₁ is integrated numerically for A₀ (eq. 4.18) (p. 286–287).
- *Near field.* g(η) (eq. 5.2) by FFT; then the integral of eq. 5.1 by FFT at each of a set of regularly spaced r, giving p on a rectangular grid, which a subprogram contours (p. 289–290).
- No station spacing, FFT length or radial grid is given.

**Branch cuts** (Figs 2–3, p. 277, 279). λ(α) = (α² − ρ̄_∞M²ω²)^½ with −½π ≤ arg λ < ½π over the whole α-plane. The cuts run vertically from the branch points ±ρ̄_∞^½M(ω + iδ), with δ → 0⁺. The outer Fourier integral in η = εk uses the same cuts, an integration contour along the real axis passing between them, and a saddle point whose trajectory is sketched in Fig. 3.

## Key equations
All read from rendered pages. Variables are dimensionless (scales above). n: azimuthal mode number; M: jet Mach number (u_j/a_j); ρ̄_∞: ambient density over ρ_j.

- **Mean flow** (eq. 2.1, p. 275): $\bar{\boldsymbol u} = (\bar u(r,s),\ \epsilon\bar v_1(r,s),\ 0)$, and for $r \ge r_m(s)$: $\bar u = 0$, $\epsilon\bar v_1 = \epsilon\bar v_\infty/r$.
- **Linearised equations** (eq. 2.2, p. 276), for $p'(x,r,\phi,t) = \mathrm{Re}[p(r,x)e^{in\phi - i\omega t}]$ etc.:
$$-i\omega v + \bar v\frac{\partial v}{\partial r} + v\frac{\partial\bar v}{\partial r} + \bar u\frac{\partial v}{\partial x} + u\frac{\partial\bar v}{\partial x} = -\frac{1}{\bar\rho}\frac{\partial p}{\partial r},$$
$$-i\omega w + \bar v\frac{\partial w}{\partial r} + \frac{\bar v w}{r} + \bar u\frac{\partial w}{\partial x} = -\frac{in}{\bar\rho r}p,$$
$$-i\omega u + \bar v\frac{\partial u}{\partial r} + v\frac{\partial\bar u}{\partial r} + u\frac{\partial\bar u}{\partial x} + \bar u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = -\frac{1}{\bar\rho}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x},$$
$$-i\omega p + \bar v\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} + \bar u\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{M^2}\left(\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial vr}{\partial r} + \frac{in}{r}w + \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right) = 0.$$
  - > The last equation is printed with $\bar u\,\partial p/\partial r$; it should be $\bar u\,\partial p/\partial x$ (convection of p by the axial mean flow). The 1/M² factor is γp̄ in these units.
  - > My observation: the momentum and pressure equations contain no density-perturbation terms, ρ′(ū·∇)ū and γp′∇·ū. Both are O(ε) in a slowly spreading jet and would enter the O(ε) amplitude equation. The paper does not discuss them.
- **Inner (WKB) expansion** (eqs. 2.3–2.4, p. 276):
$$\begin{bmatrix}u\\v\\w\\p\end{bmatrix} = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\delta_m(\epsilon)\begin{bmatrix}\hat u_m(r,s)\\ \hat v_m(r,s)\\ \hat w_m(r,s)\\ \hat p_m(r,s)\end{bmatrix}e^{i\theta(s)/\epsilon},\qquad \frac{d\theta}{ds} = \alpha(s),\quad s = \epsilon x .$$
- **mth-order system** (eq. 2.5, p. 276), with R₀ = Q₀ = R₁ = Q₁ = 0 (p. 277):
$$\frac{\partial\hat p_m}{\partial r} - i\bar\rho(\omega - \alpha\bar u)\hat v_m = R_m,$$
$$\frac{\partial\hat v_m}{\partial r} - \frac{i}{\bar\rho(\omega - \alpha\bar u)}\left[\bar\rho M^2(\omega - \alpha\bar u)^2 - \frac{n^2}{r^2} - \alpha^2\right]\hat p_m + \left[\frac{1}{r} + \frac{\alpha}{\omega - \alpha\bar u}\frac{\partial\bar u}{\partial r}\right]\hat v_m = Q_m .$$
- **Outside the jet** (eqs. 2.6–2.8, p. 277): $\partial\hat p_0/\partial r - i\bar\rho_\infty\omega\hat v_0 = 0$ and $\partial\hat v_0/\partial r - \frac{i}{\bar\rho_\infty\omega}\left(\bar\rho_\infty M^2\omega^2 - n^2/r^2 - \alpha^2\right)\hat p_0 + \hat v_0/r = 0$, with solutions
$$\begin{bmatrix}\hat p_0\\ \hat v_0\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}H_n^{(1,2)}(i\lambda r)\\ \dfrac{-i}{\bar\rho_\infty\omega}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial r}H_n^{(1,2)}(i\lambda r)\end{bmatrix},\qquad \lambda(\alpha) = (\alpha^2 - \bar\rho_\infty M^2\omega^2)^{1/2}.$$
- **One-term inner solution** (eqs. 2.9–2.10, p. 277–278): $[\hat p_0, \hat v_0]^T = A_0(s)[\zeta_1^p, \zeta_1^v]^T + B_0(s)[\zeta_2^p, \zeta_2^v]^T$, where ζ₁ and ζ₂ equal the H^(1) and H^(2) solutions for r > r_m, and the combination must stay bounded as r → 0.
- **Outer equation** in r̄ = εr after Fourier transform in s (eq. 3.2, p. 278):
$$\left(1 - \frac{\epsilon^4\bar\rho_\infty M^2\bar v_\infty^2}{\bar r^2}\right)\frac{\partial^2\tilde u}{\partial\bar r^2} + \left(1 + 2\epsilon i\bar\rho_\infty M^2\omega\bar v_\infty + \frac{\epsilon^4\bar\rho_\infty M^2\bar v_\infty^2}{\bar r^2}\right)\frac{1}{\bar r}\frac{\partial\tilde u}{\partial\bar r} - \left(k^2 - \frac{\bar\rho_\infty M^2\omega^2}{\epsilon^2} + \frac{n^2}{\bar r^2}\right)\tilde u = 0 .$$
- **Outer solution** (eqs. 3.3–3.4, p. 278), exact for n = 0 and valid to O(ε²) for $\bar r > O(\epsilon^{1-1/N})$ otherwise:
$$p^o(\bar r,s) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(k,\epsilon)\left(1 + \frac{i\epsilon^3\bar v_\infty}{\omega\bar r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar r}\right)\Big[(\bar r^2 - \epsilon^4\bar\rho_\infty M^2\bar v_\infty^2)^{-\frac12 i\epsilon\bar\rho_\infty M^2\omega\bar v_\infty} H_\nu^{(1)}\!\Big(i(\epsilon^2k^2 - \bar\rho_\infty M^2\omega^2)^{1/2}(\bar r^2 - \epsilon^4\bar\rho_\infty M^2\bar v_\infty^2)^{1/2}/\epsilon\Big)\Big]e^{iks}\,dk,$$
$$\nu = (n^2 - \epsilon^2\bar\rho_\infty^2\omega^2M^4\bar v_\infty^2)^{1/2},\qquad g(k,\epsilon) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tilde A(s,\epsilon)\,e^{i\theta(s)/\epsilon - iks}\,ds .$$
  The complex order ν and the power of $(\bar r^2 - \dots)$ both come from the entrainment velocity $\bar v_\infty$.
- **Intermediate variable** (eq. 4.1, p. 279): $\tilde r = \epsilon^{1/N}r$, N large. In η = εk the outer solution becomes eqs. 4.2–4.3 (p. 279), and $\tilde A = \tilde A_0(s) + \epsilon\tilde A_1(s) + o(\epsilon)$ (eq. 4.4, p. 280).
- **Intermediate limit of the outer solution** (eq. 4.5, p. 280), by two saddle-point evaluations:
$$p^o \sim e^{i\theta/\epsilon}\Big\{\tilde A_0[1 - (\epsilon\ln\epsilon)\,i\bar\rho_\infty M^2\omega\bar v_\infty]H_n^{(1)}(i\lambda\epsilon^{-1/N}\tilde r) + \epsilon\Big[(\tfrac12\pi\bar\rho_\infty M^2\omega\bar v_\infty\tilde A_0\delta_{n0} + \tilde A_1 + E)H_n^{(1)} + F\,\frac{\partial H_n^{(1)}}{\partial(\epsilon^{-1/N}\tilde r)}\Big] + O(\epsilon^2\ln\epsilon)\Big\},$$
  with $E = -i\bar\rho_\infty M^2\omega\bar v_\infty\tilde A_0\ln(\epsilon^{-1/N}\tilde r) - \frac{i\alpha^2\alpha'\tilde A_0}{2\lambda^4}\big((\lambda\epsilon^{-1/N}\tilde r)^2 + n^2\big)$ and $F = \frac{i\bar v_\infty\tilde A_0}{\omega\epsilon^{-1/N}\tilde r} + \frac{i}{2\lambda^4}(\bar\rho_\infty M^2\omega^2\alpha'\tilde A_0 - 2\lambda^2\alpha\tilde A_0' + \alpha^2\alpha'\tilde A_0)\epsilon^{-1/N}\tilde r$, where α′ = dα/ds and δ_{n0} = 1 for n = 0, 0 otherwise.
- **Order-one matching** (eqs. 4.7–4.8, p. 280): $A_0(s) = \tilde A_0(s)$ and $B_0(s) = 0$.
- **Gauge functions** (eq. 4.9, p. 280): $\delta_1(\epsilon) = \epsilon\ln\epsilon$, $\delta_2(\epsilon) = \epsilon$. Then $[\hat p_1, \hat v_1] = -i\bar\rho_\infty M^2\omega\bar v_\infty A_0[\zeta_1^p, \zeta_1^v]$ (eq. 4.10, p. 281).
- **Order-ε inner solution** (eqs. 4.11–4.13, p. 281): with the fundamental matrix $\boldsymbol\Psi = \begin{pmatrix}\zeta_1^p & \zeta_2^p\\ \zeta_1^v & \zeta_2^v\end{pmatrix}$ and Wronskian
$$W(\zeta_1,\zeta_2) = -\frac{4(\omega - \alpha\bar u)}{\pi\bar\rho_\infty\omega^2 r},$$
$$\begin{bmatrix}\hat p_2\\ \hat v_2\end{bmatrix} = A_1(s)\begin{bmatrix}\zeta_1^p\\ \zeta_1^v\end{bmatrix} + \boldsymbol\Psi\int_0^r\boldsymbol\Psi^{-1}(r',s)\begin{bmatrix}R_2(r',s)\\ Q_2(r',s)\end{bmatrix}dr' .$$
  For r > r_m the integrands are products of Hankel functions and powers of r′, integrable in closed form (Watson; Gradshteyn & Ryzhik).
- **Order-ε matching** (eqs. 4.14–4.16, p. 281–282). The intermediate limit of the three-term inner solution contains $(\bar E + E)H_n^{(1)} + F\,\partial H_n^{(1)} + D\,H_n^{(2)}$ at O(ε), with
$$\bar E = A_1 - \frac{\pi\bar\rho_\infty\omega^2}{4}\left[\int_0^{r_m}\frac{r}{\omega - \alpha\bar u}(\zeta_2^vR_2 - \zeta_2^pQ_2)\,dr + \int_{r_m}\frac{r}{\omega}(\zeta_2^vR_2 - \zeta_2^pQ_2)\,dr\right] - \frac{in(2\alpha A_0' + \alpha' A_0)}{2\lambda^2} + \frac{2i\alpha^2\alpha' A_0(n^2 + 2n)}{4\lambda^4},$$
$$D = -\frac{\pi\bar\rho_\infty\omega^2}{4}\left[\int_0^{r_m}\frac{r}{\omega - \alpha\bar u}(\zeta_1^pQ_2 - \zeta_1^vR_2)\,dr + \int_{r_m}\frac{r}{\omega}(\zeta_1^pQ_2 - \zeta_1^vR_2)\,dr\right].$$
  Matching requires $\bar E = \tfrac12\pi\bar\rho_\infty M^2\omega\bar v_\infty\tilde A_0\delta_{n0} + \tilde A_1$ (eq. 4.15) and $D = 0$ (eq. 4.16).
  - > The upper limit of the second integral in $\bar E$ and D is not printed (presumably the running radius r).
- **Amplitude equation** (eqs. 4.17–4.18, p. 282):
$$I_1(s)\frac{dA_0}{ds} + I_2(s)A_0 = 0,\qquad A_0(s) = \hat A_0\exp\left[-\int_0^s\frac{I_2}{I_1}\,ds\right].$$
- **I₁ and I₂** (Appendix B, p. 294), with $\hat\omega = \omega - \alpha\bar u$ and all Hankel functions of argument iλr_m:
$$I_1 = \frac{\alpha r_m^2}{\bar\rho_\infty\omega}\left[(H_n^{(1)})^2 - H_{n+1}^{(1)}H_{n-1}^{(1)}\right] + \int_0^{r_m}\frac{\omega r}{\hat\omega}\left[\bar\rho\bar u(\zeta_1^v)^2 - M^2\bar u(\zeta_1^p)^2 - \frac{\omega}{\hat\omega}\zeta_1^p\zeta_1^u - \frac{n\bar u}{\hat\omega r}\zeta_1^p\zeta_1^w - \frac{\alpha}{\bar\rho\hat\omega}(\zeta_1^p)^2\right]dr,$$
$$I_2 = \frac{\bar v_\infty}{\bar\rho_\infty\omega^2}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial r}H_n^{(1)}\right]^2 + M^2\bar v_\infty[H_n^{(1)}]^2 + \frac{\alpha' r_m^2}{2\bar\rho_\infty\omega}\left[(H_n^{(1)})^2 - H_{n+1}^{(1)}H_{n-1}^{(1)}\right] + \frac{\alpha\alpha'\lambda' r_m^2}{\bar\rho_\infty\omega\lambda}H_{n+1}^{(1)}H_{n-1}^{(1)} + \int_0^{r_m}\frac{\omega r}{\hat\omega}\Big[\bar\rho\bar v_1\zeta_1^v\frac{\partial\zeta_1^v}{\partial r} + \bar\rho(\zeta_1^v)^2\frac{\partial\bar v_1}{\partial r} + \bar\rho\bar u\zeta_1^v\frac{\partial\zeta_1^v}{\partial s} - M^2\bar v_1\zeta_1^p\frac{\partial\zeta_1^p}{\partial r} - M^2\bar u\zeta_1^p\frac{\partial\zeta_1^p}{\partial s} - \frac{\omega}{\hat\omega}\zeta_1^p\frac{\partial\zeta_1^u}{\partial s} - \frac{n\bar v_1}{\hat\omega r}\zeta_1^p\frac{\partial\zeta_1^w}{\partial r} - \frac{n\bar v_1}{\hat\omega r^2}\zeta_1^p\zeta_1^w - \frac{n\bar u}{\hat\omega r}\zeta_1^p\frac{\partial\zeta_1^w}{\partial s} - \frac{\alpha\bar v_1}{\hat\omega}\zeta_1^p\frac{\partial\zeta_1^u}{\partial r} - \frac{\alpha}{\hat\omega}\frac{\partial\bar u}{\partial s}\zeta_1^p\zeta_1^u - \frac{\alpha}{\bar\rho\hat\omega}\zeta_1^p\frac{\partial\zeta_1^p}{\partial s}\Big]dr .$$
  - > λ′ in I₂ is not defined in the paper (dλ/ds would be αα′/λ, which makes the printed term look redundant). ζ₁^u and ζ₁^w, the u and w eigenfunctions, are not defined explicitly either. Check before coding.
- **Near field to lowest order** (eqs. 5.1–5.3, p. 282):
$$p(r,x,\phi,t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(\eta)H_n^{(1)}(i\lambda(\eta)r)\,e^{i(\eta x + n\phi - \omega t)}\,d\eta,\qquad g(\eta) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}A_0(\epsilon x)\,e^{i\theta(\epsilon x)/\epsilon - i\eta x}\,dx,$$
$$(\overline{p^2})^{1/2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(\eta)H_n^{(1)}(i\lambda r)\,e^{i(\eta x + n\phi)}\,d\eta\right|,\qquad \lambda(\eta) = (\eta^2 - \bar\rho_\infty M^2\omega^2)^{1/2}.$$
- **Far field** (eqs. 5.4–5.8, p. 282–283), with x = R cos χ, r = R sin χ and χ measured **from the downstream jet axis**:
$$p \underset{R\to\infty}{\sim} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{g(\eta)\exp\{i[i\lambda(\eta)\sin\chi + \eta\cos\chi]R + in\phi - i\omega t - \frac12 in\pi - \frac14 i\pi\}}{(\frac12\pi i\lambda(\eta)R\sin\chi)^{1/2}}\,d\eta,$$
  with the stationary point at $\eta = \bar\rho_\infty^{1/2}M\omega\cos\chi$, so
$$p \underset{R\to\infty}{\sim} \frac{2}{R}\,g(\bar\rho_\infty^{1/2}M\omega\cos\chi)\exp\left[i(\bar\rho_\infty^{1/2}M\omega R + n\phi - \omega t) - \tfrac12 i(n+1)\pi\right],\qquad D(\chi) = \lim_{R\to\infty}\tfrac12R^2|p|^2 = 2\left|g(\bar\rho_\infty^{1/2}M\omega\cos\chi)\right|^2 .$$
  In these units $\bar\rho_\infty^{1/2}M = u_j/a_\infty$ for a pressure-matched jet of the same gas (my identity). So the stationary point is the acoustic wavenumber times cos χ, and the branch point $\eta_c = \bar\rho_\infty^{1/2}M\omega$ is where the phase speed ω/η equals a_∞ (p. 289).
- **Mean-velocity profiles** (eqs. 6.1–6.3, p. 283–284):
$$\text{core: } \bar u = \begin{cases}1 & (r < h)\\ \exp\left[-(\ln 2)\left(\dfrac{r - h(x)}{b(x)}\right)^2\right] & (h \le r)\end{cases},\qquad \text{developed: } \bar u = u_c(x)\exp\left[-(\ln 2)\left(\frac{r}{b(x)}\right)^2\right],$$
$$\text{transition: } \bar u = \begin{cases}u_c(x) & (r < h)\\ u_c(x)\exp\left[-(\ln 2)\left(\dfrac{r - h(x)}{b(x)}\right)^2\right] & (h \le r)\end{cases},\qquad r_m = h + 3b .$$
- **Appendix A** (p. 294). From $\tilde v = \frac{1}{ik}\frac{\partial\tilde u}{\partial\bar r}$, $\tilde w = \frac{n}{k\bar r}\tilde u$ and $\tilde p = \frac{\bar\rho_\infty}{k}\left[\frac{\omega}{\epsilon}\tilde u + i\epsilon^2\frac{\bar v_\infty}{\bar r}\frac{\partial\tilde u}{\partial\bar r}\right]$ (A 1), and with $y = \bar r^2 - \epsilon^4\bar\rho_\infty M^2\bar v_\infty^2$, eq. 3.2 becomes (A 2), whose right-hand side $-n^2\epsilon^4\bar\rho_\infty M^2\bar v_\infty^2\tilde u/[4y^2(y + \epsilon^4\bar\rho_\infty M^2\bar v_\infty^2)]$ vanishes for n = 0 and is O(ε²) for $y \ge O(\epsilon^{2-1/N})$ otherwise. Dropping it gives
$$\tilde u = y^{-\frac12 i\epsilon\bar\rho_\infty M^2\omega\bar v_\infty}H_\nu^{(1)}\big(i(k^2\epsilon^2 - \bar\rho_\infty\omega^2M^2)^{1/2}y^{1/2}/\epsilon\big)\quad\text{(A 3)}.$$

## Relevance to this project
This is the founding calculation behind the instability-wave (wavepacket) picture of supersonic jet mixing noise. It is the "Tam & Burton (1984)" that [Tam 1995](tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) uses as the solution step of his stochastic model (that note, p. 24–27), that [Tam & Hu 1989](tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md) and [Tam & Ahuja 1990](tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md) use for finite-thickness eigenvalues and Gaussian profiles, and that [Tam et al. 1986](tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md) uses for its instability wave. It sits in CLAUDE.md's second research area, rocket-noise modelling, through Mach wave radiation, and in the modelling side the user wants covered as fully as experiments.

**How it fits and what has changed since**
- **Acoustic matching, stated in 1984.** D(χ) = 2|g(η = (u_j/a_∞)ω cos χ)|² (eq. 5.8) is the far-field rule that [Cavalieri 2019](cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md) restate for line-source wavepackets: only |k_x| < ω/a_∞ radiates, and for supersonic convection the energetic wavenumber already lies inside that band (that note, eqs. 40–43, p. 12–13). Tam & Burton get g from a computed, non-parallel wave rather than a postulated Gaussian, and they show that the near field is that wave plus its Hankel-function continuation.
- **The precursor of PSE wavepacket models.** The inner solution (local eigenproblem plus an amplitude equation from matching) is the multiple-scales stage in the hierarchy of [Jordan & Colonius 2013](jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise.md) (that note, §3.1, p. 182–183). Its recipe is the one [Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md) later used with PSE: fit Gaussian-type profiles to the measured mean flow, spline the parameters in x for smoothness, march a linear wave and fix one amplitude per (m, ω) (that note, eq. 3.2, p. 105).
  - The two share a failure. Both put the St 0.2 peak upstream of the data; Gudmundsson & Colonius blame non-parallel effects at wavelengths comparable to the core length (that note, p. 110), and Tam & Burton leave it unexplained (p. 287). That the multiple-scales correction, which is non-parallel by construction, fails the same way suggests a cause beyond weak non-parallelism, e.g. the Orr mechanism later found by resolvent analysis (my reading; see [linear-models](../Topics/linear-models.md)).
- **It settles Michalke's dispute for the outer field.** [Michalke 1984](michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md) records that multiple-scales solutions are not uniformly valid far from the shear layer (Tam & Morris 1980), and the Crighton & Gaster against Morris & Tam clash over locally parallel phase speeds (that note, p. 192). Matching to an outer Fourier solution removes the first objection; the second is moot here because only the Fourier spectrum of the whole wave enters the radiation.
- **Linear saturation.** The claim that the wave peaks because the jet diverges, not through nonlinear saturation (p. 275), agrees with Michalke's divergence-capped gain (Michalke note, p. 190–191) and with the modern "linear on the mean" consensus. Here, though, the forcing is gentle and the mean flow is the forced one, so this is not evidence for natural jets.
- **Convective Mach number.** The computed spectral peaks give c ≈ 0.80–0.91u_j and radiation near 35–44° (my reading of Figs 9–10), matching the stated lobes. For this cold M 2.1 jet Papamoschou–Roshko's M_c is only 0.89 ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md)), so their convective speed would predict no Mach waves at all. The Mach waves here come from KH waves faster than a_∞, not from the supersonic family of Tam & Hu 1989, which needs M_j > 2.37 at this temperature ratio (my calculation). This supports the topic-page caution that the eddy Mach number U_c/a_∞, not M_c, sets Mach-wave direction.
- **Limits.** One forced, cold, moderate-Re jet; two frequencies; three modes from a point exciter; amplitudes fitted (centreline peak, one near-field point); a shift of the whole St 0.2 field; no far-field data; no heated jets. Natural turbulent jets need a statistical description of the wave amplitudes ([Tam & Chen's](tam-1994-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md) stochastic model, as summarised in the [Tam 1995](tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) note, p. 24–27) and, at subsonic convection, jitter ([Cavalieri 2019](cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 14–16).

**Methods and data for the lab workflow (CLAUDE.md)**
- **What the model needs.** A mean flow, which non-time-resolved PIV (or Pitot surveys, as here) can supply, plus one complex amplitude per (n, ω). Here the amplitudes came from forced, single-frequency data; for a natural jet they would come from time-resolved data, e.g. the leading SPOD mode at the frequency of interest from TR schlieren or a near-field array.
- **Wavenumber filtering of SPOD modes.** The split of g(η) at η_c = ω/a_∞ (in u_j units) is the criterion for filtering the radiating part of an SPOD or HPOD mode: take the streamwise Fourier transform of the mode along a line or cylinder outside the shear layer and keep |k_x| < ω/a_∞. The paper does this for model fields only. For the lab's cold jets at M_j ≈ 1.1–1.5, u_j/a_∞ ≈ 1.0–1.25 (my calculation), so KH waves at 0.6–0.7u_j are subsonic relative to a_∞. Their radiation then comes only from the envelope's spread in wavenumber, which makes the k_x filter, not the convection speed, the useful diagnostic.
- **Near-to-far-field extrapolation.** Outside r_m the mean flow is quiescent, so eq. 5.1 propagates any azimuthal-mode pressure known on a cylinder to the far field with Hankel functions. That is a cheap alternative to FW-H for a near-field array, for SPOD modes of TR data, or for a cylinder in an LES (my suggestion, not in the paper).
- **Time variation.** The theory is for time-harmonic, constant-amplitude waves and says nothing about intermittency, mode switching or amplitude modulation. In the SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD pipeline, a slowly varying HPOD envelope A(x, t) could be fed into eq. 5.2 frame by frame to estimate time-varying Mach-wave directivity, provided the modulation is slow compared with 1/f (my suggestion).

**Modelling side and the PyFR staging LES**
- The PyFR LES (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`) is a cold jet at M_j ≈ 1.1, where u_j/a_∞ ≈ 1.0 and Mach waves are absent, so the paper's Mach-wave result does not apply directly. Two pieces transfer:
  - the Hankel-function continuation (eq. 5.1) as an FW-H cross-check on a cylindrical surface outside the shear layer, mode by mode;
  - the mean-flow recipe (half-Gaussian core, Gaussian far field, C¹ splines in x) as a cheap, smooth base flow for LSA or PSE of the LES mean flow. The shocks would need the Floquet/PFE extensions on [shock-cell models](../Topics/shock-cell-models.md).
- The numerical points still matter for anyone writing a local spatial solver: the bounded Frobenius start at the axis, Hankel starting values at r_m, the complex-r detour around the critical layer for damped waves, and the second Riemann sheet of λ for damped supersonic waves (p. 286).

**Rocket noise.** Mach waves from large-scale structures dominate rocket noise ([Lubert 2022](lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 7–9). This paper is the first quantitative demonstration, with a predicted lobe direction, that linear instability waves on the mean flow produce such Mach-wave fields. Rocket plumes are hot and far faster relative to a_∞ (κ ≈ 0.31 in that review), are not forced, and include the supersonic family; the method itself (non-parallel wave plus outer Fourier/Hankel field) carries over unchanged.
