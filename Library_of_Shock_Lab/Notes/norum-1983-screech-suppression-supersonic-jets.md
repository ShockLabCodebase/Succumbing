## Summary
Unheated, choked jets issue from straight tubes (D = 6.22 mm) fed through 1.5 m of tubing, so the exit flow is fully developed turbulent pipe flow. They run from just above sonic to β = 1.25 (NPR 1.9–4.3) in a small anechoic chamber, with one microphone traversed on a 92D arc and continuous-light shadowgraphs (p. 235–236). The baseline tube (lip 0.27D) shows four stages, A1, A2, B and C, identified by their wavelength branches. At least two coexist in the 15 s spectra over most of the range, and B dominates from β 0.3 to 0.7 (p. 236, 239). Near the B → C transition the shock-cell pattern switches between two states every few seconds. A reflecting baffle moved along the tube makes the dominant stage change 13 times in 6D, and placing it moves the stage boundaries by up to ΔNPR ≈ 0.4 (p. 236–237). Powell's model, a stationary array of monopoles at the shock-cell ends phased by U_c = 0.7U_j, is used to predict the directivity of each harmonic. It is judged "excellent", and its upstream-reinforcement condition λ = L(1 + 1/M_c) tracks stage C (p. 237–238). Exit changes then suppress screech by amounts that depend on the stage (p. 238–240). Removing the lip cuts A and C by 6–14 dB but leaves B, a short slot helps only B, and long slots or a bevel that break the axisymmetric shock cells cut 13–29 dB. External grooves enhance screech, and a reflecting baffle flush with the exit adds about 5 dB and turns the main lobe downstream. The modelling is limited to the monopole-array directivity and the phase criterion; there is no flow model or simulation.

## Key findings
- **Stages from wavelength branches** (Fig. 3, p. 236). The 20° spectra of the baseline tube (No. 1) contain stages A1, A2, B and C, with "at least two of these existing at a given pressure ratio over most of the β range". Harmonics appear whenever the fundamental reaches about 100 dB. The stages are named by matching wavelength branches to the earlier compilation (Fig. 2), with no azimuthal measurement.
  - Branch ranges (Fig. 3, read). A1 runs from β 0.10 to 0.845 (λ/D 1.50 → 2.08); B from 0.10 to 0.96 (2.00 → 2.88); C from 0.62 to 1.25 (2.20 → 3.17). A2 is only a few points at β ≈ 0.70–0.73, λ/D ≈ 1.78. Full table below.
  - A2 lies about 9 % shorter in wavelength (higher in frequency) than A1 at the same β, and C about 11 % shorter than B. These are the upward jumps of A1 → A2 and B → C (my arithmetic).
  - The compilation of Sherman et al., Powell, Davies and Merle (Fig. 2, p. 236) shows five stages with "reasonable scaling with jet exit diameter". By my reading its ranges are: A1 at β 0.33–0.55 (λ/D 1.43–1.72), A2 at 0.55–0.95 (1.42–2.15), B at 0.52–0.97 (2.15–2.95), C at 0.88–1.25 (2.35–3.15), and D at β ≈ 1.04–1.09 (≈ 3.3).
- **Dominant stages** (Fig. 9, p. 239). At 20° from the inlet axis A1 dominates below β ≈ 0.3, B from 0.3 to 0.7 and C above. Amplitudes repeat to about ±2 dB. Levels I read from Fig. 9 (92D, 120 Hz band):
  - A1 peaks at about 100 dB near β 0.2.
  - B rises from about 78–80 dB at β 0.1 to a peak of about 116 dB at β 0.63–0.68, then collapses across β 0.70–0.72.
  - C rises from about 92 dB at β 0.62 to about 128–129 dB at β 1.0–1.1, then falls to about 117–119 dB at β 1.25.
  - The non-dominant tones persist about 20–35 dB down: A1 at about 91–96 dB through the B and C ranges, B at about 93 dB at β 0.8–0.95.
- **Switching between states at the B → C transition** (p. 236). At β = 0.7 the shadowgraph shock-cell system "switched back and forth between different states every few seconds", with an audible change in the noise. A solid object such as a hand near the exit could hold the jet in either state.
- **Reflector-driven mode hopping** (p. 236–237). A metal baffle was moved from the exit plane to 6D upstream in 0.1D steps at β = 0.7, with the microphone at 20°.
  - Usually one stage dominated. The dominant stage changed 13 times among A1, A2, B and C over the 6D, with each stage holding for 0.2–0.8D of baffle travel. B dominated at more than 50 % of positions.
  - Within each window the tone frequency fell by 2–4 % as the baffle moved away ("a longer feedback cycle"). The tone then weakened sharply and the jet "locked onto" another stage, a Poldervaart-type phase effect.
  - The peak level at 20° was "relatively independent" of which stage dominated. Levels were lowest near transitions, and at one baffle position no tone could be seen at all.
  - Shock cells visible in the shadowgraphs: about 5 when B dominated (the rest obscured by large-scale undulations), about 8 for C, and 9–11 for either A stage. Where two stages had similar amplitudes, the shock-cell system switched between the two patterns.
  - The spacing of at least the first four cells did not change perceptibly with stage. The ends of the 3rd and 4th cells moved visibly during B.
  - When the baffle was "nudged" at an A2 → B transition, the downstream A-stage shocks lengthened before B's undulations obscured them. Norum takes this lengthening to explain B's longer wavelength and concludes that "the primary sources of screech appear to be associated with the downstream shock cells" (p. 237).
- **A baffle moves the stage limits** (Fig. 4, p. 237). With the baffle at positions chosen at β = 0.7, β was swept from 0.35 to 1.0 (boundaries read from Fig. 4):

  | Baffle position | Dominant stage against β |
  |---|---|
  | none | B to 0.71, then C |
  | "B" position | B to 0.88, then C |
  | "C" position | B to 0.66, then C |
  | "A2" position | A1 to 0.66, A2 0.66–0.75, then C; no B, except weak tones near β 0.4 |

  The B/C boundary thus ranges over β 0.66–0.88, NPR 2.42–2.90 (my conversion). Norum concludes that differences between investigators "should be expected", since objects near the nozzle change both the loop gain and the downstream shock development (p. 237).
- **Directivity** (p. 237–238). At every β where directivity was measured, the fundamental of the dominant stage peaked upstream at about 20° from the inlet axis (160° from the jet axis). The second harmonic generally dominates at 90°, and the higher harmonics show the extra lobes of a phased array (p. 237). Figure readings (mine):
  - C fundamental at β 1.1 (Fig. 5a): about 128 dB over 15–30°, falling to about 104 dB at 68°, a null at 90° (below the plot's 98 dB centre), and a downstream lobe of about 114 dB at 160°.
  - B fundamental at β 0.65 (Fig. 6a): about 115 dB at 20°, 99 dB at 50°, below 85 dB near 90°, and about 100–102 dB at 150–160°.
  - C second harmonic (Fig. 5b): two lobes of about 115–116 dB, at 20–30° and at about 90°.
  - B second harmonic (Fig. 6b): one main lobe of about 111 dB at 90°, about 14 dB above its upstream lobe.
- **Powell's monopole array** (p. 237–238). In Eq. 4 the directivity depends strongly on M_c and L/λ. The number of sources and their strength taper matter little: three equal sources give "the same general features" as nine with a parabolic taper. Norum calls the agreement "such good agreement… for all harmonics of both cases" and "Powell's model is an excellent representation of the acoustic process" (p. 238). My reading of Figs 5–6 qualifies this:
  - **Lobe angles agree** wherever Eq. 5 has a solution (table below): every C harmonic, and the 2nd and 3rd harmonics of B.
  - **Levels away from the main lobe do not.** For the C fundamental the predicted downstream lobe (about 118 dB at 148°, rising towards 126 dB near 175°) is about 10 dB above the measured one, and the predicted intermediate lobes of the C 3rd harmonic are about 10 dB high. The predicted curves appear to be normalised to the measured maximum, which is not stated.
  - **The B fundamental is not reproduced.** With λ/L = 2.70 and M_c = 0.74, Eq. 5 has no solution for n = 1. The predicted pattern is a broad plateau of about 107–110 dB, rising only at ψ → 0° and 180°, whereas the measured pattern has a strong upstream lobe and a deep minimum at 60–130°.
- **The phase criterion against the stages** (Fig. 7, p. 238). Eq. 6 with n = 1, L = 0.9 × the measured third-cell length and U_c = 0.7U_j is "very close" to stage C and falls within the measured range of wavelengths. By my reading of Fig. 7 it lies 4–6 % above C for β ≥ 0.9, crosses C near β 0.75, and runs between A1 and B at lower β (A1 is 6–13 % shorter, B 9–23 % longer). So it gives one smooth curve and no staging. With the paper's L, B would fit Eq. 6 only with U_c ≈ 0.56U_j, and C with U_c ≈ 0.73U_j (my arithmetic).
- **Exit geometry and shock structure** (p. 239).
  - Thinner lips (Nos. 2, 3) and the external "tabs" (grooves, No. 9; a wire, No. 10) leave the wavelengths as in Fig. 3, and the shadowgraphs show no change in the first few cells.
  - A short slot (No. 8) slightly perturbs the shock cells and gives slightly shorter wavelengths for all three stages.
  - A long slot (No. 7) relieves the internal pressure so much that almost no shock cells develop. Only a single low-amplitude tone remains, above β 0.6, with a wavelength between those of C and B. Six long slots (No. 6) behave alike, but the tone persists down to β 0.4.
  - The bevelled tube (No. 4) has no discernible low-pressure screech, and at higher β a single tone slightly shorter in wavelength than C. Shocks are visible, but their symmetry about the centreline is "completely destroyed".
- **Suppression relative to the baseline** (Figs 8, 10, 11, p. 239–240). Suppression is the maximum level of the configuration's power spectrum minus the Fig. 9 baseline curve at the same β, at 20°. Negative values mean quieter.
  - At β 1.1 the directivity shape is unchanged, but eliminating the lip lowers the level by about 10 dB and disrupting the periphery with slots by another 10 dB (Fig. 8). My reading: about 126, 113 and 102 dB at 20° for Nos. 1, 3 and 6.
  - Reducing lip thickness (Nos. 2, 3) lowers A and C but has little effect on B (Fig. 10a). The wire tab (No. 10) gives a "consistent 5–10 dB" suppression but stays louder than the same thin tube without it (No. 3), except in B. The grooved exterior (No. 9) barely suppresses and enhances screech below the C range.
  - Of the internal changes (Fig. 10b), the short slot (No. 8) suppresses B more than the thin lip does but "destroys" the C suppression. The long slots (Nos. 6, 7) suppress "extensively" at all pressures. The bevel (No. 4) does similarly, except for "substantial increases" in amplitude at high β. A solid symbol means no tone was found, so the value plotted is a lower bound measured to the broadband peak.
  - A metal baffle flush with the exit (an orifice-like, infinitely thick lip) turns the main lobe downstream. Its maximum is about 5 dB above the largest unbaffled level, consistent with the lip-thickness trend. A foam baffle slid over the tube, covering the lip and extending 2D beyond the exit, gives only a slight upstream reduction, much less than removing the lip (No. 3) (Fig. 11, p. 240). A lower-porosity foam baffle and other β gave similar results (p. 240).
- **Conclusions** (p. 240).
  1. The stage correlates with jet "stability" as judged by the number of visible shock cells. Large-scale changes in the flow appear "at the instant of mode transition", and multiple stages at one condition imply that these changes "can occur randomly with time".
  2. The monopole array is "a correct representation for the generation of screech tones".
  3. Screech amplitude depends on "the amount of solid surface area near the jet exit". An external tab reduced screech only for "the most unstable mode, stage B".
  4. Internal slots act through "the degree of change to the axisymmetric shock cell development".

## Setup / method
- **Facility** (p. 235). Small anechoic chamber, 3.9 × 3.3 × 2.5 m internal, calibrated with point-source tones up to 50 kHz and free of reflections down to 0.2 kHz. Unheated pressurised air passes through a regulator and a flowmeter, with no plenum.
  - p_t is computed from the measured mass flow assuming uniform choked flow in the tube, so it is the exit-plane stagnation pressure. With a fully developed turbulent profile the discharge coefficient is below 1, so this slightly underestimates p_t and β. The bias is largest near sonic, and the paper does not estimate it (my comment).
  - Stagnation temperature is not given; T₀ ≈ T∞ is assumed in my conversions (TR ≈ 1). The paper's M_c values confirm it: 0.7U_j/c gives 0.737 at β 0.65 and 0.867 at β 1.1 against the printed 0.74 and 0.87 (my check).
- **Jets** (Fig. 1, p. 235, 238). Straight tubes of 6.22 mm i.d. (D) joined to about 1.5 m of similar stock tubing that passes through the chamber wall. This gives a fully developed turbulent profile at the exit and leaves no reflecting surfaces near the jet.
  - Baseline tube No. 1 has a 0.27D lip, so an outer diameter of about 9.6 mm (my arithmetic; this matches 3/8 in tubing with a 1.65 mm wall, my inference).
  - No. 2 has half the lip (0.135D) and No. 3 almost none. Nos. 4–8 break the internal periphery: a bevel (No. 4) and slots, short (No. 8), long (No. 7) and six long slots (No. 6). Slot dimensions and bevel angle are not given, and tube No. 5 is never described. No. 9 has a grooved external surface and No. 10 a small wire on the surface (simulated external tabs); the paper does not say which tube No. 10 is built on, though the text compares it with No. 3.
  - Baffles: a metal reflecting disc on the tube, a foam sleeve ("baffle 5") and a lower-porosity foam baffle ("baffle 4"). These baffle numbers overlap the tube numbers in Fig. 1, whose labels cannot be read in the scan.
- **Conditions** (p. 235). Shock parameter β from Eq. 1, from just above sonic (NPR 1.9, β = 0) to about NPR 4.3 (β = 1.25), that is M_j 1.0–1.60. About 1000 runs, each one configuration, β and angle (p. 236).
- **Acoustics** (p. 235–237). One ¼-in microphone on a digitally controlled traverse rotating about the exit plane, at 92D (572 mm) and 5–165° from the inlet axis. Directivity used steps of at most 10°, and all exit-geometry comparisons used 20° (p. 239).
  - Each run gives a 1000-line narrowband spectrum to 120 kHz (120 Hz bins), averaged over 15 s. "Screech amplitude" is the level in the 120 Hz band containing the tone (p. 236).
  - The microphone's azimuth relative to a flapping or helical plane is not given. The C fourth harmonic (about 78 kHz) is above the 50 kHz chamber calibration (my arithmetic).
  - At 92D the array (about 9 cells × 1–1.5D) is only marginally in the far field at the fundamental (r ≳ L_array²/λ_ac, my estimate), which matters for the Eq. 3 approximation.
- **Shadowgraph** (p. 236). A continuous mercury-arc point source, so the images are time-averaged or long exposures, not resolved per cycle. The chamber floor wedges were removed for the optics, and the microphone was used only qualitatively during visualisation. Shock-cell counts and "switching" were judged by eye.
- **Analysis chain and data requirements.**
  - *Narrowband PSD* (15 s, 120 Hz): stage wavelengths λ = c/f, amplitudes and harmonics. This is block averaging, so tones present together in a spectrum cannot be separated into coexistence and alternation; the seconds-scale B/C switching was detected by eye and ear, not in the spectra.
  - *Directivity*: level against angle from repeated runs at one β, each run a separate 15 s average.
  - *Suppression*: configuration level minus a smooth baseline curve (Fig. 9) at the same β, at one angle (20°). It depends on the 20° choice, and it measures a lower bound when no tone is present.
  - *Shadowgraph*: number of visible cells and the third-cell length L for Eqs 4–6.
- **Modelling** (p. 237–238). Powell's stationary phased array of monopoles at the shock-cell ends (Eqs 2–4), with:
  - U_c = 0.7U_j (citing Harper-Bourne & Fisher 1973 and Norum & Seiner 1982);
  - L = 0.9 × the third-cell length from shadowgraphs, on the grounds that the downstream spacing represents the sources and spacing shrinks downstream;
  - nine sources with strengths falling parabolically from the central one.

  Assumptions, stated or implied: point monopoles fixed in space, equal spacing, a far-field (Fraunhofer) phase, a uniform ambient medium with no refraction by the jet, no source directivity, and no azimuthal structure. B (flapping) and C (helical) are treated the same way. The harmonics are evidently computed with λ/k in Eq. 4; the paper does not write this out. The frequency condition, Eq. 6, is the upstream (ψ = 0) case of Eq. 5. No flow, stability or numerical model is solved.

## Key equations
Checked against the rendered pages (p. 235, 237).
- **Shock parameter** (eq. 1, p. 235): $\beta^2 = M_j^2 - 1 = 5\,(p_t/p_a)^{2/7} - 6$. Inverted, NPR = [(β² + 6)/5]^{7/2}: β = 0 gives 1.893, and β = 1.25 gives 4.255 (my check of the stated 1.9 and 4.3).
- **Phased monopole array** (eq. 2, p. 237): $p = \sum_j \dfrac{S_j}{r_j}\exp\!\left[i\,\dfrac{2\pi c}{\lambda}\left(t - \dfrac{r_j}{c} - \dfrac{x_j}{U_c}\right)\right]$. Here λ is the screech wavelength, x_j the distance from the exit to source j, r_j the source–observer distance, U_c the disturbance convection velocity, c the ambient sound speed and S_j the strength of source j.
- **Far-field approximation** (eq. 3): $r_j \simeq r_0 + x_j\cos\psi$, where ψ is the observer angle from the **inlet** (upstream) axis and r₀ the distance from the exit.
- **Equal spacing L, x_j = jL** (eq. 4): $p = \exp\!\left[\dfrac{i2\pi(ct - r_0)}{\lambda}\right]\sum_j \dfrac{S_j}{r_j}\exp\!\left[-i2\pi j\,\dfrac{L}{\lambda}\left(\dfrac{1}{M_c} + \cos\psi\right)\right]$, with M_c = U_c/c.
- **Reinforcement** (eq. 5): $\dfrac{L}{\lambda}\left(\dfrac{1}{M_c} + \cos\psi\right) = n$, n an integer.
- **Upstream reinforcement, the screech wavelength** (eq. 6, ψ = 0): $\lambda = \dfrac{L}{n}\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{M_c}\right)$. With n = 1 and f = c/λ this is Powell's formula $f = U_c/[L(1 + M_c)]$ (my rewriting). The paper adds that the real wavelength is "a more complicated function" of the loop's lengths, but must be close to Eq. 6 for upstream amplification (p. 237).
- **Harmonic k** (my form of how Eq. 5 is applied to the harmonics): lobes where $\cos\psi = n\lambda/(kL) - 1/M_c$. At ψ = 90° the phase step between sources is L/(λM_c) cycles. That is 0.547 for C and 0.501 for B, close to half a cycle, which gives the measured null of the fundamental and the 2nd-harmonic lobe at 90° (my arithmetic).

## Notation
- β: shock parameter (M_j² − 1)^½; M_j: fully expanded Mach number; p_t, p_a: jet stagnation and ambient pressures; NPR = p_t/p_a.
- D: tube inner diameter (6.22 mm); t: lip thickness; λ: acoustic wavelength of the screech tone (= c/f); c (a∞): ambient sound speed.
- L: source (shock-cell) spacing, taken as 0.9 × the third-cell length; x_j, r_j, r₀: source position, source–observer and exit–observer distances; S_j: source strength; n: integer.
- U_c, M_c = U_c/c: disturbance convection velocity and Mach number; U_j: fully expanded velocity.
- ψ: observer angle from the **inlet (upstream) axis**. Library notes that use the downstream axis (θ or φ) have θ = 180° − ψ, so "20° from the inlet axis" is "Norum's 160°".
- Stage labels A1, A2, B, C, D after Merle and Davies & Oldfield; configuration numbers 1–10 refer to the tubes in Fig. 1.

## Relevance to this project
Norum 1983 is the classic reference for three claims the library already cites second-hand: the screech fundamental peaks about 20° from the inlet axis (160° from the jet axis) with the second harmonic at 90°; a thicker lip means louder screech; and slots or tabs suppress it. It is also an early, mostly overlooked staging study: reflector-driven mode hopping, seconds-scale switching between shock-cell states, and stage limits that move with the surroundings. It fits the CLAUDE.md research area (resonance and feedback in shock-containing jets, acoustics plus flow visualisation), and its single-microphone, block-averaged data are what modern time-resolved methods would revisit.

**How it fits the staging thread** ([screech-staging-and-mode-switching](../Topics/screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md))
- **Stage limits belong to the rig.** In this 0.27D-lip tube with fully developed pipe flow, B dominates from NPR ≈ 2.0 (β 0.3) and C from ≈ 2.5 (β 0.71) (Fig. 9). The thick-lip nozzle of [Powell 1992](powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md) has A1/A2 ≈ 2.3, A2/B ≈ 2.5 and B/C ≈ 3.2 (that note, Fig. 17). [Ponton 1992](ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) has the B → C switch at M_j 1.43–1.44, against 1.23 here.
  - So here C takes over about 0.2 lower in M_j, though each branch's wavelength agrees with the Fig. 2 compilation within a few per cent (my comparison). The branches are where the literature puts them; only which one dominates shifts.
  - The shift is in the direction of Ponton's thick-lip result (a wider C range). The thick, turbulent pipe-flow shear layer is the other obvious candidate, given that shear-layer thickness matters more than shock strength in the models ([Nogueira 2022](nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md); see [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](../Topics/screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md)). The paper cannot separate the two (my inference).
- **Moving one reflector moves the B/C limit over NPR 2.42–2.90** (Fig. 4), more than twice the spread of A1 → A2 NPRs across the six facilities in the topic page's table (2.17–2.35). This is the strongest quantitative evidence in the library that a staging NPR is not a property of the jet alone. It parallels [Henderson & Powell 1993](henderson-1993-tones-choked-jet-impinging-flat-plates.md) (a reflector swaps impinging tones) and [Weightman 2019](weightman-2019-nozzle-external-geometry-azimuthal-mode-impinging-jet.md) (the exterior selects the loop at fixed frequencies).
- **Switching, not only coexistence.** At β 0.7 the shock-cell pattern alternates "every few seconds" and a hand can hold either state (p. 236). This is an earlier report than Powell 1992's "switches rapidly" (A2/B), and its seconds-scale dwell times match [Mancinelli 2019](mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md)'s 1–4 s at A1/A2.
  - Where two stages had similar amplitudes, the visible shock-cell pattern switched between the two stages' patterns (p. 236). That is an early qualitative sign that each loop has its own mean flow ([Mazharmanesh 2026](mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) p. 11–13). The cell counts (about 5 in B, 8 in C, 9–11 in A) parallel [André 2013](andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md)'s 6 cells against 11 with and without screech.
  - The 15 s spectra still show at least two stages at most β (p. 236), and the paper cannot tell whether those coexist or alternate; the abstract's "present at most jet operating conditions" refers to block averages.
- **Frequency drift before a hop.** Within each baffle window the tone falls 2–4 % before the jet jumps to another stage (p. 236). This is the phase-criterion picture directly: the reflector detunes the loop until another loop wins.
  - My arithmetic: a reflector-phase cycle needs λ/2 ≈ 1.0–1.3D of baffle travel at β 0.7 (A1 to B). The observed 0.2–0.8D windows, 13 changes in 6D (mean ≈ 0.43D), fit four loops sharing that cycle.
  - This is the receptivity/reflection term that [Mancinelli 2019](mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md)'s resonance model fits as a phase.
- **The sources are downstream; the stages differ there** (p. 236–237). The first four cells do not change with stage, while the downstream spacing does, lengthening at the onset of B. Norum's inference that the sources lie downstream agrees with the 3rd–4th-cell sources of [Mercier 2017](mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) and [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md).
  - My speculation: a stage change visible only in the downstream cells is what the k_s2 picture of [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) predicts. Its k_s2 is a quasi-periodicity starting at the 5th–8th cell, and A2 closes on k_s2 while B closes on k_s1. Lengthening downstream A2 cells as B sets in would then be the downstream wavenumber reverting from k_s2 to k_s1.
  - A caveat on the A labels: they come from wavelength branches only. Here A1 is traced, as a weak tone, up to M_j 1.31, and A2 appears only near M_j 1.22. The A1 branch at β ≥ 0.5 overlaps where Fig. 2 puts A2, though A2 still sits about 9 % above A1 in frequency, as the modern A1 → A2 jump requires (cf. 0.606 → 0.694 in Mazharmanesh 2026).
  - None of the stage labels has azimuthal confirmation, so B and C are assumed flapping and helical from the literature.

**How it fits the screech mechanism and amplitude thread** ([screech](../Topics/screech.md))
- **Directivity.** The fundamental peaks about 20° from the inlet axis for both B and C (p. 237). This is the "Norum's 160°" used by [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) (p. 21–23) to argue that one leakage source is enough. The 90° minimum of the fundamental with a 90° harmonic lobe is the pattern that paper cites "for m = 1 modes".
  - The library notes quote this correctly: [Tam 1995](tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) (fundamental upstream, first harmonic at 90°; a thick lip about 10 dB louder with the same directivity) and [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) (upstream/downstream balance).
  - [Samimy 1998](samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md) puts the fundamental "near 150°"; the figures here give 15–30° from the inlet, that is 150–165°.
  - Norum gives directivities only for C and B. No A-stage (m = 0) directivity appears, so the statement "at all pressure ratios" (p. 237) covers only the conditions measured.
- **Amplitude scales with solid area near the exit.** Removing the lip cuts A and C by 6–14 dB, and a flush reflecting baffle raises the peak by about 5 dB (p. 239–240). This agrees with [Ponton 1992](ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) and [Powell 1992](powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md) (more lip, more loop gain).
  - The stage dependence is new information: B's amplitude barely changes with lip thickness, and only B responds to the wire tab and the short slot. Norum calls B "the most unstable mode" (p. 240).
- **Two kinds of suppression.** Thin lips leave the first cells unchanged and act on receptivity or reflection. Long slots and the bevel act by destroying the axisymmetric shock train (p. 239), cutting 13–29 dB.
  - [André 2013](andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md)'s shallow notches are the modern version of the first kind: they remove the tones while keeping the first cells' spacing and strength.
  - The NASA tab of [Norum 1982](norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md) (16–21 dB) and the intrusive tab of [Johnston 2016](johnston-2016-underexpanded-jet-intrusive-tab-visualisation.md) protrude into the flow and also change the shocks. The external "tabs" here (grooves, wire) do not protrude, and they are weak or even amplify (p. 239).
  - [Suzuki & Lele 2003](suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md) cite these slots as structure disruption; the paper itself attributes the slot effect to the shock cells, not to the vortices.
- **The data behind the 1982 report.** [Norum 1982](norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md) is a separate NASA data report (D ≈ 4 cm nozzles, a tab). Its fundamental "near 3 kHz" at β 1.00 gives λ/D ≈ 2.9 (that note's values, my arithmetic). That falls between this paper's C (2.62) and B (≈ 2.9, extrapolated) branches, so it cannot be labelled from frequency alone.

**Modelling side** ([screech-frequency-and-global-modes](../Topics/screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md))
- Eq. 6 is Powell's formula, the first row of the topic page's prediction table. This paper is the quantitative test of it that [Edgington-Mitchell 2019](edgington-mitchell-2019-aeroacoustic-resonance-review.md) (p. 124–125) cites for its sensitivity to M_c and L/λ.
- **What the test shows.** With U_c fixed at 0.7U_j and L = 0.9 × the third cell, it tracks C to 4–6 %, but it is one smooth curve and misses A1 by 6–13 %, A2 by 20 % and B by 9–23 % (Fig. 7, my ratios).
  - Matching B needs U_c ≈ 0.56U_j with the paper's L (my arithmetic), within the 0.52–0.59U_j near-nozzle range of [Mercier 2017](mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md). So the model fits each stage only with a stage-specific U_c or spacing, which is the "tuned constants" point on the topic page.
  - L = 0.9 × the third cell is in effect a shortened downstream spacing, like Tam's 0.8 factor and [Panda 1999](panda-1999-experimental-investigation-screech-noise-generation.md)'s L_sw ≈ 0.8L. In today's terms it is a stand-in for the k_s2 that closes C (Edgington-Mitchell 2022) (my inference).
  - This contrasts with Powell 1992's derivation, where B fits Powell's formula with the second-cell spacing and C sits 24 % above it (that note). The spacing definitions differ, so the two are not directly comparable.
- **The directivity model** gets the harmonic lobe angles right because they depend only on L/λ and M_c (table above). It misses the level of non-upstream lobes by about 10 dB and the B fundamental entirely.
  - A monopole array ignores refraction by the jet, source directivity and the azimuthal structure of flapping and helical modes. A B (flapping) fundamental measured in an unknown plane is exactly where it should fail.
  - The modern alternatives give directivity from a single anisotropic leakage source (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage), a distributed Mach-wave source (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 20) and harmonic resolvent beams for 2St_s ([Farghadan 2026](farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md)). None has yet been compared quantitatively with Figs 5–6. These figures, with their caption parameters, are a ready validation set.
- The return path in Eqs 5–6 is free-stream sound. Tones near the lower edge of the guided-jet-mode band travel at about a∞ (topic page), which is why the formula works as well as it does.

**Methods and data for the lab workflow (CLAUDE.md)**
- Everything here is block-averaged acoustics (15 s, 120 Hz bins) plus visual observation. The seconds-scale switching and the 2–4 % frequency drift are exactly what time-resolved methods should quantify:
  - wavelets or short-time spectra of a long microphone record at the B/C point;
  - SPOD of time-resolved schlieren, then reconstruction of each stage's band, then Hilbert POD to give each stage's instantaneous amplitude and frequency, with JPDFs to test mutual exclusion (anti-correlated amplitudes, as in Mazharmanesh 2026);
  - a reflector traversed slowly through one window should show the instantaneous frequency drifting 2–4 % before the hop.
- The shock-cell counts per stage correspond to conditioned mean fields. With time-resolved schlieren, averaging the frames in each state (classified from the reconstructed amplitudes) would give per-stage shock spectra (k_s1, k_s2) directly. Non-time-resolved PIV cannot classify frames by state unless a simultaneous microphone tags them.
- **Directivity in the lab:** 20° from the inlet axis is where the fundamental peaks, so a single upstream microphone suits stage detection. The 90° position is dominated by the 2nd harmonic for B and C (p. 237), and a 90° microphone can miss the fundamental entirely. Samimy 1998 met this problem.
- Record lip thickness, exterior shape, nearby surfaces and the inflow (contraction or pipe) for every campaign. Here they shift stage limits by ΔNPR ≈ 0.4 and amplitudes by up to about 29 dB.

**PyFR staging-continuation LES** (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`)
- The baffle results argue for modelling the nozzle exterior and any facility surfaces as built. A reflecting plane within a few D of the exit can move a stage limit by more than the A1/A2 bistable band the LES is trying to resolve.
- A cheap numerical analogue of Fig. 4: add an annular reflecting surface at two axial positions and check whether the A1/A2 limits move while the branch frequencies stay put.
- The LES inflow (a contraction with a thin boundary layer against developed pipe flow) should be stated when comparing with tube data like these.
- The directivity tables above (lobe angles and levels for B and C harmonics at 92D) are a far-field check for an FW-H post-process of LES states, though the LES targets A1/A2, for which this paper has no directivity.

**Rocket noise.** Full-scale plumes do not screech ([Lubert 2022](lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md)). The paper's interest for launch noise is the reflector result: a flush plate at the exit turns the main lobe downstream and adds about 5 dB (p. 240), a qualitative reminder that pads and deflectors redirect jet noise. The introduction notes that screech has caused structural damage in flight (Hay & Rose 1970; p. 235).
