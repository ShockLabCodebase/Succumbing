## Summary
The paper asks what a single triangular tab, pointing radially into the exit of a 15 mm converging nozzle, does to the screech and the near-nozzle flow of an underexpanded jet. Over NPR 2.1–4.5 a microphone 10D from the jet shows that the tab raises the screech Strouhal number at every NPR and changes where the stage jumps fall (p. 53). At NPR 2.6 the tab moves the tone from St 0.30 to about 0.46–0.48, removes the harmonics and adds a large broadband shock-noise hump (p. 54). High-resolution, non-time-resolved schlieren shows the tab's counter-rotating vortex pair cutting off the shock cells on the tabbed side, and two-point correlations of images taken in two orthogonal views show the antisymmetric B flapping pattern of the baseline jet replaced by a pattern symmetric about the axis, weaker on the tabbed side, which the authors call a "punctuated toroid" (p. 54–58). Correlation wavelengths with an assumed U_c = 0.6U_j reproduce both tones to within 10% (Table 2, p. 58), and brightest-pixel tracking over 5000 images gives a probability map of the vortex-pair path, tight to x/D ≈ 0.5 and spread over about 0.5D by x/D ≈ 2.5 (p. 58–59). There is no model or simulation.

## Key findings
- **Background the paper builds on** (p. 50–51):
  - Stages A–D after Powell (1953b): A toroidal, B lateral "flapping" (Davies & Oldfield 1962), C helical (Westley & Woolley 1975; Umeda & Ishii 2001), D a flap rotating slowly and unsteadily about the axis (Powell et al. 1992) (p. 50).
  - Lip devices: teeth gave up to 10 dB at the peak (Westley & Lilley 1952); "even one tab" changes subsonic jet development dramatically (Bradbury & Khadem 1974); notched supersonic nozzles shed trailing vortices (Pannu & Johannesen 1976); Tanna (1977) used lip projections to remove screech and study BBSAN; tabs weaken the shock structure and split the core into streamwise "fingers" (Samimy et al. 1993); delta tabs give the most mixing for a given blockage (Zaman et al. 1993) (p. 50).
  - A tab sheds a counter-rotating streamwise vortex pair from two sources, a "pressure hill" upstream of the tab (dominant) and reorientation of vortex filaments shed from its sides (Zaman et al. 1993); weaker horseshoe-type vortices form where the boundary layer wraps round the tab (Reeder & Samimy 1996) (p. 50–51).
  - Gap claimed: no earlier work on the vortex trajectories or the staging of a supersonic tabbed jet (p. 51).
- **Screech over NPR 2.1–4.5** (Fig. 2, p. 53):
  - "Screeching" means a spectral peak at least 7 dB above the surrounding SPL (p. 53).
  - The baseline jet screeches over the whole range, NPR 2.1–4.5. The tabbed jet screeches from NPR 2.3 to 4.5, with tones as strong as the baseline's only at NPR 2.6–2.8 (p. 53).
  - Baseline stage jumps at NPR 2.4, 3.0 and 3.8, with the stages labelled A, B, C, D in Fig. 2 (p. 53). A1 and A2 are not separated.
  - The tab raises St "for all NPR" (p. 53). Its first jump comes 0.1 NPR later than the baseline's (between NPR 2.4 and 2.5), and a "less distinct" jump falls between NPR 3.1 and 3.2 (p. 53). The tabbed jumps are smaller than the baseline's (p. 54–55).
  - Digitised from Fig. 2 (note author, ±0.005 in St): baseline A 0.64 → 0.51 (NPR 2.1–2.3), B 0.34 → 0.27 (2.4–2.9), C 0.31 → 0.25 (3.0–3.7), D 0.22 → 0.20 (3.8–4.5); tabbed 0.61 → 0.58 (2.3–2.4), 0.49 → 0.37 (2.5–3.1), 0.34 → 0.25 (3.2–4.5).
  - Jump sizes (digitised): baseline A → B −0.17, B → C +0.04, C → D −0.04; tabbed −0.09 at 2.4 → 2.5 and −0.03 at 3.1 → 3.2. The baseline B → C jump goes *up* and A → B and C → D go down, the pattern of [Powell et al. 1992](powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md) (p. 2825). The tabbed jet has no upward jump and no counterpart of the baseline C → D drop (note's reading).
- **Spectrum at NPR 2.6** (Fig. 3, p. 54; microphone 10D from x/D = 4 at θ = 120°):
  - The tab lowers the screech peak, but less than Samimy et al. (1993) found (p. 54). Read from Fig. 3: about 140 dB at St 0.30 (baseline) against about 137.5 dB at St 0.46 (tab).
  - Tone shift δSt = 0.16 (p. 54).
  - The baseline harmonics (read at St 0.60, 0.90 and 1.20, about 134, 130 and 120 dB, with weak peaks near 1.5, 1.8 and 2.1) are absent with the tab, but the tabbed SPL rises just above the first baseline harmonic (p. 54, 59).
  - The tab adds a "large hump" of BBSAN beginning at St ≈ 0.5 (p. 54). Read: it peaks at about 127.5 dB near St 0.65, against the baseline's broad hump of about 119 dB near St 0.8, with a second, flatter tabbed hump near St 1.3–1.5.
  - André et al. (2013b; a tab of about half the penetration and blockage, microphones beyond 50D) saw only a slight BBSAN rise. The authors attribute their large hump mainly to shear-layer turbulence from the relatively large tab rather than to the loss of screech (p. 54).
  - At high frequency the tabbed jet is about 3 dB louder, put down to mixing noise from the extra mixing, since no other source is known there (Tam 1995) (p. 54).
- **Schlieren at NPR 2.6** (Figs 4, 5, 7, p. 54–57):
  - The tab changes the position, shape and size of the shock cells and appears to thicken the shear layer (p. 54).
  - The vortex pair starts at the tab tip (Fig. 7, point A) and limits the radial extent of the shock cells on the tabbed side (points B and C, at about x/D 0.95, r/D −0.28 and x/D 1.8, r/D −0.18, read). The authors' explanation: the vortices increase mixing, shift the sonic line inwards, and the sonic line sets the shock reflection point, so the shocks end nearer the axis (p. 54, 58).
  - The pair is clearest as a sharp white streak from the tab tip in the ∂ρ/∂r RMS image; in the ∂ρ/∂r mean it is a bright streak between darker regions (p. 54).
  - Note's reading of the mean and RMS ∂ρ/∂x images (shock-reflection features near the untabbed boundary): baseline at x/D ≈ 1.10 and 2.17 (second cell ≈ 1.07D), tabbed at x/D ≈ 0.98 and 1.88 (≈ 0.90D), so the tab shortens the cells by about 16%. The paper gives no spacing. For comparison, Prandtl–Pack 1.306 √(M_j² − 1) D_j gives 1.01D at M_j 1.25 (note's calculation).
- **Instability mode from two-point correlation** (Fig. 8, p. 55–58):
  - Baseline: alternating red and blue bands that change sign across the axis, identified as the B flapping mode, citing Edgington-Mitchell et al. (2014) (p. 56).
  - Tab, off-axis view (tab in the image plane, at the bottom): strong correlation on the untabbed side, weaker on the tabbed side, and the pattern is now *symmetric* across the axis (p. 56).
  - Tab, on-axis view (camera looking along the tab): symmetric, with similar magnitudes on both sides (p. 56–57).
  - Symmetric patterns in two orthogonal planes are read as a change from the antisymmetric B mode to a "somewhat axisymmetric" mode like the A mode of lower NPR, similar "in axisymmetry only" (p. 57). The hypothesis is a toroid "punctuated or indented" on the tabbed side, with structures strengthening with angular distance from the tab and strongest opposite it (p. 57–58). The authors suggest the tab vortices interacting with the shear-layer structures cause the change (p. 58).
  - The correlation also picks up the acoustic field, because the images capture acoustic waves (p. 56). In Fig. 8 the bands reach r/D ≈ ±2, far outside the jet, so much of the pattern is the near field rather than the shear layer itself (note's reading).
- **Wavelength–frequency check** (Table 2, p. 56–58):
  - The tone is taken as set by convecting structures passing the stationary shocks, so f follows from the structure spacing and a convection velocity (p. 56). U_c ≈ 0.6U_j at NPR 2.6 is taken from "prior high-speed schlieren imaging", with no reference (p. 56).
  - Peak-to-peak wavelength λ_c 1.8 (baseline) and 1.2 (tab) gives St 0.33 and 0.50, against 0.30 and 0.48 measured (Table 2, p. 58): 10% and 4% high.
  - Inverting the measured tones gives U_c/U_j = St λ_c/(D_j/D) ≈ 0.53 (baseline) and 0.56 (tab, 0.54 with St 0.46), slightly below the assumed 0.6 (note's calculation, λ_c in D, D_j/D = 1.024).
- **Vortex-pair path** (Figs 9–10, p. 58–59):
  - Single-image tracks are "somewhat uneven" (Fig. 9). Over 5000 images the probability of the vortex position has a well-defined core between x/D = 0 and 0.5, then spreads quickly; by the end of the image the band is more than 0.5D wide (p. 58).
  - Conclusion: the path is constrained near the exit and increasingly variable downstream, diverging after x/D = 0.5 and spanning 0.5D by x/D = 2.5 (p. 59).
  - Note's reading of Figs 9–10: the path starts at r/D ≈ −0.35 (the tab tip; a 0.128D tab on the 15 mm lip puts the tip at −0.37) and drifts towards the axis, to r/D ≈ −0.05 by x/D ≈ 2.7, with the band spanning roughly r/D −0.3 to +0.15 there.
- **Conclusions** (p. 59): the tab changes the NPR 2.6 instability mode, raises the fundamental and removes its harmonic, raises SPL near the baseline harmonic, and the tabbed structures form a punctuated toroid: as strong as the baseline on the untabbed side, weaker in the plane perpendicular to the tab axis, weakest on the tabbed side.

## Setup / method
**Facility** (p. 51–52; Fig. 1):
- The LTRAC Supersonic Jet Facility (text cites Mitchell et al. 2012, i.e. [Mitchell 2012](mitchell-2012-visualization-acoustic-feedback-loop-impinging-jet-schlieren.md); the Fig. 1 caption cites Mitchell et al. 2013, i.e. [Mitchell 2013](mitchell-2013-near-field-structure-underexpanded-elliptic-jets.md)).
- Mixing chamber fed by the university compressed-air system (up to 7 bar), then a plenum with a honeycomb and wire meshes (screens).
- Converging nozzle with a smooth contraction on a 62 mm radius, ending in a parallel section; exit diameter D = 15 mm.
- NPR set by a high-precision valve and measured by an RS-461 transducer (0–10 bar, ±0.25%).
- Not stated: stagnation temperature, lip thickness, exit boundary layer, room treatment, and whether the rig is in an anechoic space.

**Modular nozzle tip** (Fig. 1b, p. 51–52; Fig. 6, p. 57):
- A tip fits on the parent nozzle. Two tips: one with a single tab and an untabbed "baseline" (p. 52).
- The tab is a triangle, base 3.8 mm and sides 2.7 mm, pointing into the flow along the radial direction (in the exit plane). Area blockage "just over 2%", maximum penetration 12.7% at the tip; silver-soldered on (p. 52). The Fig. 6 sketch draws it as a pointed, house-shaped piece reaching in from the tip ring.
- Check (note's calculation): height √(2.7² − 1.9²) = 1.92 mm = 0.128D; area 3.65 mm² = 2.06% of the 15 mm exit. This matches the stated values if the triangle's base sits on the 15 mm jet boundary, as the vortex path starting at r/D ≈ −0.35 (Fig. 10) suggests. Fig. 1b labels the tip bore Ø17.0 above the Ø15.0 parent exit; with the base on a 17 mm ring the intrusion would be only about 0.06D.
- Coordinates: x along the jet from the centre of the parent nozzle exit; r radial, outwards (p. 52).
- Views: "on-axis" (camera axis along the tab axis) and "off-axis" (camera axis perpendicular to it), set by rotating only the tip, with the optics untouched (p. 55). Fig. 5 (tab at the bottom, pointing up) is the off-axis view. The baseline needs one view (p. 55).

**Schlieren** (p. 52; Table 1):
- pco.dimax, 2016 × 2016 px, up to 1279 frames s⁻¹; 740 px/D; field of view 2.73D × 2.73D (40.9 mm).
- Light source: one rectangular LED (Luminus CBT-120), overdriven and pulsed only while the shutter is open (Willert et al. 2012). Pulse length, optical layout (Z-type or lens), mirror focal length and cut-off fraction are not stated.
- Knife-edge in two orientations, giving ∂ρ/∂x and ∂ρ/∂r images (Figs 4–5).
- The authors note the usual limits: path integration (a 2-D projection of a 3-D field; Settles 2001) and the trade between sensitivity (needed for acoustic waves) and range (needed for shocks) (p. 52).
- Not time-resolved: at most 1279 fps against a 7–13 kHz tone (note's estimate). Every analysis below works on uncorrelated snapshots.
- Image counts: 5000 for the vortex-path statistics (p. 58); not stated for the means, RMS and correlations.
- The acoustic source region (x/D ≈ 4) lies outside the imaged field, which the authors acknowledge (p. 53).
- The correlation maps of Fig. 8 span about 4.5D × 4.5D (x/D 0–4.5, r/D ±2.25), larger than the Table 1 field of view, so they come from a second, undescribed magnification (note's reading).

**Acoustics** (p. 52–53):
- G.R.A.S. type 46BE microphone, 16-bit, 4 Hz–80 kHz, 30 dBA–166 dB; the text calls it "highly directional" (p. 52).
- 10D from x/D = 4, at θ = 120° from the downstream axis. x/D = 4 is taken as the dominant source, from studies placing it at shock cells 3–4 (Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2014; [Shen & Tam 2002](shen-2002-three-dimensional-numerical-simulation-jet-screech.md); Umeda & Ishii 2002; Gao & Li 2010) (p. 53).
- For the tabbed jet the microphone is on the side opposite the tab (p. 53).
- Not stated: sampling rate, record length, spectral estimator and resolution, dB reference (per Hz or per bin), room treatment.

**Two-point spatial correlation** (eq. 1, p. 52; p. 55–56):
- Correlates the intensity at a reference point with every other pixel over the image set (after Willert et al. 2012 and Mason-Smith et al. 2015) (p. 52).
- Assumes coherent shear-layer structures appear as dark and bright bands in ∂ρ/∂x images, so a reference point in the shear layer sees large intensity fluctuations (p. 52).
- Reference point in the shear layer, marked by dashed lines at x/D = 2, r/D ≈ 0.8 in all three maps (Fig. 8, read). Contours from −1 to 1.
- Needs only independent snapshots. It gives the dominant spatial pattern only: no frequency, no time history and no separation of coexisting or alternating modes. Two orthogonal views are needed for symmetry because of path integration.

**Vortex-pair tracking** (p. 52–53, 58):
1. Take ∂ρ/∂r images, where streamwise vortices show as bright (or dark) spots from the radial density gradient (Settles 2001); assume the brightest point marks the vortex pair (p. 53).
2. Low-pass filter each image in the streamwise direction to suppress shear-layer structures and shocks (p. 58).
3. Start at the tab tip. Take a 1 px-wide vertical slice 1 px downstream, and a window several pixels high centred on the previous point, growing downstream to follow the spreading pair (p. 58).
4. Take the brightest pixel in the window as the vortex position; step one pixel downstream and repeat to the end of the image (p. 58).
5. Repeat for 5000 images and accumulate a matrix of positions, shown as a probability density over the RMS image on a nonlinear colour scale (0.04–28%; Fig. 10, p. 58–59).
- Filter cut-off, window sizes and growth rate are not given. The two vortices of the pair are not separated; the track is one brightest point per column.

**Modelling and simulation.** None. The only quantitative model is the kinematic relation f = U_c/λ_c with an assumed U_c (p. 56), plus the qualitative sonic-line argument for the truncated shocks (p. 54). No stability analysis, resonance model or computation is run.

## Key equations
- Two-point correlation (eq. 1, p. 52):
  $$R(\Delta x,\Delta r)=\frac{\sum I(x_0,r_0)\cdot I(x_0+\Delta x,\, r_0+\Delta r)}{\sigma(x_0,r_0)\cdot\sigma(x_0+\Delta x,\, r_0+\Delta r)}$$
  R: "covariance" (normalised); I: point intensity; σ: its standard deviation; (x₀, r₀): reference point; the sum is over images.
  > Checked on the rendered page: as printed there is no mean subtraction and no 1/N, but Fig. 8 spans −1 to 1, so I is presumably the fluctuation about the mean and the sum normalised by N.
- Strouhal number (eq. 2, p. 53): $St = f D_j/U_j$, with D_j and U_j the ideally expanded diameter and velocity.
- Implied by Table 2 (p. 56, 58; note's reconstruction): $St_{calc} = (U_c/U_j)/(\lambda_c/D_j)$ with U_c = 0.6U_j. Then 0.6/1.8 = 0.333 and 0.6/1.2 = 0.50, matching the table. The Fig. 8 axes are in x/D, so λ_c was presumably read in D and the 2% difference between D and D_j ignored; with λ_c in D the values would be 0.34 and 0.51.
- Not printed: the isentropic relation behind M_j = 1.25 at NPR 2.6, $M_j=\{[2/(\gamma-1)](NPR^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}-1)\}^{1/2}$, which gives 1.253.

## Notation
- D: nozzle exit diameter (15 mm); D_j, U_j, M_j: ideally expanded diameter, velocity and Mach number; NPR: plenum-to-ambient pressure ratio; Re: ideally expanded Reynolds number (definition not given).
- x: axial distance from the exit centre; r: radial distance (negative towards the tab in the off-axis images); θ: microphone angle from the downstream axis.
- St = f D_j/U_j; δSt: shift of the tone with the tab.
- I: schlieren image intensity; σ: its standard deviation; R: normalised two-point correlation; (x₀, r₀): correlation reference point.
- U_c: convection velocity of the shear-layer structures (assumed 0.6U_j); λ_c: peak-to-peak structure wavelength from the correlation maps.
- ∂ρ/∂x, ∂ρ/∂r: the two knife-edge orientations.
- A, B, C, D: the screech stages, attributed in the paper to Powell (1953b) (A toroidal, B flapping, C helical, D a slowly rotating flap; p. 50). On-axis/off-axis: camera along/perpendicular to the tab axis.
- BBSAN: broadband shock-associated noise.

## Relevance to this project
This group paper (CLAUDE.md: resonance and feedback in Shock Lab jets, schlieren plus acoustics) is the library's only round-jet study of how an azimuthally *local* lip change alters screech staging and mode shape. It is experiment only, on non-time-resolved data.

- **Loop selection by the lip, with a mean-flow confound.**
  - Like lip thickness ([Ponton 1992](ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md)) and the nozzle exterior of an impinging jet ([Weightman 2019](weightman-2019-nozzle-external-geometry-azimuthal-mode-impinging-jet.md)), the tab changes which mode the jet selects and moves the first stage jump by +0.1 NPR (p. 53).
  - Unlike Weightman's case, where the tones stay near St 0.25 and 0.39, the frequencies move at every NPR, because the tab also changes the mean flow: the sonic line moves in, the shocks are truncated (p. 54) and, by the note's reading of Figs 4–5, the second shock cell shrinks from ≈ 1.07D to ≈ 0.90D. So this is not a clean receptivity test.
  - A loop that keeps its shape should rise in frequency in proportion to the shock wavenumber (Powell's f = U_c/[L_s(1 + M_c)]; the k_kh − k_s picture of [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md)), so by about 1.2 here. The digitised branches fit this in part: the tabbed first branch is 1.20 × baseline A at NPR 2.3, and the third branch is 1.18–1.20 × baseline C over NPR 3.2–3.7. The second branch is about 1.5 × baseline B over NPR 2.5–2.9, more than the cell shortening explains, which is where the correlation at NPR 2.6 shows the B → A-like change (note's arithmetic and interpretation; the spacing was measured only at NPR 2.6 and only for the first two cells).
  - Only NPR 2.6 was imaged, so the mode at other NPRs, and what the tabbed 3.1 → 3.2 jump is, are untested. The tabbed jet also shows no C → D drop at NPR 3.8.
  - The baseline staging itself is rig-specific. The baseline A branch falls steadily from 0.64 to 0.51 over NPR 2.1–2.3 with no upward A1 → A2 jump, while the same lab's 15 mm nozzle gives A2 at St 0.63–0.65 at NPR 2.25 ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) data, see `Data/screech_tones.csv`). With 0.1 NPR steps an A1/A2 pair could be missed, but the values suggest the modular tip changes the low-NPR loop too (note's interpretation). The baseline B identification at NPR 2.6 matches the precessing flapping B mode of [Tan 2017](tan-2017-broadband-velocity-fluctuations-screeching-jets.md) at the same NPR in the same lab.
- **Tab size and strength of suppression.**
  - This tab (0.25D wide at the base, 0.128D deep, about 2% blockage) only shifts the tone and lowers it by about 2–3 dB at NPR 2.6 (Fig. 3, read).
  - The smaller Langley tab (0.125d wide, 0.063d deep) removed the tones and cut peak narrowband levels by about 16–21 dB at β = 1.0 ([Norum 1982](norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md) note).
  - Shallow notches remove screech while leaving the first shock cells unchanged ([André 2013](andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 666–668).
  - Condition (M_j 1.25 against 1.41), microphone distance and tab shape all differ, so the contrast is qualitative. It is consistent with the authors' remark that their reduction is smaller than Samimy et al. (1993) found (p. 54).
  - André 2013 finds the screech-free BBSAN hump up to about 4 dB louder and *higher* in frequency. Here the tabbed hump is about 8 dB above the baseline's broad hump and peaks *lower* (St ≈ 0.65 against ≈ 0.8, read), even though shorter cells should raise the BBSAN peak frequency. That supports the authors' view that the hump is mostly tab-generated turbulence (p. 54) rather than the effect of removing screech, though one microphone at 10D cannot separate the causes (note's reading).
- **Azimuthal coupling.**
  - A single tab breaks axisymmetry, so m = 0 and m = ±1 no longer decouple. The "punctuated toroid" is in effect an m = 0-dominated mode carrying phase-locked m = ±1 content set by the tab (note's interpretation).
  - This is the strong, localised version of the weak |m| = 2 streaks that split m = ±1 into flapping and wagging modes in [Nogueira 2026](nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md). An azimuthal-Floquet or biglobal stability analysis of a tabbed mean flow could test whether the tab makes the varicose-like loop the least stable (suggestion; no tabbed mean flow exists in the library).
  - Asymmetric trailing-edge cutouts in rectangular jets also generate streamwise vortices and suppress screech ([Samimy 1998](samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md)).
- **Method dependence on data type.** Every analysis here uses non-time-resolved snapshots (at most 1279 fps against a 7–13 kHz tone):
  - Means and RMS of ∂ρ/∂x and ∂ρ/∂r suit any snapshot set, including PIV.
  - Two-point correlation gives the dominant pattern and a wavelength, but a frequency only through an assumed U_c (Table 2). It cannot show switching or several modes ([Knast 2018](knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) note, same technique), and here it mixes acoustic and hydrodynamic correlation (p. 56).
  - The vortex-path PDF is a snapshot statistic: it shows meandering but not whether the meander is periodic.
  - The POD-pair route of the CLAUDE.md workflow would give the mode phase from the same kind of data, and a POD pair in each of the two views would test the punctuated-toroid symmetry more directly than single-point correlations.
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.**
  - Long time-resolved schlieren in the on- and off-axis views would test the punctuated-toroid hypothesis directly: SPOD at the tabbed tone gives its shape and symmetry in each view, and an azimuthal microphone ring would give its m content.
  - Reconstructing that band and running HPOD would show whether the tabbed-side "indentation" is steady or intermittent. Near the tabbed jumps (NPR 2.4–2.5, 3.1–3.2) it would show whether the jet alternates between loops, which the paper's spectra and correlations cannot.
  - Conditioning the tracked vortex position on the reconstructed screech phase would show whether the 0.5D spread at x/D 2.5 (p. 59) is screech-driven motion of the vortex pair or turbulent wandering (suggestion).
- **PyFR staging LES** (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`):
  - A 2% asymmetric lip change moved the first stage jump by 0.1 NPR and every tone (p. 53), so nozzle-tip details and any asymmetry belong in the LES geometry.
  - Conversely, a small deliberate lip perturbation is a candidate way to bias loop selection in a continuation run, though a tab of this size changes the mean shock train as well; a non-intrusive perturbation (notch or forcing) would separate receptivity from mean-flow effects.
- **Rocket noise** (minor): tabs led to chevrons (p. 51). The extra BBSAN and the 3 dB higher high-frequency noise (p. 54) show that mixing devices trade tones for broadband noise, which matters for shock-containing plumes.
