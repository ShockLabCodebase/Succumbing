# Spreading characteristics of compressible jets from nozzles of various geometries

- **Authors:** K. B. M. Q. Zaman, NASA Lewis Research Center, Cleveland, Ohio (p. 197). Single author; not Shock Lab group work. The RANS results are by his colleagues C. J. Steffen and D. R. Reddy (p. 218). J. Panda and G. Raman helped with some of the data acquisition (p. 227).

## Summary
Zaman compares how much jets from round, 3:1 elliptic, 3:1 rectangular and six-lobed convergent nozzles, from tabbed round and rectangular nozzles, and from rectangular orifices of AR 1–32 entrain, using the axial mass flux integrated from Pitot surveys of whole cross-sections (p. 198, 200–201). At subsonic M_j the asymmetric nozzles entrain only slightly more than the round one, and the orifices show no gain at all until AR exceeds about 10, so stretching the shear-layer perimeter is an inefficient mixer; the larger gains reported by others are put down to cleaner initial conditions that let low-speed asymmetric jets self-excite (p. 204–205, 216–218). At M_j 1.63 the screeching elliptic and rectangular jets entrain much more than the round jet and switch axes, while the lobed nozzle, which never screeches, entrains least (p. 207, 211). On the round nozzle the mass flux at x/D = 14 follows the screech stage: highest in flapping B, lower in helical C, high again in flapping D, with a steady RANS curve as the no-screech reference (p. 214–216). Tabs give the most spreading at every M_j even though they remove screech, through the induced motion of their streamwise vortex pairs, which RANS reproduces at x/D = 1; Table 1 sets that gain against flow blockage and thrust loss (p. 216–225). A control-volume scaling with the exit density and pressure-thrust term reproduces the fall of the far-field entrainment rate with M_j without any compressibility effect on mixing (p. 208–210).

## Key findings
- **Overall evolution and axis switching** (Figs 2–6, p. 202–205):
  - At M_j 1.63 the time-averaged laser-sheet cross-section of the plain rectangular jet becomes oval; with tabs the mixing layer has curled up by x/D = 1 and the jet has switched axes by x/D = 2 (Fig. 2, p. 202–203).
  - Instantaneous schlieren: tabs on the rectangular nozzle weaken the shock/expansion cells "drastically" and widen the minor-axis view; the major-axis width is at first smaller than without tabs, because of the rapid switch, and larger only far downstream (Fig. 3, p. 203). Tabs on the round nozzle spread it fairly uniformly in azimuth (Fig. 4, p. 203–204).
  - Iso-surfaces from the Pitot surveys: the plain rectangular and elliptic jets have switched axes by the first station, x/D = 10, at M_j 1.63 (Fig. 5), but show no switch over x/D 2–19 at M_j 0.3 (Fig. 6). The tabbed rectangular jet switches at both M_j and spreads most; the lobed jet does not spread more, "especially" at M_j 1.63 (p. 204).
- **Mass flux at three M_j** (Fig. 7, p. 204–207):
  - *M_j 0.3:* the round jet is lowest at every x; elliptic and rectangular jets are less than 10 % higher, against about 55 % for a 2:1 ellipse at x/D = 5 in Ho & Gutmark 1987 (p. 204–205). By the last station all jets except the tabbed rectangle are round in section and grow at a common rate, d(ṁ/ṁ_e)/d(x/D) ≈ 0.28, in line with Ricou & Spalding 1961 and Crow & Champagne 1971 (p. 205).
  - *M_j 0.95:* similar trends, but the round jet's flux is "consistently higher" than the rectangular and elliptic ones. The author conjectures an unsteady normal shock at the slightly rounded edge of the plastic round nozzle excited it (p. 207).
  - *M_j 1.63:* the round jet's slope is about 0.18; the lobed jet is below the round one; elliptic and rectangular jets are "significantly higher"; the four-tab round jet entrains vigorously upstream but its slope falls back to the round jet's by the end; the tabbed rectangle is highest throughout (p. 207). Read from Fig. 7(c) at x/D = 14 and 30: round 2.99 and 6.08, lobed 2.65 and 5.32, rectangular 4.07 and 8.09, elliptic 3.83 and 7.56, four-tab round 5.21 and 7.81, tabbed rectangle 5.75 and 10.60.
- **Asymptotic entrainment rate** (§3.1, Figs 8–9, eqs. 1–5, p. 208–210):
  - The round jet's far-field slope K falls with M_j: 0.28, 0.24 and 0.18 at M_j 0.3, 0.95 and 1.63 from Figs 7(a)–(c) (p. 208). Fig. 8 extends the round-jet surveys to x/D ≈ 66 at M_j 0.31, 0.96, 1.14, 1.37, 1.63 and 1.97, measuring farther downstream as M_j rises "until the streamwise variation was deemed linear" (p. 208).
  - Normalising by (ρ_a/ρ_e)^{1/2}, as dimensional analysis suggests (Ricou & Spalding 1961; Witze 1974), does not collapse the data: the normalised slope jumps across the transonic range (Fig. 9b, p. 208).
  - A control-volume momentum balance with the exit pressure-thrust term ξ, matched only to K = 0.28 at low M_j, reproduces both panels of Fig. 9 "quite good[ly]"; the normalised slope K₂ξ^{1/2} stays at 0.28 to M_j = 1 and tends to 0.367 at large M_j (p. 209–210).
  - Conclusion: the fall in K is "entirely" due to the exit density and static pressure, a consequence of normalising by exit conditions, and not the compressibility-driven drop in shear-layer growth (Papamoschou & Roshko 1988; [Papamoschou 1988](papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md)) (p. 210). A convergent–divergent follow-up is promised (p. 210).
- **Screech and spreading** (§3.2, Figs 10–13, p. 211–216):
  - *Far-field spectra at M_j 1.63* (Fig. 10, p. 211): the round jet screeches at "about 9 kHz" (≈8.65 kHz read, with a harmonic at ≈17.7 kHz); four tabs remove it. Rectangular and elliptic jets screech (fundamental ≈6.35–6.45 kHz with harmonics, read). Tabs on the rectangle leave weak tones at shifted frequencies (≈13.9 and 27.6 kHz, read). The lobed nozzle shows no screech here or anywhere in the M_j range studied.
  - *Lobed-nozzle explanation:* all non-tabbed jets except the lobed one screech at M_j 1.63, so the lobed jet's low spreading is attributed to its lack of screech (p. 211).
  - *Mechanism (argued, not measured):* screech organises the azimuthal vortical structures (less random, periodic) and intensifies them (tighter cores), as artificial excitation does; their later breakdown raises entrainment. Crow & Champagne's excited round jet gained about 15 % volume flux at x/D 8–10, mostly near the end of the potential core (p. 212). In asymmetric jets the initially asymmetric structures also deform by self-induction, which causes both axis switching and extra entrainment (Hussain & Husain 1989; Ho & Gutmark 1987; Zaman 1996b), and explains why the supersonic, screeching asymmetric jets switch while the subsonic ones do not (p. 212).
  - *Stages, round nozzle* (Figs 11–12, p. 213): A1 and A2 axisymmetric, B and D flapping, C helical. B is unstable and "has been observed to switch between helical and flapping motions" (Ponton & Seiner 1995). Read from Fig. 12 (top): A1 from M_j ≈ 1.09 (St 0.65) to 1.15–1.16 (0.51), A2 at 1.17–1.18 (0.55–0.56), B from 1.17 (0.39) to 1.44 (0.21), C from 1.45 (0.265) to 1.70 (0.175), D from 1.68 (0.15) to 1.79 (0.13).
  - *Stages, AR 3 rectangular and elliptic nozzles* (Fig. 11, p. 213): one frequency jump in the range, B′ → D′ around M_j 1.65 (p_t/p_a 4.61), "with a certain amount of overlap", for both nozzles. Unsteady pressure surveys (in Zaman 1996a) showed both B′ and D′ to be flapping "about the major axis plane" (p. 213). Read: B′ falls from St 0.62 at M_j 1.11 to 0.16 at 1.64–1.66; D′ sits at 0.20–0.185 over M_j 1.64–1.71, so the jump is **upward** in St and D′ lies on the extension of the round jet's C curve (note's reading).
  - *Open questions posed by the author* (p. 214): why B′ → D′ jumps with "seemingly similar" motion, why the round jet jumps from A1 to A2 when both are axisymmetric, what sets the other mode shapes, and what picks the helical direction and the flapping plane. All are left "far from clear".
  - *Mass flux against M_j, round nozzle* (Fig. 12, p. 214–215): fluxes at x/D = 14 are highest in flapping B, "relatively high" again in flapping D, and drop substantially at the onset of helical C. Read: ≈3.7–3.95 for M_j 0.32–1.10; 3.32 at 1.16 (A1/A2/B junction); 4.64 at 1.20; 5.64 at 1.32 and 5.55 at 1.43 (B); 3.58 at 1.456, 3.12 at 1.63 and 2.92 at 1.68 (C); ≈3.6 at 1.71–1.74 (D).
  - *No-screech reference:* the steady RANS fluxes in Fig. 12 fall monotonically, 4.2 at M_j 0.3, 3.5 at 1.0, 3.2 at 1.2, 2.8 at 1.4 and 2.2 at 1.6 (read); "had there been no screech" the supersonic fluxes would be lower (p. 215). Questions remain on the turbulence model and procedures (p. 215).
  - *Agreement with centreline data:* Glass (1968) and Sherman, Glass & Duleep (1976) saw the largest centreline total-pressure drop at pressure ratios in the B mode (p. 215).
  - *Lip sensitivity:* screech amplitude, but not frequency or mode shape, depends on details such as lip thickness, so flux amplitudes may not repeat with another nozzle, but the trend with stages should (p. 214–215).
  - *Why the round jet looked weak at M_j 1.63:* it was in helical C, the low-flux stage, so the round–lobed difference would be larger at B or D (p. 215–216); and at M_j 1.63 the asymmetric jets flapped while the round jet was helical, "perhaps the main reason" for their higher flux (p. 216). A flapping mode can be built from two opposite helical modes (p. 216).
  - *Six nozzles against M_j* (Fig. 13, p. 215–216): the elliptic and rectangular jets peak in B′ (read: ellipse 5.31 at M_j 1.27; rectangle 4.86 at 1.30), and fall as D′ is approached (ellipse 2.83 at 1.68). The author suggests screech amplitude, which "diminishes as a stage jump is approached", is responsible (p. 216). Both tab cases stay above every plain nozzle at all M_j, subsonic and supersonic (p. 216).
- **Shear-layer perimeter stretching** (§3.3, Fig. 14, p. 216–218):
  - At M_j 0.95 the orifice jets of AR 1, 2, 4 and 8 are "basically indistinguishable" from the round nozzle; spreading rises at AR 16 and clearly at 32 (p. 216). Read at x/D = 14: ≈3.6–3.7 for AR ≤ 8, 4.3 for 16:1, 5.0 for 32:1.
  - The six-lobed nozzle's wetted perimeter equals that of an AR ≈ 16 rectangle, and its subsonic gain is comparable to the 16:1 orifice (p. 216–217). Many more lobes would be needed to match screech or tabs (p. 217).
  - Threshold: a "noticeable" increase needs AR ≈ 10, i.e. a diameter ratio of about 2 (printed as D_H/D; values 1.8, 2.4 and 3.29 for 8:1, 16:1, 32:1) (p. 217, 226). These values are in fact D/D_H, the perimeter relative to a round jet of the same area (note's check below).
  - *Explaining the literature:* the low-Mach, low-Re jets of Ho & Gutmark and Hussain & Husain had laminar boundary layers and quiet cores, so background disturbances could organise the roll-up ("self excitation"). At M_j > 0.3 the present boundary layers are only "nominally laminar" with high broadband fluctuation, so roll-up is less organised, axis switching is delayed and spreading is not accentuated (p. 217–218). Hussain & Husain's tripped ellipse moved its switch from 3.5D to 15D and spread "significantly less" (p. 218).
  - Streamwise vorticity from upstream secondary flow in the contracting nozzles, absent in the orifices, may explain the nozzles' slightly larger subsonic fluxes (p. 218).
- **Streamwise vortex pairs** (§3.4, Figs 15–16, p. 218–221):
  - Hot-wire ω_x at x/D = 1, M_j 0.3, on a larger, geometrically similar tabbed 3:1 rectangular nozzle (D = 6.35 cm, Re_D = 450 000) shows the two primary pairs from the tabs plus two inner pairs of opposite sense, attributed to reorientation of the approach boundary layer (Fig. 15a, p. 218–219).
  - NPARC RANS reproduces the distribution and amplitudes, including the inner pairs (Fig. 15b). At M_j 1.63 it gives a similar pattern (Fig. 15c), which the author calls the first "proof" that tabs introduce streamwise vortex pairs at supersonic conditions; the higher plotted amplitude (colour scale ±3 against ±1) is "partly" due to the velocity scale used (p. 220).
  - Generation: a "pressure hill" upstream of the tab plus vorticity shed from the tab edges, reinforced when the tab leans downstream; Foss & Zaman (1999) found peak ω_x largest at φ ≈ 135° and circulation largest at 90°; an upstream lean (45°) gives less of both and more thrust loss (p. 220).
  - Dynamics (schematic, Fig. 16, p. 220–221): in-flow pairs first move to the axis by mutual induction, then regroup into out-flow pairs that eject core fluid and propel themselves outward; this gives the rapid axis switching and lateral spreading of the tabbed rectangle and the cross-shaped section of the four-tab round jet. Two smaller tabs on the narrow edges spread the rectangle much less (Zaman 1996b). Actual mixing still needs small-scale unsteady motion (p. 221).
- **Flow blockage and thrust** (§3.4.3, Table 1, Figs 17–18, p. 221–225), all at p_t/p_a = 4.47 (M_j 1.63):
  - Thrust loss for four delta tabs grows roughly as (w/D)², i.e. with geometric area blockage, up to w/D = 0.28, then less than linearly (p. 223).
  - Measured flow blockage does not follow geometric blockage and depends on orientation: delta tabs at φ = 135° block 6.2 % against about 8 % geometric; the same tabs inverted (45°) block 14.1 % (p. 223).
  - C_f falls steeply with tab size and then levels off; ṁ/ṁ_e at x/D_e = 14 rises steeply and levels off beyond w/D ≈ 0.28 (6 % blockage), put down to interference between adjacent tabs (Fig. 18, p. 224–225).
  - The non-dimensional circulation per tab, Γ/(U w), is about 0.1 for tabs an order of magnitude larger and smaller (Foss & Zaman 1999; Bohl & Foss 1996; Zaman et al. 1994), so circulation grows with tab size (p. 224).
  - The optimum four-tab size is w/D ≈ 0.28 by the performance factor (p. 225). The best case is the 3:1 rectangle with two delta tabs on the short edges: ṁ/ṁ_e at x/D = 14 from 3.12 to 5.63, about 120 % more entrained flow, for about 5 % loss in C_f (p. 225–227). The same tabs on the long edges interfere, resist axis switching and barely beat the plain nozzle (p. 225).
- **Conclusions** (p. 225–227): asymmetric nozzles add little subsonic spreading; perimeter stretching matters only above AR ≈ 10; screech raises supersonic spreading for every nozzle and most in the flapping stages; tabs beat everything, supersonic and subsonic, by streamwise vortex dynamics, at a thrust cost; "manipulation of the streamwise vortex pairs may hold the key" to further gains (p. 226).

## Setup / method
- **Facility** (p. 199): open jet; compressed air through a plenum with flow conditioning, exhausting into the quiescent laboratory. Plenum total temperature equals ambient. No anechoic treatment is described. Details are in Zaman 1996a,b.
- **Nozzles** (Fig. 1, p. 199–200):
  - All convergent, entering and exiting axially; exit velocity profiles top-hat for every nozzle and orifice (p. 199–200).
  - Asymmetric nozzles machined from aluminium blocks; interior along the major and minor axes contoured with third-order polynomials, the rest faired. 3:1 elliptic, 3:1 rectangular and six-lobed, all with equivalent diameter D = 1.47 cm (p. 200).
  - Round nozzle made earlier from clear plastic, D = 1.27 cm, with a slightly rounded exit edge (p. 200, 207). All nozzles had "endwalls" for mounting tabs (p. 200). Lip thicknesses are not given.
  - Tabs: "delta tabs", triangular, base on the wall, apex leaning downstream at φ ≈ 135°, apex angle about 90° (Fig. 1c). Four tabs with w/D = 0.28 on the round nozzle (≈2 % area blockage each, ≈8 % total) and two with w/D = 0.53 spanning the narrow edges of the rectangle (≈6 % each, ≈12 % total), chosen from parametric studies for large spreading (p. 200). Table 1 adds w/D 0.14–0.62, an inverted case and long-edge tabs.
  - Orifices: rectangular, AR 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, all with D = 2.54 cm; entrance faired with ≈5 mm radius; plate 6.35 mm thick (p. 200). Chosen because upstream secondary flow, and so streamwise vorticity, should be minimal (p. 216).
- **Conditions:**

  | Quantity | Value |
  |---|---|
  | M_j | 0.3–2.0 overall (p. 197, 226); full 3-D surveys at 0.3, 0.95, 1.63 (Figs 5–7) |
  | NPR | 4.47 at M_j 1.63; 4.61 at M_j 1.65 (p. 213, 223); isentropic, M_j from p_t/p_a (p. 199) |
  | Temperature | cold: plenum total temperature = ambient (p. 198–199); value not stated |
  | Exit boundary layer | "nominally laminar": round nozzle at low subsonic M_j, θ/D = 1.0/Re_D^{1/2}, core turbulence ≈0.5 %, but high broadband fluctuation inside the layer; similar for the large rectangle, with thickness non-uniform near the corners; not measured at higher M_j (p. 200) |
  | Re_D | ≈0.84 × 10⁶ at M_j 1.63 (round, p. 224); 4.5 × 10⁵ for the 6.35 cm hot-wire nozzle at M_j 0.3 (p. 219) |
  | Survey range | x/D 10–30 (no tabs) and 5–30 (tabs) at M_j 1.63; 2–19 at 0.3; to ≈66 for the round jet at M_j 1.97 (Figs 5, 6, 8) |

- **Pitot surveys and mass flux** (p. 200–202):
  - Automated surveys with a rake of three Pitot probes, taken only where the flow had become fully subsonic, to avoid supersonic-probe errors (p. 200).
  - The Pitot field on each cross-plane is integrated into the axial mass flow ṁ, assuming static pressure equals ambient; plenum and ambient pressures are monitored and each run normalised by its current conditions; transducer zeros checked before each run (p. 200–201).
  - Because the integrand never decays to zero in the induced potential flow (Crow & Champagne 1971), integration is truncated where the Mach number falls below 1 % of the local centreline value. Most data repeat within ±4 %, better upstream, worse at very low M_j (p. 201–202).
  - ṁ_e for normalisation: calculated for the plain nozzles; for the two tab cases measured by an upstream orifice meter, which the earlier conference papers did not do, so their tab fluxes differ slightly (p. 205).
- **Hot wire** (p. 202): two X-wire probes measure mean V and W on cross-planes; their gradients give ω_x (details in Zaman 1996b). Boundary-layer surveys at low subsonic M_j (p. 200).
- **Far-field acoustics** (p. 211): a ¼-inch B&K microphone about 70D from the jet axis, about 110° from the downstream axis. Spectral parameters (sampling, block length, averaging) are not given. Fig. 10 offsets each pair of spectra by one major ordinate division, so absolute levels cannot be read reliably.
- **Mode identification:** unsteady pressure-field surveys, in Zaman 1996a, not shown (p. 213).
- **Flow visualisation** (p. 202–203): unseeded laser sheet lighting moisture that condenses where entrained ambient air meets the cold supersonic core, so it marks the mixing layer (time-averaged images); instantaneous schlieren.
- **Thrust and mass flow** (p. 221–222): load cell with the plenum on linear bearings; simultaneous orifice-meter mass flow on the supply line (details in Zaman 1996a). Least-squares curves of about 10 points per configuration (Fig. 17, p. 223).
- **Computation (NPARC)** (p. 215, 219–220): steady-state, time-marching full Navier–Stokes (RANS) code; details in Steffen, Reddy & Zaman 1997 (AIAA 97-0709) and Reddy, Steffen & Zaman 1997 (ASME 97-GT-257).
  - Turbulence model, grid and resolution are not stated in this paper. The choice of turbulence model mattered little for the initial tab vortices, presumably because their source is pressure-driven; harder hurdles were inflow/outflow boundary conditions able to supply the jet's "entrainment appetite" and grid generation for the tabbed geometry (p. 219–220).
  - Uses: ω_x at x/D = 1 for the tabbed rectangle at M_j 0.3 (validated against hot wire) and 1.63 (no data to compare) (Fig. 15); round-jet ṁ/ṁ_e at x/D = 14 against M_j as a screech-free reference (Fig. 12).
  - A steady RANS has no self-excited coherent structures, so it is screech-free by construction (note's reading).
- **Analyses and their data needs:** everything here is time-averaged. Mean Pitot fields, integrated flux, mean ω_x and the iso-surfaces need no time resolution; the only time-resolved signals are the microphone spectra (tone frequencies) and the unpublished unsteady surveys. No phase averaging, correlation or modal decomposition is used.

## Key equations
Eqs. (1)–(5) and the exit-state relations were checked on the rendered pages.

- **Fully expanded Mach number** (p. 199): $M_j=\left\{\left[(p_t/p_a)^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}-1\right]\dfrac{2}{\gamma-1}\right\}^{1/2}$. The same expression gives the exit Mach number M_e for subsonic flow; M_e = M_D = 1 once choked (p. 210).
- **Asymptotic entrainment, dimensional analysis** (eq. 1, p. 208): $\dot m=K_1\dot F^{1/2}\rho_a^{1/2}x$, with $\dot F$ the momentum flux in the far field.
- **Momentum flux from the exit** (eq. 2, p. 209): $\dot F=A_e\rho_eU_e^2+(p_e-p_a)A_e$.
- **Pressure-thrust factor** (eq. 3, p. 209): $\dot F=\xi A_e\rho_eU_e^2$, so $\xi=1+(p_e-p_a)/\rho_eU_e^2$.
- **Normalised flux** (eq. 4, p. 209): with $\dot m_e=A_e\rho_eU_e$, $A_e=\tfrac14\pi D^2$ and $K_2=K_1(4/\pi)^{1/2}$, $\dfrac{\dot m}{\dot m_e}=K_2\,\xi^{1/2}\left(\dfrac{\rho_a}{\rho_e}\right)^{1/2}\dfrac{x}{D}$, so $K=K_2\xi^{1/2}(\rho_a/\rho_e)^{1/2}$ (p. 210).
- **Choked exit** (eq. 5, p. 210): $p_e/p_t=\left[1+\tfrac12(\gamma-1)M_D^2\right]^{-\gamma/(\gamma-1)}$ and, with $U_e=M_D(\gamma RT_e)^{1/2}$, $\xi=1+\dfrac{1}{\gamma M_D^2}\left(1-\dfrac{p_a}{p_e}\right)$. Subsonic: p_e = p_a, ξ = 1.
- **Exit density** (p. 210): $\rho_e/\rho_t=\left[1+\tfrac12(\gamma-1)M_e^2\right]^{-1/(\gamma-1)}$, $\rho_t=p_t/RT_t$. K is matched to 0.28 at low M_j, the only fitted constant; K₂ξ^{1/2} → 0.28(1 + 1/γ)^{1/2} = 0.367 at large M_j (p. 210).
- **Flow blockage and effective diameter** (p. 222): blockage % $=(\dot m_{ideal}-\dot m_{meas})/\dot m_{ideal}\times100$ with $\dot m_{ideal}=\rho_eA_eU_e$; $D_e=\left[(4/\pi)\,\dot m_{meas}/(\rho_eU_e)\right]^{1/2}$; $C_f=T_{meas}/T_{ideal}$, with T_ideal from eq. (2) on the area of D_e.
  > The PDF prints "A_e = πD_e²" (p. 222); from the D_e definition it must be ¼πD_e². Check on the PDF.
- **Performance factor** (p. 225): $\dfrac{\Delta\dot m}{1-C_f}=\left(\dfrac{\dot m_{tab}-\dot m_{no\text{-}tab}}{\dot m_{no\text{-}tab}}\right)\Big/(1-C_f)$, at x/D = 14; indeterminate as tab size → 0, so the author warns against over-reading it.
- **Boundary layer** (p. 200, 224): $\theta/D=1.0/Re_D^{1/2}$ (low subsonic fit), extrapolated to θ ≈ 0.001D at M_j 1.63; the smallest tab protrudes about 0.05D.

## Notation
- D: equivalent diameter from exit area (1.47 cm asymmetric nozzles, 1.27 cm round, 2.54 cm orifices); D_e: effective diameter from measured mass flow (§3.4.3 only); D_H: hydraulic diameter; AR: aspect ratio.
- p_t, p_a, p_e: plenum, ambient and exit static pressures; ρ_t, ρ_a, ρ_e: plenum, ambient and exit densities; T_t, T_e: total and exit temperatures; U_e: exit velocity; M_e, M_D (= 1), M_j: exit, design and fully expanded Mach numbers.
- ṁ: axial mass flux through a cross-plane (Pitot-integrated); ṁ_e: exit mass flux; K = ∂(ṁ/ṁ_e)/∂(x/D): entrainment slope; $\dot F$: momentum flux; ξ: pressure-thrust factor; K₁, K₂: constants.
- f_s: screech frequency; St = f_s D/U_e; A1, A2, B, C, D: round-jet stages; B′, D′: AR 3 stages.

## Relevance to this project
This is the classic integral (mass-flux) comparison of round, non-round, lobed and tabbed jets across the subsonic and screeching range, and the primary source for "screech mode decides how much a jet spreads", which the library had only through [Raman 1998](raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) (p. 94, 97). Three results matter for the group: nozzle shape alone barely changes spreading; screech stage changes it a lot, abruptly; and stationary streamwise vortices beat both.

**Reading it critically**
- **The stage effect is the strongest evidence; the nozzle comparison is weaker.**
  - On one fixed nozzle the flux falls from 5.55 to 3.58 (−35 %) between M_j 1.43 (B) and 1.456 (C) (Fig. 12, read). A smooth M_j effect cannot do that, so the time-averaged far field depends on the stage. This is the earliest library evidence, at the level of integral entrainment, of what [Mazharmanesh 2026](mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) later showed with tone-conditioned short-time means (each loop holds its own mean flow).
  - The nozzle-to-nozzle comparison at M_j 1.63 mixes geometry, stage (round C against AR 3 flapping) and screech presence (lobed none), as the author concedes (p. 215–216). The lobed jet's low flux is attributed to absent screech, but no lobed case with screech exists to test that.
  - Subsonic differences between shapes (≈7–17 % at x/D = 14, Fig. 13 read) are only two to four times the ±4 % repeatability (p. 202).
- **What counts as "screech raises spreading" depends on the baseline** (note's reading of Fig. 12).
  - Against the RANS no-screech curve (interpolated), the round jet gains about +90–110 % in B (5.64 against ≈2.95 at M_j 1.32; 5.55 against ≈2.7 at 1.43), about +50 % in C at M_j 1.63 (3.12 against ≈2.1) and roughly +80–100 % in D (≈3.6 against ≈1.8–2.0, with the RANS curve extrapolated beyond M_j 1.6), but only +1–12 % in A (3.32 at M_j 1.16 against ≈3.3; 3.77 at 1.10 against ≈3.4), no more than the RANS's subsonic under-prediction.
  - Against the measured subsonic level (≈3.8), only B gains (about +50 %); C and D sit lower.
  - [Raman 1998](raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md)'s summary (B about +60 %; A1, A2, C, D not significant; from Zaman's earlier data) is the subsonic-baseline reading. This paper says D is "relatively high" too (p. 214).
  - The RANS curve carries its own error: it over-predicts the subsonic flux by ≈6 % at M_j 0.3 and under-predicts by ≈7 % at 1.0 (read), and its turbulence model is not given here.
- **Axisymmetric screech does not raise spreading** in these data (note's reading): A1 and the A1/A2/B junction (M_j 1.10–1.16) give 3.77 and 3.32, at or below the subsonic level and on the RANS curve. That matters for the PyFR A1/A2 work: integral entrainment at x/D = 14 is unlikely to separate A1 from A2, but it should jump at B onset (+40 % between M_j 1.16 and 1.20).
- **Flux minima sit at stage jumps:** 3.32 at M_j 1.16 (A/B), 2.92 at 1.68 (C/D) and, for the ellipse, 2.83 at 1.68 (B′/D′). The author's explanation, falling screech amplitude near a jump (p. 216), fits the parabolic amplitude-against-M_j trend within a stage. Time-averaged Pitot data cannot tell weaker screech from alternation between two loops at those M_j (see methods below).
- **Asymptotic-slope model.** The eq. (4) fit is good to about ±10 % (my check), and the scatter in the supersonic range follows the stage: the A-mode point at M_j 1.14 sits 11 % low and the B point at 1.37 sits 9 % high (Fig. 9, read). So stage effects may persist into the "asymptotic" slopes over the measured ranges (my reading; the slopes rest on two or three far points). The conclusion that compressibility plays no role concerns the far-field slope only: those slopes are measured 30–66 D downstream, where the jet is subsonic, while the near-field effect of M_c ([Papamoschou 1988](papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md)) would show as the lower near-field fluxes in the RANS curve, not in K.
- **Strouhal values:** see the caveat under Data tables. The figures' stage boundaries are usable, but the B, C and D frequencies appear about 20 % low.
- **AR 3 staging.** The single B′ → D′ jump keeps the flapping symmetry and goes up in St (≈0.16 → ≈0.20) with overlap at M_j 1.64–1.66 (Fig. 11, read). This looks like a k_s1 → k_s2 transition of the kind A1 → A2 and B → C are in round jets ([Nogueira 2021, A1/A2 closure](nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md); [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md)) and the AR 2 ellipse's II → III is ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015, staging](edgington-mitchell-2015-staging-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)); the reading is mine.
  - It matches Edgington-Mitchell 2022's count of one stage change at AR 3 and none at AR ≥ 4 (p. 18–19).
  - The naming "D′" by analogy with the round jet's D is misleading, since the round D jump is downward.
  - The lowest-M_j AR 3 points (1.11–1.21, St 0.62–0.43) lie on the round jet's A1/A2 curve and are assigned to B′ without shown mode evidence. A varicose stage near choking, as in AR 2 jets, is not excluded. Both AR 3 fluxes rise only after M_j ≈ 1.2 (Fig. 13, read).
- **Axis switching without screech.** The plain AR 3 jets switch when screeching and not at M_j 0.3 (Figs 5–6), as [Krothapalli 1986](krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md) (p. 133) and [Alkislar 2003](alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md) (p. 135–136) found. But the tabbed rectangle, with its screech almost removed, switches earliest (x/D ≈ 2), through stationary vortex pairs.
  - This bears on Alkislar's prediction that suppressing screech should remove the crossover (p. 140, 153): it holds only if no other streamwise vorticity is introduced.
  - It supports the streamwise-vortex route of [Samimy 1998](samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md) (off-design cutouts, mixing up to about 50 %) and the forced-switch picture of [Suzuki 2026](suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md) (stronger forcing switches earlier).
- **Initial conditions.** The "nominally laminar but noisy" boundary layer and its weaker natural excitation are inferred, not measured, above M_j 0.3 (p. 200, 218). The argument implies that each lab's switch point and spreading depend on its boundary layer and lip. It matches the rig-dependence of staging NPRs on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](../Topics/screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md).
- **Tabs and screech.** Four tabs with 8 % geometric blockage remove the round jet's tone (p. 211). [Johnston 2016](johnston-2016-underexpanded-jet-intrusive-tab-visualisation.md) shows one 2 % tab already moves the stage (B → an A-like toroid at NPR 2.6). The four-tab flux gain of 1.79 at x/D_e = 14 (Table 1, case 4) matches the 1.8 tabulated from Zaman et al. 1994 in [Gutmark 1995](gutmark-1995-mixing-enhancement-supersonic-free-shear-flows.md) (Table 1).
- **Elliptic against rectangular.** [Gutmark 1995](gutmark-1995-mixing-enhancement-supersonic-free-shear-flows.md) report sonic elliptic jets spreading slightly faster than rectangular ones of the same AR (p. 395). Here the order flips with M_j: the ellipse is higher at the B′ peaks, the rectangle at M_j 1.63 and subsonically (Figs 7c, 13, read).
- **Harmonic at 110°.** The rectangular jet's harmonic (≈12.7 kHz) is as strong as or stronger than the fundamental (Fig. 10, read), like the AR 4 jet's harmonic at 90° in [Alkislar 2003](alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md) (Fig. 6).

**Methods and data for the lab workflow (CLAUDE.md)**
- **Non-time-resolved data.** Everything here is a time average. The integrated mass flux is a cheap scalar to add to the group's PIV or Pitot mean fields, and it separates stages sharply (Fig. 12).
  - For round jets a single plane plus axisymmetry suffices.
  - For the AR 2 ellipse and rectangle it needs cross-planes, or two planes with a shape assumption, because the section switches axes.
  - Use the 1 %-of-centreline truncation, or a fixed velocity threshold, consistently, since the integrand never decays (p. 201).
  - POD-pair phase recovery adds nothing to an integral of the mean, but conditional means binned by POD-pair amplitude would test the amplitude explanation of the flux minima (my suggestion).
- **Time-resolved data and the SPOD → reconstruction → Hilbert POD pipeline.** The paper's open points are dynamic, and the pipeline addresses them directly.
  - Near a stage jump (B′/D′ overlap at M_j 1.64–1.66; B, which switches between helical and flapping, p. 213), rebuild each tone from SPOD and track its Hilbert amplitude. The time-averaged flux dip could then be split into "both loops weak" and "alternating dwells", with the jet width or spreading conditioned on the active loop.
  - Mazharmanesh 2026's per-tone short-time means already do this for shock spacing, and the group's NPR 2.199 A1/A2 record (`Codebase/HPOD_Investigation/`) is the round-jet analogue.
  - From schlieren, a width or intensity-spread proxy (for example the shear-layer edge from the mean or RMS image) can stand in for mass flux. Calibrating it once against PIV-integrated flux would make it quantitative (my suggestion).
- **Microphones** give tone frequencies and stage identity only. With the St caveat above, recompute St from the stated frequencies rather than reading Figs 11–12.

**Modelling side and the PyFR staging LES**
- **Models used.** The only quantitative models are the far-field control-volume scaling (eqs. 1–5) and steady RANS (NPARC).
  - The scaling is exact in its assumptions: self-similar far field, conserved momentum flux including the exit pressure thrust, 1-D isentropic choked exit, T_t = T_a, and one constant matched at low M_j. It explains the M_j trend of K without any mixing physics.
  - RANS validates well for the pressure-driven tab vortices at x/D = 1 (Fig. 15). Without self-excited structures it is a screech-free baseline, which is how the paper uses it.
  - Neither model can predict the stage-dependent near field. That needs scale-resolving simulation or a linear model of the screech loop, with the screech amplitude coming from the nonlinear saturation that no model in the library predicts yet ([screech](../Topics/screech.md)).
- **For the PyFR LES** (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`; round convergent nozzle at the A1/A2 limit):
  1. The paper poses "why A1 → A2 when both are axisymmetric" as unexplained (p. 214). The k_s1/k_s2 closure now answers it ([Nogueira 2021, A1/A2 closure](nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md)).
  2. A1 → A2 here falls at M_j ≈ 1.16–1.17 (NPR ≈ 2.30–2.33 by isentropic conversion), the high end of the 2.17–2.27 range on the staging page, like Powell's thick lip ([Powell 1992](powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md)). The plastic nozzle's lip is not specified.
  3. The data predict little difference in integral entrainment between A1 and A2 and a large rise at B onset. Integrated mass flux (or momentum-thickness growth) of the converged states, taken as far downstream as the domain allows, is a cheap check that the LES gets the stage-dependent mean flow right.
  4. A steady RANS on the same nozzle would give the screech-free reference, as in Fig. 12.
- **Tabs in simulation.** Tabs inject pressure-driven streamwise vorticity that RANS captures (p. 219–220). An LES with a tab, compared with [Johnston 2016](johnston-2016-underexpanded-jet-intrusive-tab-visualisation.md), would show how a small blockage moves the staging map (my suggestion).
