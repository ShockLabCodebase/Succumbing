## Summary
The paper simulates a free, strongly overexpanded Mach 3.1 hot jet (T_i = 1900 K, p_j/p_∞ = 0.6) that matches a 1/40-scale hydrogen–air motor test at ONERA's MARTEL facility (p. 3). The nozzle and jet are computed by an unstructured second-order finite-volume LES with a Smagorinsky model. The sound is carried to a 41.6D microphone arc by a fourth-order nodal DG solver of the full Euler equations, coupled two-way to the LES at every time step across a conformal conical interface (p. 3–5). Two changes from the group's earlier run fix the jet's initial development: about three times the azimuthal resolution at the lip, and an axisymmetric step in the convergent (h = 0.01D) that trips the nozzle boundary layer. The trip survives relaminarisation in the throat with ≥ 2% fluctuations (p. 5–9). The flow matches LDV profiles and spread rates, and band-limited OASPL (0.01 ≤ St ≤ 0.22) is within about 1 dB at most angles. The earlier simulation was about 4–6 dB high at the peak angle (Fig. 16, my reading) (p. 12–16, 28). The analysis then separates Mach waves (θ′ < 70°, mostly m = 0) from BBSAN (θ′ > 90°, mostly m = 1, peak frequency given by Tam & Tanna's formula), and reports an upstream component at the θ → 180° BBSAN frequency (St 0.066) radiating near θ′ ≈ 145–160° (p. 17–24). Nonlinearity metrics show steep near-field events by the shear layer as well as cumulative steepening out to 40D. A linear FW-H extrapolation from r = 11.2D gets the OASPL right but misses the derivative skewness and wave steepening factor, which the Euler solver captures in part; the 0.22 cut-off limits the shocks it can resolve (p. 24–27).

## Key findings
- **Initial conditions decide the jet** (p. 6–9, Appendix p. 28–29).
  - The earlier ONERA run on the same case (Langenais et al. 2017) had too few shear-layer fluctuations at x* = 1. Its transition was too fast, its vortex pairing too strong, and its noise too high at mid frequencies (p. 6).
  - Step C (x_step = −1.38D, h = 0.01D, top edge parallel to x) gives u′/u_j > 3% near the lips in the reduced-domain parametric study, against < 0.5% with no step on a comparable grid (p. 7). A 0.6% step gives no more than the step-free case. Steps of 1% height give > 3%. A step in the divergent (D) makes extra attached and reflected shocks and disturbs the whole flow (p. 28–29).
  - In the full run the recirculation behind the step is about 4h long. Axial fluctuations reach about 15% of the axis velocity (about 0.18 from Fig. 6(a), my reading), with a broadband spectrum at St_step = fh/u₀ ≈ 0.01 (p. 7–8).
  - The flow accelerates and **relaminarises** through the throat. Fluctuations fall to about 2% at the throat and stay at least that high to the exit (p. 8). Liu et al. saw the same stretching with a sawtooth trip.
  - The exit boundary layer is not fully turbulent: H ≈ 7 at M_j = 3.1. The trip only aims to destabilise the shear layer early (p. 9).
- **A spurious-looking lip separation.** The flow separates from x = −0.17D to the exit, in a layer about 0.015D thick, although the Morrisette & Goldberg criterion (p_j/p_∞ < 0.3 at M 3.1) says it should not. The authors attribute it to the fine wall mesh resolving the subsonic sub-layer, as in Liu et al. (p. 8). The separation is steady. All lengths are therefore scaled on D_j = 0.97D (superscript *), except domain sizes (p. 8).
- **Mean flow and shock cells** (p. 10–12).
  - The first centreline shock cell is L*_1st shock = 2.6 in both experiment and LES. Tam & Tanna's eq. (7) with Tam et al.'s K = 0.9 gives 2.6 (my arithmetic gives 2.69) (p. 11).
  - A small Mach disk with triple points sits at x = 1.33D, at the first shock convergence. The triple points generate vorticity that seeds centreline turbulence, an improvement over earlier runs where levels rose only beyond x* ≈ 5 (p. 10–11).
  - The supersonic core is L*_sonic ≈ 21 (p. 11).
  - Shocks sit at different x on the centreline and the lipline (Fig. 10, p. 11). The lipline spacing shrinks downstream: the mean between the 1st and 6th lipline shocks is L*_shock = 2.2 ± 0.6 (p. 19).
- **Centreline discrepancy** (Fig. 11, p. 11–12). The LES has a strong velocity undershoot at the first shock (u_x/u_j ≈ 0.35 against ≈ 0.9 measured, my reading). Upstream of that shock its turbulence is < 0.5%, against > 6% in the experiment, whose inflow comes from turbulent combustion that the LES omits. LDV particle lag may also smooth the measured profile. Downstream, levels agree for 1.33 < x* < 10. The centreline skewness and kurtosis peaks at x* = 6.2 and 7.5 are captured in position, but not in level.
- **Shear layer** (Figs 12–14, p. 12–14).
  - Lipline u′_x/u_j ≈ 0.15 at x* = 1, as measured.
  - The asymptotic spread rates are dδ*_sl/dx* = 0.13 (LES) against 0.11 (experiment), and dθ*_sl/dx* = 0.022 against 0.020. Both are consistent with Troutt & McLaughlin (M_j 2.1) and with Hussain & Zedan's 0.16 and 0.029 at u′/u_j = 0.08 (p. 13). These values use incompressible thicknesses (ρ = ρ₀), because the experiment has no density data.
  - L*_core ≈ 8 (δ*_sl = 2δ*_0.5). That is shorter than in the previous run. Greska et al.'s hot-jet formula gives 8.6, while the Nagamatsu & Horvay, Eldred and Lau formulas do not fit. The fast centreline decay hints that the LES core may still be short (p. 13–14).
  - Lipline velocity spectra are broadband, with a St^−5/3 range above St ≈ 0.1. Intensity peaks just downstream of each shock, most clearly in the first two cells at high St. Mid frequencies dominate for x* < L*_core and low frequencies beyond (p. 14).
- **Far-field validation** (Figs 16, 18, p. 16–17).
  - OASPL is integrated over 0.01 ≤ St ≤ 0.22. The lower bound sits above the MARTEL absorber cut-off (St 0.007 ≈ 200 Hz); the upper bound is the CAA cut-off.
  - The LES is within about 1 dB at most angles (p. 28), with a deficit at the shallowest angles. Mesh coarsening beyond x* = 20 or ground reflections in the test could explain it (p. 16).
  - Read from Fig. 16 (my reading): measured ≈ 146.8 dB at 30–40° and ≈ 129.5 dB at 130°. The LES gives ≈ 145–145.5 dB at 30–40°. The 2017 NS–FW-H and NS–Euler runs peaked near 152.5 and 151 dB.
  - The LES peak is at θ_peak ≈ 40° (θ′_peak ≈ 48°) (p. 16).
  - In the PSDs (Welch, 50% overlap, "natural" windowing; 11 LES blocks, ΔSt = 0.01; 1000 experimental blocks, ΔSt = 0.005), mid frequencies including the BBSAN bump are reproduced for θ ≥ 60°. The effective cut-off equals St_c = 0.22. Low frequencies are slightly low upstream, high frequencies are low at 30°, and there are differences below St 0.02 (p. 16–17).
- **Mach waves and convection speed** (Fig. 19, p. 17–20).
  - The Oertel-type convective Mach numbers are M_c1 = 1.3, M_c2 = 1.9 and M_c3 = 2.5, with Mach angles θ′ = 40°, 58° and 66° (eqs. 15–18). Greska's "Oertel convective Mach number" is M_co = 2.2, with θ′ = 63° (eq. 19).
  - The double FFT along the lipline gives a most likely u_c/u_j = 0.43 (stated as M_c = 2.2), between the M_c2 and M_c3 slopes (p. 18).
  - Pairwise cross-correlation gives u_c(x) in three regions. For x* < 2 it is near zero, because the array misses the deflected shear layer. For 2 < x* < 12 it oscillates around M_c3, with a maximum near the first lipline shock (x* ≈ 3; ≈ 0.59u_j from Fig. 19(b), my reading), which gives θ′_max = 70°. Beyond the core (x* > 12) it falls almost linearly from M_co to M_c1, which gives Mach waves at 40° < θ′ < 60° (p. 18).
  - The far-field peak (θ′ ≈ 48°) does not match θ′_Mco = 63°. The authors blame geometry: the sources are distributed and the 41.6D arc is too close, even after re-centring on the core end (p. 19).
- **BBSAN follows Tam & Tanna with the lipline spacing** (eq. 22, p. 19).
  - With L*_shock = 2.2 ± 0.6 and M_c = M_c3, the formula gives St_BBSAN = 0.15 ± 0.05 at θ′ = 100.9° and 0.09 ± 0.02 at 128.6°. Both match the PSD peaks (Fig. 18(c, d)).
  - The θ′ = 180° limit gives St_up = 0.066 ± 0.018, the estimated screech frequency. No tone is seen, as expected for a hot jet (p. 19).
  - The far-field spectrogram follows St_BBSAN(θ′) from θ′ ≳ 90° (Fig. 23, p. 21–22).
- **Near-field waves at r = 2.5D (array B)** (Figs 20–21, p. 19–20).
  - For x* < 4 the spectra are quiet except for a peak at St_up. The phase speed is c_φ/c_∞ = −1.2, so the waves travel upstream at θ″_up ≈ 145°.
  - For 4 < x* < 14 the phase speed is c_φ/c_∞ ≈ 3.3, giving θ″ = 72°, close to θ′_max = 70°. Further downstream it falls with x. A faint slow component (c_φ/c_∞ = 0.2) is flow reaching the array intermittently.
  - The paper ties the 2 < x* < 12 lipline region to the mid-frequency near field over 4 < x* < 14. It places BBSAN generation at roughly 2 < x* < 16 (p. 19).
- **Azimuthal content** (72-point rings, m ≤ 35; Figs 22, 26, p. 21, 24).
  - Lipline velocity: modes m ≤ 4 are negligible at first, with about 90% of the energy spread over higher modes. For 2 < x* < 14, m = 1, 2 and 3 dominate and m = 0 stays weak even past the core, as in de Cacqueray et al. and Liu et al. Lipline shock positions coincide with peaks of m = 1, 2 and valleys of m ≥ 4 (p. 21).
  - The authors say that a POD would be needed to tell flapping from helical (p. 21).
  - Near-field pressure: m = 1 dominates for 0 < x* < 12, with inverse bumps in m ≥ 2. m = 0 rises beyond x* = 12 and leads for x* > 17. The authors invoke Bogey & Bailly's mechanism: intermittent intrusions of vortical structures into the end of the potential core (p. 21).
  - Far field: for θ′ > 90°, more than a third of the energy is in m = 1 (BBSAN). Over 70° < θ′ < 90°, m = 0 and 1 drop in favour of higher modes, a transition region. For θ′ < 70°, m = 0 dominates (about 50–60% at the shallowest angles from Fig. 26, my reading), with a strong m = 1 share for 60° < θ′ < 70° (p. 24).
- **"MW-BBSAN": upstream radiation at St_up** (Figs 24–25, p. 22–24).
  - For θ′ ≳ 145° the peak frequency is uniformly St_up = 0.066. Eq. (22) would give 0.077 at 145°.
  - The authors invoke upstream-travelling instability-wave–shock components with supersonic negative phase speed (eq. 23), which radiate as Mach waves at θ′ > 160° (eq. 18). These waves also dominate over 145–160°, which they relate to the axial variation of u_c and L_shock.
  - This upstream component is about 15 dB weaker than the downstream Mach waves (Fig. 20(a)).
  - At St_up the SPL map shows a strongly directional upstream lobe near θ′ = 155°, which is absent at St 0.060 and 0.100. Its phase field shows the shock-cell periodicity and fronts in antiphase across the axis, i.e. m = 1 (Fig. 25, p. 24).
  - My check: with the paper's values (u_c = 0.5u_j, L*_shock = 2.2, u_j/c_∞ ≈ 4.9), the phase speed of eq. (23) at St_up is −1.00c_∞. That is exactly sonic: St_up is by construction the frequency at which the k − k_shock wave turns sonic upstream, as in [Tam et al. 1986](tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md). The 0.066 against 0.077 difference also lies inside the ±0.018 uncertainty the paper quotes for St_up. The case for a component distinct from the BBSAN upstream limit therefore rests mainly on the uniform frequency and the narrow 155° lobe.
- **Nonlinear propagation** (Figs 27–29, Table III, p. 24–27).
  - In the field maps, the pressure-derivative metrics single out the downstream Mach-wave generation and radiation region. The raw-pressure metrics do not (p. 25).
  - Near the shear layer at x ≈ 25D, S(ṗ) and K(ṗ) are high and WSF exceeds 1, indicating sharp pressure rises or drops. This is a near-field crackle-like source, as reported for crackling jets (p. 25).
  - In the far field the metrics still change out to 40D, WSF falling below 0.6 in places, so waves are still steepening. Γ exceeds 100 in two lobes in the peak direction (p. 25).
  - A linear FW-H extrapolation from a cylinder at r_FWH = 11.2D, inside the Euler domain, gives the same OASPL as the Euler solution for θ ≤ 100° (Fig. 28). The authors read this as most nonlinear effects occurring inside r_FWH (p. 25).
  - The waveform statistics differ, though. At θ = 40° the FW-H signal is almost Gaussian (S(ṗ) = −0.04, WSF = 1.00), while NS–Euler gives +1.21 and 0.73 and the experiment +1.79 and 0.68. Across angles, NS–Euler follows the measured S(ṗ) and WSF trends, with peaks near θ′ = 48°; FW-H does not (Table III, Fig. 29).
  - K(ṗ) is the most discriminating metric but also has the largest LES–experiment gap (5.50 against 9.79 at 40°). S(ṗ) and WSF are judged the more robust measures of cumulative nonlinearity (p. 27).
  - The LES keeps S(ṗ) below 1.5 at 40D, while the experiment exceeds 1.75. By Reichman et al.'s threshold (> 1.5 means shocked waveforms), shocks exist in the experiment but are not captured. St_c = 0.22 is too low to resolve them (p. 27).

**Operating condition** (Table I, p. 3)

- **Paper's notation:** M_j = u_j/c_j is the centreline Mach number at the exit plane, and M_d is the ideally expanded Mach number. In this library's convention these are M_e (≈ design) and M_j. D_d = 51 mm is the fully expanded diameter (p. 3, 11).
- **Gas and conditions** (p. 3).
  - The jet is an equivalent H₂–air combustion gas with γ = 1.3. The ambient is air at γ = 1.4, T_∞ = 293.15 K and p_∞ = 10⁵ Pa.
  - Total conditions are p_i = 30 × 10⁵ Pa and T_i = 1900 K. The exit conditions are T_j/T_∞ = 2.6 and p_j/p_∞ = 0.6.
  - Levels exceed 130 dB out to 40D. The dB reference is 2 × 10⁻⁵ Pa.
  - My isentropic checks with γ = 1.3: M = 3.1 and p_j/p_∞ = 0.6 give NPR ≈ 28.7, and NPR 30 gives M_d ≈ 2.82. T_j = 1900/2.44 ≈ 778 K gives T_j/T_∞ = 2.65. The area ratio between M 3.1 and 2.8 gives D_d ≈ 50.6 mm. All are consistent with Table I.
  - u_j is not stated. From the M_c values (eqs. 15–17) u_j/c_∞ ≈ 4.9; 650 D/u_j = 130 D/c_∞ (p. 4) gives 5.0; and the u_c axes of Fig. 19(b) give 5.0. So u_j ≈ 1.7 km s⁻¹ and c_j ≈ 0.55 km s⁻¹ (my estimates). The facility's maximum exit velocity is 1800 m s⁻¹ (p. 3).
- **Experiment (MARTEL)** (Fig. 1(a), p. 3).
  - A semi-anechoic test bench with H₂–air combustion: p_i = 25–30 × 10⁵ Pa and T_i = 500–2100 K. It models launcher lift-off with a motor at about 1/40 of an Ariane 5 solid booster, free or impinging.
  - Two-component LDV gives mean, RMS, skewness and kurtosis of u_x and u_r along the axis and along radii at x* = 1, 3, 3.67 and 6.
  - Far-field microphones sit every 10° on a 41.6D arc centred on the nozzle exit, from 30° to 130° from the downstream axis, with 50 kHz bandwidth. The ground is reflective 50D below the axis, with wedges to absorb reflections.
  - My conversions: 41.6D = 2.50 m; St_c = 0.22 is about 6.4 kHz; 50 kHz is about St 1.7.
- **Earlier runs.** Troyes et al. (2016) ran LES with FW-H, and Langenais et al. (2017) re-ran the same near-field mesh with an Euler far field ("Langenais 2017", the paper's comparison database) (p. 3, 5).
- **Domain** (Fig. 1(b, c), p. 3–4).
  - Zone Z1 is the LES (nozzle, jet and acoustic near field). Zone Z2 is the Euler far field. They meet at a conical interface S1, r = (2.7x + 3.8)D, chosen from the previous solution to follow the jet spreading.
  - The domain extends 33.5D upstream, 81.6D downstream and 59.4D laterally. It has no ground, but a stretched buffer zone, a characteristic non-reflecting condition on the Z2 boundary, and an imposed-pressure outflow in Z1.
  - Probes sit at the microphone and LDV positions. Arrays A (lipline, r = 0.5D) and B (r = 2.5D) are rings of 72 azimuthal points.
- **Flow solver (CEDRE, Z1)** (p. 4).
  - Filtered compressible Navier–Stokes on general unstructured elements, with a Smagorinsky model (C_s = 0.1) and van Driest damping, and no wall model.
  - c_p is a 7th-order polynomial for air and constant for the propellant gas; viscosity follows Sutherland's law.
  - Implicit second-order Runge–Kutta time stepping with a GMRES linear solver. Second-order MUSCL reconstruction with an HLLC flux.
  - Resolution is set by points per wavelength, St_c = c_∞D_j/[(PPW × K × Δ)u_j] (eq. 1), with K = 1.2 fitted to observed cut-offs. CEDRE needs PPW = 20 for near-field propagation; its damping peaks at 0.5 dB/λ at the cut-off.
- **Acoustic solver (SPACE, Z2)** (p. 4–5).
  - Full Euler equations by a nodal DG method on unstructured tetrahedra, with constant air properties (γ = 1.4). Explicit second-order RK time stepping, spatial order up to 4.
  - The ideal requirement is PPW = 14 at second order and only 3 at fourth order. PPW = 5 at fourth order is used, which gives dissipation of about 10⁻³ dB/λ, the order of atmospheric viscous damping.
  - **p-adaptation**: elements are second order at the interface, where conformal meshing makes them small, then third and fourth order further out. This smooths the accuracy jump from the second-order LES.
  - The non-reflecting conditions and shock capturing are described in Langenais et al. (2018).
- **Two-way coupling** (p. 5).
  - The CWIPI library over MPI locates boundary cells on both sides of the interface and exchanges values at every time step. Meshes are conformal tetrahedra with no overlap, so both solvers use the same time step.
  - SPACE's conservative variables are rebuilt from CEDRE's primitive (p, T, u) with SPACE's thermodynamics. This keeps p, T and u continuous across the multi-species/air interface. The exchanged values feed each solver's interface flux.
  - For this free jet no acoustic feedback is expected. The benefits are meshing the nozzle and carrying the entrained secondary flow (p. 5).
- **Grid** (Table II, Fig. 2, p. 5–6).
  - Mostly tetrahedra, refined at the nozzle wall, lips, shear layer and the first two centreline shock cells, with about a dozen hexahedral layers on the motor body and nozzle wall.
  - The wall is not resolved (y⁺ ≈ 12 at the throat, ≈ 10 at the separation point; 5–15 points across the boundary layer, p. 9), but it is fine enough to carry the trip's fluctuations.
  - The lip has about 1300 cells in azimuth, about 3× the previous grid. The tetrahedra stretch at d(d_cell)/dx = 0.001, two orders of magnitude below the shear-layer spread rate.
  - The NS acoustic-field size is kept to x = 20D and then doubled over 20D–42D. The Euler cell size is kept to x = −28D, x = 42D and r = 42D.
  - Cut-offs (eq. 1): St_c = 0.30 in the NS acoustic field (PPW 20) and 0.22 in the Euler field (PPW 5), against 0.15 in the previous run.
  - The mesh has 290 × 10⁶ cells: 235 × 10⁶ in Z1 (17.7 × 10⁶ for the trip) and 55 × 10⁶ in Z2.
- **Run** (p. 4).
  - 1736 Broadwell cores (1512 NS, 224 Euler) on ONERA's SATOR, 4.5 × 10⁶ CPU-h.
  - 1180 D/u_j simulated, of which 650 D/u_j = 130 D/c_∞ is established flow. That is about 23 ms (my conversion), comparable to de Cacqueray & Bogey and Brès et al.
  - St_min = 1.5 × 10⁻³ in principle, and 8 × 10⁻³ in practice for five periods. The time step is not stated.
- **Trip parametric study** (Table IV, Figs 30–31, p. 28–29).
  - Done on a reduced, coarsened free-field domain, with nozzle meshing as in Table II except near the step, and with ambient pressure slightly lowered to avoid wall separation.
  - Fluctuation intensity √(u_x′² + u_r′² + u_θ′²)/u_j at x* = 0.4, from Fig. 31 (my reading): about 0.14 (C), 0.13 (B), 0.06 (D), 0.02 (A) and < 0.01 (no step).
- **Analysis chain and what each needs.** Everything is computed on time-resolved LES/CAA probe signals.
  - *Statistics* along lines: mean, RMS, skewness, kurtosis (eqs. 8–9), compared with LDV. They are sensitive to record length and sampling rate (p. 12).
  - *Boundary-layer and shear-layer thicknesses* from mean profiles (eqs. 3–6, 10–12).
  - *Welch PSDs*: 50% overlap, "natural" window, 11 blocks in the LES (ΔSt = 0.01) against 1000 in the experiment (ΔSt = 0.005). Azimuthally averaged over the 72-point rings, and shown as x–St and θ–St spectrograms (Figs 14, 20, 23).
  - *Double FFT (x, t)* of lipline u_x and near-field p: wavenumber–frequency maps whose ridge slopes give convection or phase speed (Figs 19(a), 21(a)). This needs signals that are time-resolved and densely sampled along x.
  - *Pairwise cross-correlation* of neighbouring probes: the lag of maximum correlation gives u_c(x) or c_φ(x), averaged over azimuth (eqs. 20–21). Plane fronts are assumed, so θ″ = arccos(c_∞/c_φ).
  - *Azimuthal Fourier decomposition* (FFT in t and θ) on the 72-point rings and the far-field ring (Figs 22, 26).
  - *Single-frequency SPL and phase maps* in a cut plane (Fig. 25), and maps of peak-PSD frequency and p_rms (Fig. 24).
  - *Nonlinearity metrics* on far- and near-field pressure: S and K of p and ṗ, WSF and the Goldberg number Γ at the peak frequency (eq. 24, ISO 9613-1 absorption; Fig. 27, Table III).
  - *FW-H check*: a linear, permeable cylindrical surface at r = 11.2D inside the Euler domain (p. 25).
- **Modelling side.** The models are used only as checks.
  - Tam & Tanna's first-cell length (eq. 7) and BBSAN peak frequency (eq. 22), with the mean lipline spacing rather than eq. (7), because the cells shorten downstream.
  - The Oertel/Greska convective Mach numbers and Mach angles (eqs. 15–19).
  - Greska's hot-jet core length (eq. 13).
  - A supersonic upstream phase-speed criterion for the k − k_shock wave (eq. 23).
  - No instability or stability analysis is solved. The physical argument for MW-BBSAN draws on Tam (1995) and Bailly & Fujii (2016).

## Key equations
All checked on rendered pages. Superscript * means length over D_j = 0.97D.

- **Cut-off Strouhal number** (eq. 1, p. 4): $St_c=\dfrac{f_cD_j}{u_j}=\dfrac{c_\infty D_j}{\lambda_c u_j}=\dfrac{c_\infty D_j}{(\mathrm{PPW}\times K\times\Delta)\,u_j}$, with K = 1.2 and Δ = d_cell.
  > Check (my arithmetic, u_j/c_∞ = 4.91): Euler field, d_cell = 0.150D and PPW = 5, gives St_c = 0.22. NS field, d_cell = 0.0272D and PPW = 20, gives 0.30. Both match the text.
- **Equivalent cell diameter** (eq. 2, p. 5): $d_{cell}=\Delta=\dfrac{6V}{A}\simeq\dfrac{\sqrt6}{6}e$, where A is the total face area, V the volume and e the tetrahedron edge. It is exact only for regular tetrahedra.
- **Boundary-layer thicknesses** (eqs. 3–6, p. 9):
  - $\delta^*_{bl}(x^*)=[r^*_{wall}(x^*)\mid u=0]-[r^*(x^*)\mid u=0.99\,\|u\|]$
  - $\delta^*(x^*)=\int_{r^*_{wall}}^{0}\Big(1-\dfrac{\rho u}{\rho_0u_0}\Big)dr^*$
  - $\theta^*(x^*)=\int_{r^*_{wall}}^{0}\dfrac{\rho u}{\rho_0u_0}\Big(1-\dfrac{u}{u_0}\Big)dr^*$
  - $H=\delta^*/\theta^*$, with wall curvature neglected (δ_bl ≪ r_wall).
- **First shock-cell length, Tam & Tanna** (eq. 7, p. 11): $L^*_{1st\,shock}=K\pi\,(M_d^2-1)^{0.5}\,\dfrac{D_d}{\mu_1D_j}$, with μ₁ = 2.405, D_d = 51 mm and K = 0.9 (Tam, Parrish & Viswanathan 2014; originally K = 1).
  > Printed result 2.6. My arithmetic: 0.9π√6.84 × 51/(2.405 × 58.2) = 2.69.
- **Skewness and kurtosis** (eqs. 8–9, p. 11): $S=\overline{s^3}/\sigma^3$ and $K=\overline{s^4}/\sigma^4$, with σ = [N⁻¹Σ(s_i − s̄)²]^{1/2}. Gaussian values are 0 and 3.
- **Shear-layer measures** (eqs. 10–12, p. 12):
  - $\delta^*_{0.5}=r^*\mid u=0.5u(x^*,0)$
  - $\delta^*_{sl}=[r^*\mid u=0.05u(x^*,r_0)]-[r^*\mid u=0.95u(x^*,r_0)]$
  - $\theta^*_{sl}=\int_{r_0(x^*)}^{\infty}\dfrac{u}{u_0}\Big(1-\dfrac{u}{u_0}\Big)dr^*$
  - r₀(x*) is set to exclude the shock cells; incompressible forms are used (ρ = ρ₀).
- **Greska hot-jet core length** (eq. 13, p. 13): $L^*_{core}=3.134\exp(1.043M_d-M_c)\times D_d/D_j=8.6$, with M_c = u_j/(c_j + c_∞) = 1.9.
  > My arithmetic: 3.134 e^{1.043×2.8−1.9} = 8.70, and × D_d/D_j (51/58.2) = 7.6. The printed 8.6 matches the formula without the diameter ratio. Check the PDF and Greska et al. (2008).
- **Observation angle re-centred on the core end** (eq. 14, p. 16): $\theta'=\arctan\!\Big(\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta-L_\theta/R_\theta}\Big)$ for θ < arccos(L_θ/R_θ), plus π for θ > arccos(L_θ/R_θ). Here L_θ = L_core = 8 ± 1D_j and R_θ = 41.6D, giving about ±1° uncertainty in θ′.
  > The printed pairs (30° → 36.6°, 60° → 70.4°, 90° → 100.9°, 120° → 128.6°) reproduce with L_θ/R_θ = 8/41.6, i.e. both lengths in the same unit, not 8 × 0.97/41.6 (my check).
- **Oertel-type convective Mach numbers** (eqs. 15–17, 19, p. 17–18):
  - $M_{c1}=\dfrac{u_j-c_j}{c_j+c_\infty}=1.3$
  - $M_{c2}=\dfrac{u_j}{c_j+c_\infty}=1.9$
  - $M_{c3}=\dfrac{u_j+c_j}{c_j+c_\infty}=2.5$
  - $M_{co}=\dfrac{u_j+0.5c_j}{c_j+c_\infty}=2.2$
  - Mach-wave conditions M_c2 > 0.8 and M_c3 > 1.25 (Greska; Panda & Seasholtz).
  - M_c2 is the Papamoschou–Roshko form, and also the speed of Tam & Hu's most amplified supersonic wave ([Tam & Hu 1989](tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md)).
- **Mach angle** (eq. 18, p. 18): $\theta'=\arccos(1/M_c)$. It gives 40°, 58°, 66° and 63° for M_c1, M_c2, M_c3 and M_co.
- **Convection velocity from cross-correlation** (eqs. 20–21, p. 18): $u_c(x^*)=\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{\Delta x^*}{2\pi\,\tau_{max}(x^*,\Theta)}\,d\Theta$, where τ_max maximises the cross-correlation [u_x(x*, t) ⋆ u_x(x* + Δx*, t)](τ).
- **BBSAN peak frequency, Tam & Tanna** (eq. 22, p. 19): $St_{BBSAN}=\dfrac{u_c}{\overline{L^*_{shock}}\,(1-M_c\cos\theta')\,u_j}$, with M_c = u_c/c_∞.
  > Check (my arithmetic, u_c/u_j = 2.5/4.91 = 0.51, L = 2.2): 0.157 at 100.9°, 0.091 at 128.6°, 0.066 at 180°, as printed.
- **Upstream supersonic phase-speed condition** (eq. 23, p. 24): $c_\varphi<-c_\infty$, i.e. $\dfrac{\omega}{\alpha-k_{shock}}<-c_\infty$, with ω = 2πf, α ≃ ω/u_c and k_shock = 2π/(D_j L*_shock).
  > Evaluated at the paper's values (ω = 2πSt_up u_j/D_j, u_c = 0.5u_j, L* = 2.2, u_j/c_∞ ≈ 4.9), c_φ ≈ −1.00c_∞ (my arithmetic), so the condition is only just met. Setting c_φ = −c_∞ in eq. (23) reproduces eq. (22) at θ′ = 180°.
- **Goldberg number** (eq. 24, p. 25): $\Gamma=\dfrac{\beta\,\omega\,p_{rms}\,\lambda}{\rho_\infty c_\infty^3\,\alpha(\omega)}$, with β = (1 + γ)/2 and α(ω) the dimensionless atmospheric damping from ISO 9613-1. Γ < 1 means linear and Γ ≫ 1 nonlinear propagation.
- **Wave steepening factor** (p. 25, in words): WSF = |mean negative slope| / mean positive slope of the waveform. It is 1 for a harmonic wave and 0 for a perfect sawtooth, and exceeds 1 (→ ∞) for discontinuous pressure drops.

## Notation
- D: nozzle exit diameter (60 mm); D_j = 0.97D: separation-corrected exit diameter used for all scaling (not the fully expanded diameter); D_d = 51 mm: ideally expanded diameter; superscript * = length/D_j.
- M_j, u_j, c_j, T_j, p_j: centreline exit-plane Mach number, velocity, sound speed, temperature, pressure; M_d: ideally expanded Mach number; p_i, T_i: total (generating) pressure and temperature; ∞: ambient.
- x, r, Θ: cylindrical coordinates from the nozzle exit; θ: polar angle from the downstream axis centred on the nozzle exit; θ′: the same centred on the end of the potential core (eq. 14); θ″: near-field radiation angle from the phase speed.
- St = fD_j/u_j; St_c: cut-off; St_up: BBSAN frequency at θ′ = 180°; St_step = fh/u₀.
- PPW: points per wavelength; K (eq. 1): cut-off correction constant; Δ = d_cell: equivalent cell diameter; e: tetrahedron edge.
- δ_bl, δ, θ (boundary layer), H: boundary-layer thickness, displacement and momentum thicknesses, shape factor; δ_0.5, δ_sl, θ_sl: half-velocity radius, shear-layer thickness, momentum thickness.
- M_c1, M_c2, M_c3, M_co: convective Mach numbers (eqs. 15–17, 19); u_c: convection velocity; c_φ: near-field phase speed; L_shock: mean shock spacing; k_shock: shock wavenumber; α: instability wavenumber.
- m: azimuthal mode number; arrays A (r = 0.5D) and B (r = 2.5D).
- S, K: skewness, kurtosis; ṗ: pressure time derivative; WSF: wave steepening factor; Γ: Goldberg number; β = (1 + γ)/2: nonlinearity coefficient; α(ω): dimensionless atmospheric damping.
- MW-BBSAN: the paper's name for upstream, Mach-wave-like BBSAN at St_up.

## Relevance to this project
This is the only paper in the library that simulates a rocket-like hot, strongly overexpanded jet end to end: nozzle interior, jet, near field and nonlinear far field. It is a validated reference for the rocket-noise modelling thread in CLAUDE.md, and it gives concrete numerics against which the PyFR work and the lab's own heated-jet ideas can be placed.

**Rocket-noise models**
- **High-fidelity prediction at subscale.** [Lubert 2022](lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) lists LES of launch systems with one-way coupling to nonlinear Euler CAA and names Langenais et al. as developing two-way coupling (that note, p. 31). This paper is that work. Here the two-way feature is not exercised, since the free jet has no feedback. Its accuracy (about 1 dB on band-limited OASPL) is better than the 2–4 dB over-predictions of [Tsutsumi 2015](tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md) (LES + linear FW-H, p. 12–13) and [Casalino 2009](casalino-2009-hybrid-empirical-caa-rocket-noise.md) (empirical sources + CAA). But it is only to St 0.22 (about 6.4 kHz at this scale) and for a free jet without a pad.
  - The earlier run on the same case was about 4–6 dB high at the peak with either FW-H or Euler propagation (Fig. 16, my reading). That shows the initial conditions (trip and lip resolution), not the propagation method, set most of the error.
- **Directivity.** The far-field peak at θ′ ≈ 48° on a 41.6D arc does not match the M_co angle of 63°, and the authors blame arc distance and distributed sources (p. 19). This is the near-field bias that [James 2012](james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md) found for 80 D_e arcs. The 30–40° measured maximum is far below the 64–72° full-scale peak angles ([Mathews 2021](mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md); [Kellison 2026](kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md)).
  - The paper's M_c set adds data on which convective Mach number to use. The most likely lipline u_c/u_j = 0.43 (κ in Lubert's M_c = κU_e/c_a) lies between the rocket value 0.31–0.32 and lab values 0.6–0.85 (Lubert note, p. 14–15). Along x, the speed runs from about M_c3 (θ′ 66–70°) before the core end down to M_c1 (40°) beyond it.
- **Peak frequency.** Far-field PSD peaks run from St ≈ 0.02 at the shallowest angles to 0.2 at the sideline (p. 21). The peak-direction value at 40° is 0.066–0.072 (Table III). This hot M 3.1 jet sits well below the lab value of about 0.2 and above the rocket value of Sr ≈ 0.01–0.025, approaching it only at the shallowest angles (D_j and D_e differ by 3%, so the definitions are comparable). The paper does not discuss the scaling.
- **BBSAN at subscale but not at full scale.** BBSAN is clear here: it dominates upstream and follows Tam & Tanna with the lipline spacing. Yet full-scale rockets show none ([Lubert 2022](lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 9), and nor did the 1/42 Epsilon free jet at 90–120° ([Tsutsumi 2015](tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 13).
  - The conditions differ in temperature: T_i/T_∞ = 6.5 here against 12.0 for Epsilon, with similar p_e/p_∞ (0.6 against 0.5) and M_j (2.8 against 3.3). That is consistent with Lubert's suggestion that BBSAN saturates with temperature or is masked by Mach waves, but one pair of tests cannot separate temperature from facility differences (my comparison).
- **Source extent.** Mach-wave and BBSAN generation spread over 2 < x* < 16, with low-frequency, m = 0-dominated noise from beyond the core end (p. 19, 21, 28). This supports distributed-source models over compact ones. The m = 0 dominance at shallow angles and m = 1 upstream are azimuthal facts that no SP-8072-type model contains.

**Nonlinear propagation and crackle**
- OASPL is unchanged by nonlinear propagation from 11.2D to 41.6D (FW-H against Euler, Fig. 28), but the waveform statistics are not. This is the same split as at full scale, where nonlinearity hardly changes OASPL but reshapes spectra and waveforms ([Gee 2008](gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 12). It supports keeping linear OASPL models and adding nonlinear spectral corrections.
- The subscale experiment reaches S(ṗ) ≈ 1.8 at 40° on a 2.5 m arc. Full-scale values are much higher: Sk{∂p/∂t} > 30 near the RSRM peak, growing with distance ([Gee 2012](gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 7–8). Derivative skewness depends on sampling bandwidth and on range in shock-formation distances, so the numbers are not directly comparable.
- Near-field WSF > 1 and high S(ṗ) by the shear layer at x ≈ 25D (p. 25) favour a source-side contribution to crackle, alongside the cumulative steepening further out. The [jet-noise-mechanisms](../Topics/jet-noise-mechanisms.md) page notes that the library had no study of crackle generation; this is simulation evidence, not a mechanism study.

**Jet-noise mechanisms**
- The analysis is a worked example of the [Tam 1995](tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) three-component picture in a hot jet, without screech.
  - The Tam & Tanna BBSAN formula ([Tam & Tanna 1982](tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md)) works only with the shorter lipline spacing (2.2 rather than the 2.6 centreline first cell), a practical point for overexpanded jets.
  - BBSAN is mostly m = 1, consistent with [Wong 2020](wong-2020-azimuthal-decomposition-radiated-noise-shock-containing-jets.md), where m = 0 BBSAN is 3–4 dB/St below m = ±1 in cold jets.
- **The "MW-BBSAN" component.** St_up is the θ → 180° limit of BBSAN, which [Tam et al. 1986](tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md) identify with the screech frequency. My check that c_φ = −c_∞ exactly at St_up (Key equations) shows the paper's upstream Mach-wave mechanism is that same limit, not a new source. The distinct observations are the frequency-locked upstream lobe at θ′ ≈ 155° and its m = 1 antiphase structure. A non-screeching hot jet still radiating a directional upstream lobe at the screech-estimated frequency bears on the screech-closure literature (upstream radiation without a closed loop). This is the note writer's link; the paper does not make it.
- Heating lowers u_c/u_j in LES of rectangular jets (0.80 → 0.64 for TR 1–3; [Gojon 2019](gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 16). The 0.43 found here at TR 6.5 continues that trend (my comparison; different geometry and M).

**Simulation methods and the PyFR staging LES**
- **Recipe differences from the Charles family** ([Brès 2021](bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md)): no wall model but a geometric trip, an implicit FV LES with Smagorinsky, no co-flow, and a DG Euler far field instead of FW-H. Common ground: the nozzle is in the domain and the exit state is controlled deliberately.
- **Transferable lessons** (my reading).
  - In strongly accelerating nozzle flow, trip-generated turbulence relaminarises at the throat. A convergent rig nozzle also accelerates its boundary layer to the exit, so the exit state, not the trip, is what to check and report.
  - Wall refinement alone created a lip separation that the criterion forbids (p. 8). Over-resolving a laminar-ish sub-layer can change the exit flow.
  - The PPW × K × Δ cut-off rule (eq. 1, K = 1.2) is a quick way to size a mesh for a target St.
  - PyFR's flux reconstruction is in the same family as SPACE's nodal DG. SPACE's PPW = 5 at fourth order suggests similar economy for ℘ = 3 in PyFR's acoustic region, and SPACE's p-adaptation near a resolution jump is an option PyFR also offers.
- **Record length.** 650 D/u_j gave 11 Welch blocks and about 43 periods at St 0.066 (my arithmetic). Low-frequency convergence and the higher-order statistics are limited, as the paper says (p. 4, 12). This matches the "records are short" caution on the [simulation-methods](../Topics/simulation-methods.md) page.

**Methods and data for the lab workflow (CLAUDE.md)**
- Everything here needs **time-resolved** signals: Welch PSDs, k–St maps from double FFTs along a line, pairwise cross-correlation for u_c and c_φ, azimuthal FFTs on rings, and S(ṗ)/WSF, which need high sampling rates. None of it works on non-time-resolved PIV.
- **Lab equivalents.**
  - Time-resolved schlieren along the lipline gives the same k–St map and u_c(x) (Fig. 19) directly from image intensity. That is the group's route to testing which M_c sets Mach-wave directivity.
  - Two-microphone azimuthal decomposition (Wong 2020) gives the far-field m split of Fig. 26.
- **Not done here:** no POD, SPOD or BMD. The authors say POD would be needed to tell flapping from helical m = 1 (p. 21).
  - SPOD of the LES fields would replace the band-wise azimuthal shares with coherent wavepackets per St and m.
  - BMD would test whether the shock-cell modulation (peaks of m = 1, 2 at shock positions, Fig. 22) is a triadic interaction with the k_shock wavenumber.
- **Intermittency.** The m = 0 downstream noise is attributed to intermittent intrusions of vortical structures into the end of the core (p. 21), but this is only inferred from averaged energy shares. An SPOD → reconstruction → Hilbert POD pass on the m = 0, low-St band would test intermittency directly: bursty amplitude envelopes correlated with centreline events. So would conditional sampling on high-S(ṗ) events at the lipline.
