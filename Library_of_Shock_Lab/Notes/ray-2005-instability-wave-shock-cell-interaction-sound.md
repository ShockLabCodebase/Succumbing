## Summary
Ray, Cheung and Lele compute the sound from a single axisymmetric instability wave interacting with the shock cells of an imperfectly expanded jet, M_j = 1.28 from an M_d = 1.5 nozzle (p. 2). The aim is a first-principles version of Tam's semi-empirical BBSAN model (p. 1–2). The instability wave comes from linear PSE and the shock cells from Tam, Jackson & Seiner's zero-frequency stability modes, both on a RANS mean flow. Their quadratic products are source terms in the inhomogeneous linearised Euler equations, which are solved with a spectral time-domain code (p. 2–3). A locally parallel Lilley–Goldstein (Rayleigh-equation) Green's function solution with the same sources is the check (p. 4). A Gaussian-wavepacket model problem shows that only the k_t − k_s difference wave radiates upstream, at its Mach angle (p. 4–5). This "local Mach angle" picture explains the St = 0.6 isothermal far field (p. 9). At St = 0.9, and in the heated jet at the same ω, the sources sit off the axis as ring sources. Interference then splits the radiation into narrow lobes, which the Green's function of a source at r/D = 0.5 reproduces (p. 12–13). The authors call the study preliminary: the LEE runs are partly unconverged or under-resolved, and there are no comparisons with acoustic measurements (p. 9–10, 13).

## Key findings
- **Model problem: the difference wave radiates upstream** (eqs. 7–8, p. 4–5).
  - The source is a Gaussian-enveloped travelling wave (the instability wave) times a neutral standing wave (one shock mode). In the far field this gives two Gaussian lobes, one for k₁* = k_t − k_s and one for k₂* = k_t + k_s (eq. 8a).
  - Each lobe is strong near its Mach angle θ_M = cos⁻¹(1/c_s,j) when the source phase velocity is supersonic, |c_s,j| > 1 (p. 5).
  - c_s,2 is usually subsonic, and its radiation is downstream, where mixing noise dominates. Near the end of the potential core, where most shock noise is thought to originate, k_t < k_s, so c_s,1 < 0 and the k₁* term radiates strongly upstream (p. 5).
  - **Envelope width:** a longer envelope L makes the sound louder and its directivity narrower. Higher frequencies have shorter envelopes, so their sound should be weaker and spread over more angles (p. 5, citing Tam 1987).
  - The authors relate this to Tam's model and call it the shock-noise counterpart of Crighton & Huerre's "superdirective" radiation from forced shear layers (p. 5).
- **Instability waves (PSE)** (Fig. 2, p. 5–6).
  - St 0.6 (I1): the u_x eigenfunction is sharply peaked in the shear layer at first. Within a few diameters its peak broadens and moves toward the axis (at x/D = 6 it peaks at r/D ≈ 0.1–0.2, read from Fig. 2). Much of the interaction is therefore spread over the radius (p. 5–6).
  - Density fluctuations peak about 25% lower than u_x and do not move toward the axis (p. 6).
  - St 0.9 (I2): the wave stays in the shear layer and decays before it can spread. It peaks near x/D ≈ 1.5 (p. 6–7).
  - The low-frequency amplitude gain is about twice the high-frequency one, so its sound field is louder (p. 6). Read from Fig. 2: the peak u_x is about 2.2 (I1, x/D = 3) against about 1.2 (I2, x/D = 1). Both are normalised by the maximum inlet u_x (p. 6).
- **Shock cells** (Fig. 3, p. 6–7).
  - Following Tam, Jackson & Seiner (1985), the shock structure is a sum of zero-frequency linear stability modes. The higher modes carry the fine structure near the nozzle; near the end of the core only the first mode matters (p. 6).
  - The sum of the first three modes agrees well with Norum & Seiner's unheated mean static pressure at r/D = 0 and 0.25 (Fig. 3, p. 6–7).
  - The local source phase velocity c_s, from the local PSE and shock-mode wavenumbers, is supersonic only for the first shock mode at St 0.6, and also for part of the second mode at St 0.9 (p. 6).
  - To spare the numerics, the I1 LEE run uses only mode 1, and the I2 run uses modes 1 and 2 (p. 7).
- **LEE sources** (Fig. 4, p. 7–8). The total-energy source S_e (eq. 3c) is the one shown. For I1 it is a regular wavelike pattern spread over the jet core. For I2 the second shock mode's sharp near-nozzle variation, together with the shear-layer-bound instability wave, gives localised sources: a "ring source" (p. 7).
- **Near and far fields, isothermal** (Figs 5–7, p. 7–10).
  - In both cases the shear layer fills with instability waves excited by the sources themselves. These travel supersonically and radiate downstream Mach waves (lobe M; p. 7).
  - **I1** (St 0.6): the shock noise is upstream plane waves (lobe S1). LEE and the L-G solution agree well in amplitude and directivity (Fig. 6, p. 9).
    - The centreline S_e peaks between x/D ≈ 2 and 6. Over that range the local Mach angle runs from about 100° to 140°, the angles that make up lobe S1 (Fig. 7, p. 9).
    - Read from Fig. 6: S1 peaks near 115–120° at |p_ff|πR ≈ 0.35, and lobe M near 30°.
  - **I2** (St 0.9): the field is much more complicated (p. 9).
    - Lobe S2, the farthest upstream (near 160° in Fig. 6, read from the figure), comes from shock mode 2. This is the only case with an S2 lobe.
    - Mode 1 radiates at two distinct angles: S1a, made of three lobes near 60°, and S1b near 90°.
    - LEE and L-G give similar lobe numbers and angles but quite different relative amplitudes (p. 9–10). A single Mach-angle argument cannot explain the several S1 lobes (p. 10).
    - The I2 LEE run had not become fully periodic in time. The authors think the gross features are right but the quantitative accuracy may suffer (footnote b, p. 9).
- **Heated jet, T_j/T∞ = 2.0** (Figs 8–11, p. 10–12).
  - Heating hardly changes the first shock mode: its local wavelength and growth rate are almost identical (Fig. 8, p. 10). Any heating effect on the sound therefore comes through the instability waves (p. 10).
  - Only the first shock mode is a significant radiator by the Mach-angle argument, although H2 also includes mode 2 (p. 10).
  - The hot waves resemble the cold ones qualitatively. The lower-frequency wave again grows faster and peaks inside the jet core, while the H2 u_x peak stays in the shear layer; the contrast is weaker than in the isothermal pair (p. 10).
  - Heating raises the instability-wave gains in general, and the temperature fluctuations especially (Fig. 9, p. 10–11).
  - LEE and L-G directivities match very well for H1 and H2 (Fig. 10, p. 10–11).
    - The H1 LEE amplitude is far below the L-G one. The authors blame under-resolution of the LEE (p. 10).
    - The rise in L-G level from I1 to H1 follows the rise in instability-wave amplitude (p. 10).
  - The Mach-angle rule also works for H1. The trends quoted are k^(t)_H1 ≈ k^(t)_I1 + 1, giving θ_M,H1 ≈ θ_M,I1 + 15° (p. 10). It does not apply straightforwardly to H2 (p. 10). See the check below: the sign of the wavenumber shift looks inconsistent.
- **Momentum against thermodynamic sources** (Lilley–Goldstein split, Fig. 11, p. 10–12).
  - For I1 the thermodynamic (temperature-dipole) term gives less than 5% of the total pressure amplitude (p. 12).
  - For H2 the largest thermodynamic sound is about 20% of the largest total. Momentum still dominates, although the instability wave's temperature fluctuations are much larger than its u_x (p. 12).
  - Cases H1 and I2 are left out of this comparison because of their amplitude inconsistencies (p. 12).
- **Ring sources and interference** (Fig. 12, p. 12–13).
  - At higher frequency the instability waves peak away from the axis. The I1 and H1 u_x, in contrast, penetrate the core and reach their maxima near or on the axis (p. 12).
  - As a localised source moves off the axis, an axisymmetric problem turns from point-source to ring-source radiation. Interference between the elements of the ring gives narrow lobes (p. 12).
  - The far-field Green's function of the L-G equation for a source at r/D = 0.5 lines up with the I2 LEE lobes. For H2 it matches the main lobes reasonably, and a "sliver" of LEE sound near 60° falls on a Green's-function lobe (Fig. 12, p. 12–13).
  - **The authors' view** (p. 12–13):
    1. The local wavenumbers of the instability wave and shock cells near the source peak set a range of Mach angles at which the sources can radiate strongly.
    2. At low frequency the sources are strongest on the axis, and their sound fills that range.
    3. At high frequency the sources are strongest off the axis, and interference selects narrow lobes within the range.
- **Status** (p. 13): preliminary. The next steps listed are better-resolved LEE runs, a parametric study, comparison with experiment, and assessing nonlinear and stochastic effects.

## Setup / method
**Cases** (Table 1, p. 5; all with M_j = 1.28, M_d = 1.5, axisymmetric m = 0 waves):

| Case | T_j/T∞ | St | ωD/a∞ |
|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | 1.0 | 0.6 | 4.83 |
| I2 | 1.0 | 0.9 | 7.24 |
| H1 | 2.0 | 0.42 | 4.83 |
| H2 | 2.0 | 0.64 | 7.24 |

> Checked (my arithmetic): ωD/a∞ = 2π St M_j√(T_j/T∞). That gives 4.83 and 7.24 for the isothermal cases, and St = 0.425 and 0.637 for the heated cases at the same ω. So T_j/T∞ is the **static** jet-to-ambient temperature ratio, St = fD/U_j, and the hot and cold pairs share the same dimensionless ω, not the same St. The stagnation ratios would be TR ≈ 1.33 and 2.66 (my arithmetic, not in the paper).

- **Flow regime.** M_j = 1.28 from an M_d = 1.5 nozzle is overexpanded: β = √|M_j² − M_d²| ≈ 0.78 and NPR ≈ 2.70 (my arithmetic). The paper only says "imperfectly-matched" (p. 1). No nozzle geometry is modelled; the computation starts from a RANS mean flow.
- **Nondimensionalisation** (p. 2): fully expanded diameter D, ambient density ρ∞ and ambient sound speed a∞. θ is measured from the downstream jet axis (p. 2).
- **Decomposition** (eq. 2, p. 2): f = F̄ + f^t + f^s + f′ + f^other. F̄ is the steady base flow, f^t the instability wave, f^s the shock cells, f′ the sound from their interaction, and f^other unrelated and higher-order nonlinear terms. f′ is assumed small, so it obeys the LEE with the t × s cross products as sources (eqs. 3a–c).
- **Base flow** (p. 3): RANS following Choi & Lele (2001). The turbulence model, inflow conditions and whether the RANS mean is the shock-free equivalent jet are not stated. Since the shocks are added as a linear perturbation f^s, it is presumably shock-free (my reading).
- **Instability waves** (p. 3, 5–6): axisymmetric linear PSE, the axisymmetric version of Cheung & Lele's (2004) planar mixing-layer code. Each wave is normalised by its maximum inlet u_x (p. 6), so absolute levels are arbitrary and only comparisons between cases mean anything. The inlet station and marching step are not given.
- **Shock cells** (p. 3, 6): locally parallel "quasi-viscous" linear stability equations at zero frequency, following Tam, Jackson & Seiner (1985), i.e. a multiple-scales sum of shock modes. The text does not say how the mode amplitudes are set; in TJS they come from the exit pressure mismatch. The local eigenvalue of mode 1 is shown in Fig. 8 (p. 10).
- **LEE solver** (p. 3):
  - Explicit standard 4th-order Runge–Kutta time stepping; axisymmetric cylindrical (x, r).
  - Two radial subdomains joined by Hesthaven's (1997) characteristics-based patching. Chebyshev transforms for radial derivatives and Fourier transforms for streamwise derivatives within each subdomain.
  - Physical-space windowing (damping) along the boundaries, similar to Colonius & Ran's (2002) super-grid scale model, and a symmetry condition on the axis.
  - Fig. 1 shows a physical region of roughly 8.7D × 4D, with the subdomain interface near r/D = 3 and damping out to r/D ≈ 5.5.
  - Grid sizes, time step, run length and convergence criteria are not given. Two runs are flagged as deficient: I2 not periodic (p. 9) and H1 under-resolved (p. 10).
- **Far-field extraction from the LEE** (p. 3). This is a Kirchhoff-type cylindrical-surface method that needs a time-periodic solution:
  1. Record the axisymmetric pressure on a cylinder r = r₀, far enough out that mean-flow gradients and radial mean flow can be neglected.
  2. Fourier transform in time and keep the forcing frequency.
  3. Fourier transform in x and match each wavenumber to an outgoing Hankel-function solution of the wave equation.
  4. Invert the x transform by stationary phase to get the far field.

  The value of r₀ is not given. Plotted amplitudes are |p_ff|πR (Fig. 6 text, p. 9).
- **Rayleigh / Lilley–Goldstein solution** (p. 4). Make the left-hand side of eq. 3 locally parallel, which reduces it to the third-order convective wave equation (Pridmore-Brown / Lilley).
  - The sources follow Goldstein's (2001) exact form of Lilley's equation (eqs. 5–6), with a term L(p^(t)p^(s)) neglected (p. 4).
  - Fourier transforming in x and t gives the inhomogeneous Rayleigh equation (eq. 4). It is solved by computing its Green's function and convolving with the source, which gives the pressure outside the shear layer. The far field then follows by stationary phase as in step 4 (p. 4).
  - The mean profile comes from a single station: x/D ≈ 4.0 for I1 and ≈ 3.5 for I2 (Fig. 6 caption, p. 9). The heated-case stations are not given.
  - The L-G solution omits the M lobe, which would need the discrete instability-wave pole in the Fourier integral (p. 9).
- **Diagnostics of the model.** The local source phase velocity c_s = ω/(k_t − k_s) comes from the local PSE and shock-mode wavenumbers, and gives a local Mach angle θ_M(x) (Fig. 7). A Green's function for a source at r/D = 0.5 is the ring-source test (Fig. 12).
- **Assumptions overall:**
  - linear, inviscid propagation;
  - a single deterministic (fully coherent) instability wave per frequency, m = 0 only;
  - one-way coupling: the interaction makes sound but does not change f^t or f^s;
  - steady shocks;
  - no stochastic, turbulent or nonlinear content (p. 2–3, 13).

## Key equations
All from rendered pages; notation as printed.

- **Harper-Bourne & Fisher peak frequency** (eq. 1, p. 1): $f_p=\dfrac{U_c}{L\,(1-M_c\cos\theta)}$, with L the shock-cell spacing and M_c the convective Mach number.
- **Decomposition** (eq. 2, p. 2): $f=\overline F+f^t+f^s+f'+f^{other}$.
- **Inhomogeneous LEE** (eqs. 3a–c, p. 2–3), with $m'_i\equiv\rho'\overline U_i+\bar\rho u'_i$:
  - $\dfrac{\partial\rho'}{\partial t}+\dfrac{\partial m'_j}{\partial x_j}=-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left(\rho^{(t)}u_j^{(s)}+\rho^{(s)}u_j^{(t)}\right)$ (3a)
  - $\dfrac{\partial m'_i}{\partial t}+\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left(m'_i\overline U_j+m'_j\overline U_i-\rho'\overline U_i\overline U_j\right)+\dfrac{\partial p'}{\partial x_i}=-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\rho^{(t)}u_i^{(s)}+\rho^{(s)}u_i^{(t)}\right]-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[m_j^{(t)}u_i^{(s)}+m_j^{(s)}u_i^{(t)}+\left(\rho^{(t)}u_j^{(s)}+\rho^{(s)}u_j^{(t)}\right)\overline U_i\right]$ (3b)
  - $\dfrac{\partial e'}{\partial t}+\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[u'_j(\overline E+\overline P)+\overline U_j(e'+p')\right]=-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\overline U_j\left(\rho^{(t)}u_j^{(s)}+\rho^{(s)}u_j^{(t)}\right)+\bar\rho\,u_j^{(t)}u_j^{(s)}\right]-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[u_j^{(t)}\left(e^{(s)}+p^{(s)}\right)+u_j^{(s)}\left(e^{(t)}+p^{(t)}\right)\right]$ (3c)

  A perfect-gas equation of state closes the system (p. 3).
- **Inhomogeneous Rayleigh equation** (eq. 4, p. 4): $\dfrac{\bar n^2}{r}\dfrac{d}{dr}\left(\dfrac{r}{\bar n^2}\bar c^2\dfrac{d\tilde p}{dr}\right)+\omega^2\left[\bar n^2-\bar c^2\kappa^2\right]\tilde p=\tilde S(k,r,\omega)$, with $\kappa\equiv k/\omega$ and $\bar n\equiv1+\overline U_x\kappa$.
  > The + sign in n̄ implies a Fourier convention opposite to the model problem's $e^{i(k_tx-\omega_0t)}$, for which the Doppler factor is 1 − Ū_xκ. Check against the PDF before reusing.
- **Goldstein's form of Lilley's equation** (eqs. 5a–b, p. 4): the variable is $\pi\equiv(p'/\overline P)^{1/\gamma}-1$ (as printed; Goldstein's variable uses the total pressure), with
  - $S=\dfrac{D_0}{Dt}\dfrac{\partial f_j}{\partial x_j}-2\dfrac{\partial\overline U_1}{\partial x_j}\dfrac{\partial f_j}{\partial x_1}$ (5a)
  - $f_i=-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_j}(1+\pi)u'_iu'_j-(\gamma-1)T'\dfrac{\partial\pi}{\partial x_i}$ (5b)
- **Interaction source** (eq. 6, p. 4): $f_i=-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left(u_i^{(t)}u_j^{(s)}+u_i^{(s)}u_j^{(t)}\right)-(\gamma-1)\left[T^{(t)}\dfrac{\partial p^{(s)}}{\partial x_i}+T^{(s)}\dfrac{\partial p^{(t)}}{\partial x_i}\right]$. The first group is the "momentum" source and the second the "thermodynamic" one (p. 10). The neglected term is L(p^(t)p^(s)).
- **Model source** (eqs. 7a–c, p. 4): $S=f^{(t)}g^{(s)}$, $f^{(t)}=\hat f(r)\,e^{i(k_tx-\omega_0t)}e^{-4\ln2\,(x/L)^2}$, $g^{(s)}=\hat g(r)\left[e^{ik_sx}+e^{-ik_sx}\right]$.
- **Model far field** (eqs. 8a–c, p. 4–5):
  $P_{ff}\sim\dfrac{e^{i(\omega_0R-\omega_0t)}}{\pi R}\,C(\theta)\sqrt{\dfrac{\pi L^2}{4\ln2}}\left\{e^{-\frac{(k_1^*L)^2}{16\ln2}\left[1-c_{s1}\cos\theta\right]^2}+e^{-\frac{(k_2^*L)^2}{16\ln2}\left[1-c_{s2}\cos\theta\right]^2}\right\}$,
  with $k_1^*=k_t-k_s$, $k_2^*=k_t+k_s$ and $c_{s,j}=\omega_0/k_j^*$. C(θ) carries the radial source structure and is dropped (p. 5).
  > Checked (my arithmetic): the Fourier transform of $e^{-ax^2}$ with $a=4\ln2/L^2$ is $\sqrt{\pi/a}\,e^{-k^2/4a}=\sqrt{\pi L^2/4\ln2}\,e^{-k^2L^2/16\ln2}$. Stationary phase selects $k=\omega_0\cos\theta$ (a∞ = 1), and $k_j^*-\omega_0\cos\theta=k_j^*(1-c_{s,j}\cos\theta)$, which reproduces eq. 8a. With this envelope, L is the full width at half maximum (the envelope is ½ at x = ±L/2); the paper calls it the half-width (p. 5).
- **Mach angle** (p. 5): $\theta_M=\cos^{-1}(1/c_{s,j})$ for $|c_{s,j}|>1$.
- **Link to eq. 1** (my derivation): setting $1-c_{s1}\cos\theta=0$ with $k_t=\omega/U_c$ and $k_s=2\pi/L$ gives $\omega(1/U_c-\cos\theta)=k_s$, i.e. $f=U_c/[L(1-M_c\cos\theta)]$ with $M_c=U_c/a_\infty$. That is the Harper-Bourne & Fisher / Tam & Tanna peak, as the paper says (p. 2, 5). The upstream limit θ_M = 180° is $c_{s1}=-1$, i.e. $k_t-k_s=-\omega/a_\infty$, which is Tam's screech condition $k_h-k_{sc}=-k_s$ in [mercier-2017](mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) (eq. 3.3).

## Notation
- D: fully expanded jet diameter; ρ∞, a∞: ambient density and sound speed (all scaling, p. 2). M_j, M_d: fully expanded and design Mach numbers. T_j/T∞: static jet-to-ambient temperature ratio (see the Table 1 check). St = fD/U_j; ω: angular frequency in D/a∞ units.
- x, r: axial and radial coordinates; R, θ: spherical far-field coordinates, θ from the downstream axis.
- Superscripts (t), (s), ′: instability wave, shock-cell structure and sound; overbars: base flow. m′_i: momentum disturbance; e′: total-energy disturbance; E, P: base-flow energy and pressure.
- k_t, k_s: instability-wave and shock-mode wavenumbers (local wavenumbers when applied to the PSE and shock solutions); k₁* = k_t − k_s, k₂* = k_t + k_s; c_s,j = ω₀/k_j*: source phase velocity; θ_M: Mach angle; L: Gaussian envelope width; C(θ): radial-structure factor.
- κ = k/ω; n̄ = 1 + Ū_xκ; c̄: mean sound speed; p̃, S̃: transformed pressure and source; π: Goldstein's nonlinear pressure variable; f_i: L-G source vector; T′: temperature disturbance; L(·): wave operator.
- S_e: source of the total-energy equation (3c). k_r, k_i: real and imaginary parts of the shock-mode eigenvalue.
- Cases I1, I2 (isothermal) and H1, H2 (heated); lobes M (Mach waves of the source-excited instability waves), S1, S1a, S1b (shock mode 1), S2 (shock mode 2). L-G: Lilley–Goldstein. LEE: linearised Euler equations.

## Relevance to this project
This is a modelling paper on BBSAN: no experiments, and its only data are the Norum & Seiner shock-cell pressures used for validation. It matters to the user's work (CLAUDE.md: resonance and feedback in supersonic jets, plus rocket noise) because it computes, rather than postulates, the KH × shock-cell product that underlies both BBSAN and screech closure. It sits between [Tam & Tanna 1982](tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md), with analytic Pack shocks and assumed instability waves, and the modern inside-out model of [Wong et al. 2021](wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), with PSE/LES wavepackets and PIV-corrected shocks.

**How it fits and where it agrees or disagrees**
- **Confirms Tam & Tanna.**
  - Only the difference wave k_t − k_s radiates upstream; the sum wave is subsonic and points downstream (p. 5). This agrees with [Tam & Tanna 1982](tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md) (p. 9), who found only k − λ₁ effective.
  - The Mach-angle condition reproduces the Harper-Bourne & Fisher peak formula (my derivation in Key equations; p. 2, 5).
  - The new element is a **local** Mach angle: the source's axial extent maps onto a fan of angles through the x-dependence of k_t and k_s (Fig. 7). That is the deterministic, single-frequency version of the frequency–angle relation [Tam et al. 1986](tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md) plot as a_∞/f_pD against cos θ (p. 3, 8 of that note).
- **Same algebra as wavepacket superdirectivity.** Eq. 8a is the closed form that [Cavalieri et al. 2019](cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md) give for a Gaussian wavepacket, PSD ∝ exp[−½k_h²L²(1 − M_c cos θ)²] (p. 9, 11 of that note), with k_h replaced by the difference wavenumber. A longer envelope means louder and narrower radiation (p. 5). This is why envelope length matters.
  - Shock-free PSE envelopes decay too fast in shock-containing jets ([Nogueira et al. 2022](nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 18–19). Ray et al.'s PSE runs on a (presumably) shock-free mean flow with one-way coupling, so their envelopes and amplitudes are probably too short and too low downstream. The same weakness appears in Wong's PSE, which falls below the LES beyond x ≈ 6D (Wong note, p. 17).
- **Second shock mode.** The [Wong 2021](wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md) note records that the n = 2 secondary peak is "absent from Ray & Lele (2007)" (Wong p. 23, 29). This 2005 paper did include mode 2 at the higher frequency. It produced the farthest-upstream lobe S2 for I2 only, and the authors judged mode 1 to be the only significant radiator (p. 9–10).
  - The 2007 omission may therefore follow from this paper's single-frequency directivity argument. Wong's spectra show that mode 2 controls a separate spectral peak at higher St.
  - The two views are compatible (my inference): at one ω, mode 2's c_s is supersonic only over part of the domain (p. 6). Across frequency, its k − k_s2 Mach-wave condition is met at higher St for a given angle.
- **Entropy (thermodynamic) source.** It is below 5% in the isothermal jet and about 20% at T_j/T∞ = 2 (p. 12). This supports Wong et al. neglecting the entropy term in T_ij for their cold jets (Wong p. 20, 22). For the heated jets relevant to rocket plumes it is not negligible.
- **Source location.** At St 0.6 the strongest sources lie on the centreline at x/D 2–6 (Fig. 7), inside the plume and near the end of the core. This is consistent with Wong's sources inside the plume at cells 3–5, 2D–8D (Wong p. 27). It also fits the full-scale BSN sources concentrated near the end of the potential core in [Mathews 2025](mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) (p. 32). The paper itself only asserts that most shock noise comes from there (p. 5). At higher St the source moves to a ring at r/D ≈ 0.5, x/D ≲ 3 (Fig. 4), so "where BBSAN comes from" is frequency dependent.
- **Shock spacing in overexpanded C-D jets.** The TJS multiple-scales modes on a RANS mean reproduce the measured shock train of this mildly overexpanded jet (β ≈ 0.78) for about five cells (Fig. 3, p. 6–7). [Mathews 2025](mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) finds spacing formulas failing for overexpanded C-D jets (p. 34–35), so this model is a candidate for that open question on the jet-noise page. It is untested at strong overexpansion, high temperature or with a Mach disc.
- **Heating hardly touches the shocks** (Fig. 8), so heating acts through the instability waves. This fits [Tam & Tanna 1982](tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md), where heating moves the measured peaks by the u_j ratio, about 1.5, as the convection-speed formula predicts (p. 16), and cold jets are up to 3 dB quieter (p. 17–20).

**Reading it critically**
- **The heated-case wavenumber shift looks wrong in sign** (p. 10). With c_s1 = ω/(k_t − k_s) and k_t < k_s, a larger k_t brings k_t − k_s closer to zero, so |c_s1| grows and θ_M moves toward 90°, not upstream. My check, at k_s = 2π/0.88 and the I1 lobe at 115°: k_t + 1 gives θ_M ≈ 102°, and k_t − 1 gives ≈ 129° (+14°).
  - Physically the hot jet convects faster at the same ω (U_j is √2 larger), so k_t should fall. Keeping U_c/U_j fixed gives k_t ≈ 3.6 against 5.1 and θ_M ≈ 137°.
  - The +15° statement is therefore consistent with k_H1 ≈ k_I1 − 1 (or with k defined negative), not with the printed +1.
- **Fig. 9 against the text.** The text says heating raises instability-wave gains in general and puts the H1 u_x maximum inside the core (p. 10). The plotted H1 u_x peaks (≈ 1.2) are about half those of I1 (≈ 2.2), at r/D ≈ 0.3–0.5. Only the temperature panels show the increase (≈ 6.8 against 1.6).
- **Unconverged and under-resolved runs.** The amplitudes of I2 (not periodic) and H1 (under-resolved) are unreliable (p. 9–10). That leaves I1 and H2 as the only quantitatively trustworthy LEE cases.
  - The L-G comparison uses a single-station parallel mean flow, so part of the high-frequency mismatch may be non-parallel rather than "ring-source". The authors attribute it to the latter (p. 12).
- **Arbitrary amplitude.** PSE amplitudes are normalised at the inlet (p. 6), and no turbulence statistics set them. Absolute levels and the I/H comparisons are therefore relative to equal inlet u_x, not to equal turbulence.
- **No experiment for the sound.** All comparisons are model against model; the authors defer validation (p. 13). For the experimental test, see Wong 2021 and Tam's stochastic model ([Tam 1995](tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 32–36).
- **Self-excited KH in the forced LEE.** The source terms excite instability waves in the LEE, which radiate their own Mach waves (lobe M; p. 7). In a linear solver about an unstable mean flow these grow and convect out. Their presence near x/D = 6–9 in Fig. 5 is a reminder that forced LEE/LNS runs on jet means mix sound with hydrodynamic response.
- **Rayleigh-equation sign convention** (eq. 4) and π defined with p′ instead of the total p (eq. 5): check against Goldstein (2001) before reuse.

**Methods and data for the lab workflow**
- **Model inputs need only mean fields.** The shock-cell model and the PSE base flow need only a mean flow: RANS here, but the group's **non-time-resolved PIV** or mean BOS would do, as Wong et al. did with Shock Lab group PIV. The validation in Fig. 3 uses mean static pressure only.
- **Time-resolved data test the wave content.** The instability wave's amplitude, envelope L and local k_t(x) are what control the directivity (eq. 8a, Fig. 7). In experiment they come from time-resolved schlieren or TR-PIV through SPOD: the phase gradient of the leading mode gives k_t(x), and its magnitude gives the envelope. Mean schlieren or PIV gives k_s(x) from the local shock spacing. The two together give a measured θ_M(x) map like Fig. 7 (suggestion).
- **Fully coherent, single frequency.** The model has no intermittency, jitter or amplitude modulation. In the user's **SPOD → reconstruction → Hilbert POD** pipeline, HPOD of a band-limited reconstruction gives time-varying amplitude and envelope position. Through eq. 8a's dependence on L and k_t, that predicts time-varying BBSAN directivity and level.
  - Comparing that with simultaneous far-field microphones would test the envelope-length sensitivity directly (suggestion, not in the paper).
  - The ring-source interference (Fig. 12) also means that m = 0 radiation from off-axis sources has deep angular nulls. Single-angle microphone spectra may show apparent dips that are geometric, not dynamic (my inference).
- **Far-field extrapolation.** The cylindrical Hankel matching (p. 3) is a cheap Kirchhoff method for any axisymmetric (or azimuthally decomposed) single-frequency field. It applies to SPOD modes of LES pressure on a cylinder, and to near-field array data on a line if azimuthally decomposed. It needs the field to have decayed at the ends of the line, or a window.

**Modelling side and the PyFR staging LES**
- The PyFR LES (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`, underexpanded convergent nozzle, NPR 2.10–2.25) has small β, so its BBSAN is weak. Its screech closure uses the same KH × shock product (the k_kh − k_s "difference wave" in [edgington-mitchell-2020](edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 14–17).
- **Local screech map.** θ_M(x) = 180° is the local screech condition (Key equations). A Fig. 7-type map from the LES, with k_t(x) from the screech-frequency SPOD mode and k_s(x) from the mean shock spacing, would show where along the jet the A1 and A2 loops satisfy k_t − k_s = −ω/a∞. That can be compared with the 4th-shock source of [mercier-2017](mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) (suggestion).
- **Cheap linear check.** A forced LNS/LEE on the LES mean with PSE-or-SPOD × shock sources (eq. 3) would separate the linear interaction sound from the self-sustained loop. The LEE here lacks any feedback, so it can only give the "BBSAN" part.

**Rocket noise.** The paper is motivated by coannular heated jets in flight, which Tam's model does not easily cover (p. 2). Its heating results bear on the "why no BBSAN at full scale" question on the jet-noise page:
- the shock structure barely changes with heating (Fig. 8);
- the thermodynamic source grows to about 20% (p. 12);
- the predicted levels follow the instability-wave gain (p. 10);
- the BBSAN lobe moves upstream with heating (θ_M +15°; p. 10).

Nothing here suggests saturation with temperature, but T_j/T∞ = 2 is far below rocket values, and full-scale rocket data show no clear BBSAN ([lubert-2022](lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 9). My suggestion: a shock-noise extension of SP-8072-type or SATURN models would need both the entropy source and the frequency-dependent (ring-source) directivity found here.
