## Summary
The paper classifies the screech modes of underexpanded jets from AR 2 rectangular and elliptical converging nozzles using 150 kHz schlieren and SPOD. To recover three-dimensional structure from a path-integrated technique, the nozzle is rotated about its axis in 10° steps from 0° to 180° and SPOD is repeated for each viewing angle θ. The angle dependence of the screech-frequency SPOD eigenvalue λ_s, the symmetry of the spatial modes and a symmetric-plane inner product β are used as fingerprints: varicose modes are symmetric with λ_s independent of θ; flapping modes are antisymmetric in the minor-axis view and λ_s falls to a minimum at θ = 90°. Two new modes appear when screech is multimodal at high NPR. In the rectangular jet (NPR 4.0, St_s 0.27) a "bi-axial flapping" mode flaps in the minor-axis plane near the nozzle and wags in the major-axis plane downstream, the switch lining up with axis switching of the shock cells. In the elliptical jet (NPR 3.6, St_s 0.28) a "quasi-helical" mode is antisymmetric from every angle with λ_s independent of both θ and axial sub-domain. The authors conclude that axial evolution of the mean flow lets non-axisymmetric jets support screech modes beyond those predicted from the nozzle-exit shape. The paper is purely experimental; no stability or simulation model is solved.

## Key findings
- **Staging and multimodality** (p. 6–7, Fig. 2–3):
  - At NPR 2.0 and 2.6 (rectangular, θ = 0°) every significant SPOD peak is a harmonic of St_s, so one screech loop is active. At NPR 4.0 several non-harmonic peaks appear, i.e. more than one loop is active at once (multimodal; Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2022a) (p. 6, Fig. 2).
  - Both jets stage at NPR ≈ 2.2, where the tone jumps discontinuously from the varicose mode to the flapping mode (p. 7, Fig. 3).
  - Multimodal behaviour occurs in both jets and is visible in both the minor-axis and major-axis views (p. 6–7). The elliptical jet's multimodal pattern differs from the rectangular jet's (p. 16, Fig. 3).
- **Varicose mode** (p. 7–9, Fig. 4–5):
  - Rectangular, NPR 2.0, St_s = 0.92: symmetric from every view, non-zero on the centreline, |φ| peak at x/D_e ≈ 1.4 (p. 7).
  - The projected wavepacket widens as θ goes from minor to major axis, implying a non-axisymmetric initial ring vortex, consistent with Nogueira et al. (2023) (p. 7).
  - Path integration puts the cross-stream peaks at the shear layer in the minor-axis view and near the centreline in the major-axis view (p. 7–8).
  - β > 0.93 for all symmetric plane pairs (p. 9, Fig. 5a). Corrected λ_s is roughly constant with θ for both the rectangular (St_s 0.92) and elliptical (NPR 2.0, St_s 1.03) jets (p. 9, Fig. 5b).
- **Flapping mode** (p. 10–11, Fig. 6–7):
  - Rectangular, NPR 2.6, St_s = 0.39: antisymmetric at θ = 0° with zero centreline amplitude. The antisymmetry fades as θ rises and is gone at θ = 90°, which rules out a helical mode (a helix would look the same from every view) (p. 10).
  - |φ| peaks at x/D_e ≈ 2.5 for all θ (p. 10).
  - λ_s falls from θ = 0° to a minimum at 90° and recovers towards 180° (a "v-shape"), confirming strong flapping in the minor-axis plane (p. 11, Fig. 7b). Elliptical NPR 2.6 flapping is at St_s = 0.42 and behaves similarly (p. 11).
  - β > 0.75 for planes 0–60°; lower near 90° because the major-axis-view modes are weak and likely unconverged (p. 11, Fig. 7a).
- **Bi-axial flapping mode, rectangular jet** (p. 11–14, Fig. 8–10):
  - NPR 4.0, St_s = 0.27 (the middle of three tones; the others are 0.23 and 0.30) (p. 11, 15).
  - Strongly antisymmetric with zero centreline amplitude from every view (p. 11).
  - The |φ| peak moves downstream with θ: x/D_e ≈ 6.5 in the minor-axis view to 8.1 in the major-axis view. The authors say a single-frequency wavepacket with a view-dependent axial envelope had not been reported before (p. 11–12).
  - Axis switching, judged crudely from the spanwise extent of the dark (expansion) regions of the mean shock cells, occurs at x/D_e ≈ 6.5, the jet's fourth shock cell (p. 13, Fig. 9).
  - Full-domain λ_s is nearly independent of θ, which on its own would suggest varicose or helical; the spatial modes rule out varicose and the moving envelope rules out a pure helix (p. 14, Fig. 10a).
  - Sub-domain SPOD separates the behaviour: 2.5 < x/D_e < 5.0 (before switching) has the flapping v-shape with minimum at 90°; 9.0 < x/D_e < 14.5 (after switching) has the same pattern shifted by 90° (maximum at 90°), i.e. wagging in the major-axis plane; 5.0 < x/D_e < 9.0 mixes both and looks like the full domain (p. 14, Fig. 10a).
  - β > 0.75 for symmetric planes (p. 14, Fig. 10b).
- **Co-existing modes at NPR 4.0, rectangular** (p. 15–17, Fig. 11–14):
  - St_s = 0.23: a plain minor-axis flapping mode in every sub-domain (insensitive to axis switching); β > 0.85 for 0–60° pairs (p. 15, Fig. 11–12).
  - St_s = 0.30: varicose, symmetric from every view, with no view-dependent shift in its envelope; β > 0.8 (p. 15–17, Fig. 13–14).
  - So at one NPR, a flapping, a bi-axial flapping and a varicose loop coexist, and only the bi-axial one responds to axis switching.
- **Quasi-helical mode, elliptical jet** (p. 16–19, Fig. 15–17):
  - NPR 3.6, St_s = 0.28: strongly antisymmetric from every view (p. 16).
  - The mean shock cells do not fully axis-switch within the field of view, but major-axis cells contract faster; at the seventh shock cell (x/D_e ≈ 7.5) the two spanwise extents nearly meet, close to the |φ| peak at x/D_e ≈ 8. The cross-section becomes less eccentric, possibly near-circular (p. 16, 18, Fig. 16).
  - λ_s ≈ 10⁷ and roughly constant with θ for the full domain, and every sub-domain (0–3, 3–11, 11–14.5 D_e) has the same θ dependence at lower level (p. 18, Fig. 17a).
  - Antisymmetric from all views, no streamwise shift and θ-independent λ_s → closest to a helical mode, but labelled "quasi-helical" because the mean flow is not axisymmetric and can't be decomposed in azimuth; confirming helicity needs cross-plane velocity data (p. 18–19). β > 0.8 (p. 19, Fig. 17b).
  - St_s = 0.32 at the same NPR is a classical se1 minor-axis flapping mode, v-shaped λ_s in every sub-domain, β > 0.7 for 0–60° pairs (p. 19, Fig. 18–19).
- **This contradicts / extends earlier views** (p. 3, 20–21): Edgington-Mitchell et al. (2015b) had speculated, from two-plane velocity data, that axis switching lets an elliptical jet support a helical-looking screech mode; this paper gives the first further evidence for it. Raman (1997) had briefly reported antisymmetric major-axis modes in an AR 5 rectangular jet (p. 3).
- **Convergence** (p. 22–23, Appendix A, Fig. 20–21): modes from 100,000 images match those from 200,000 with β > 0.95 (St_s 0.28) and > 0.8 (St_s 0.32, weak at θ = 90°); λ_s changes by < 6 % when the record is doubled. Similar for the rectangular jet (not shown).

## Setup / method
- **Facility** (p. 4): "Supersonic-Gas-Jet Facility at Monash University", a Shock Lab group facility (the paper doesn't name the lab space). Continuous high-pressure air, NPR 2.0–5.0 (plenum/ambient), screens and honeycombs in the plenum, contraction > 100:1 to the throat.
- **Nozzles** (p. 4, Fig. 1b):

| Nozzle | Type | AR | D_e (area-equivalent) | Lip |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rectangular | purely convergent | 2.0 | 13.6 mm | non-uniform between major and minor axes (uniform external geometry) |
| Elliptical | purely convergent | 2.0 | 10 mm | 0.05 D_e |

- **Coordinates** (p. 4–5): x streamwise; y, z fixed to the nozzle (θ = 0° is the minor-axis plane, θ = 90° the major-axis plane); y* is the spanwise coordinate of the current view (y* = y at 0°, z at 90°).
- **Schlieren** (p. 5):
  - Z-type Toepler, two mirrors of focal length 2032 mm; knife-edge sensitive to ∂ρ/∂x.
  - Photron Fastcam SA-Z 2100k at 150 kHz, exposure 1/197647 s (≈ 5.1 µs), 512 × 200 px; pulsed LED illumination (Willert et al. 2010).
  - 100,000 images per condition and per view angle.
  - **Multi-view:** the nozzle (not the camera) is rotated about x from 0° to 180° in 10° steps, giving 19 views per condition.
  - Field of view: x/D_e ≈ 0–8 for the low-NPR cases (Fig. 4, 6); for NPR > 3.5 a larger field, 12.5 D_e long, starting at x/D_e = 2.5 for the rectangular jet, because the wavepacket extends further (p. 11). The elliptical NPR 3.6 views span 0–14.5 D_e (Fig. 15, 17).
  - Pixel scale and knife-edge orientation details beyond ∂ρ/∂x are not given.
- **SPOD** (p. 5–6), standard Welch-based (Towne et al. 2018; Schmidt & Colonius 2020) on mean-subtracted **light intensity**, so eigenvalues are only qualitative between cases (p. 6):
  - **Requires time-resolved data.** n_t = 100,000, n_fft = 4096, n_ovlp = 2048 (50 %), n_blk = 47 (p. 6).
  - Derived (my arithmetic): Δf = 150,000/4096 ≈ 36.6 Hz; block length ≈ 27.3 ms; record ≈ 0.67 s. The window function and weight matrix W used are not stated.
  - Only the leading mode is analysed at each screech frequency.
- **St** (p. 6): St = f D_e/U_j with U_j the ideally expanded velocity. U_j values (or M_j) are not tabulated.
- **Broadband correction of λ_s** (p. 9): because the broadband floor differs between views, a local average of non-screech frequencies around each peak is subtracted from the peak, referenced to θ = 0°. The authors say results are not strongly dependent on this correction. The exact averaging band isn't given.
- **Symmetric-plane inner product β** (eq. 3.1, p. 8–9): compares the complex leading SPOD mode at θ = i with that at 180° − i, i = 0°, 10°, …, 80°; complex modes remove arbitrary phase differences. β ≈ 1 symmetric about the z-axis, ≈ −1 antisymmetric, ≈ 0 uncorrelated.
- **Sub-domain SPOD** (p. 13–19): SPOD is rerun on axial windows of the image (before, across and after axis switching) to localise how λ_s depends on θ along the jet.
- **Mean-flow axis-switching criterion** (p. 13, Fig. 9): time-averaged schlieren in the minor- and major-axis views, binarised, with the outer boundaries of the dark regions of each shock cell traced; where the two spanwise extents cross is taken as the switch. The authors call it crude.
- **Modelling side:** none in this paper. The discussion draws on linear spatial stability of elliptical jets (Crighton 1973; Morris 1988; Morris & Bhat 1995), which predicts five instability classes: varicose ce0, flapping se1, wagging ce1, diagonal double-flapping ce2 and sideways double-flapping se2 (p. 3), and on the 3-D OWNS wavepacket predictions of Nogueira, Weightman & Edgington-Mitchell (2023) for the non-axisymmetric varicose structure (p. 7). The screech framework assumed is the guided-jet-mode "weakest-link" model with triadic KH–shock wavenumber selection (p. 2). No dispersion relations, base flows or resonance predictions are computed for the new modes.

## Key equations
- SPOD (p. 5), for Fourier realisations at frequency l stacked in $\hat{\mathbf Q}_l = [\hat{\mathbf q}_l^{(1)},\dots,\hat{\mathbf q}_l^{(n_{blk})}]$:
  $$\frac{1}{n_{blk}}\hat{\mathbf Q}_l^{*}\mathbf W\hat{\mathbf Q}_l\boldsymbol\Psi_l = \boldsymbol\Psi_l\boldsymbol\Lambda_l,\qquad \boldsymbol\Phi_l = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{blk}}}\hat{\mathbf Q}_l\boldsymbol\Psi_l\boldsymbol\Lambda_l^{-1/2}$$
  with $\boldsymbol\Lambda_l = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_l^{(1)} \ge \lambda_l^{(2)} \ge \dots)$ (unnumbered).
- Symmetric-plane similarity (eq. 3.1, p. 8):
  $$\beta_i = \frac{\langle \phi_{\theta=i},\,\phi_{\theta=180^\circ-i}\rangle}{\lVert\phi_{\theta=i}\rVert\,\lVert\phi_{\theta=180^\circ-i}\rVert},\qquad i = 0^\circ, 10^\circ,\dots,80^\circ$$
  using the Frobenius norm. With complex modes the inner product is complex; the paper plots a real β between −1 and 1 without saying whether the real part or magnitude is taken.
- Convergence measure (eq. A1, p. 22): the same normalised inner product between the leading mode from the 50,000- or 100,000-image set (i = 1, 2) and the 200,000-image mode at St_s.

## Notation
- AR: aspect ratio. D_e: area-equivalent diameter. U_j: ideally expanded velocity. NPR: plenum/ambient pressure ratio.
- θ: viewing angle (nozzle rotation about x); y*_θ: spanwise coordinate in that view.
- φ (φ_r, |φ|): leading SPOD spatial mode (real part, magnitude). λ_s: leading SPOD eigenvalue at the screech frequency St_s.
- β: normalised inner product between modes viewed from symmetric planes θ and 180° − θ.
- Elliptical-jet stability modes: ce0 (varicose), se1 (flapping, minor-axis plane), ce1 (wagging, major-axis plane), ce2, se2 (double-flapping). "Wagging" = flapping in the major-axis plane.
- Classical round-jet screech stages: A1, A2 (toroidal, m = 0), B and D (flapping), C (helical, |m| = 1).

## Relevance to this project
This is a Shock Lab group paper (the user is a co-author) on resonance in non-axisymmetric jets, one of the flow classes listed in CLAUDE.md, and its whole analysis is the time-resolved-schlieren branch of the user's workflow.

- **Method template for time-resolved schlieren:**
  - SPOD of high-speed schlieren light intensity (needs time-resolved data: 150 kHz, n_fft 4096, 50 % overlap, 47 blocks). λ_s is in arbitrary light-intensity units, so compare it only across views or sub-domains of one condition, after removing the local broadband floor.
  - **Rotating the nozzle** gives 19 path-integrated views without tomography. Reading mode type from λ_s(θ) and from symmetry is a cheap test that separates varicose (flat, symmetric), flapping (v-shaped, antisymmetric only in one view) and helical-like (flat, antisymmetric in all views) modes.
  - **Sub-domain SPOD** (rerunning SPOD on axial windows) localises how a single-frequency mode changes along the jet. It is a simple add-on to any existing SPOD script and could be applied to impinging-jet or other schlieren data where the mean flow changes along x.
  - β (eq. 3.1) is a reusable similarity metric for comparing complex modes between views, runs or record lengths, as used for convergence in Appendix A.
- **Intermittency and mode switching:** at NPR 3.6–4.0 two or three screech loops show up in the same block-averaged SPOD spectrum (p. 6, 15, 19). SPOD cannot tell whether they truly coexist or alternate in time. This is exactly where the user's SPOD → reconstruction → Hilbert POD pipeline would add information: rebuild the data from the leading mode at each St_s, then use HPOD (see [raiola-2025](raiola-2025-hilbert-pod.md)) or Hilbert/wavelet amplitude tracking to see whether the bi-axial (0.27), flapping (0.23) and varicose (0.30) tones switch or co-exist. With tones only ≈ 0.03–0.04 St apart, check the SPOD frequency resolution first; [yeung-2024](yeung-2024-adaptive-spod-broadband-tonal.md) (multitaper) or [von-saldern-2026](von-saldern-2026-band-ensemble-spod.md) (band-ensemble) may separate close tones from broadband better than Welch blocks. Wavelet-based intermittency tools appear in [mancinelli-2019](mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) and [esfahani-2021-coupling-modes](esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md).
- **For non-time-resolved data:** none here, but the same mode classes could be checked in PIV with POD mode pairs, e.g. in cross-plane PIV, which the authors say is needed to confirm helicity of the quasi-helical mode (p. 18–19); compare the two-plane approach in [edgington-mitchell-2018](edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) and the swirling-jet POD-pair 3-D reconstruction in [oberleithner-2011](oberleithner-2011-swirling-jet-vortex-breakdown-stability.md).
- **Modelling gap:** the paper offers no model of the new modes. Natural next steps, using notes already in the library:
  - Local or Floquet stability on a measured, axially evolving non-axisymmetric mean flow, extending [nogueira-2021-absolute-instability](nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) and [nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech](nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), where shock-spacing changes select A1/A2. Here axis switching changes the mean-flow shape rather than just shock spacing.
  - One-way marching (the cited 3-D OWNS work of Nogueira et al. 2023; see [zhu-2021](zhu-2021-recursive-one-way-navier-stokes.md) for OWNS-R) could test whether an se1 wavepacket evolves into ce1 through axis switching.
  - The upstream leg is assumed to be the guided jet mode ([edgington-mitchell-2018](edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), [edgington-mitchell-2020](edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md)); which guided mode closes a loop whose downstream wavepacket changes its flapping axis is left open.
