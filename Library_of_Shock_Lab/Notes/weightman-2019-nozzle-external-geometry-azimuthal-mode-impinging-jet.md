## Summary
An NPR 3.4 jet from a 15 mm converging nozzle impinges on a flat plate 5D away. Changing only the nozzle's outside changes the resonance. With a thin lip (t/D 0.1, conical exterior) the jet has one tone at St 0.255 and one dominant helical POD pair. With an effectively infinite lip (a 15D flat cap, t/D 7) the dominant tone is at St 0.385 with a toroidal (m = 0) pair, plus a weaker helical pair at St 0.241 and their difference tone (p. 428–433). Two explanations are tested with more nozzles. An intermediate lip (t/D 0.33, still conical) should entrain like the thin lip but behaves like the infinite lip, so the entrainment angle is ruled out as the cause. A foam cap with the infinite lip's shape but no reflecting surface removes the tone and the coherent structures, so the acoustic path matters (p. 433–439). Partial foam covers show that the thin-lip helical loop survives foam on the inner base but disappears with foam on the outer base. The authors conclude that this loop is closed only by sound from the stand-off shock that reflects off the outer surface of the nozzle base and then reaches the lip (p. 440–441, 446). Almost every configuration still produces St ≈ 0.25 and/or ≈ 0.39. Powell's feedback equation, with p = 0 from schlieren, shows that direct, inner-base and outer-base paths can each give both tones for some integer N. The authors read this as the jet choosing whichever path maximises Powell's gain criterion (p. 441–446). There is no stability analysis or simulation.

## Key findings
- **Mean flows match, fluctuations do not** (thin vs infinite lip; Fig. 4, p. 428–429):
  - Both reach about 1.8 U_E before the first shock, and both wall jets reach about ±1.1 U_E transverse (p. 428).
  - Only the infinite lip has a slower region behind the first shock. In instantaneous fields it is an intermittent low-speed region that grows and then "detaches" downstream (p. 428). It is absent in the intermediate-lip and foam cases, so it is unique to the infinite lip (p. 437).
  - Infinite lip: a large region of high **axial** variance on the centreline at the stand-off shock, from axial oscillation of that shock. Powell (1988) found it largest for axisymmetric modes (p. 428–429).
  - Thin lip: spots of high **transverse** variance where the shocks cross the centreline. This transverse shock oscillation is what a helical or flapping mode would cause (p. 429).
- **Tones** (Fig. 5, p. 430; St = f D_j/U_j, D_j = 0.016 m, U_j = 413 m/s):
  - Thin lip: one tone at St 0.255 with harmonics.
  - Infinite lip: several strong tones. The highest is 0.385; 0.241 is taken as the second true impingement tone because it lies near the thin-lip tone. The others, including 0.144, are linear combinations of the two, which the authors link to nonlinear interaction of two instabilities ([Tam & Norum 1992](tam-1992-impingement-tones-large-aspect-ratio-rectangular-jets.md); Henderson & Powell 1993; Thurow et al. 2002) (p. 430, 433).
  - Two inharmonic tones suggest two separate feedback loops in one flow (p. 430).
- **POD** (over 10,000 snapshots per case, u′ and v′ stacked; Figs 6–7, p. 430–433):
  - *Thin lip:* modes 1–2 hold 17.7% and 16.1%. Mode 3 holds 3.9% and the rest under 2% each. Their phase portrait is an annulus, so they are a pair. Modes 3–4 give a cardioid, so they are not a pair (p. 430–431).
  - *Thin-lip mode shape:* axial components antisymmetric and transverse components symmetric about the axis, so the mode is helical or flapping. A precessing flapping mode would spread its energy widely (Tan et al. 2017), whereas here one pair dominates, so the mode is taken as helical, m = ±1 (p. 431–432).
  - *Infinite lip:* four energetic modes, 12.1, 11.3, 6.6 and 5.6%. Modes 1–2 are an m = 0 pair (axial symmetric, transverse antisymmetric). Modes 3–4 look like the thin-lip helical pair. Their phase portrait is a filled disc, not a clear annulus, so the pairing rests on spatial structure (p. 431–432).
  - The authors relate this m = 0 plus helical superposition to the LES of an underexpanded impinging jet by Gojon & Bogey (2017) (p. 432).
- **Convective velocity from POD wavelength** (p. 432–433):
  - Measured as the peak-to-peak spacing of the transverse POD component along the axis: λ = 2.0D (thin lip) and 1.94D (infinite-lip helical).
  - With the tones 0.255 and 0.241 this gives U_c = 0.48 U_j and 0.44 U_j, as in [Krothapalli et al. (1999)](krothapalli-1999-flow-field-noise-supersonic-impinging-jet.md) and Gojon et al. (2016).
  - No structure in the data matches St 0.144, which supports 0.241 as the real helical tone (p. 432–433).
  - Check (mine): U_c/U_j = St λ/D_j. 0.255 × 2.0 × 15/16 = 0.478, and 0.241 × 1.94 × 15/16 = 0.438.
- **Contrast with free jets** (p. 433): in Ponton & Seiner (1992) a thicker lip moved a free jet from B (flapping) to C (helical), which differs from this toroidal switch. Vinoth et al. (2011) did see toroidal and helical modes together in a screeching jet with upstream reflectors.
- **Two hypotheses** (§4.1, p. 433–434):
  - (1) *Acoustic path.* For a thin lip, the initial expansion of the underexpanded jet may block the higher-frequency (toroidal) wave from reaching the lip. A thicker lip offers more reflecting surface, as in highly underexpanded free jets (Raman 1997). If so, the helical wave of the thin-lip jet must reach the lip from another direction.
  - (2) *Entrainment.* The external geometry changes the angle of the entrained flow: nearly radial inflow for the infinite lip, inflow from upstream for the thin lip (Fig. 8). This could change the growth rates of the modes.
- **Intermediate lip (t/D 0.33, conical exterior)** (p. 434, 437–439):
  - Tones at St 0.383 and 0.253, a spectrum very like the infinite lip's.
  - POD: an m = 0 pair (14.9%, 13.5%) and a weak helical pair (3.3%, 2.8%). The authors suggest the helical pair's lower energy may go with its weaker tone.
  - Its variance looks like the infinite lip's, at lower levels. Its entrainment streamlines look like the thin lip's (Fig. 15).
  - **So entrainment angle has "no measureable effect"** on which mode grows (p. 438). The caveat is that the relevant entrainment may lie closer to the exit than the PIV resolves (p. 439).
- **Infinite foam over the thin lip** (p. 434–439): this cover mimics the infinite lip's entrainment without its reflecting surface, and leaves the top of the lip uncovered (unlike [Norum's 1983](norum-1983-screech-suppression-supersonic-jets.md) screech suppression).
  - The St 0.255 tone almost disappears, and overall SPL falls by about 3–7 dB.
  - Broadband-to-peak falls from 25 to 4 dB, "more than an order of magnitude" in pressure fluctuation.
  - POD shows no dominant modes (4.4, 3.8, 2.8, 2.5%) and no clear pairs. Shear-layer growth and fluctuations are lower than in every other case. The authors conclude the feedback is "significantly weakened".
  - Because the mode disappears, this case cannot test entrainment (p. 439).
- **Recessed foam over the thin lip** (top 10 mm removed): the helical tone is still suppressed, and a weaker tone appears at St 0.409, probably toroidal from its frequency. Schlieren could not confirm the mode because the two facilities disagreed for this case (p. 434–435).
- **The thin-lip helical loop needs the outer base** (§5.2, p. 440–441):
  - Foam over the inner base: negligible change.
  - Foam over the outer base: the St 0.255 tone is removed and a new, weaker tone appears at St 0.472, "close to the first harmonic" of 0.255 (p. 440–442).
  - Conclusion: for this facility, the gain-maximising loop "involves a feedback loop with an exclusive path" off the outer surface of the nozzle base (p. 441). The authors know of no earlier free or impinging case where the loop is closed exclusively by reflection off an upstream surface (p. 435).
  - They contrast this with reflector studies that cancel or reinforce tones by placing a node or antinode at the lip (Nagel et al. 1983; Raman et al. 1997). The foam and metal surfaces here are not positioned to put a node at the lip, so they read the effect as blocking a path (p. 441).
- **Intermediate lip with foam** (Fig. 16, p. 439–440):
  - The toroidal tone stays but shifts to St 0.398 at lower SPL. Schlieren shows the jet still toroidal, with shock-cell spacing about 0.05D longer. The authors suggest a thinner shear layer, and hence a change in the most unstable frequency (Morris 2010).
  - The helical tone (about 0.255) survives with either foam cover; broadband-to-peak falls by only 1–2 dB.
  - So the intermediate lip's helical loop does not depend only on the base reflection. Its path differs from the thin-lip one (p. 440).
- **Grouping of all nine spectra** (Fig. 18, p. 441–442):
  - Both tones: infinite, intermediate, and intermediate with either foam.
  - Helical tone only: thin, and inner-foam/thin.
  - Toroidal tone only: rec-foam/thin.
  - Outside the groups: inf-foam/thin (no tone, "all possible feedback closure paths being blocked") and outer-foam/thin (St 0.472).
  - The recurring St ≈ 0.25 and ≈ 0.39 "are assumed" to be the base flow's most unstable frequencies (p. 441). The jet is "almost always capable" of finding a loop at one of them (p. 442).
- **Powell's equation** (§5.3, p. 442–445; Table 5):
  - *Source and phase lag:* the source is put at the stand-off shock, where the schlieren shows a periodic shock-leakage event (Manning & Lele 2000) rather than a wall-jet source (Figs 20–21, Movie 1). The schlieren shows no delay between a vortex passing the stand-off shock and the wave appearing, so p = 0 (p. 443).
  - *Paths:* each wave travels at 340 m/s along one of three straight paths: (1) directly to the lip; (2) to the inner base and back to the lip; (3) to the outer base and back to the lip (Fig. 19).
  - *Why paths 2 and 3 can reach the lip:* the acoustic wavelength is comparable to the width of the reflecting surface, so the reflection scatters in many directions. The peak is at the specular angle, but a smaller part goes straight back towards the nozzle, and this is "evidently sufficient" to sustain the loop (p. 443).
  - *Result:* with U_c/U_j = 0.44 or 0.48, each path gives a tone near 0.25 and one near 0.39 for some integer N (N = 4/6, 6/9 and 7/11 for paths 1, 2 and 3) (Table 5, p. 445).
  - *Reflection point:* where the wave reflects matters strongly. Across the outer base (r = 4.9D to 8.5D), path 3 with N = 11 gives St 0.34 to 0.42. The midpoint was used (p. 444).
  - The authors conclude that many paths satisfy eq. 1.1, so the choice must come from the gain criterion (1.2) (p. 444, 446).
- **Models ignore the boundary conditions** (p. 444): bulk-flow tone models (Powell 1988; Gojon et al. 2016; Bogey & Gojon 2017) predict impinging tones well but do not account for the nozzle's boundary conditions.

## Setup / method
- **Facility** (Fig. 2, p. 424–425): the LTRAC gas jet facility, a Shock Lab group facility (the paper does not name a lab space).
  - Perspex cage 42D on a side (21D from the axis in Fig. 2). Compressor-fed air. Plenum stagnation pressure from an RS-461 transducer, ±0.25%. T₀ = 287 K (p. 428).
  - Seeding: ViCount 1300 smoke into the plenum. The entrainment field is seeded by the jet's own flow, helped by the plate. Particles are about 600 nm, with a relaxation time of about 2 µs (Mitchell et al. 2013; Melling 1997), and diffraction-limited images of 18 µm (p. 424).
  - Schlieren was taken in "a different jet facility" with the same characteristics but no smoke generator or cage. Spectra were compared between the facilities for each condition. For the outer-foam/thin and recessed-foam/thin cases they disagreed, so schlieren was not used for those cases (p. 426, 435, 442).
- **Nozzle and covers** (Fig. 3, p. 424–425):
  - Base nozzle: 15 mm converging, lip t = 0.1D. The paper gives no internal contour or contraction ratio.
  - Fig. 3(a) dimensions: the lip is 3.0D above the outer base and 2.6D above the raised inner base (my reading of the dimension lines), and the base is 15D wide.
  - Infinite lip: a 15D-diameter metal cap fitted over the base nozzle, flush with the exit. Intermediate lip: t/D 0.33 with a stepped conical exterior.
  - Foam covers (acoustic dampening foam, never covering the top of the lip):
    - infinite foam and recessed foam (top 10 mm removed), each on the thin and on the intermediate lip;
    - inner foam (wedges on the inner base next to the nozzle) and outer foam (blocks on the outer base), on the thin lip.
- **Plate:** square metal, 17.7D on a side, at z = 5.0D. That is downstream of the third shock cell of the free jet at the same NPR and well before the end of the core (Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2014). The condition was chosen because preliminary schlieren showed fast vortex growth and strong acoustic waves (p. 425).
- **Conditions** (Table 2, p. 426): thin, infinite and intermediate lips all at NPR 3.4, M_j 1.42, z/D 5.0, Re 8.5 × 10⁵. U_E = 310 m/s (isentropic choked exit at T₀ = 287 K, p. 428); D_j = 0.016 m and U_j = 413 m/s "from isentropic relations" (p. 430).
  > Isentropic NPR 3.4 gives M_j = 1.447, and U_j = 413 m/s also implies about 1.45 (my arithmetic; same as thesis-note verify item 1). The Table 2 value of 1.42 looks like a slip.
- **Which diagnostics each case got** (Table 1, p. 426):
  - PIV, acoustics and schlieren: thin, infinite, intermediate and inf-foam/thin.
  - Acoustics and schlieren only: the other five foam cases (schlieren unusable for two of them, see Facility).
- **Acoustics** (p. 426):
  - G.R.A.S. 46BE ¼-inch microphone (20 Hz–100 kHz), aimed at the lip, 30D from the axis in the plane of the lip, outside the PIV test chamber (so outside the cage).
  - NI DAQ, 500k samples at 200 kHz, i.e. 2.5 s. Calibrated with a G.R.A.S. 42AB, p_ref = 20 µPa.
  - Spectra: moving window of 2¹² samples, 50% overlap, stated Δf = 24.4 Hz. Window type not stated in the paper.
  - Non-anechoic, so amplitudes are "primarily qualitative".
  > A 2¹² window at 200 kHz gives Δf = 48.8 Hz; 24.4 Hz corresponds to 2¹³ (my arithmetic; also flagged in the thesis note).
- **Ultra-high-speed schlieren** (p. 426):
  - Toepler Z-type layout, Shimadzu HPV-1 camera (260 × 312 px) at 500 kfps with 0.5 µs exposure.
  - Frames per sequence and knife-edge orientation are not stated (the thesis gives at most 102 frames).
  - Used qualitatively: mode type (cases not shown), and the source at the stand-off shock (Figs 20–21).
- **PIV** (Tables 3–4, p. 426–427): planar and not time-resolved, single-exposed image pairs.
  - Optics: 200 mm Micro-Nikkor with a PK-12 extension ring, magnification 0.47. Light from a dual-cavity Nd:YAG (532 nm, 200 mJ) as a diverging sheet about 1 mm thick. Pulse separation 884 ns stated in the text.
  - Processing: multigrid cross-correlation (Soria 1996), minimum resolvable displacement 0.1 px. Validation by normalised median threshold 3.0 and a dynamic mean test of 1.0 with standard deviation 1.0 (p. 427).
  - Masking: part of the inf-foam field is masked because of bright laser reflections from the foam (p. 436–437).
- **Statistics and POD** (§2.2, §3, p. 427–431): first- and second-order statistics, normalised by U_E, from more than 10,000 fields (thin and infinite lip, p. 428). Snapshot POD (Sirovich 1987) on mean-subtracted u′ and v′ stacked in one column (eq. 2.3).
  - **Pair identification:** a joint PDF of (a_i, a_j)/⟨r_m⟩, normalised by its peak, with the mean-radius (Lissajous) curve overlaid (Oberleithner et al. 2011). A circle means an oscillation with the two modes 90° apart, i.e. a pair. A cardioid or blob means no pair (p. 431).
  - **Mode type:** azimuthal mode from the symmetry of the axial and transverse components about the axis. Helical rather than flapping from the energy argument (p. 431–432).
  - **Data needs:** none of this needs time resolution. Phase is not mapped to time here (no phase averaging in this paper), and the tone frequency comes from the microphone.
- **Powell feedback model** (§1, §5.3, p. 422–423, 442–445): eq. 1.1 with p = 0, a constant U_c (0.44 or 0.48 U_j), U_s = 340 m/s and straight acoustic paths. The effective reflection points are at the middle of each base surface, and the acoustic paths are assumed to run from there to the lip. N is unknown, so only the N closest to St ≈ 0.25 and 0.39 are reported.
- **Modelling and simulation:** none of the paper's own beyond Powell's equation and gain criterion. Cited modelling that bears on the result:
  - Barone & Lele (2005): theory and numerics of compressible-mixing-layer receptivity (η_r) (p. 423).
  - Beneddine, Mettot & Sipp (2015): a global stability analysis of a screeching jet shows the same lip-driven change of azimuthal mode (p. 423).
  - Stegeman et al. (2016): numerical study of coherent structures in underexpanded jets, cited for lip thickness mattering (p. 423).
  - Gojon, Bogey & Marsden (2016) and Bogey & Gojon (2017): LES of impinging jets. They combine the Ho & Nosseir aeroacoustic model with Tam & Ahuja's (1990) neutral-wave analysis to predict tone frequency and whether the mode is axisymmetric or helical. A tone is more likely when upstream waves exist with group and phase velocity near the ambient sound speed (p. 424).
  - Morris (2010): q_h depends on frequency (p. 423, 440).
  - Manning & Lele (2000): shock leakage (p. 424, 443).

## Key equations
Checked against rendered pages 422–423 and 427–428.

- **Powell's feedback (timing) equation** (eq. 1.1, p. 422):
  $$\frac{N+p}{f}=\int_0^{h_c}\frac{\mathrm dh}{u_c}+\frac{h_s}{U_s}$$
  - N: new vortices formed at one azimuthal location per cycle.
  - h_s: straight acoustic path from the source to the lip; U_s: mean wave speed (usually the local sound speed).
  - h_c, u_c: vortex path and its varying convective velocity.
  - p: phase delay between the vortex at the source and the emitted wave (Powell 1988; Weightman et al. 2017b).
  - With p = 0 and constant U_c (as in §5.3): $f=N/(h_c/U_c+h_s/U_s)$.
- **Powell's gain criterion** (eq. 1.2, p. 423): $q_h\,\eta_r\,\eta_p\,\eta_g\ge 1$.
  - q_h: amplification of shear-layer disturbances along h; depends on frequency, not on the acoustic path.
  - η_r, η_p, η_g: efficiencies of receptivity, propagation and generation.
  - The path with the largest product forms the dominant loop "regardless of the feedback path required" (p. 423).
- **Snapshot POD** (eqs. 2.1–2.5, p. 427–428): $\mathbf R\boldsymbol\nu=\lambda\boldsymbol\nu$, $\mathbf R=\mathbf V^{\mathrm T}\mathbf V$, $\mathbf V=[\mathbf u'^1\cdots\mathbf u'^N]$ with each column holding u′ then v′ over the l × m grid.
  - Modes: $\boldsymbol\phi_n=\mathbf V\boldsymbol\nu_n/\lVert\mathbf V\boldsymbol\nu_n\rVert$. Coefficients: $\mathbf a_n=\boldsymbol\nu_n\lVert\mathbf V\boldsymbol\nu_n\rVert$.
  - Eigenvalues are sorted λ₁ > λ₂ > … > λ_N = 0, and their percentages are called "specific kinetic energy".
- **Phase-portrait normalisation** (p. 431): coefficients are divided by $\langle r_m\rangle=\overline{\sqrt{a_i(t)^2+a_j(t)^2}}$, the mean pair radius.
- **Velocity magnitude** (p. 433): $U_{Mag}=\sqrt{U^2+V^2}/U_E$.
- **Convective velocity from a POD wavelength** (p. 432, implied): $U_c=f\lambda$, i.e. $U_c/U_j=St\,\lambda/D_j$.

### Reading it critically
- **"Exclusively" is stronger than the evidence.** Foam on the outer base removes the tone, and foam on the inner base does not (p. 440–441). That shows the outer-base reflection is *necessary* for the thin-lip helical loop. It does not show that the direct path contributes nothing.
  - Under the gain criterion, direct and reflected waves both reach the lip. Losing the reflected part could push the product below 1 even if the direct part is non-zero.
  - Interference is excluded only by where the surfaces sit (no node at the lip; p. 441). The relative phase of direct and reflected waves at the lip is not computed.
  - A cleaner test would vary the base reflection's path length (moving or tilting the outer base). This would map tone amplitude against path phase, as in the reflector tests of [henderson-1993](henderson-1993-tones-choked-jet-impinging-flat-plates.md) (p. 318–319).
- **The outer base is far upstream.** The specular reflection is aimed away from the lip, and only a back-scattered fraction returns (p. 443). That this is enough fits the thesis's later claim that the thin, laminar-like initial shear layer is very receptive (thesis p. 89), but neither paper measures η_r or η_p.
- **Facility dependence.** The conclusion is hedged "for this facility" (p. 441), with good reason:
  - Two foam cases gave different spectra in the uncaged schlieren facility (p. 426, 442).
  - The microphone sits outside a Perspex cage whose wall is 21D from the axis (Fig. 2). The cage is itself a reflecting surface that could feed or weaken a weakened loop (my inference).
  - With the infinite lip, the 15D cap and the 17.7D plate 5D apart also form a parallel-surface gap whose own acoustics are not discussed (my observation).
- **Helical vs flapping from planar data.** One planar PIV plane cannot separate m = ±1 helical from flapping. The helical label rests on one dominant pair against the broad energy spread of a precessing flapping mode (p. 431–432). No azimuthal microphones were used.
- **Coexistence vs switching of the two infinite-lip loops.** The paper says two loops "may be present within the flow" (p. 430). Non-time-resolved POD and averaged spectra cannot say whether they run together or alternate (the thesis note's open question).
  - (My inference) The strong difference tone at 0.144 needs both oscillations at once: it is louder than the 0.241 tone (Fig. 5, read from figure). A quadratic product of an m = 0 and an m = ±1 wave would be m = ±1, which suggests the loops coexist for a large fraction of the time.
  - A bispectrum or BMD of the (0.241, 0.144) → 0.385 triad would confirm it.
- **"Most unstable frequencies" is an assumption** (p. 441, 446).
  - The same St ≈ 0.25 and 0.39 recur across paths whose comb spacings differ (0.034–0.065 in St, my arithmetic above). That suggests something path-independent sets the frequency, which supports the assumption but does not prove it.
  - The tones also lie outside the vortex-sheet guided-mode bands (helical 31–35% below the (1, 1) cut-off, toroidal 9–10% below (0, 2)), which supports external closure. That band check is in [tam-1990](tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md) (my-calculation section, M_j 1.42).
- **The Powell fits are not discriminating** (see Data tables). For the thin lip at its own U_c = 0.48 U_j, path 3 gives 0.244 (−4%), path 2 0.267 (+5%) and path 1 0.275 (+8%) against 0.255. For the infinite lip, where only path 1 is open (p. 443), the toroidal 0.385 fits well (0.390 at N = 6), but the helical 0.241 is 8% off (0.260 at N = 4).
- **Entrainment is rejected only within the PIV field**, as the authors note (p. 439). The infinite-foam cap removes the mode, so the one case that mimics the infinite lip's entrainment cannot test it.

### Agreement and contradiction with other notes
- [ponton-1992](ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) (free jet, t/D 0.015–0.625): proposes the same acoustic-path idea (sound reflected by the larger exit face; p. 548). There, though, thick lips widen the helical C range and revive B, while here a thick lip brings in a toroidal mode (p. 433).
- [henderson-1993](henderson-1993-tones-choked-jet-impinging-flat-plates.md): reflectors placed a set fraction of a wavelength behind the exit suppress or promote impinging tones, so both large- and small-plate loops close at the nozzle (p. 318–319). That is the interference mechanism this paper says it is *not* seeing (p. 441). With a t/d ≈ 0.28 lip, close to the intermediate lip here, Henderson & Powell find the toroidal L1 line alongside helical L2/L3 lines. That fits this paper's intermediate lip, where toroidal and helical loops coexist.
- [bogey-2017](bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md) and [tam-1990](tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md): the guided-mode school. Those LES and models have no nozzle exterior beyond a thin lip (Bogey & Gojon: t = 0.1r₀, outer width 1.25r₀) and work for nearly ideally expanded jets. This paper's point that they "do not account for differences in boundary conditions" (p. 444) is the experimental counterweight. [nogueira-2024](nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) (p. 31–33) and [edgington-mitchell-2025](edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md) (p. 3–4, 8, 14–17) reconcile the two: a source outside the core (stand-off-shock leakage, the shocklet) allows free-stream closure.
- [edgington-mitchell-2025](edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md) reuses the same round nozzles (p. 5). It reports that ray tracing predicts significant leakage for the Weightman et al. (2019) PIV (p. 17–18). It also warns that vortex–wall impact could displace the stand-off shock and mimic leakage in schlieren (p. 18), which weakens the source identification used here (p. 443).
- [edgington-mitchell-2021](edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md): free-jet leakage starts once the saddle point behind a vortex reaches the shock (p. 26). That makes "no time delay ... p = 0" (p. 443) depend on the reference event (see the phase-lag discussion on the impinging-jets topic page).
- [edgington-mitchell-2014-coherent-structure-helical-screech-mode](edgington-mitchell-2014-coherent-structure-helical-screech-mode.md): the free jet at the same NPR 3.4 with a t/D 0.33 lip is helical (C, St 0.30). Here the same lip thickness with a plate at 5D is toroidal-dominant (intermediate lip). So the plate, not just the lip, changes the selection. The thin-lip impinging tone (0.255) is close to the group's free-jet C-mode St 0.26 in [edgington-mitchell-2020](edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) (the coincidence noted on the impinging-jets topic page).
- [ho-1981](ho-1981-dynamics-impinging-jet-feedback-phenomenon.md) claims impinging feedback is "independent of initial conditions". This paper contradicts that for supersonic jets: changing only the exterior changes the mode and can kill the tone.
- [raman-1998](raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) and [edgington-mitchell-2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) (p. 6, 16): upstream reflectors and an upstream plate change free-jet staging and produce multimodality. This is the free-jet counterpart of the boundary-condition effect.
- [powell-1992](powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md): a very thick-lipped (t/D 0.8) free jet whose A1/A2 boundary sits above other rigs'. That note offers it as more evidence that the staging NPR belongs to the rig.
- [jordan-2018](jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md) cites the group's impinging work as evidence that free-stream sound can close impinging loops (p. 2).
- [oberleithner-2011](oberleithner-2011-swirling-jet-vortex-breakdown-stability.md): the source of the phase-portrait pairing method (p. 431).

### Methods and data for the lab workflow (CLAUDE.md)
- **Non-time-resolved PIV:** statistics, then POD on u′ and v′, then phase-portrait pairing and symmetry, then mode type. The tone frequency from a microphone turns the POD wavelength into U_c.
  - This is the NTR route of the workflow. It identifies modes and gives U_c, but no timing, no coexistence-vs-switching information and no cycle-to-cycle variation.
  - The infinite-lip modes 3–4 show the route's limit: a filled-disc portrait paired only by spatial shape (p. 431).
  - Planar symmetric/antisymmetric decomposition about the axis would split m = 0 from m = ±1 content before POD, giving cleaner pairs when two loops coexist (my suggestion).
- **Time-resolved schlieren (HPV-1, 500 kfps):** short records, used only to see the source and the mode. They are too short for SPOD or BMD (thesis note: ≤ 102 frames).
- **Acoustics (2.5 s at 200 kHz):** long enough for time-resolved tone tracking on the existing data (my suggestion).
  - Band-pass each tone of the infinite and intermediate lips (0.241/0.253 and 0.383/0.385). Compare their Hilbert envelopes: correlated envelopes mean coexistence, anti-correlated ones mean switching.
  - Compute the bicoherence of the 0.144 (or 0.13) difference tone with its two parents.
  - Both are cheap first tests of the coexistence question, if the raw records survive.
- **SPOD → reconstruction → Hilbert POD:** with new long-record time-resolved schlieren of the infinite- and intermediate-lip jets:
  1. Run SPOD at each tone, after symmetric/antisymmetric splitting.
  2. Reconstruct each tone band separately ([nekkanti-2021](nekkanti-2021-spod-reconstruction-denoising-frequency-time.md)).
  3. Run HPOD ([raiola-2025](raiola-2025-hilbert-pod.md)) for each loop's instantaneous amplitude and frequency, and look at their joint statistics.

  This would settle coexistence vs switching and show whether the weakened loops (foam cases) are intermittent rather than absent. BMD ([schmidt-2020-bispectral-mode-decomposition](schmidt-2020-bispectral-mode-decomposition.md)) of the difference-tone triad would show where the nonlinear interaction happens.

### Modelling side, PyFR LES and rocket noise
- **The gain criterion is the missing model.** The paper explains selection by eq. 1.2 but never estimates a term. Models that could:
  - Acoustic scattering (BEM or an LEE calculation) of a stand-off-shock source with the real nozzle exterior and plate, giving η_p for each path.
  - A receptivity model of the lip with incidence angle (Barone & Lele 2005), giving η_r.
  - Local or global stability of the measured mean flows, giving q_h at 0.25 vs 0.39 and testing the "most unstable frequency" assumption. Beneddine et al. (2015) already show that lip geometry can switch the mode in a global analysis.
- **PyFR staging LES** (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`): its jets are free, not impinging. Still, this paper and [ponton-1992](ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) show that surfaces upstream of the exit can open or close loops. The LES should model the nozzle exterior, the flange and any upstream rig surfaces as built, and should not put a sponge or outflow zone where a reflecting surface exists in the experiment. Running the same NPR with a changed exterior (thin lip vs flange) is the receptivity test listed on the staging topic page: do the A1/A2 limits move while the frequencies stay fixed?
- **Rocket noise:** launch mounts, the vehicle base and flame-trench walls are the full-scale counterparts of the nozzle base and caps. Foam's effect here (25 → 4 dB broadband-to-peak) shows how strongly absorbing or blocking one path can suppress a tone. This is one candidate reason, alongside masking, why pad tones are absent at full scale ([lubert-2022](lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 16–20). No full-scale test of this exists in the library.
