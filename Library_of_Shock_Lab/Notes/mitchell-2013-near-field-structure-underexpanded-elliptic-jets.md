## Summary
The paper reports two-component planar PIV of an underexpanded jet from a smoothly contoured converging AR 2 elliptical nozzle (20 × 10 mm, equivalent diameter d = 14.14 mm) at NPR 2.2, 2.6, 3.4 and 4.2, measured separately in the major- and minor-axis planes over 0–10d (p. 3, 5). Before the results, it estimates the smoke tracers' response: a relaxation time of 2.0 µs (about 600 nm particles) measured across a round-jet Mach disc, a Stokes number of 0.07, and predicted artificial fluctuations behind the first shock that match the measured excess (p. 4–5). All four jets switch axes, but the near-nozzle shear layers are clearly asymmetric (major-axis layer pinching in, minor-axis layer pushed out) only at NPR 2.2 and 2.6. Shear-layer fluctuations are strongest in the major-axis plane, and mixing is greatest at NPR 2.6 (p. 6, 12). Broad centreline v_rms peaks at the shock spacing and a bimodal centreline v PDF in the minor-axis plane only point to a flapping mode at NPR 2.2–3.4, absent at 4.2 (p. 7–9). A narrow centreline low-speed streak in the major-axis plane from NPR 2.6 is read as the slip-line wake of a resolvable Mach stem (p. 9–11). A pair of outward-angled streaks in the minor-axis plane at NPR 4.2 is read as a Hussain & Husain cut-and-connect vortex bifurcation, previously seen only for AR > 3.5 (p. 11–12). No model or simulation is run; the interpretation leans on published vortex dynamics, characteristics arguments and RANS/schlieren shock studies.

## Key findings
- **Tracer response** (Sect. 2.3, Fig. 3, Table 2, p. 4–5).
  - Melling's relation (eq. 1) with Kn_d ≈ 0.4 gives τ_p = 0.48 µs for the generator's nominal 300 nm, but that size is for free discharge, not injection into a mixing chamber (p. 4).
  - Measured across the Mach disc of a strongly underexpanded **axisymmetric** jet, using the minimum velocity behind the disc as the fluid velocity and the 1/e criterion, **τ_p = 2.0 µs, i.e. a mean diameter of 600 nm**, twice the nominal (p. 4).
  - With τ_f ≈ 29 µs (smallest shock spacing over exit velocity), the Stokes number is ≈ 0.07 (eq. 2, p. 4). The spatial ratio is SR ≈ 0.4 (eq. 3, p. 5).
  - The predicted artificial fluctuation behind the first shock (noiseless cross-correlation model, Soria 2006, Mitchell et al. 2011) rises with NPR, from 1 % to 5 % (monodisperse) or 1 % to 8 % (narrow log-normal), against 2, 2.5, 7 and 10 % measured above the local level (Table 2, p. 5). The authors say the measured values "very closely match" a narrow distribution about 600 nm, and that the predictions are lower bounds (p. 5).
  - Because SR is low, the PDF across the NPR 4.2 normal shock is a single very broad peak (U/U_e ≈ 1.0–1.6 at x/d = 1.3), not a split peak; upstream (x/d = 1.2, U/U_e ≈ 1.8) and downstream (x/d = 1.5, U/U_e ≈ 0.5) it is narrow (Fig. 4, p. 5; values read from the figure).
  - From the round jet at the same NPRs (Mitchell 2013 thesis), the artificial fluctuation behind the NPR 4.2 normal shock extends only about 0.2d and adds 0.093U_e to u_rms; it is weaker and shorter at lower NPR (p. 6). The u_rms spikes at the shocks in Figs 11–13 are this artefact (p. 6–7).
- **Mean flow** (Figs 5–7, Table 3, p. 5–7).
  - Shock spacing and strength increase with NPR, as in round jets. The mean-field boundary shrinks along the major axis in all four jets, "most pronounced" at NPR 4.2, and expands along the minor axis much faster than a round jet at the same NPR (p. 5).
  - Shock trains (Table 3, p. 7): 4 cells, mean spacing 0.6d, ending at x/d = 2.6 (NPR 2.2); 5 cells, 0.9d, 4.7 (2.6); 6 cells, 1.3d, 7.7 (3.4); 1.5d and not ending inside the 10d field (4.2).
  - Mean fields are averages of 1,100 samples (p. 5).
- **Velocity fluctuations** (Figs 8–13, p. 6–9).
  - Major-axis plane: the shear layer pinches inwards at x/d ≈ 3.5 (NPR 2.2) and 4.0 (2.6), with a sharp rise in u_rms (p. 6).
  - Minor-axis plane: the peak-fluctuation line moves rapidly outwards from x/d ≈ 2 until x/d ≈ 3.5–4, about where the major-axis layer pinches (p. 6).
  - At NPR 3.4 and 4.2 the fluctuation fields look like those of a round jet, with symmetric growth into the core and the ambient in both planes (p. 6).
  - **Fluctuations are much stronger in the major-axis plane at every NPR**, read as more mixing there. This agrees with Schadow et al. 1989 (hot wire, shear layer only) but not with Kinzie & McLaughlin's ideally expanded elliptic jet, which had more mass-velocity fluctuation in the minor-axis plane (p. 6).
  - Centreline u_rms peaks at x/d ≈ 6.0 for NPR 2.2 and 2.6 (≈ 0.15 and 0.17U_e, read from Figs 10–11) and is still rising at x/d = 10 for 3.4 and 4.2 (p. 6).
  - Centreline u statistics in the two planes match closely, as they should at the shared axis; this is a consistency check on the two separate acquisitions (p. 7).
- **Flapping in the minor-axis plane** (Figs 9–14, p. 7–9).
  - For all but NPR 4.2, centreline v_rms rises much more steeply in the minor-axis plane (p. 7). Read from the figures: about 0.12U_e by x/d ≈ 2.5 (NPR 2.2), peaks of ≈ 0.19U_e near x/d ≈ 4 (2.6) and x/d ≈ 6.5–7 (3.4), with the major-axis plane lagging by 1–3d.
  - The minor-axis plane shows broad centreline v_rms peaks far too wide to be the shock artefact, with wavelength 0.6d (NPR 2.2) to 1.3d (3.4), equal to the shock spacing and placed just downstream of each shock (p. 7).
  - Centreline v PDFs at NPR 3.4 (x/d = 5.0 and 7.6) are near-Gaussian in the major-axis plane but symmetrically bimodal, with a weaker peak at zero, in the minor-axis plane (Fig. 14, p. 7–10). Side peaks sit near v/U_e ≈ ±0.2 at x/d = 5.0 (read from the figure).
  - As in round jets (Mitchell 2013 thesis), alternating vortices on either side displace the shocks and produce centreline transverse velocity. Peaks in one plane only mean the instability is "two-dimensional": a lateral, flapping mode in the minor-axis plane, i.e. oscillation about the major axis (p. 8–9, 12). In this library's convention this is flapping (se₁).
  - Its strength grows from NPR 2.2 to 3.4. At NPR 4.2 it is absent until x/d ≥ 6.0, and much weaker there (p. 9). Read from Fig. 13, the NPR 4.2 centreline v_rms stays below ≈ 0.1U_e, with the major-axis value equal to or above the minor-axis value.
- **Mach reflection** (Figs 6, 15, p. 9–11).
  - At NPR 2.6, 3.4 and 4.2 a narrow centreline low-speed streak appears downstream of the first shock cell in the major-axis plane only, lasting until the shock train ends. It is the slip-line wake of a Mach stem large enough to resolve (p. 5, 9).
  - Its width never exceeds r/d = 0.12, and its deficit away from the normal shock is about 6 % (Fig. 15, p. 10).
  - At the same resolution, the round jet at the same NPRs showed no such region except at NPR 4.2 (p. 9).
  - Menon & Skews (2010) saw no Mach reflection at NPR 3.8 for an AR 2 ellipse. The authors suggest the contoured (not bevelled) nozzle, or PIV picking up a weak shear layer that shadowgraph misses (p. 9–10).
  - Why only the major-axis plane? An elliptic Mach disc should have its axes switched relative to the exit and so show a larger shear layer in the minor-axis plane. The explanation offered is resolution: the focal depth (≈ 0.1d) is close to the streak width, so out-of-plane averaging erases the small velocity differential in the minor-axis plane (p. 10–11).
  - After an initial narrowing behind the stem, the streak keeps a nearly constant width to the next cell at NPR 3.4. At NPR 4.2 it contracts quickly by mixing (p. 11–12). Possible reasons for the lack of growth are too little time for instability before the next shock, or an unresolved 3-D change in the streak (p. 11).
- **Vortex bifurcation at NPR 4.2** (Figs 16–17, p. 11–12).
  - In the minor-axis plane a pair of low-speed streaks starts just downstream of the first shock cell, angled outwards towards the jet boundary (p. 11).
  - Centreline u_rms is about 0.02U_e before the Mach disc, rises across it (the artefact), then spikes at x/d = 1.8, "the origin of the trajectories". At x/d = 2.1, peaks in u_rms and v_rms sit at r/d = ±0.12, moving out to r/d = 0.2 by x/d = 3.2 (p. 11).
  - The mean axial-velocity profiles have local minima at the inner edge of the supposed vortex rings, moving outwards downstream (p. 12).
  - The trajectories follow the jet boundary: they move out quickly when the boundary expands and stay put when it contracts. A strong u_rms rise where they meet the second shock cell points to vortex–shock interaction (p. 12).
  - These match the hot-wire signatures of Hussain & Husain (1989): a u_rms peak at the cut-and-connect point and outward-moving radial peaks. Until now these had been seen only for AR > 3.5 (p. 11–12).
  - Two possible causes are offered: the greater shear-layer curvature at large pressure mismatch, or a stronger acoustic feedback loop supplying the excitation that Hussain & Husain found necessary. The question is left open (p. 12).
  - The conclusion suggests that the bifurcation may suppress the flapping seen at lower NPR (p. 12).

## Setup / method
- **Facility** (Fig. 1, p. 2). A continuous high-pressure air supply. Seeded and unseeded air mix in a chamber, then pass through a straight pipe into a plenum with screens and honeycombs. Stagnation pressure is measured in the plenum with an RS-461 transducer (0–10 bar, ±0.25 %). The temperature is not stated.
- **Nozzle** (Fig. 2, p. 3). A smooth transition from a circular section to an AR 2 ellipse over 70 mm, with major and minor exit diameters of 20 and 10 mm. The contour converges to the exit (Fig. 2), so M_d = 1. The internal length varies with azimuth, so the exit momentum thickness θ_e does too. Mild steel, hand polished, Ra ≈ 1.6 µm by tactile comparison. Fig. 2 shows a 25 mm outer diameter at the exit and a 72.5 mm inlet height label ("725" in the text layer). It is the same nozzle as [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) and [Ivelja 2024](ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md).
- **Coordinates and scales** (p. 3). Cylindrical (x, r, θ), with θ = 0 the major axis and θ = 90° the minor axis. Only u and v are measured. d = 2(ab)^½ = 14.14 mm (Hussain & Husain 1989), which needs a and b to be the semi-axes (10 and 5 mm). Re = ρUd/μ = 4 × 10⁵ to 8 × 10⁵ over the four NPRs. Velocities are scaled by the exit velocity U_e (≈ 313 m s⁻¹ for a sonic exit at T₀ = 293 K, my estimate).
- **Conditions.** NPR 2.2, 2.6, 3.4, 4.2. The paper gives neither M_j nor U_j. My isentropic M_j values are 1.12, 1.25, 1.45 and 1.59.
- **PIV** (p. 3, Table 1).
  - Two PCO 4000 cameras (4008 × 2760 px) with 105 mm Micro Nikkor lenses at f/5.6; New Wave Solo Nd:YAG (532 nm, 120 mJ). The light sheet is 1.5 mm thick at the measurement point. The combined field is 2d radially by 10d axially.
  - Resolution 23 µm px⁻¹. The particle images are diffraction limited at 1.2 px mean diameter, and the velocity histograms show little peak locking (p. 3).
  - Multigrid cross-correlation (MCCDPIV, Soria 1996), with a 64 px initial and 16 px final window, 50 % window overlap and 8 px grid. Each camera's field is interpolated onto a global grid with an adaptive Gaussian convolution window (Agui & Jimenez). In the 7.5 % overlap between cameras, the window width is set from each field's vector spacing (p. 3).
  - Error estimates are quoted from other work rather than measured: 0.04 px on synthetic images with 16² px multigrid windows (Scarano 2002), and 0.32 px at 95 % confidence for 64² px initial windows on real images (Soria 1996) (p. 3–4).
  - Seeding: a Vicount 1300 ethylene-glycol smoke generator (nominal 300 nm) seeds both the jet and the ambient (p. 4).
  - **Not time-resolved.** The acquisition rate is not given, and the 1,100 samples per case are treated as independent ensemble members (p. 5). Δt is not stated.
- **Analysis chain and data requirements.** Everything here works on non-time-resolved data.
  - Mean and RMS velocity fields in each plane (Figs 5–9), and centreline and radial profiles (Figs 10–13, 15–17).
  - Single-point velocity PDFs, used to diagnose artefacts (Fig. 4) and as an oscillation detector (Fig. 14).
  - Shock-cell counting from the mean field (Table 3).
  - A particle-response model: Melling's τ_p, the experimental τ_p from a round-jet Mach disc, the Stokes number and spatial ratio, and the predicted artificial fluctuation for monodisperse and log-normal size distributions, using the noiseless cross-correlation function (Soria 2006) adapted to shocks (Mitchell et al. 2011). The pre-shock Mach number in Table 2 comes from the measured pre-shock velocity through the isentropic relations (p. 5).
  - No POD, spectra, two-point correlation, phase information or acoustics. The later POD-pair analyses of the same database are [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) (NPR 2.2) and [Ivelja 2024](ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) (NPR 2.6, 3.4).
- **Modelling side.** No model is solved. The interpretation draws on:
  - Biot–Savart self-induction of elliptic vortex rings for the first axis switch, interacting vortex structures (corner hairpins in square jets) for later switches, and Hussain & Husain's cut-and-connect bifurcation for AR > 3.5 (p. 1).
  - Schadow et al. 1989 (AR 3): an integral-equation linear stability analysis gives a higher phase velocity for the dominant mode on the major-axis side. In their experiments the axis switch moves from x/d_e = 23 (subsonic) to 20 (M = 1) to 3 (underexpanded), and minor-axis spreading doubles when underexpanded (p. 2).
  - The Courant–Friedrichs argument: the incident shock forms where reflected characteristics coalesce on the curved boundary. Menon & Skews (2010, shadowgraph and RANS) used it to explain why the stronger major-axis curvature forms the incident shock on the major axis first, at lower NPR, and why an elliptic Mach disc with switched axes forms at high NPR (p. 2).
  - In a round jet, regular reflection at the axis is impossible; some Mach stem must form, though it is often below resolution at low NPR (p. 2).

## Key equations
All checked against the rendered pages (p. 3–5).

- **Equivalent diameter** (p. 3): $d = 2(ab)^{1/2} = 14.14$ mm, with a = 10 mm and b = 5 mm (semi-axes).
- **Reynolds number** (p. 3): $Re = \rho U d/\mu$.
- **Velocity lag and relaxation time** (p. 4): $|\hat V| = |\hat U_p - \hat U_f|$. τ_p is the time after a step change for $|\hat V|$ to fall by $1/e = 0.368$.
- **Melling's relaxation time** (eq. 1, p. 4): $\tau_p = \dfrac{\rho_p d_p^2}{18\mu}\,(1 + 2.7\,Kn_d)$, with Kn_d ≈ 0.4 behind the normal shock.
- **Stokes number** (eq. 2, p. 4): $S_t = \dfrac{\tau_p}{\tau_f} \approx 0.07$, with τ_f ≈ 29 µs. (My check: 2.0/29 = 0.069; 0.6d/U_e = 8.5 mm/313 m s⁻¹ = 27 µs.)
- **Spatial ratio** (eq. 3, p. 5): printed as $SR = \dfrac{\xi}{IW} \approx 0.4$, with ξ the tracer relaxation distance and IW the interrogation window.

> **Check eq. 3.** The text says it reproduces Ragni et al.'s definition, which is SR = WS/ξ_p, window over relaxation length ([Ragni 2011](ragni-2011-particle-tracer-response-shocks-piv.md)). Its reasoning ("due to the high magnification … despite the relatively rapid response … the spatial ratio is quite low") also fits WS/ξ. With IW₁ = 16 px = 0.37 mm and ξ = τ_pU ≈ 0.6–1.0 mm (U = 300–500 m s⁻¹), IW/ξ ≈ 0.4–0.6, while ξ/IW ≈ 1.6–2.7. The printed form gives 0.4 only with the 64 px initial window and ξ ≈ 0.6 mm. The printed fraction is probably inverted (my check).

- **Figs 10–13 (p. 8–9):** centreline U, u_rms and v_rms in both planes for each NPR. This is the key flapping evidence: the minor-axis v_rms rises earlier and oscillates at the shock spacing for NPR 2.2–3.4, but not at 4.2.
- **Fig. 14 (p. 10):** centreline v PDFs at NPR 3.4, x/d = 5.0 and 7.6. Gaussian in the major-axis plane, bimodal in the minor-axis plane.
- **Fig. 15 (p. 10):** radial U profiles at x/d = 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 4.0 for NPR 2.6, 3.4, 4.2 in both planes. The centreline Mach-stem streak in the major-axis plane. At NPR 4.2 the deficit at x/d = 1.5 is wider in the minor-axis plane (my reading), as an elliptic Mach disc with switched axes would give.
- **Figs 16–17 (p. 11):** NPR 4.2 minor-axis mean U and u_rms with profile stations, and the radial U, u_rms, v_rms profiles across the proposed vortex-bifurcation region.

## Notation
- d: equivalent diameter 2(ab)^½ = 14.14 mm (D_e in this library's CSVs); a, b: semi-axes; x, r, θ: axial, radial, azimuthal (θ = 0 major axis, 90° minor axis); "major-axis plane" is the plane containing the major axis.
- u, v: axial and radial velocity; U, V: means; u_rms, v_rms (u′_rms, v′_rms): RMS fluctuations; U_e: nozzle-exit velocity; M₁: Mach number before the first shock.
- NPR: nozzle pressure ratio; AR = a/b; Re = ρUd/μ; θ_e: exit momentum thickness.
- τ_p: particle relaxation time; τ_f: fluid time scale; S_t: Stokes number (not a Strouhal number here); ρ_p, d_p: particle density and diameter; μ: viscosity; Kn_d: particle Knudsen number; Û_p, Û_f: particle and fluid velocity; ξ: relaxation distance; IW, IW₀, IW₁: interrogation window (initial, final); SR: spatial ratio.

## Relevance to this project
This is the database paper behind the group's AR 2 elliptical-jet thread. The same nozzle and PIV were used for POD at NPR 2.2 in [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), and for POD pairs, k_x filtering and Floquet LSA at NPR 2.6 and 3.4 in [Ivelja 2024](ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md). The staging of this ellipse is treated in [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md). It is also the original source of the group's smoke-tracer numbers (τ_p = 2.0 µs, artificial-fluctuation table), reused in the round-jet PIV papers. It sits in the CLAUDE.md research area (non-axisymmetric underexpanded jets, PIV, the "statistics first" step) but stops before modal decomposition.

**How it fits and what has changed since**
- **Axis switching against NPR.** The paper gives no switch location, only that the mean-field contraction of the major-axis boundary is strongest at NPR 4.2 (p. 5). The shear-layer asymmetry and the mixing peak at the low NPRs instead (NPR 2.6, then 2.2; p. 6, 12). Two different processes may be mixed in the word "switching" (my reading):
  - At high NPR the mean boundary is shaped mainly by the elliptic expansion and shock system. Schadow's underexpanded switch at x/d_e ≈ 3 (cited p. 2) and Menon & Skews' switched Mach disc are gas-dynamic.
  - At low NPR it is driven by the shear-layer dynamics that flapping strengthens, as in screeching rectangular jets ([Krothapalli 1986](krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md); [Alkislar 2003](alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md)).
  - The pinch at x/d ≈ 3.5 (NPR 2.2) and 4.0 (2.6) sits just upstream of the half-width crossing at 4.3d ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), NPR 2.2) and the fitted AR = 1 point near 5d ([Ivelja 2024](ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), NPR 2.6). This is consistent with the switch drifting downstream with NPR, though all three use different metrics.
- **Flapping, first evidence in this nozzle.** The minor-axis-only centreline v_rms peaks at the shock spacing, and the bimodal v PDF (p. 7–10), are the same shock-displacement signature that Edgington-Mitchell 2015 later used ("no such peaks in the major-axis plane, so uniaxial flapping"). The POD pairs confirm flapping on this database: 15 % of transverse energy at NPR 2.2, 23.6 % at 2.6 and 35 % at 3.4 (those notes). That matches the growth from NPR 2.2 to 3.4 reported here (p. 9).
- **What happens at NPR 4.2 is open.** The paper attributes the loss of flapping to the vortex bifurcation (p. 12), but there were no acoustics. Two other readings fit the library (both my reading):
  - The same ellipse stages several times in 2 ≤ NPR ≤ 5 ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 18).
  - A different AR 2 ellipse adds quasi-helical and second-flapping branches above NPR ≈ 3.4–3.8 ([Mazharmanesh 2025](mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md); [Mazharmanesh 2026](mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md)).
  - So NPR 4.2 may simply be a different stage or symmetry. The centreline v_rms at NPR 4.2 is weak and slightly larger in the major-axis plane (Fig. 13), which would fit a wagging or helical-like component better than flapping. Time-resolved schlieren SPOD in both views at NPR 4.2 would settle it.
- **"Vortex bifurcation" or Mach-disc slip surface?** The NPR 4.2 minor-axis pair of outward-angled low-speed streaks, with u_rms and v_rms peaks along them, starts just downstream of the first cell. That is where the Mach disc sits (x/d ≈ 1.3, Figs 4, 13).
  - At x/d = 1.5 the velocity deficit behind the disc is wider in the minor-axis plane (Fig. 15, my reading), as Menon & Skews' switched elliptic Mach disc predicts. The paper itself expected a larger slip-line shear layer in the minor-axis plane (p. 10).
  - A planar cut through the annular slip surface behind a Mach disc gives exactly two streaks either side of the axis that diverge with the expanding cell. The round-jet PIV at the same NPR shows this as an inner shear layer lasting 4–5 cells ([Edgington-Mitchell 2014, Mach disk](edgington-mitchell-2014-underexpanded-jet-mach-disk-shear-layer.md)). The paper's own observation, that the trajectories move out when the boundary expands and stay put when it contracts (p. 12), is what streamline-following slip lines would do.
  - So the streak pair may be a shock-structure feature rather than a cut-and-connect event (my reading). Time-averaged planar PIV cannot tell them apart. Cross-plane (r–θ) PIV at x/d ≈ 2–3, or volumetric data, would: an elliptic annular slip surface against two discrete near-circular vortex rings.
- **Mach reflection appears at lower NPR than in round jets.** A resolvable Mach stem appears from NPR 2.6 in the ellipse, against only NPR 4.2 in the round jet at the same resolution (p. 9). [Ivelja 2024](ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) see the same "low-velocity streak at z = 0" in the major-axis mean flow of this database. Linear shock-cell models that miss the first-cell Mach reflection even in round jets ([Nogueira 2022, PFE](nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 8–9, 16–17, as summarised on the shock-cell-models page) will be further off for elliptic jets near the nozzle. The measured mean spacings (0.6–1.5d) run about 11 % below a round-jet Pack estimate on d (17 % at NPR 4.2; my arithmetic), the direction Tam's minor-dimension scaling would give ([Tam 1988](tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md)).
- **Which plane mixes more.** The paper finds stronger u_rms in the major-axis plane at every NPR (p. 6), although the flapping is in the minor-axis plane. One possible extra contribution (my reading): out-of-plane flapping sweeps the thin ends of the ellipse through the major-axis light sheet, adding apparent axial fluctuation there. The paper's "more mixing on the major axis" should be read with that caveat; [Suzuki 2026](suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md) show that the major-axis "contraction" is a shift of the shear layer, not a thinning.

**Methods and data for the lab workflow (CLAUDE.md)**
- **Non-time-resolved PIV (all of this paper).** Mean, RMS and single-point PDFs are the "basic statistics" step. Two transferable tricks:
  - *Centreline transverse-velocity statistics per plane as a symmetry test.* Peaks at the shock spacing in one plane mean flapping; in both planes, helical; none, axisymmetric. The test needs no phase information, but only the oscillation that displaces the shocks is detected.
  - *Single-point PDFs as a cheap oscillation and intermittency detector.* A pure sinusoid of amplitude A gives an arcsine PDF with peaks at ±A, so the ±0.2U_e side peaks at NPR 3.4, x/d = 5.0, roughly measure the shock-displacement velocity amplitude (my reading). The "weaker peak at zero" (p. 7–10) is not what a sinusoid plus Gaussian noise gives (that has a dip). It suggests a mixture of snapshots with and without strong flapping: possible intermittency or a second state, which non-time-resolved data cannot order in time (my reading).
  - The obvious next step (POD pairs of v, phase portraits, k_x filtering) was taken in Edgington-Mitchell 2015 and Ivelja 2024. Their caveat that POD of planar velocity is not a compressible energy norm applies.
- **Time-resolved data.** Nothing here resolves frequency or time. Three follow-ups for the SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD pipeline:
  - Whether the zero-centred PDF mode is intermittent flapping: SPOD of TR schlieren in the minor-axis view, reconstruct the flapping tone band, Hilbert POD for its instantaneous amplitude. The amplitude PDF should be bimodal if flapping switches on and off.
  - Which mode replaces flapping at NPR 4.2: SPOD in both views, as in Mazharmanesh 2025.
  - Whether the NPR 4.2 streak pair is coherent and periodic (vortices) or steady (slip surface): SPOD energy at the tone along the streaks.
- **PIV practice.** The τ_p = 2.0 µs (600 nm) smoke number, Stokes number and Table 2 are where the group's later PIV error statements come from.
  - On the flow scale the Stokes number is 0.07 (p. 4). At this database's tones it is 2πfτ_p ≈ 0.16, 0.13 and 0.10 at NPR 2.2, 2.6 and 3.4 (my arithmetic, using St 0.52, 0.38 and 0.27 from the related notes, T₀ ≈ 293 K). That is at or above the usual 0.1 guideline, so coherent velocity amplitudes are slightly attenuated.
  - Shock artefacts are confined to ≈ 0.2d behind each shock (p. 6). Keep PIV-derived k_x spectra, POD and validation targets clear of those bands, or model and subtract them with Table 2.
  - Thin structures narrower than the depth of field (≈ 0.1–0.17d) can vanish from one plane by out-of-plane averaging (p. 10). Planar PIV of 3-D shock systems is plane-dependent in a way the mean field alone does not reveal.

**Modelling side and the PyFR staging LES**
- No model is solved here, but the dataset is a validation and base-flow target. Ivelja's RANS mean flow of this nozzle did not reproduce axis switching or the fast NPR 2.6 centreline decay (that note). LES of the same nozzle at NPR 2.6–4.2 could test three things here: the plane-dependent Mach-stem streak, the NPR 4.2 streak pair (cross-plane slices), and whether flapping disappears at 4.2.
- For the round-jet PyFR staging LES (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`):
  - *Mach-stem resolution.* At the A1/A2 NPRs the group's round-jet PIV cannot resolve a Mach stem (only NPR 4.2 could, p. 9), so near-axis LES–PIV differences after the first cell there are not evidence of a modelling error.
  - *Centreline v′ per plane.* This separates the m = 0 stages A1/A2 (no centreline v′) from the |m| = 1 B and C stages, and flapping (B) from helical (C), in 3-D LES slices as in PIV.
  - *PIV shock spikes.* When comparing with the group's round-jet PIV, ignore u_rms spikes within ≈ 0.2D behind shocks, since they are tracer artefacts.

**Rocket noise.** Launch-vehicle nozzles are round and screech is absent at full scale. The only link is qualitative: non-circular exits change near-field shock structure (earlier Mach reflection) and mixing.
