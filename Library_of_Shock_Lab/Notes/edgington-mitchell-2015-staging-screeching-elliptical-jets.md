## Summary
The paper maps screech staging of the group's AR 2 converging elliptical nozzle (D_e = 14.14 mm) from NPR 1.95 to 4.3 with a single microphone, and uses non-time-resolved high-resolution schlieren at five NPRs (2.0, 2.6, 3.2, 3.4, 4.2) to identify each stage's shape (p. 1008–1010). The dominant tone falls into five stages: a varicose mode I at St ≈ 1, flapping modes II, III and IV about the major-axis plane, and a mode V that two-point correlations suggest is helical (p. 1009–1017). Modes II and IV lie on one St trendline and III and V on another (p. 1010). Three length scales are measured from the images: the shock spacing, the vortex spacing from two-point correlations, and the spacing of near-field lobes of intensity fluctuation, which Panda's relation identifies as the hydrodynamic–acoustic standing wave (p. 1017–1020). Shock and standing-wave spacings agree within 5 % in modes II and IV but differ by about 15 % in mode III, where a Powell-type prediction from the standing-wave spacing beats the one from the shock spacing (Tables 3–4, p. 1020–1021). The authors read the II → III jump as a switch from the "weakest-link" (free acoustic) feedback to the acoustic-waveguide feedback of Shen & Tam, and conclude that a feedback mechanism need not fix the mode shape (p. 1020–1022). No model is solved; the modelling content is the Powell and Panda frequency formulae and appeals to linear stability (Morris & Bhat 1995; Morris 2010) and to numerical work on the two feedback paths. In hindsight the length-scale mismatch is the suboptimal-shock-peak (k_s2) signature later found in this same dataset by [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) (p. 18).

## Key findings
- **Five stages from the dominant tone** (Fig. 2, p. 1009–1010). Microphone in the minor-axis plane at β = 20° and z/D = 23; St = f D_j/U_j with D_j and U_j the ideally expanded equivalent diameter and velocity (p. 1009). Values read from a 300 dpi render (±0.01–0.02 in St); 48 points at ΔNPR = 0.05:
  - **Mode I** (NPR 1.95–2.05, 3 points): St 1.03, 0.96, 0.88. "At very low levels of underexpansion … St ≈ 1" (p. 1009).
  - **I → II** "at NPR = 2.05" (p. 1009): in the figure 2.05 is still mode I (St 0.88) and the first mode-II point is NPR 2.10 (St 0.57), so the jump lies between 2.05 and 2.10 (my reading). The text says the tone drops to St ≈ 0.6.
  - **Mode II** (2.10–3.10, 21 points): smooth fall from St 0.57 to 0.30; St ≈ 0.51 at NPR 2.2 (the companion paper's 0.52, [Edgington-Mitchell 2015, multimodal](edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) p. 2743) and 0.38 at 2.6.
  - **II → III at NPR 3.15**, an increase in St (p. 1009). **Mode III** (3.15–3.35, 5 points): St 0.36 → 0.34 (0.35 at 3.2).
  - **III → IV at NPR 3.4**, a drop (p. 1009). **Mode IV** (3.40–3.90, 11 points): St 0.27 → 0.24.
  - **IV → V at NPR 3.95**, an increase (p. 1009). **Mode V** (3.95–4.30, 8 points): St 0.28 → 0.26.
  - Jump sizes between adjacent points (my arithmetic): I → II ×0.65, II → III ×1.20, III → IV ×0.80, IV → V ×1.17. With T₀ = 293 K assumed (not stated), the tones are about 20.5 → 13.5 kHz, 8.1 → 9.8 kHz, 9.4 → 7.5 kHz and 6.7 → 7.9 kHz across the four jumps (my isentropic estimates).
  - Only the fundamental of the dominant tone is plotted; no spectra, secondary tones, sweep direction or repeat runs are shown, so coexistence and hysteresis cannot be judged from the paper.
- **Two trendlines, possibly three modes** (p. 1010). Modes II and IV "appear to lie along a shared trendline, likewise regions III and V", as the round-jet D mode is often taken as a continuation of B; the data "might only represent three distinct modes" (p. 1010).
- **Mean shock structure** (Fig. 3, 1000-image means of ∂ρ/∂y, p. 1010–1011). At NPR 2.0 a series of weak shock cells. As NPR rises a normal shock grows on the axis with a slip line around it. The normal shock appears at much lower NPR than in an equivalent round jet and is ellipsoidal rather than a circular disc; slip lines from the triple point extend much farther downstream than in a round jet (citing [Edgington-Mitchell 2014, Mach disk](edgington-mitchell-2014-underexpanded-jet-mach-disk-shear-layer.md)). The disc widens and the shock spacing grows with NPR, with "no other visible macroscopic changes" (p. 1010). This agrees with the PIV Mach-stem streak from NPR 2.6 in [Mitchell 2013](mitchell-2013-near-field-structure-underexpanded-elliptic-jets.md) (p. 9).
- **Instantaneous images** (Fig. 4, p. 1010–1011). NPR 2.0: symmetric KH vortices in the minor-axis plane, harder to resolve in the major-axis plane. NPR 2.6, 3.2 and 3.4: an asymmetric vortex pattern in the minor-axis plane and no visible coherent structure in the major-axis plane. NPR 4.2: no coherent structure discernible by eye in either plane.
- **Standard deviation and near-field lobes** (Fig. 5, ∂ρ/∂x, p. 1011). Faster shear-layer spreading in the minor-axis plane. Lobes of higher intensity fluctuation outside the shear layer at NPR 2.6, 3.2 and 3.4 are not co-located with the shock cells (p. 1011, 1018); these become the standing-wave measurement.
- **Mode shapes from two-point correlation** (eqs. 4–5, Figs 6–10, p. 1012–1017). Reference point X chosen in the shear layer to give "a strong global correlation", from 2000 images per case (p. 1014). My reading of the figures puts X near the lip line at x/D ≈ 0.7–1.6.
  - **NPR 2.0 (I):** strong symmetric correlations in both planes, "indicative of a varicose instability mode". The correlated regions differ in spanwise size between planes, suggesting an initially elliptical ring vortex that becomes more axisymmetric downstream; no coherence beyond x/D ≈ 5.0 (p. 1014–1015). Correlation extends outside the shear layer into the acoustic field, strongest upstream, "in accordance with the directionality of the screech phenomenon" (p. 1015).
  - **NPR 2.6 (II):** strongly antisymmetric in the minor-axis plane, no strong correlation in the major-axis plane: flapping about the major axis, again correlated with upstream-going waves near the nozzle. I → II is "the transition from symmetric to flapping instability predicted by linear stability theory" (Morris & Bhat 1995; p. 1015).
  - **NPR 3.2 (III):** again antisymmetric in the minor-axis plane only, so still flapping, but the correlated regions curve downstream with increasing radius. "Due to the qualitative nature of schlieren" no more is inferred; II → III "appears to be a switch between two different flapping instabilities" (p. 1015–1016).
  - **NPR 3.4 (IV):** flapping, without the curvature, resembling NPR 2.6, which supports mode IV as a continuation of II; the spanwise extent of the correlation is "greatly increased" (p. 1016).
  - **NPR 4.2 (V):** "very weak antisymmetric patterns" in both planes (|R| ≲ 0.3 by my reading of Fig. 10), read as a helical or multi-axial mode. The authors suggest that strong underexpansion deforms the jet from its elliptical shape so fast that "the flapping mode never develops" (p. 1017, citing the companion AIAA J. paper).
- **Vortex spacing** λ_L from the distance between correlation peaks (Table 1, p. 1017–1018): 0.8D, 1.6D, 2.1D, 2.6D and 3.1D at NPR 2.0, 2.6, 3.2, 3.4 and 4.2. With the measured tones, the convected-wave relation St = (U_c/U_j)(D_j/λ_L) gives U_c/U_j ≈ 0.77, 0.59, 0.70, 0.66 and 0.72 (my arithmetic, isentropic D_j).
- **Standing waves** (Table 2, Fig. 11, p. 1018–1020). Lobe spacing λ_SW from peak-to-peak distances on an axial profile of the intensity standard deviation outside the shear layer (p. 1018); the radial position of the profile is not given. Compared with Panda's relation (eq. 2) using Table 1's λ_L and λ_A = a∞/f at laboratory conditions: measured / calculated 0.97/0.95 (NPR 2.6), 1.14/1.11 (3.2), 1.37/1.39 (3.4) (p. 1019). The authors call this "very close agreement", supporting the lobes as the standing wave.
  - Fig. 11 (NPR 3.4, minor-axis plane): with X placed in or between the near-field lobes (my reading: r/D ≈ 1.2, x/D ≈ 1.6, 2.5, 3.0, 3.7), correlations outside the shear layer are much stronger when X sits on a lobe, and the spacing of the correlation peaks equals that found with X in the shear layer, so the λ_L estimate does not depend on X (p. 1019–1020).
- **Characteristic length scales** (Table 3, p. 1020), as printed: λ_S/D, λ_SW/D, λ_L/D = 0.93, 0.98, 1.6 (NPR 2.6); 1.14, 1.30, 2.1 (3.2); 1.37, 1.37, 2.6 (3.4). Shock and standing-wave spacings "match to within 5 %" at NPR 2.6 and 3.4; at 3.2 there is a "disparity of 15 %" (p. 1020–1021).
  > **The NPR 3.2 entries of Table 3 appear to be swapped** (my check; verify against the PDF). Table 2 gives a *measured* λ_SW = 1.14D at NPR 3.2, the same number as Table 3's λ_S. Table 4 gives St 0.34 from the standing wave and 0.30 from the shock spacing, which needs λ_SW < λ_S. My reading of the centreline shock crossings in Figs 3 and 5 gives ≈ 1.33D over the first four cells at NPR 3.2 (≈ 1.34D at 3.4, where the table says 1.37). And measured/Prandtl–Pack spacing is 0.92, 0.94 at NPR 2.6, 3.4 but 0.84 with 1.14D at 3.2 against 0.96 with 1.30D. So the consistent set is **λ_S ≈ 1.30D, λ_SW ≈ 1.14D at NPR 3.2**, and the rest of this note uses it where marked.
- **Frequency predictions** (Table 4, eqs. 1 and 3, p. 1021): measured / Powell with λ_S / Panda form with λ_SW = 0.38 / 0.41 / 0.40 (NPR 2.6, mode II); 0.35 / 0.30 / 0.34 (3.2, III); 0.27 / 0.28 / 0.28 (3.4, IV). The two agree at 2.6 and 3.4, but "the standing wavelength based model performs significantly better at NPR = 3.2" (p. 1021). U_c and M_c used are not stated.
  - My reconstruction: U_c = 0.64–0.65U_j (the value measured at NPR 2.2 in the companion paper, p. 2744), M_c = U_c/a∞ with T₀ = T∞, St on the isentropic D_j, and λ_S = 1.30D, λ_SW = 1.14D at NPR 3.2 reproduce all six predicted values to ±0.01 (0.41–0.42, 0.39–0.40; 0.30, 0.34; 0.28, 0.28). With Table 3 as printed at NPR 3.2 the two predicted columns would be interchanged.
- **Staging mechanisms, the authors' reading** (Sect. 4, p. 1020–1022):
  - I → II: varicose dominates in subsonic and low-supersonic jets; as M_j rises the helical modes (and the flapping modes formed by superposing helices of opposite sign) outgrow it (Morris 2010), driving the switch (p. 1020).
  - II → III: no change of instability geometry, and linear stability theory "does not suggest the existence of multiple flapping modes about the same axis". Since the instability is forced by screech, the two feedback paths of [Shen & Tam 2002](shen-2002-three-dimensional-numerical-simulation-jet-screech.md) are offered instead: the classical "weakest-link" path tied to the shock spacing, and the waveguide path tied to the standing-wave spacing (citing Singh & Chatterjee 2007) (p. 1020).
  - Where λ_S and λ_SW match (modes II and IV) the classical weakest link is taken to dominate, as for A1 and B in round jets (Chatterjee et al. 2009), so II and IV are "lateral flapping modes driven by the weakest link feedback process" (p. 1020). Mode III, with two length scales, allows both mechanisms; only one dominant tone appears, and the jump implies the waveguide mechanism governs at NPR 3.2 (p. 1021).
  - II → III matches the round-jet B → C transition in relative frequency change, but the elliptical mode stays locked to the major axis instead of becoming a single spinning helix (p. 1021).
  - Mode V continues III on the trendline but is helical by Fig. 10. As the boundary deforms away from an ellipse, modes not locked to an axis become possible, and higher underexpansion does this closer to the nozzle, so NPR 4.2 is helical while 3.2 flaps "despite ostensibly being part of the 'same' mode" (p. 1021–1022). Hence "the change in length scales and feedback mechanism are not necessarily dependent on a change in the geometrical nature of the screech instability" (p. 1022).
- **Conclusion** (p. 1022): five stages; low NPR varicose with short hydrodynamic wavelength; then flapping about the major axis; a discontinuous jump with flapping retained, associated with a mismatch between shock spacing and standing wavelength, i.e. the same change of feedback mechanism as in round jets; at higher NPR the waveguide feedback produces a helical mode, attributed to rapid distortion of the jet boundary. Feedback mechanisms may have "preferred" shapes but "do not require a particular instability geometry".

## Setup / method
- **Facility** (Fig. 1, p. 1008). Continuous high-pressure air; air passes a mixing chamber and a straight pipe into a plenum with screens and honeycombs. Stagnation pressure from an RS-461 transducer in the plenum (0–10 bar, ±0.25 %). Stagnation temperature not stated (the companion paper says ambient, p. 2740–2741).
- **Nozzle** (Fig. 1, p. 1008–1009). Smooth transition from a circular section (72.5 mm inlet label) to an AR 2 ellipse over 70 mm, by azimuthal interpolation between the major- and minor-axis contours; exit 20 × 10 mm; Ø25 mm outer face. Purely converging (M_d = 1). Internal length varies with azimuth, so the exit boundary-layer thickness does too. Mild steel, hand polished, Ra ≈ 1.6 µm by tactile comparison. Same nozzle as [Mitchell 2013](mitchell-2013-near-field-structure-underexpanded-elliptic-jets.md), [Edgington-Mitchell 2015, multimodal](edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) and [Ivelja 2024](ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md); not the thin-lip D_e = 10 mm ellipse of [Mazharmanesh 2025](mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md). The lip is thick and azimuthally varying (about 2.5–7.5 mm from the Ø25 mm face, as derived in the companion note).
- **Coordinates and scales** (p. 1009). Cylindrical, θ = 0 on the major axis and 90° on the minor axis. D = 2(ab)^½ = 14.14 mm (Hussain & Husain 1989), the area-equivalent diameter when a, b are semi-axes. St = f D_j/U_j with D_j, U_j ideally expanded (p. 1009); NPR = p₀/p_a.
- **Conditions.** Acoustic sweep NPR 1.95–4.3 in steps of 0.05 (Fig. 2); schlieren at NPR 2.0, 2.6, 3.2, 3.4, 4.2. M_j, U_j, T₀ and tone frequencies in Hz are not given. My isentropic values: M_j 1.05, 1.25, 1.40, 1.45, 1.59 and D_j/D 1.001, 1.024, 1.057, 1.069, 1.115 at the five imaging NPRs (table below).
- **Schlieren** (p. 1009). Toepler Z-type, 2032 mm focal-length mirrors. PCO DiMax, 2016 × 2016 px, 1.6 µs pulsed-LED illumination ([Willert, Mitchell & Soria 2012](willert-2012-high-power-led-high-frame-rate-schlieren.md)). Razor-blade cut-off: parallel to the axis for ∂ρ/∂y (used instead of ∂ρ/∂r because it does not change sign at the centreline), perpendicular for ∂ρ/∂x. Both planes imaged (major- and minor-axis views). **Not time-resolved**: the sampling rate is "low enough that the samples are statistically independent" (p. 1013); the rate is not stated. 1000-image means (p. 1010) and 2000-image correlation ensembles (p. 1014).
- **Acoustics** (p. 1009). One G.R.A.S. Type 46BE 1/4" pre-amplified microphone (4 Hz–80 kHz), 12-bit NI DAQ at 250 kHz, 250,000 samples (1 s), PSD with a sliding 4096-point Hanning window (Δf ≈ 61 Hz, ΔSt ≈ 0.002; overlap not stated). Position (Fig. 2 caption): minor-axis plane, β = 20°, z/D = 23. With the introduction's convention (β = 180° upstream, p. 1006), this is 20° from the downstream axis, as in [Ivelja 2024](ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md); z is not defined. The room is "not … anechoic".
- **Analysis chain and data requirements.** All image analysis works on independent snapshots.
  - Mean and standard-deviation schlieren (Figs 3, 5): any schlieren.
  - Two-point correlation of intensity fluctuations (eqs. 4–5), zero time lag, X chosen by hand in the shear layer (p. 1013–1014). Symmetric or antisymmetric patterns in each of two separately acquired views classify the mode; the planes cannot be correlated with each other.
  - Vortex spacing λ_L: distance between correlation peaks (p. 1017).
  - Standing-wave spacing λ_SW: peak-to-peak spacing of an axial profile of intensity standard deviation outside the shear layer (p. 1018).
  - Shock spacing λ_S: method, plane and number of cells not stated (Table 3).
  - Tone St: microphone PSD (time-resolved signal), dominant peak only.
- **Modelling side.** No model is solved.
  - Powell's formula (eq. 1) with the shock spacing and the "alternative" Panda form (eq. 3) with the standing-wave spacing predict St (Table 4). Neither U_c nor M_c is stated; see the reconstruction above.
  - The mechanism argument rests on literature: Shen & Tam 2002's 3-D simulation, in which free-stream acoustic feedback predicted A1 and B but not A2 and C, and Tam & Hu 1989's neutral acoustic waves guided by the jet ([Tam 1989](tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md)) as the waveguide path (p. 1007); numerical simulations tying the classical path to the shock spacing and the waveguide path to the standing-wave spacing, with A1 dominant when the two match (Singh & Chatterjee 2007; Chatterjee, Ghodake & Singh 2009; p. 1007, 1020).
  - Linear stability of elliptic jets (Morris & Bhat 1995; Morris 2010) for the varicose → flapping switch (p. 1015, 1020). No base flow, dispersion relation or growth rate is computed.

## Key equations
All checked against the rendered pages (p. 1006, 1009, 1012–1013).

- **Equivalent diameter** (p. 1009): $D = 2(ab)^{1/2} = 14.14$ mm.
- **Strouhal number** (p. 1009): $St = f D_j/U_j$, with D_j, U_j the ideally expanded equivalent diameter and velocity; $NPR = p_0/p_a$.
- **Powell's frequency formula** (eq. 1, p. 1006): $f = \dfrac{U_c}{s\,(1 + M_c)}$, s the shock spacing, U_c the convection velocity, M_c the convective Mach number.
- **Panda's standing-wave relation** (eq. 2, p. 1006): $\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{SW}} = \dfrac{1}{\lambda_A} + \dfrac{1}{\lambda_L}$, with λ_A the screech acoustic wavelength and λ_L the hydrodynamic wavelength.
- **Standing-wave frequency formula** (eq. 3, p. 1006): $f_s = \dfrac{U_c}{\lambda_{sw}\,(1 + M_c)}$.
  > With λ_L = U_c/f and λ_A = a∞/f, eq. 2 gives 1/λ_SW = f(1 + M_c)/U_c for M_c = U_c/a∞, which is eq. 3. So eq. 3 is eq. 2 rearranged (an identity, as [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) p. 3 notes for Panda's formula). Its "prediction" in Table 4 only checks that the measured lobes are the standing wave at the tone frequency, given the assumed U_c; the informative column is the shock-spacing one (my reading).
- **Two-point correlation of schlieren intensity** (eq. 4, p. 1012): $R(I')(\mathbf X;\delta\mathbf X,t;\delta t)=\dfrac{\overline{I'(\mathbf X,t)\,I'(\mathbf X+\delta\mathbf X,t+\delta t)}}{\sigma_{I'(\mathbf X,t)}\,\sigma_{I'(\mathbf X+\delta\mathbf X,t+\delta t)}}$.
- **Zero-lag form for independent snapshots** (eq. 5, p. 1013): $R(I')(\mathbf X;\delta\mathbf X)=\dfrac{\overline{I'(\mathbf X)\,I'(\mathbf X+\delta\mathbf X)}}{\sigma_{I'(\mathbf X)}\,\sigma_{I'(\mathbf X+\delta\mathbf X)}}$, with $\mathbf X = (x, r, \theta)$.
- **Convected-wave check** (my form, not in the paper): $St = (U_c/U_j)(D_j/\lambda_L)$.

## Notation
- D: equivalent nozzle diameter 2(ab)^½ = 14.14 mm (D_e in this library's CSVs); a, b: semi-axes; D_j, U_j: ideally expanded equivalent diameter and velocity; x, r, θ: axial, radial, azimuthal (θ = 0 major axis, 90° minor axis); β: microphone angle (180° upstream); z: microphone distance (undefined in the paper).
- NPR = p₀/p_a; St = f D_j/U_j; f, f_s: screech frequency; U_c, M_c: convection velocity and convective Mach number; s, λ_S: shock spacing; λ_SW (λ_sw): standing-wave spacing; λ_A: acoustic wavelength; λ_L: hydrodynamic (vortex) spacing.
- I, I′: schlieren intensity and its fluctuation; σ: standard deviation; R(I′): correlation coefficient; X, δX, δt: reference point, separation, lag.
- Modes I–V: this paper's stage labels; "flapping": oscillation about the major-axis plane, seen in the minor-axis view (se₁ in the Mathieu naming of [non-axisymmetric-jets](../Topics/non-axisymmetric-jets.md)).

## Relevance to this project
This is the group's staging map of its AR 2 elliptical nozzle, the dataset that [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) later reanalysed (Fig. 14, p. 18–19) and the parent of [Ivelja 2024](ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) (NPR 2.6 and 3.4 are modes II and IV here). It fits the CLAUDE.md research area directly: resonance and staging in a non-axisymmetric jet, from schlieren and acoustics with the "statistics first" workflow. Its length-scale argument is the closest experimental precedent in the library for a stage jump with no change of symmetry, the elliptical analogue of the round-jet A1 → A2 switch that the PyFR staging LES targets.

**How it fits and what has changed since**
- **The mechanism label is superseded; the length-scale observation survives.** The paper explains II → III as classical (free acoustic) feedback giving way to the waveguide path of [Shen & Tam 2002](shen-2002-three-dimensional-numerical-simulation-jet-screech.md). Later work found that A1 and A2 both close on the same guided jet mode ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md); [Gojon 2018](gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md)), so the path does not change at staging. What changes is the shock wavenumber the KH–shock triad uses: k_s1 in A1 and B, a suboptimal k_s2 in A2 and C ([Nogueira 2022](nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md); [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md)). Edgington-Mitchell 2022 placed this jet's G-JM at k_kh − k_s1 at NPR 2.6 and near k_kh − k_s2 at 3.2 (p. 18).
  - On that reading λ_SW = 2π/(k_KH + |k_up|) is the wavelength of the shock component the loop uses. With the corrected Table 3, k_sw/k_s1 = 1.30/1.14 = 1.14 at NPR 3.2, against 0.95 and 1.00 at 2.6 and 3.4 (my arithmetic). That is the same signature as Panda's near-field crests at 1.17–1.24 times the mean shock wavenumber in his A2 and C jets ([Panda 1999](panda-1999-experimental-investigation-screech-noise-generation.md), via that note) and Mercier's 1.28 at A2 ([Mercier 2017](mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md), p. 214), slightly smaller than k_s2/k_s1 ≈ 1.26 in Mazharmanesh's ellipse ([Mazharmanesh 2026](mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 20–21) and below the ×1.20 frequency jump.
  - Table 2 itself argues against a distinct waveguide wave: Panda's relation with the *free-stream* λ_A reproduces the measured lobes within 2–3 % in all three modes, including III (p. 1019). The near-field data cannot separate a free acoustic return from a G-JM near its branch point, which travels at about a∞; Ivelja's LSA G-JM wavelengths (2.28D, 3.07D against λ_A ≈ 2.46D, 3.26D) imply a phase speed of about 0.93a∞ (my arithmetic).
  - Eq. 3 is an identity given eq. 2 (Key equations), so the Table 4 success of the standing-wave column is guaranteed once the lobes are the standing wave; the result is that Powell's formula with the mean shock spacing fails by 14 % in mode III and works within 8 % in II and IV.
- **Varicose-to-flapping at NPR ≈ 2.05–2.10, not absent.** The staging topic page contrasts a varicose → flapping jump at NPR ≈ 2.2 in Mazharmanesh's thin-lip ellipse ([Mazharmanesh 2025](mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 7) with this nozzle "already flapping at NPR 2.2 with no varicose tone". This paper shows the same nozzle does have a varicose mode I at St 1.03–0.88 up to NPR 2.05, switching to flapping between 2.05 and 2.10 (Fig. 2). The difference is a switch about 0.1 NPR earlier, not a missing stage. Both ellipses give St ≈ 1 for the varicose tone at NPR 2.0 (0.96 here; 1.03 there, on D_e) and St ≈ 0.4 for flapping at 2.6 (0.38; 0.42).
- **A bounded k_s2 window.** Mode III occupies only NPR 3.15–3.35 and the tone then returns to the II/IV trendline with λ_S = λ_SW (k_s1). In round jets the k_s2 stages (A2, C) do not revert to the k_s1 branch. In Mazharmanesh's ellipse a single flapping loop on k_s1 (NPR 2.3–3.3) gives way to multimodal loops on k_s2 from NPR 3.4, with a quasi-helical tone dominating from 4.1 ([Mazharmanesh 2026](mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 21–22). The onset NPRs nearly coincide (3.15 here, 3.4 there), but the return to k_s1 here does not (my comparison; different nozzles and lips). Whether mode III coexists with a weaker II/IV loop is unknown, since only the dominant tone is plotted.
- **Mode V is the weakest assignment.** It is called helical from weak (|R| ≲ 0.3) antisymmetric correlations in two separately acquired views (Fig. 10). As in the companion paper, two independent planes cannot test the π/2 phase lock of a helix; the same pattern fits alternating flapping and wagging, a precessing flapping plane, or several loops at once. Mitchell 2013 found flapping absent at NPR 4.2 until x/d ≥ 6, with major-axis centreline v_rms equal to or above the minor-axis value ([Mitchell 2013](mitchell-2013-near-field-structure-underexpanded-elliptic-jets.md), p. 9), consistent with "not flapping" but not proof of a helix.
  - The waveguide label for V rests only on the III/V trendline (p. 1021–1022); no λ_S or λ_SW is given at NPR 4.2. My numbers point the other way: eq. 2 with λ_L = 3.1D gives λ_SW ≈ 1.52–1.60D, against ≈ 1.64D read from Fig. 5e, so k_sw/k_s ≈ 1.03–1.08, closer to the II/IV match than to III. IV → V may instead be a change of azimuthal family on a similar shock peak, one of the routes to new tones in Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 1, 17). This is my reading of figure-read numbers.
  - The parallel with the quasi-helical tones of another ellipse (St 0.28 at NPR 3.6, [Mazharmanesh 2025](mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) p. 16; dominant above NPR 4.1 in Mazharmanesh 2026) is suggestive; their multi-view and BMD methods are what would test mode V.
- **The "flow deformation allows helical modes" idea** (p. 1017, 1021) is the same proposal as the companion paper's downstream helical-like pair at NPR 2.2, now invoked at the nozzle for high NPR. The symmetry-breaking view ([Rodríguez 2023](rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md); [Nogueira 2026](nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md)) gives it a mechanism where the cross-section passes through AR ≈ 1, but [Suzuki 2026](suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md) shows that a switched mean flow can host a separate SA-family mode born at the switch, which would also look antisymmetric in both views. The axis-switching thread on [axis-switching-and-mean-flow](../Topics/axis-switching-and-mean-flow.md) carries this question.
- **The I → II explanation** (helical growth overtaking varicose with M_j; p. 1020) is the elliptic version of [Tam 1995](tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md)'s toroidal-to-helical selection and, like it, does not explain the later jumps; the authors say so for II → III (p. 1020).
- **Lip and rig.** This nozzle's thick, azimuthally varying lip and the unrecorded upstream geometry may move the staging NPRs relative to the thin-lip ellipse, as lip thickness moves round-jet stages ([Ponton 1992](ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md)). The Monash rig's upstream plate was blamed for unusual multimodality in round jets (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 6, 16); whether it was present here is not stated.
- **Two-point correlation as a mode classifier.** Same tool as [Knast 2018](knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) and [Johnston 2016](johnston-2016-underexpanded-jet-intrusive-tab-visualisation.md). Fig. 11 adds a useful practice point: X placed on a near-field standing-wave lobe gives strong far-reaching correlation, X between lobes almost none, but the derived spacing does not change (p. 1019–1020).

**Methods and data for the lab workflow (CLAUDE.md)**
- **Non-time-resolved schlieren (all imaging here).** Mean and standard-deviation images, zero-lag two-point correlation, correlation-peak spacing and std-lobe spacing all work on independent snapshots, and give three length scales (λ_S, λ_L, λ_SW) per NPR. Combined with a microphone tone, Panda's relation checks the standing wave and the convected-wave relation gives U_c, all without time resolution. Limitations: hand-picked X; the planes are acquired separately; shock spacing method unstated; λ_SW is read from one radial line.
- **Time-resolved data (microphone only here).** Every staging question the paper leaves open needs time resolution:
  - SPOD of TR schlieren at each NPR, with the KH and upstream parts split by the sign of k_x, gives k_KH + |k_up| directly; compare it with the k_s1, k_s2 peaks of the mean-image shock spectrum (the Edgington-Mitchell 2022 recipe; Mazharmanesh 2026's BMD k_S does the same per loop). This would replace the hand-measured λ_SW and λ_S and test the swapped NPR 3.2 values.
  - SPOD → reconstruction → Hilbert POD of the mode II/IV band and the mode III band across NPR 3.05–3.45: anti-correlated amplitudes would show exclusive switching (as in Mazharmanesh 2026); simultaneous ones, coexistence. The same for IV/V near NPR 3.9–4.0.
  - For mode V, simultaneous two-view TR schlieren (or nozzle rotation as in Mazharmanesh 2025) with a phase lag of ±π/2 between views would identify a helix; a lag of 0 or π, or random sign changes of the lag in HPOD phase, would point to flapping/wagging alternation or a precessing plane.
  - The acoustics should be repeated with spectra, secondary tones, up and down sweeps and finer NPR steps around 2.05, 3.15, 3.4 and 3.95.

**Modelling side and the PyFR staging LES**
- No model is solved, and the paper predates guided-mode and suboptimal-shock-peak modelling. A Floquet LSA or weakest-link model on fitted mean flows, as [Ivelja 2024](ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) did at NPR 2.6 and 3.4 and [Nogueira 2025](nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md) did for a rectangular jet, run at NPR 3.2 with k_s2 and at 4.2 for the ce₁/se₁ and helical-like families, would test both the III and V assignments.
- For the round-jet PyFR staging continuation (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`):
  - *A cheap state label.* Measure the near-field r.m.s.-lobe spacing λ_SW and the mean shock spacing in each converged state. λ_SW/λ_S ≈ 1 should mark the k_s1 (A1) state and ≈ 0.8–0.88 the k_s2 (A2) state, as here across II → III; the jump should appear at the switch and be independent of which loop is visible acoustically.
  - *Stage jump without symmetry change.* Mode II → III is a ×1.20 tone jump at fixed (flapping) symmetry, the non-round counterpart of A1 → A2 (m = 0 both sides). Its short k_s2 window (ΔNPR ≈ 0.2, then back to k_s1) is a second, two-sided staging target if an elliptical LES is ever run.
  - *Check U_c.* The convected-wave U_c/U_j from λ_L spans 0.59–0.77 across stages (my arithmetic), so a fixed U_c in a Powell- or Tam-type check will mislabel a stage by up to ±10 % in St.

**Rocket noise.** Screech is absent at full scale ([Lubert 2022](lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md)). The link is only through the elliptic Mach-disc and shock-structure observations (p. 1010), relevant to non-circular exhausts qualitatively.
