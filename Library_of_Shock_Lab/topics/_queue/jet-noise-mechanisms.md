## jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise (queued 2026-10-22)

[Jordan & Colonius 2013](../notes/jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise.md) is the founding review of the wavepacket view of mixing noise, and the page currently cites only its successors ([Cavalieri et al. 2013](../notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), [Cavalieri et al. 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md)). It fixes the cold-jet far-field facts the wavepacket model must explain: peak at 20°–40° from the downstream axis at St ≈ 0.2 with steep decay, broadening to St ≈ 0.4 at 90°, OASPL ∝ U_j^n with n = 8 at 90° and n > 8 at shallow angles, almost all St < 1 sound in m = 0–2, and bursts at St ≈ 0.2 (p. 176). The m ≤ 2 result comes from the Michalke & Fuchs radial-compactness Bessel factor (p. 176), the same argument Cavalieri 2013 uses for why velocity energy at m ≈ 4–11 is silent. The mechanism is acoustic matching (Fig. 5, p. 177): a homogeneous subsonic wave is silent, envelope modulation leaks sound to φ = arccos(αa∞/ω), superdirective for m = 0, and supersonic phase speed gives Mach waves; Cavalieri 2019 later gave the closed form. Energetically the coherent part is small (≈ 15% of low-frequency fluctuation energy in unforced jets against ≈ 50% when forced, p. 180) but efficient through coherence and intermittency (p. 181, 189), and loud far-field events coincide with wavepacket breakdown near the end of the potential core (Hileman et al. 2005, M_j 1.28; p. 186). It adds to the open question **"Two sources or one jittering wavepacket?"**: it explicitly rejects the two-source reading of [Tam et al. 1996](../notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), because spectral shape evolves smoothly with angle and aft and sideline peaks span similar bands (p. 181), while conceding the wavepacket share of sideline noise "remains to be quantified" (p. 189) — so it states the one-source side but does not settle it. Control evidence (p. 188–189) fits [Samimy et al. 2006](../notes/samimy-2006-plasma-actuators-high-speed-jet-flow-noise-control.md): higher-m, higher-St forcing reduces noise; forcing at LSA-stable frequencies thickens the mean flow and lowers m = 0 gain, at unstable ones adds coherent Reynolds stress and noise (Koenig 2011). Scope caveat for the page: cold/low-supersonic round jets only, no BBSAN, screech or rocket conditions; for supersonic phase speeds it says calibrated PSE gives the low-frequency aft directivity (p. 185), supporting the rocket Mach-wave point.

## wong-2019-coherence-decay-wavepacket-bbsan (queued 2026-10-22)

- This is the group's first wavepacket BBSAN paper. It extends Cavalieri & Agarwal's (2014) jittering line source to a Gaussian wavepacket × Prandtl–Pack shock-cell source at NPR 3.4 (M_j 1.45), with Gaussian coherence decay of length L_c = 1.0D (p. 973–977).
- Unlike in subsonic jets, coherence decay lowers the BBSAN peak by about 2–5 dB and broadens the directivity (Fig. 2, p. 978).
- The double wavenumber transform shows why. The difference wave k_h − k_s (A⁻) is the only acoustically matched lobe and already lies inside the radiation square, so jitter smears energy out of it. The sum wave and the mixed (B) terms are silent (p. 981–983). This confirms Tam & Tanna's k − λ₁ ([tam-1982](../notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md)).
- With ten Pack modes, the coherent model gives narrow secondary peaks and dips at high St and upstream angles. Coherence decay pulls the n = 2 lobe into the radiation square and fills the "missing sound" of Ray & Lele (2007) and Suzuki (2016) (p. 984–987). The authors argue the deficit comes from missing higher shock modes, not from a failure of linear theory (p. 980).
- It is the model-problem precursor of [wong-2021](../notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md). Both agree that coherence is not needed for peak level (2021: coherent PSE within ±2 dB/St) and that jitter fills the gaps between shock-mode lobes (2021: up to 15 dB/St).
- They differ on the secondary peaks. Here they are called "artificial" (p. 984, 987). In 2021, and in [tam-1986](../notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), the n = 2 peak is real. The note reads "artificial" as too narrow and too deep. The Norum & Seiner comparison data (p. 979) are of unstated spectral resolution and screech state, and the model peaks were matched to them, so only spectral shape was tested.
- A unifying reading from the note, derived and to be flagged as ours: Cavalieri et al. 2019's closed form applied to the k_h − k_s wave gives a peak drop of 10 log₁₀(L_m/L), i.e. −3.9, −2.9 and −1.8 dB at St 0.3, 0.4 and 0.6, which matches Fig. 2. Jitter then attenuates acoustically matched difference waves and amplifies subsonic ones (n = 1 below St ≈ 0.22, n = 2 below St ≈ 0.51). This turns the page's existing bullet "k − k_s components can be subsonic even in a supersonic jet" into a demonstrated mechanism.
- It also supports the page's reading that the long streamwise coherence needed by far-field-fitted statistical models belongs to the wavepacket: the paper argues that only low-order wavepacket coherence, not bulk turbulence statistics, matters (p. 972, 987). This bears on the open question "Statistical or wavepacket sources?".
- It adds to the open question "What fills the BBSAN dips?": jitter can fill them without shock unsteadiness, at least in the model.
