## alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv (queued 2026-10-21)

The paper's central claim is about **amplitude**: screech intensity is set "largely" by coherent vorticity strength together with shock-cell strength (p. 153). It belongs in *Amplitude and receptivity*, beside Raman's growth × interaction × efficiency × receptivity budget.
- **Evidence.** The circulation of R_uu-defined coherent structures (R_uu > 0.6, phase 135°) peaks at the ends of the 3rd and 5th shock cells, highest at the 3rd, where Fig. 1 shows the wave being born. Read from Fig. 18: Γ_A/(hU_j) ≈ 0.65 at x/h 9–10 at M_j 1.69, against ≤ 0.35 over 6–18h at M_j 1.74 (p. 142–144).
- **Weakness.** The evidence is one phase and two conditions. By the Fig. 8 fit M_j 1.74 is only about 1–3 dB quieter (my reading), and its circulation is higher near the nozzle.
- **Source location.** The result supports the 3rd-cell source of krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet (p. 123). It parallels edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet (coherent vorticity peaking just before the 3rd shock, p. 2747). edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage (p. 26–30) refines it: leakage happens at the saddle between vortices, and vortex strength decides which cell leaks first. So the paper locates where leakage can start, not the emission point.
- **Harmonic.** At the sideline (R = 25h, 90°) the first harmonic (≈132 dB) slightly exceeds the fundamental (≈129 dB) (Fig. 6, read). This fits Powell's antisymmetric directivity (Raman 1998, p. 49) and contradicts Krothapalli 1986's "fundamental dominates at 90°" (p. 125) for an AR 16.7 convergent jet.

The paper changes no consensus item. It gives a measured, though weak, flow-side handle on the amplitude open question, and its circulation diagnostic is cheap to repeat on POD-phased PIV or LES.

## edgington-mitchell-2014-coherent-structure-helical-screech-mode (queued 2026-10-21)

This paper is the origin of the group's NPR 3.4 (M_j 1.45, St 0.30) C-mode PIV dataset. [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) and [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) re-analyse it. Its own source argument is statistical:
- u′ excess kurtosis peaks at the shock–boundary reflection points, strongest at the 3rd (≈ 7 against ≈ 1.5 at the 1st, read from Fig. 14), with strong negative skewness.
- Coherent vorticity fluctuation is strongest on the high-speed side over cells 2–4, peaking near the 3rd reflection point (x/D ≈ 4.35).
- Coherent energy keeps growing to the 6th cell (x/D ≈ 7.5–8) (p. 837, 840–844).

The authors conclude that the sources sit where vortices most weaken the vorticity at the sonic line (Suzuki & Lele leakage), not at the wavepacket peak (p. 843–845). At M_j 1.45 this agrees with:
- the 2021 ray-traced first leak at the 3rd cell, x/D ≈ 4.3 (same data; leakage note p. 19);
- Raman's 3rd shock ([Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) p. 71–73);
- [Mercier 2017](../notes/mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md)'s 3rd shock tip (p. 221, a flapping case).

It contradicts [Tam 1986](../notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md) (p. 4). The selection rule itself was revised in 2021: it holds at NPR 3.4 but fails at NPR 2.25, where both incidence angle and vorticity matter and leaks occur at saddles (leakage note, p. 26–28). Two further points for the page:
- The "single helix, not double helix" conclusion from centreline shock motion (p. 838) excludes m = 2 but cannot exclude flapping. [Edgington-Mitchell 2023](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2023-screeching-jet-seen-from-all-sides.md) (p. 3–4) calls it unproven until Léon et al. 2022, and [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) shows how |m| = 1 can lock into flapping.
- The shock-cell modulation of coherent axial stress (peaks just before each reflection point, p. 836) has the same wavelength as the KH–G-JM interference later found in this dataset (2π/k_s either way). It does not discriminate between the "shock modulation" and "interference" pictures (waves note, p. 20–23).

It does not change the current understanding. It is the earliest group evidence for the "source is not at the wavepacket peak" claim, and it can be cited for C-mode source location in the "Shock leakage and source location" paragraph.

## nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles (queued 2026-10-21)

- **Closure (claims 2–3).** In this sharp-throat C-D AR 2 rectangular jet, the k_x spectrum of the PIV flapping POD mode has a subsonic upstream peak near k_KH − k_sh1 (p. 4218, Fig. 14; about 0.85c∞ at NPR 4.5 by the note's reading). So the dominant flapping loop closes through the G-JM on the first shock peak. This test resolves the resonant structure itself, which makes it stronger than the band-membership evidence of Gojon 2019 for rectangular jets.
- **Claim 8 (far field ≠ loop).** Only the flapping tone reaches the far field; the varicose, second-flapping and wagging loops do not (p. 4217, 4220). This supports claim 8 in a new sense: several loops, one audible.
- **Screech at design.** The nozzle still screeches at its design NPR 3.7 (weaker, p. 4214), as Esfahani found for the OSU nozzle.

## panda-1999-experimental-investigation-screech-noise-generation (queued 2026-10-21)

Panda 1999 is the classic phase-resolved study of the acoustic side of the loop and the historical source of the near-field standing wave. Narrowband r.m.s. maps at f_s show crests 20 dB above troughs along the jet boundary (p. 79), sitting between shock tips (p. 81), with crest spacing L_p = 0.67D and 1.02D. These equal the hydrodynamic–acoustic standing wavelength L_sw from 1/L_sw = 1/λ_s + 1/λ_h (0.66D, 1.03D), not the shock spacing (0.77D, 1.28D) (Table 1, p. 81). Phase-averaged near-field pressure with phase-matched spark schlieren puts sound inception between the 3rd and 4th shocks (2.37 < x/D < 3.07) at M_j 1.19: the boundary of a passing hydrodynamic compression or rarefaction bulges radially, splits, and the outer part travels upstream in a "pause-and-go" motion, gaining strength from same-sign hydrodynamic regions ("multiple sources", p. 89–93). This agrees with the 4th-shock A2 source of [Mercier 2017](../notes/mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) (Table 1, p. 204, lists Panda as A2), with the 4th-cell A2 leakage at inter-vortex saddles in [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) (p. 23–27), and with the G-JM peak between the 3rd and 4th reflections at NPR 2.25 ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) p. 6). Panda's bulge-and-split is the near-field pressure picture of the leakage event, seen without k < 0 filtering. He treats the upstream wave as free-stream sound and cannot separate it from the outer G-JM. The later picture of several standing waves (KH + G-JM at the lipline, KH + sound further out; [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) p. 9–11) supersedes his single pair, and his traverse starts at r/D 0.6–0.7, where both apply. The note argues that his "vortex speed modulation at L_sw" inside the shear layer (p. 84–86) is most simply the same interference in the line-of-sight phase. Fitting the M_c swing gives an upstream-to-KH ratio of about 0.25–0.35, close to the 1:5 of Edgington-Mitchell 2018 (Fig. 5, p. 8). [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) (p. 20–23) reached the same conclusion for PIV. Two further points fit existing sections. The 2f_s harmonic is flow-generated, with beams at 90° and about 120° (p. 83), consistent with Farghadan 2026 (p. 20–23); this belongs in "Triads, harmonics". The 305 mm flange is the main reflector and changes both amplitude and frequency (p. 73), which belongs in "Amplitude and receptivity" alongside Raman 1998 (p. 77–78). The paper does not change the current understanding. It adds the 3rd–4th shock source location for A2, the origin of the standing-wave observable, and a "Methods" caution: Panda says same-frequency hydrodynamic and acoustic parts cannot be separated (p. 89), which k_x splitting now does. It also has an internal inconsistency to note: hydrodynamic fluctuations "an order of magnitude" larger than acoustic (p. 81) cannot give a 20 dB crest–trough contrast (p. 79), which needs an amplitude ratio of about 0.8.

## suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech (queued 2026-10-21)

- **The theory behind the group's leakage work.** Suzuki & Lele supply the theory that the group's leakage papers build on. A weak shock obeys the same eikonal as high-frequency sound. A steady layer always totally reflects it, because the critical angle θ₁^cr = arccos[−1/(a₂/a₁ + M₁)] lies below the Mach angle for any temperature ratio (p. 145–147, eq. 2.23).
- **Vortices let it leak at the saddles.** Leakage happens near the saddle points between vortices (2-D DNS base flow, M₁ = 1.2; Figs 11–13, p. 153–155). There local vorticity, which rotates the wavefront normal back into the jet at ω/2, is weak. The strain term matters as much: it turns the leaking rays outward by −60° to −97° (Table 3, p. 156).
- **Front shape and amplitude.** Eikonal fronts agree well with DNS fronts. The leaking shock kinks at the reflection point into a compression branch followed by an expansion branch, and the fronts spread nearly circularly over wide angles, upstream included (p. 158–161). Amplitude agrees only qualitatively (p. 163–164).
- **Source location.** The paper explains 2nd–4th-cell sources (Walker & Thomas 1997; Panda 1999) qualitatively (p. 164). Vortices must first develop, and further downstream small eddies destroy the clear saddles. The leaking-ray count rises from 4–8 at the first saddle to 9–12 at the second (p. 155). This is consistent with Mercier 2017 (4th tip), Raman 1998 (3rd cell) and edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage (3rd–5th cell, leakage growing with vortex strength).
- **Directivity: tension with the group's claim.** A single 2-D leakage event radiates mainly sideline. The integrated intensity points about 74–84° from the downstream axis (Fig. 27, read from figure), and amplitude falls towards zero beyond roughly 120–130° (Figs 24–26). That is against the claim in edgington-mitchell-2021 (p. 21–23) that one source already radiates mostly upstream along Norum's 160°, which rests on ray streaks without amplitude.
- **What this does to the "Directivity" open question.** It gives the "superposition of many sources" side a computed single-source pattern to beat. Suzuki & Lele themselves say superposition changes the pattern "considerably" (p. 163–164). Their DNS is one planar layer, measured in the near field (R ≈ 14 δ_V).
- **"Leakage as closure".** The paper does not address closure. Its totally reflected rays satisfy the same Snell's-law condition as the guided-jet-mode band of nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves (p. 15–17), which supports the topic page's reading that one shock–vortex event can feed both the G-JM and the radiated tone.
- **Heating (relevant to "Full scale").** Heating makes leakage easier and tilts the sound downstream, but the total amplitude falls (p. 163). Turbulence scatters thin fronts into broadband shock noise (p. 164), consistent with screech being absent at full scale.
- **Housekeeping for the topic page.** The note resolves the angle-convention question flagged in edgington-mitchell-2014-coherent-structure-helical-screech-mode (see "Cross-note items" below).

## gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets (queued 2026-10-22)

Gojon, Bogey & Mihaescu give the LES-and-vortex-sheet counterpart to [Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), from the same year; [Mancinelli 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) credits both with identifying guided-mode closure. In LES of an underexpanded M_j 1.56 jet (NPR 4.03, 2 mm pipe, Re_j 6×10⁴), n = 0 and n = 1 frequency–wavenumber spectra of density inside the jet peak at the tones on k ≈ −ω/a₀, at the lower limits of the S2 and H1 vortex-sheet modes. Their radial profiles resemble the eigenfunctions, which the authors call the first observation of these modes in a non-ideally expanded jet (p. 2921–2923). The paper notes that tones sit near the lower limits, where the guided wave moves at a₀ in both phase and group velocity, which is why free-stream-sound loop models work about as well as they do (p. 2919–2920). This fits the Mercier ±10 % return-speed caveat on the page. The evidence is weaker than claimed, by the note's reading. At a lower limit the guided mode's wavenumber equals the sound line's (−2.27 vs −2.25), so the f–k peak cannot separate guided mode from in-jet sound (EM 2018's caveat, p. 10). The profiles stop at r/D_j 0.45, so the outer evanescent decay is untested. The 4.5D_j line gives Δk D_j ≈ 1.4, comparable to the wavenumber spread the authors attribute to scattering by turbulence and shocks. The paper also finds energy along positive-group-velocity (duct-like, Towne k⁺) branches at negative k (p. 2921): an early sighting of trapped waves in a screeching jet, and support for the page's statement that negative k_x is not upstream group velocity (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 13). It supports point 2 of Current understanding (G-JM closure) without changing it. It adds a second, independent data set (helical C at M_j 1.56) to the A1/A2 PIV evidence.

## powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets (queued 2026-10-22)

The paper makes B and D one sinuous family, joined by a secondary tone (Fig. 19, p. 2833–2834), yet with different dynamics. B has a preferred plane (≈ 60° from the fixed microphone), spasmodic flips and a noise-like envelope at constant frequency. D's plane rotates unsteadily (dwell, vibration, sustained rotation from ≈ 13 to ≈ 90 Hz) and almost 100 % modulates the tone at a fixed point (Figs 10, 12–16, p. 2829–2832). C is very stable, either sense with about equal probability, and keeps its sense while running but may change it on restart (p. 2829). These are the experimental facts behind nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets (streaks fix and precess the flapping axis). Powell's linear superposition argument (sinuous = two counter-rotating helices) is the degenerate case Nogueira 2026 starts from, and his suggestion that nonlinear sinuous and helical modes are "essentially different" (p. 2834) anticipates the streak-induced splitting. Powell's candidate causes (unsteady initial swirl, irregular downstream motion; p. 2832, 2834) are alternatives to streaks. Instability-wave peaks move downstream through the stages (3–5D A, 4–6D B, 6–8D C, 8–10D D; p. 2827, 2833–2834). Source centroids are put at 5D (A2, B) and 6D (C) from single spark photos, which [mercier-2017] already lists as contrasting with its 4th-shock source (Mercier Table 1, p. 204). Near-field OASPL rises from ≈ 100 to ≈ 128 dB through stage A into B and then saturates at 125–130 dB ("some sort of limit cycle", Fig. 17, p. 2832, 2834). On receptivity, a thicker lip gives more loop gain (Sec. G, p. 2835), and on this t/D 0.8 lip the sinuous D takes over from M_j ≈ 1.62–1.67. That is probably the same family as Ponton 1992's thick-lip "re-emerging B" above M_j ≈ 1.7 (my inference; Raman 1998 p. 75 mentions a C→D switch in Ponton's data). B also spreads the most (≈ 4D wide at 7D, p. 2827), the basis of the δ = 0.25 used for m = 1 in nogueira-2021-absolute-instability (p. 7).

## andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets (queued 2026-10-22)

- The paper shows what screech does to the jet rather than how it closes. Strong screech damps the time-averaged shock train: about 6 against 11 visible cells at M_j 1.35 (my count; Figs 3–4, p. 667). The baseline centreline pressure oscillation collapses just downstream of x/D ≈ 5.3, where strong tones first appear, while the screech-free train lasts to x/D ≈ 11 (Fig. 5, p. 667–668; read).
- High-speed schlieren (not shown) points to the screech-induced jet oscillation and enhanced mixing as the cause (p. 668). This adds the shock-train counterpart to the spreading-rate evidence in "Amplitude and receptivity" ([krothapalli-1986](notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 123) and supports the mean-flow deformation thread ([mazharmanesh-2026](notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md); [farghadan-2026](notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) St = 0 correction).
- A new nonintrusive suppression method for the receptivity list: 24 shallow lip notches (1 mm × 4 mm, t/D 0.013) remove the tones at M_j 1.15–1.50 but only weaken them at 1.10 (≈ 8 dB lower, shifted to Sr ≈ 0.69; Fig. 2a, read). The authors attribute this to disrupted near-lip receptivity and possibly to slot ejection blocking upstream waves (Raman's cessation mechanism), "to be verified" (p. 668). This sits with the lip-geometry sensitivity of [ponton-1992](notes/ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) and the tab results of [norum-1982](notes/norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md). Unlike tabs, the notches leave the shock spacing and strength in the first cells unchanged (p. 668).
- The tones agree with [mercier-2017](notes/mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) (same lab and nozzle size): Sr_s ≈ 0.65 at M_j 1.10 and 1.15, 0.32 at 1.35. The screeching-jet U_c/U_j from BBSAN directivity (0.61 at M_j 1.35) equals Mercier's lip-line U_c upstream of the source (0.61).
- The 2St_s tuning (below) suggests that the screech harmonic's sideline radiation (k_kh(2St_s) − k_s1 ≈ 0; [farghadan-2026](notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 23) pulls the BBSAN peak. This is my reading.
