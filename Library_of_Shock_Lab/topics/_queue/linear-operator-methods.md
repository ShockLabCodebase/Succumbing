## jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise (queued 2026-10-22)

Eqs. 1–3 (p. 182) write each (m, ω) mode as the mean-flow-linearised operator forced by a generalised Reynolds stress R_{m,ω}, exact, with ensemble averaging for stochastic inlet forcing — the resolvent set-up before the jet resolvent papers, alongside [McKeon & Sharma 2010](../notes/mckeon-2010-critical-layer-framework-turbulent-pipe-flow.md). Its call for "a modeled, frequency-dependent Reynolds stress" and eddy viscosity (p. 183, 185) anticipates [Pickering 2021](../notes/pickering-2021-optimal-eddy-viscosity-resolvent-jets.md) and [Morra 2021](../notes/morra-2021-colour-of-forcing-resolvent-channel.md). On PSE it records that, for subsonic jets, neither a Kirchhoff surface nor an equivalent source is straightforward because both are sensitive to parabolisation error, so near-field projection with a tailored Green's function was used instead (Colonius et al. 2010, heated M∞ 0.9, m = 0 aft sound reasonable; p. 185), and that PSE agrees with linearised Euler for supersonic modes (Piot 2006) — consistent with the page's "marching methods trade upstream waves for cost". Global analysis appears as damped modes of a cold supersonic jet that predict structures and radiation (Nichols & Lele 2011, not in library) or a forced boundary-value problem (Mohseni 2002), consistent with "free jets are amplifiers". Nonlinear PSE for a subset of triads (p. 183) is the precursor of the harmonic-resolvent thread. It does not change current understanding; it is the historical anchor.
