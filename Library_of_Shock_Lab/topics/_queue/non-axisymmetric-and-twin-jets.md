## bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets (queued 2026-10-20)

- **What it resolves.** Bell 2021 explains Knast's s/D = 3 "phase anomaly" (NPR 3.4–4.4, M_j ≈ 1.45–1.62) in the m = 1 screech range. Band-pass Hilbert analysis of Knast's opposing-microphone records finds two or three coexisting tones of continuously varying frequency ("processes") there. They have a wide inter-microphone phase and dip below the threshold about 20% of the time (p. 8–14, Table 1, Fig. 7).
- **PIV evidence.** Conditional snapshot POD of about 9500 independent PIV fields at NPR 4.6, from a second Monash rig, shows three states: one jet oscillating alone, symmetric coupling, and antisymmetric coupling. The two coupled states never occur together (p. 23–28, Fig. 21). Knast's schlieren clips show the same left-only, right-only, both and neither cases (p. 28–29).
- **Link to Knast and Wong.** This confirms the unsteady coupling that [Knast 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) could only hypothesise (that note, p. 5, 8), and recasts Knast's region-2 "counter-rotating helical" mode as a mixture of states. It is the m = 1, s/D = 3 counterpart of the three A1/A2 states in [Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md) (steady, switching, uncoupled; p. 9–19). Here uncoupling already occurs at s/D 3, whereas Wong saw it only at s/D 6.
- **Labels.** Bell quotes the raw opposing-microphone phase: 0° = symmetric (mirror) coupling and 180° = antisymmetric (p. 9, 20). These numbers are the opposite of Knast's jet-frame convention in the page's naming table. Bell's symmetric NPR 5.0 pair is the 1SS pair of [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), which used the same PIV data.
- **Frequency split.** The symmetric tone is lower than the antisymmetric one: St 0.22 against 0.23 at NPR 4.6 in facility two, matched by POD wavelengths of 3.1D and 2.8D with u_c = 0.6u_j (Table 2, p. 27). In facility one it is about 0.04 lower (Fig. 5 reading). The ordering matches Wong's SS < SA and the twin-jet stability models ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 20, 22), but the split is 5–15%, not 0.3–0.6%.
- **Facility sensitivity.** The state NPRs differ between two Monash rigs with identical nozzles (enclosure reflections and a flush plenum face; p. 6, 17–18) and at OSU (anechoic room, different nozzles; p. 35). The antisymmetric → indeterminate → symmetric sequence holds in all three, which strengthens the page's facility-sensitivity point.
- **On the open question "intrinsic or driven?"** The paper's "competition between two global modes" (p. 29) is the source of the reading later used by Wong 2023 and Stavropoulos 2023. The paper tests neither supply drift nor beating. The note finds the "wide" states statistically Rayleigh-like (σ ≈ 5.5–6 dB, threshold dependence as for Gaussian noise), which favours weakly damped, noise-driven modes over switching between saturated limit cycles.
- **Suggested model test.** Run [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md)'s Floquet LSA on the NPR 4.6 mean flow, to see whether the 1SS and 1SA guided-mode bands both reach St 0.22–0.23.

## gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets (queued 2026-10-20)

- **What it is.** The library's first heated rectangular-jet screech study: seven implicit LES of the Cincinnati AR 2 C-D nozzle (M_d 1.5; NPR 3 and 4; TR 1–3). It is the single-jet counterpart of [Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), which uses the same nozzle family at NPR 3. The cold single-jet tone (St = fD_eq/u_j 0.36–0.37) equals the twin jets' 0.37, so coupling barely shifts the frequency (Table 4, p. 24).
- **Flapping axis changes with temperature** (Fig. 19, p. 21–24):
  - cold: flapping along the nozzle diagonal, i.e. antisymmetric about both planes (AA in the single-jet D2 convention, my mapping);
  - TR 2: mixed, with 180° phase jumps in the minor-axis plane and about 90° in the major-axis plane;
  - TR 2.5 and 3: pure minor-axis flapping (AS).

  A single DFT over 500D_eq/u_j cannot say whether the mixed or diagonal patterns are steady or alternating. That links to the AA/AS intermittency of Jeun 2024 (p. 9–10) and the precession of [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) (p. 20–24), and adds a candidate to the open question "what selects the flapping axis".
- **Symmetry explained by guided-mode bands.** The authors explain the antisymmetry with planar vortex-sheet (Tam & Norum 1992) bands of the upstream neutral acoustic modes. The NPR 3 tones lie only in the first antisymmetric band (p. 25–27). This is the planar precursor of [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)'s Floquet-in-azimuth argument and reinforces claim 5 of the page (guided-mode existence bands select the symmetry).
  - The model ignores the major dimension, so it cannot separate AS from AA flapping.
  - By my eq. 16 arithmetic, the hot underexpanded tones fall 8 % and 31 % below the A1 lower limit.
- **Shock cells and spacing models.** Tam's (1988) rectangular first-cell length is reproduced to 1–5 % at AR 2 (L_s/L_model 0.95–0.99, p. 15). That fills the "AR dependence could not be tested below AR 4" gap noted for [Tam 1988](../notes/tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md).
  - Cells shrink linearly (ΔL/L_s = 5.5 %), and the visible count falls from 10 to 4 with heating (p. 15).
  - Tam's screech formula with LES u_c is within 10 %. Hitting the tones needs u_c ≈ 0.60–0.67u_j (my inversion), against the 0.55u_j preferred by Raman 1998 (p. 59).
- **Axis switching and Mach waves.** The velocity-width switchover moves downstream with heating, from 7.5h to 11h (4.7–6.9D_eq, p. 13). That is comparable to the x/D_e ≈ 6.5 shock-cell switch of [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) at NPR 4. Strong minor-axis flapping makes the hot jets' Mach-wave radiation much stronger in the minor-axis plane (p. 16).
- **Frequencies agree across rigs.** St 0.24–0.25 at M_j 1.56 agrees with the group's AR 2 convergent rectangular jet at NPR 4.0 (flapping at 0.23) and with OSU twins (0.27 at 1.55) ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md)).
