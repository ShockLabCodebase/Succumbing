## bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets (queued 2026-10-20)

- **What it resolves.** Bell 2021 explains Knast's s/D = 3 "phase anomaly" (NPR 3.4–4.4, M_j ≈ 1.45–1.62) in the m = 1 screech range. Band-pass Hilbert analysis of Knast's opposing-microphone records finds two or three coexisting tones of continuously varying frequency ("processes") there. They have a wide inter-microphone phase and dip below the threshold about 20% of the time (p. 8–14, Table 1, Fig. 7).
- **PIV evidence.** Conditional snapshot POD of about 9500 independent PIV fields at NPR 4.6, from a second Monash rig, shows three states: one jet oscillating alone, symmetric coupling, and antisymmetric coupling. The two coupled states never occur together (p. 23–28, Fig. 21). Knast's schlieren clips show the same left-only, right-only, both and neither cases (p. 28–29).
- **Link to Knast and Wong.** This confirms the unsteady coupling that [Knast 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) could only hypothesise (that note, p. 5, 8), and recasts Knast's region-2 "counter-rotating helical" mode as a mixture of states. It is the m = 1, s/D = 3 counterpart of the three A1/A2 states in [Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md) (steady, switching, uncoupled; p. 9–19). Here uncoupling already occurs at s/D 3, whereas Wong saw it only at s/D 6.
- **Labels.** Bell quotes the raw opposing-microphone phase: 0° = symmetric (mirror) coupling and 180° = antisymmetric (p. 9, 20). These numbers are the opposite of Knast's jet-frame convention in the page's naming table. Bell's symmetric NPR 5.0 pair is the 1SS pair of [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), which used the same PIV data.
- **Frequency split.** The symmetric tone is lower than the antisymmetric one: St 0.22 against 0.23 at NPR 4.6 in facility two, matched by POD wavelengths of 3.1D and 2.8D with u_c = 0.6u_j (Table 2, p. 27). In facility one it is about 0.04 lower (Fig. 5 reading). The ordering matches Wong's SS < SA and the twin-jet stability models ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 20, 22), but the split is 5–15%, not 0.3–0.6%.
- **Facility sensitivity.** The state NPRs differ between two Monash rigs with identical nozzles (enclosure reflections and a flush plenum face; p. 6, 17–18) and at OSU (anechoic room, different nozzles; p. 35). The antisymmetric → indeterminate → symmetric sequence holds in all three, which strengthens the page's facility-sensitivity point.
- **On the open question "intrinsic or driven?"** The paper's "competition between two global modes" (p. 29) is the source of the reading later used by Wong 2023 and Stavropoulos 2023. The paper tests neither supply drift nor beating. The note finds the "wide" states statistically Rayleigh-like (σ ≈ 5.5–6 dB, threshold dependence as for Gaussian noise), which favours weakly damped, noise-driven modes over switching between saturated limit cycles.
- **Suggested model test.** Run [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md)'s Floquet LSA on the NPR 4.6 mean flow, to see whether the 1SS and 1SA guided-mode bands both reach St 0.22–0.23.

## gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets (queued 2026-10-20)

- **What it is.** The library's first heated rectangular-jet screech study: seven implicit LES of the Cincinnati AR 2 C-D nozzle (M_d 1.5; NPR 3 and 4; TR 1–3). It is the single-jet counterpart of [Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), which uses the same nozzle family at NPR 3. The cold single-jet tone (St = fD_eq/u_j 0.36–0.37) equals the twin jets' 0.37, so coupling barely shifts the frequency (Table 4, p. 24).
- **Flapping axis changes with temperature** (Fig. 19, p. 21–24):
  - cold: flapping along the nozzle diagonal, i.e. antisymmetric about both planes (AA in the single-jet D2 convention, my mapping);
  - TR 2: mixed, with 180° phase jumps in the minor-axis plane and about 90° in the major-axis plane;
  - TR 2.5 and 3: pure minor-axis flapping (AS).

  A single DFT over 500D_eq/u_j cannot say whether the mixed or diagonal patterns are steady or alternating. That links to the AA/AS intermittency of Jeun 2024 (p. 9–10) and the precession of [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) (p. 20–24), and adds a candidate to the open question "what selects the flapping axis".
- **Symmetry explained by guided-mode bands.** The authors explain the antisymmetry with planar vortex-sheet (Tam & Norum 1992) bands of the upstream neutral acoustic modes. The NPR 3 tones lie only in the first antisymmetric band (p. 25–27). This is the planar precursor of [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)'s Floquet-in-azimuth argument and reinforces claim 5 of the page (guided-mode existence bands select the symmetry).
  - The model ignores the major dimension, so it cannot separate AS from AA flapping.
  - By my eq. 16 arithmetic, the hot underexpanded tones fall 8 % and 31 % below the A1 lower limit.
- **Shock cells and spacing models.** Tam's (1988) rectangular first-cell length is reproduced to 1–5 % at AR 2 (L_s/L_model 0.95–0.99, p. 15). That fills the "AR dependence could not be tested below AR 4" gap noted for [Tam 1988](../notes/tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md).
  - Cells shrink linearly (ΔL/L_s = 5.5 %), and the visible count falls from 10 to 4 with heating (p. 15).
  - Tam's screech formula with LES u_c is within 10 %. Hitting the tones needs u_c ≈ 0.60–0.67u_j (my inversion), against the 0.55u_j preferred by Raman 1998 (p. 59).
- **Axis switching and Mach waves.** The velocity-width switchover moves downstream with heating, from 7.5h to 11h (4.7–6.9D_eq, p. 13). That is comparable to the x/D_e ≈ 6.5 shock-cell switch of [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) at NPR 4. Strong minor-axis flapping makes the hot jets' Mach-wave radiation much stronger in the minor-axis plane (p. 16).
- **Frequencies agree across rigs.** St 0.24–0.25 at M_j 1.56 agrees with the group's AR 2 convergent rectangular jet at NPR 4.0 (flapping at 0.23) and with OSU twins (0.27 at 1.55) ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md)).

## edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet (queued 2026-10-21)

- This is the earliest group paper on the AR 2 elliptical jet. It uses the same 20 × 10 mm nozzle (D_e = 14.14 mm) and the same PIV database as ivelja-2024, at the lower NPR of 2.2.
  - It supports Current understanding claim 1: flapping about the major-axis plane (seen in the minor-axis plane) dominates. The evidence is the antisymmetric two-point schlieren correlation in the minor-axis plane against a weak symmetric one in the major-axis plane (p. 2743), the centreline v_rms shock-displacement peaks in one plane only (p. 2744), and a minor-axis POD pair holding 15 % of the transverse-velocity energy (p. 2746).
  - With ivelja-2024 (23.6 % at NPR 2.6, 35 % at 3.4) this gives an NPR trend in one nozzle.
- What it adds is a second, uncorrelated antisymmetric POD pair with twice the wavelength (2.4D against 1.2D). It appears in both planes, saturates near x/D ≈ 7, and is labelled helical. The authors attribute it to shock-driven axis switching: the U = 0.5U_e half-widths are equal at x/D ≈ 4.3 (p. 2745–2747).
  - This is the speculation that mazharmanesh-2025 (their "2015b", p. 3, 20–21) and suzuki-2026 cite as the origin of the quasi-helical thread. It belongs in "Mode shapes … helical" and in the open question "Bi-axial and quasi-helical modes".
- The evidence for helicity is weak. The two planes were measured separately, so the π/2 phase lock a helix needs is untested. The major-axis pair grows only after x/D ≈ 3.5, which fits suzuki-2026's SA-family "new flapping" born at the switch point (p. 13–15) equally well.
  - The symmetry-breaking argument of rodriguez-2023 and nogueira-2026 suggests a mechanism: se₁ and ce₁ become nearly degenerate where the flow AR passes 1. That is the note's reading, not the paper's.
- The paper also documents the 3-D shock system: a barrel shock visible only in the major-axis plane, and minor-axis expansion-wave reflections that look like shocks in the major-axis view (p. 2741–2742). It quotes the Morris & Bhat (1995) LSA prediction of axis-locked modes and no spinning mode, and treats its helical pair as a challenge to that prediction, explained by the ideally expanded, non-switching base flow (p. 2740, 2745).
- Axis-switch location (the note's comparison, different metrics): equal half-widths at x/D ≈ 4.3 at NPR 2.2, against flow AR ≈ 1 near x/D ≈ 5 at NPR 2.6 and farther downstream at 3.4 (ivelja-2024, p. 10–11). In the same nozzle the switch therefore moves downstream as NPR rises. This could go in "Axis switching and the evolving mean flow".
- Methods: zero-lag two-point correlation of single-shot schlieren and snapshot POD of transverse velocity only are non-time-resolved classifiers. Frequency is never measured for the downstream pair (St 0.27 is inferred from λ and an assumed U_c = 0.65U_j).

## alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv (queued 2026-10-21)

Alkislar 2003 is the first quantitative flow-field study of a screeching rectangular jet. It uses phase-locked stereo PIV of an AR 4 C-D nozzle (M_d 1.44) at its loudest condition, M_j 1.69, and belongs in *Axis switching and the evolving mean flow* next to Krothapalli 1986.
- Screech raises the minor-axis spreading rate from 0.065 (ideally expanded M_j 1.44, which does not switch axes) to 0.11 over 5–20h and 0.33 beyond 21h. The axes cross at X_c = 16h ≈ 4AR (Fig. 12, p. 135–136).
- The authors attribute the switch to stationary counter-rotating streamwise vortex pairs. These form from the deformation of the screech-driven spanwise rolls (Husain & Hussain "ribs") and push the major-axis sides outward (Figs 13, 19, p. 136–140, 144).
- This agrees with krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet (crossover moves upstream at peak screech, p. 133), samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control (switching only off-design, p. 6–7) and suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets (forcing moves the switch upstream, p. 6). The mechanism is inferred, not shown dynamically, and at x/h = 6 the streamwise vortices are put down to nozzle imperfections instead (p. 136).

For *Mode shapes*:
- The jet flaps over the whole sweep, M_j 1.2–1.8 (p. 131–132). The phase-averaged ⟨v⟩ is antisymmetric for x/h > 7.5, and only the flapping frequency appears in the microphone (Fig. 14, p. 140).
- The St jump at M_d (≈0.172 at M_j 1.42 → ≈0.143 at 1.46, Fig. 7 read) is explained as a change in U_c (0.63U_j over- against 0.50U_j underexpanded, inserted into Tam 1988), not a varicose → flapping switch. This qualifies raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review (p. 54–55) for C-D nozzles. It is consistent with "AR ≥ 4 jets do not stage" (edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech, p. 18–19), since the jump crosses a no-tone gap at M_d rather than being a stage jump.
- Caveats: the overexpanded PIV behind both claims is not shown. The half-cycle mirroring used to build the full cycle enforces antisymmetry, so the symmetry of cycle-averaged fields is partly imposed (p. 130, 147).

For *Linear models*:
- U_c ≈ 0.5U_j, vorticity-weighted and constant in x (p. 143–144), sits with Raman's 0.55 and below Tam's 0.7.
- My check: Powell's formula with the measured U_c and L_s ≈ 3.2h (Fig. 11 read) reproduces St ≈ 0.09. Tam's vortex-sheet spacing (≈3.0h) is about 6% short.
- gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets (p. 26) places these data in the first antisymmetric vortex-sheet band.
- Cross-plane stereo PIV is also the measurement the page's open question on corner vortices and streaks needs (marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets). Note that esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets cites this single-jet paper for twin-jet coupling effects on spreading.

## nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles (queued 2026-10-21)

Nogueira 2025 is the library's first study of a single sharp-throat ("military-style") AR 2 C-D rectangular nozzle. It has D_eq 8.68 mm and M_d ≈ 1.5, and is the OSU twin design at about 0.45 scale. The data are two-view 150 kHz schlieren SPOD, PIV and far-field microphones over NPR 2.5–4.5 (p. 4211–4212).
- **Claim 1 (flapping dominates).** Minor-axis flapping dominates at every NPR. It is the only tone in the far field and the only POD pair in the PIV (p. 4214, 4217). Its St tracks the OSU twin jets of the same design within about 0.02 (0.40 at M_j 1.36 here against 0.40 at 1.35 there; [Esfahani 2021, coupling modes] Fig. 6, note's comparison), so neither scale nor twin coupling moves the tone much.
- **Claim 2 (multimodality).** SPOD adds a weak varicose tone (both views, from NPR 2.7), a second minor-axis flapping tone (from NPR ≈ 3.5) and a wagging tone seen only across the major axis (from NPR ≈ 4). At NPR 4.0 the four tones are St 0.265, 0.337, 0.308 and 0.272 (p. 4214–4215). This extends AR 2 multimodality to a C-D nozzle. There is no varicose→flapping jump in this range, which starts at NPR 2.5, above Mazharmanesh 2025's 2.2 jump.
- **Mode 4 and the bi-axial mode.** The wagging mode sits at x/D_eq ≈ 5–8, downstream of the fitted axis-switch point (≈ 5.1 D_eq at NPR 4.1, Fig. 9, p. 4216). This suggests the same wagging-after-the-switch behaviour as Mazharmanesh 2025's bi-axial mode and Suzuki 2026's new flapping family. That link is the note's reading; the paper does not cite Mazharmanesh 2025.
- **Axis switching.** On a fitted shock-free 3-D mean (eq. 7), the switch point moves downstream with NPR, peaking at 5.4 D_eq at NPR 3.7 (ideal expansion, where all tones are weakest), then retreats to 4.8 D_eq by NPR 4.5 (p. 4216). That fits Krothapalli's link between strong screech and earlier switching (note's reading).
- **Claim 7 (path-integration trap).** The authors put the flapping mode's signature in the major-axis view down to misalignment or poor convergence. It may instead be a diagonal (AA) component, as in Gojon 2019's cold LES of the Cincinnati nozzle of the same type (note's suggestion). This sharpens the line-of-sight trap.
- **Open questions.** Coexistence against alternation of the four loops is untested, since everything is block-averaged. Rotated or simultaneous two-view schlieren with sub-domain SPOD would test the mode-4 / bi-axial identification.
