## ho-1981-dynamics-impinging-jet-feedback-phenomenon (queued 2026-10-20)

This is the classical subsonic baseline the page lacks. For a cold M 0.3–0.9 round jet on a large flat plate, resonance appears for M > 0.7 and x0/d < 7.5 (p. 119). Pre-whitened near-field correlations and cross-spectral phase separate the two legs of a Powell loop: structures at C₁ = 0.62U, and non-dispersive upstream waves at a∞ with conical fronts at θ_a ≈ 37° → 22° as x0/d goes from 3 to 7 (Figs 6–9, p. 124–129). The two waves meet in phase at the plate and differ by exactly 5 × 2π at the lip for M 0.8, x0/d 4, f_r = 5.5 kHz (Fig. 13, p. 132–133). This supports item 1 of "Current understanding" with the zero-phase-lag (p = 0) Powell equation that [Weightman 2019](../notes/weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md) uses as eq. 1.3, and it contrasts with Henderson's N + p ≈ 4.4 for a supersonic jet.

Integer N gives the frequency staircase at M 0.9 (N = 2–6, St 0.42 → 0.33 per stage). With St_min = 0.33, eq. 7b predicts the stage jumps at x0/d = 1.25N against measured 2.40, 3.75, 4.8–5.0, 6.0–6.5 and 7.50 (Table 1, p. 134–135). My check with Fig. 14a values shows the constant-speed equation gives integer N only at stage ends. Mid-stage it gives N − 0.4 to N − 0.7, and the jumps are 11–19% instead of (N + 1)/N. This is the classical-model failure that [Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md) calls frequency squeezing and attributes to dispersive guided waves, now seen in an impinging jet (my inference, read from figures).

On "what carries the upstream wave", the authors measured only outside the jet (r/d > 2.5) and rejected in-core feedback (p. 120), so the data cannot tell free-stream sound from a guided mode. Wagner's in-core standing waves (p. 120) look like the subsonic duct-like waves of [Edgington-Mitchell 2025](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md) (Fig. 3), and the oblique arrival (θ_a ≈ 30°) bears on the cancellation argument of [Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md). The paper gives the first switching evidence in this thread: at x0/d 2.35, M 0.9, tones at ≈4.4 and ≈5.1 kHz (N = 2/3) alternate on 0.061 s windows (Fig. 15, p. 134–135). It is called hysteresis but shows no sweep direction or dwell times, which bears on the switching questions alongside [Stavropoulos 2026](../notes/stavropoulos-2026-jet-edge-interaction.md).

The "collective interaction" (forced multiple vortex merging from f_in at St 3–5, ∝ M^{1/2}, down to f_r within 1.3d; p. 135–139) is the paper's lip-receptivity mechanism. Its claim that resonance is then "independent of the initial conditions" (p. 140) is untested and sits uneasily with the lip-driven mode selection of Weightman 2019 (p. 70–81). There is no stability model or simulation: the modelling is a constant-speed loop count and a two-plane-wave model of correlation delays (eqs. 5–6, Appendix), which matches measured round-trip delays well (Fig. 12, p. 131). The Fig. 14a stage table in the note is a ready validation set for a dispersive (vortex-sheet or finite-thickness) phase model of subsonic impinging tones.

## tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets (queued 2026-10-21)

This is the founding guided-mode paper for impinging tones: the page and five notes cite the "guided-mode school", but the paper itself was missing. For cold, high-Re (> 2 × 10⁵) round jets on a wall it closes the loop with upstream neutral modes of the vortex-sheet jet (Tam & Hu 1989) rather than free sound (p. 72–75). Only modes inside the KH band St = fD/U_j < 0.6–0.7 can take part. In cold subsonic jets that leaves only the axisymmetric (0, 1) mode, so subsonic impinging resonance must be axisymmetric; at supersonic M_j the helical (1, 1) mode enters as well (Figs 8, 9, 14, p. 81–82). The paper's M_j 1.4 photographs show axisymmetric resonance at L/D 6 and helical at L/D 10 (Figs 3–4, p. 70–71).

The average tone St is predicted as that of the least-dispersive (0, 1) wave, d²ω/dk² = 0 (eq. 25). It falls from 0.73 at M_j 0.70 to 0.36 at 0.95 and matches the replotted data of Neuwerth, Wagner, [Ho 1981](../notes/ho-1981-dynamics-impinging-jet-feedback-phenomenon.md), Umeda and the authors over M_j 0.7–0.95 (Fig. 16, p. 83), although the four M_j 0.9 points sit 5–15% below it (note's reading). The low-Mach cut-off is explained as this St leaving the KH band (p. 85). The note's reproduction puts the crossing of 0.7 at M_j ≈ 0.72, not the printed "0.65".

The paper rejects Ho & Nosseir's external closure on two grounds: the (0, 1) eigenfunction is confined to the jet, and the Lepicovsky & Ahuja coflow test showed "little frequency shift" (p. 84–85). The note argues both are weak. External closure predicts only about 8% shift for M 0.23 coflow, and Ho's 5 × 2π lip phase count (Ho 1981, p. 132–133) is also met by the guided wave with n = 6–8.

**A modern reading (the note's calculation).** The inflection point of the (0, 1) branch is where the neutral saddles S1/S2 of [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) are born, at M_j ≈ 0.81 cold, against 0.82 isothermal in Towne (p. 31–32). Below that Mach number the least-dispersive wave is simply the slowest upstream wave (group velocity −0.02U_j at M_j 0.8). Because the branch is so flat, the dispersive phase condition gives a plateau at St ≈ 0.54–0.56 over L/D 2.5–5 at M_j 0.8. So the L-independent "average St" and the "frequency squeezing" of [Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md) (p. 9) are the same effect, and Neuwerth's ladder (Fig. 15, p. 82) is reproduced qualitatively. This goes some way to the dispersive phase-model test that the Ho 1981 queue item proposes. At M_j 0.85 the predicted 0.477 sits between the k⁻_d (0.52) and k⁻_p (0.46) loops of [Stavropoulos 2026](../notes/stavropoulos-2026-jet-edge-interaction.md) (p. 15–18).

**Open question "Guided or free-stream closure in a given supersonic impinging jet".** The note runs the band test that this question asks for. The cold vortex-sheet bands at M_j 1.42 are (0, 1) St 0–0.22, (0, 2) 0.42–0.53 and (1, 1) 0.369–0.371. They contain none of [Weightman 2019](../notes/weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md)'s NPR 3.4, z/D 5 tones: helical 0.241–0.255 lies 31–35% below the (1, 1) cut-off, and toroidal 0.383–0.385 lies 9–10% below the (0, 2) cut-off. This supports the thesis conclusion of external closure (p. 75, 81, 89). The helical case is robust. The toroidal case is not, because a thick shear layer lowers the bands and shocks are ignored.

The paper fits the reconciliation of [Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) (p. 31–33) and [Edgington-Mitchell 2025](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md) (p. 8, 14–17). Its subsonic loop is driven by structures hitting the wall in the core, an in-core source that must pass the guided-mode filter. Its supersonic modes are "outside the jet but guided by it" (p. 79), i.e. near the branch point C ≈ −1.

**Points for the page.**
- One contrast for the "What carries the upstream wave" section: Tam & Ahuja treat confinement of the wave in the core as the mark of in-jet feedback (p. 84), whereas Jordan 2018 (p. 10–14) finds that the same k_TH⁻ wave, once fully trapped (St ≳ 1), misses the lip and ends the tones.
- The paper also predicts that subsonic twin impinging jets should not couple, because flapping needs the (1, 1) mode (p. 70–71). This is untested.
- The paper belongs in "Current understanding" items 2 and 6 as the classical subsonic theory, and in the Papers list at 1990.
- It does not change the consensus, but it supplies the missing origin of the guided-mode school. It also gives a quantitative subsonic prediction (St against M_j) that no other paper on the page has.

## bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets (queued 2026-10-22)

This is the first impinging-jet LES note in the library. The page's Methods section says such LES "appears only as citations", and the thesis and Edgington-Mitchell 2025 frame the "two schools" against it. The paper has four LES of a cold, ideally expanded M_j 1.5 round jet (Re 6 × 10⁴, laminar exit layer, lip 0.1r0) on a plate at L/D = 3, 4, 5 and 6. Each gives four tones at St 0.165–0.57, which follow the staging paths of Krothapalli et al. (1999) (Fig. 14a, p. 576). At each tone a hydrodynamic–acoustic standing wave has N_sw cells equal to the Powell mode number N (eq. 2.2, Table 3, p. 574–576). The dominant tone moves from N = 4 to 6 as L grows. Eq. 3.1 with zero phase lag gets the dominant tones within 0.7–8% (my check; all tones sit at N to N + 0.5), consistent with "p = 0 (Gojon 2016)" in [Weightman 2019](../notes/weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md) (p. 122, 126). The tones alternate in St bands, axisymmetric below 0.225, helical to 0.355, axisymmetric to 0.5, helical above (Fig. 14b, p. 577). The authors explain this with the vortex-sheet upstream neutral modes of [Tam & Ahuja 1990](../notes/tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md), combined with the standing-wave condition (eq. 3.6, Figs 18–21, p. 579–582). They then see upstream waves inside the supersonic core and on the lip line in pressure snapshots and k–ω spectra of the L/D 6 jet (Figs 22–25, p. 583–585).

Two points bear on the page's open question "Guided or free-stream closure in a given supersonic impinging jet". First, the frequencies cannot decide it: on k = −ω/a0, eq. 3.6 reduces exactly to eq. 3.1, and at M_j 1.5 the upstream branches travel at 0.93–1.00a0 (my checks; the authors note the tones sit where the waves move at about a0, p. 582). Second, the band test the page asks for supports guided closure for the axisymmetric tones but not the helical ones. By my vortex-sheet calculation, every axisymmetric tone is inside A1 = (0, 1) or A2 = (0, 2), or up to 6.4% below the A2 lower limit of 0.390. Six of the seven helical tones lie 11–26% below the H1 (0.343–0.344) or H2 (0.638) bands. The paper's plotted H1 range (≈ 0.32–0.35) is wider than its own eq. 3.5 gives. The LES at L/D 5–6 (helical 0.255–0.29, axisymmetric 0.375–0.44) closely resembles Weightman's NPR 3.4, z/D 5 tones (helical 0.241–0.255, toroidal 0.383–0.385), which the Tam & Ahuja queue item found outside the bands in the same way. So on band evidence the two schools may agree more than the framing suggests: guided closure is plausible for the axisymmetric tones and unproven for the helical ones in both (my synthesis). The helical k–ω evidence (Fig. 25b) is at r = r0, where grazing external sound at a0 would look the same with ΔkD ≈ 1.3 (my arithmetic).

On "where the upstream wave is generated", the paper says only that the waves "seem to be generated in the region of jet impingement" (p. 566–567). [Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md)'s reading of an in-core source at the impingement point (p. 33) is an interpretation, not a result here. The jets have no strong stand-off shock, so the leakage and shocklet sources of the group's underexpanded jets may simply be absent. That supports the source-location reconciliation of [Edgington-Mitchell 2025](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md) (p. 8, 14–17). Circular waves centred near z ≈ 3r0, made where upstream waves meet shear-layer structures (p. 567), may be a leakage-like escape of the trapped wave (my speculation). Four tones coexist in each record and JetL6 has a sum tone St₂ + St₃ (helical + axisymmetric, p. 573). That bears on the page's "coexistence or switching of toroidal and helical modes" question, which the LES does not test. It does not overturn the page's consensus, but it supplies the LES evidence the page lacked. It also sharpens the band-test open question: the helical tones need a finite-thickness or non-parallel band calculation before guided closure can be claimed for them. Add it to "Current understanding" item 2 and the Papers list at 2017.

## henderson-1993-tones-choked-jet-impinging-flat-plates (queued 2026-10-22)

This is the classical map of supersonic impinging tones, and the source of the large-plate L1/L2/L3 and small-plate S4/S5 labels that [Henderson 2002] uses. The jet is a cold 8 mm convergent nozzle with t/d ≈ 0.28, on plates of D/d 0.5–38 at NPR 2.02–4.75. Large-plate tones occur at every NPR; small-plate tones appear from NPR 3.04 with steeper slopes; and multiple tones are "the rule rather than the exception" (p. 311–318).

Two-microphone cross-spectra make L1 symmetrical and L2/L3 helical (p. 323). That fits Tam 1990's prediction that both (0, 1) and (1, 1) upstream waves qualify at supersonic M_j (tam-1990, p. 82). It also fits the thesis's intermediate lip (t/D 0.33), which gives both toroidal and helical tones (weightman-2019, p. 73–79), whereas Henderson 2002's 0.5d lip gave only L1. So these three nozzles form a lip-thickness sequence for claim 5.

The reflector tests are the 1993 counterpart of the thesis foam tests (p. 319):
- A 0.30 m reflector at 2.25λ behind the exit suppresses the 28.51 kHz L1 tone (NPR 4.40, D/d 38) and promotes a 19.96 kHz tone.
- At 0.75λ it suppresses an 11.25 kHz S4 tone (NPR 4.47, D/d 2).
- So both classes close at the nozzle, contrary to Powell (1988) for small plates.
- They show that the external field at the lip matters, not that the return is external (cf. nogueira-2024, p. 31–33). They also show that a reflector can switch between loops.

Powell's equation (eq. 2, p. 314: p = 0, M_con,u = 0.73, h″/d = 0.8) counts N = 2–8 with most data near integers. That is another instance of the under-determined timing equation. My slope check: at fixed N it cannot give the S-tone log–log slopes above 1 (≈ 1.2–2, read from Fig. 11), which supports the authors' view that small-plate loops scale differently (p. 324).

Random L1 ↔ L2 switching at an L1 jump (20 against 14.25 kHz on two-average spectra, NPR 3.04, D/d 4, h/d 3.8; p. 318) extends ho-1981 (p. 134–135) to supersonic, multi-azimuthal-mode jets. It was flagged in 1993 as untested coexistence-or-switching, which belongs under the existing open question "Coexistence or switching of toroidal and helical modes".

For "Where the upstream wave is generated":
- Small plates show stand-off-shock oscillation up to d/3, with only symmetric disturbances and no link to passing disturbances (p. 322). The proposed small-plate loop goes through that shock (p. 324), in the spirit of Kuo & Dowling 1996.
- At very small spacing the fronts centre in the wall jet; at larger spacing they "appear to originate in the impingement region" (p. 322). That sits in mild tension with Henderson 2002's wall-jet source at 1.3d.

## jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement (queued 2026-10-22)

**Maps.** This is the first fine-swept experimental map in the library of round impinging-jet tones over M_j 0.7–1.5 and H/D 2–8. The jet is a cold 10 mm convergent jet with a thin lip (t/D 0.03), at Pprime SUCRE, recorded by one exit-plane microphone at r/D 1 for 30 s at 200 kHz (p. 2–4). At H/D 2 the tones are scattered, stage near M_j 1.05 and have quiet bands at M_j ≈ 1.28 and 1.35, which the authors link to Henderson 2005's zones of silence. From H/D 4 they form ordered families, each of which dies out along a curve in (M_j, St) (p. 2–3). That curve coincides "almost perfectly" with the upper existence limit S(0,1) of the m = 0, lowest-radial-order guided jet mode (Fig. 6, p. 7).

**Fit with the other notes.** This extends the band-limit cut-off of [jordan-2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md) (jet–edge, p. 12–14) and [mancinelli-2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) (screech, p. 6) to impinging jets. It is the experimental, underexpanded counterpart of [bogey-2017](../notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md) and supports [tam-1990](../notes/tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md). A phase-only KH + k_p⁻ vortex-sheet model follows the tone trends better than Powell's free-stream closure. Powell's model can be tuned to any one peak but never fits all of them, and it predicts tones where none occur (p. 7). Still, no predicted line lands on a peak. At H/D 8 the lines for φ ∈ {0, π} are only ≈ 0.03 apart in St (my estimate), so the real evidence is the extinction limit and the slopes.

**The temperature check (mine).** The S(0,1) line in Figs 4 and 6 is reproduced to within 0.003 by an *isothermal* vortex sheet, while the experimental jet is cold (T₀ = T_∞). The cold band edge is 4–12% higher for M_j 1.0–1.5, so the "almost perfect" match needs rechecking with a consistent temperature ratio.

**Closure debate (Current understanding item 2).** Extinction at S(0,1) implies an in-core source for these low-St m = 0 tones ([nogueira-2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md), p. 31–33). But at supersonic M_j many strong tones lie *above* S(0,1), for example a B-like line from St ≈ 0.45 at M_j 1.19 to ≈ 0.27 at 1.5 at H/D 6–8, read from Fig. 2. The model cannot produce them, and the paper does not discuss them. They could be (1,1) or (0,2) loops, screech, or external closure ([edgington-mitchell-2025](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md), p. 14–24).

**The user's thesis (my comparison).** The thesis tones at M_j ≈ 1.45, z/D 5 are ≈ 0.36 (toroidal) and 0.24 (helical) in fD/U_j. Both lie above S(0,1) ≈ 0.19–0.21, consistent with the thesis's external closure ([weightman-2019](../notes/weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md), p. 75, 81). The toroidal tone is near the cold (0,2) band, however (0.390–0.491 at M_j 1.5 in the Bogey 2017 note).

**Contradictions and agreements.** The paper discards duct-like upstream waves as having too little support outside the jet (p. 6–7). Later work contradicts this: such waves are seen, strongly nonlinear, in M 0.86–0.90 impinging jets at z/D 2 (Edgington-Mitchell 2025, p. 8), and k_d⁻ loops close jet–edge tones at M_j 0.82–0.89 ([stavropoulos-2026](../notes/stavropoulos-2026-jet-edge-interaction.md), p. 15–18). Screech (A1/A2 for 1.0 < M_j < 1.15, B above) appears only at H/D 8 (p. 3), which agrees with [henderson-1993](../notes/henderson-1993-tones-choked-jet-impinging-flat-plates.md) (stages merge into screech at h/d ≳ 5).

**Open questions.** The paper adds a concrete test for "guided or free-stream closure": place each tone against the cold S(0,1), S(1,1) and S(0,2) band edges. It also notes that the phase lag stays a free parameter until the plate and nozzle reflection coefficients are known (p. 10), which ties into the phase-lag open question. Methods: one microphone, so no azimuthal order; PSD only, no wavelets or bicoherence; the spectral parameters are not stated.

## weightman-2017-phase-lag-supersonic-jet-impingement (queued 2026-10-22)

- **What it adds.** This JFM Rapid is the published source of the shocklet and the phase-lag result that the topic page currently cites through the thesis (Weightman 2019, p. 129–134). Cite it directly: p. 4–6 for the shocklet life cycle, p. 7–9 for the measured terms and p. 10 for the prediction.
- **The mechanism.** 500 kfps schlieren of a C-D jet on a hemisphere (NPR 5.0) shows a wall-jet shock that forms as a vortex passes, rotates towards normal to the wall, creeps upstream and releases the returning wave shortly before the next vortex. The delay is Δt = 40 µs, p = −0.49 (p. 5–6).
- **The measured loop.** For the NPR 3.6, z/D 3.5 half-cylinder case, N = 4, u_c = 0.42–0.52U_j (mean 0.48), U_s = 331 m/s, the shocklet at (3.78, 1.43)D, and Δt = 29.4 µs from phase angles 122.5° (emission) and 34.5° (vortex arrival). The prediction is 8230 Hz against 8300 Hz measured, and 8750 Hz with Δt = 0 (p. 7–10).
- **New point for "The feedback equation and the phase lag".** The 0.8% agreement is largely built into the method (note, Key equations; my analysis).
  - Δt is the phase residual that closes the loop with the same u_c and path that enter the integral. Eq. 3.1 then returns the measured f regardless of u_c, up to the gap between the straight acoustic leg (≈ 172 µs) and the traced wave (≈ 167 µs). The check 852° + 88° + 500° = 1440° = 4 × 360° holds identically.
  - So the evidence for the shocklet as the lag rests on two things: the Δt = 0 deficit of 0.24 cycle, which a roughly 9% lower mean u_c would also explain, and the 40 µs seen directly on a different configuration.
  - This sharpens the topic page's existing "weaker than it looks" paragraph rather than contradicting it.
- **Agreement with [edgington-mitchell-2025].** That paper confirms the same life cycle on an AR 2 jet on a cylinder at NPR 5.5 and finds the shocklet consistently closes the loop only at NPR ≥ 4.5 and z/D ≤ 4.0 (p. 15–17).
