## nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles (queued 2026-10-21)

## panda-1999-experimental-investigation-screech-noise-generation (queued 2026-10-21)

Panda's two jets, M_j 1.19 axisymmetric (A2 per Mercier 2017, Table 1, p. 204) and M_j 1.42 helical (C), are both stages that [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) (p. 11–12) and [Nogueira 2022](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) (p. 9–13) assign to the suboptimal shock peak k_s2. His "unexplained" L_sw < L (p. 94) is what that model predicts. Since the G-JM sits at k_kh − k_s, the closing shock wavenumber is k_kh + k_up ≈ k_sw. The note's arithmetic gives k_sw D = 9.5 against 2π/L = 8.2 at M_j 1.19, and 6.1 against 4.9 at M_j 1.42. The ratios, 1.17 and 1.24, are close to k_s2/k_s1 ≈ 1.25 (Nogueira 2022, M_j 1.16–1.22) and ≈ 1.27 (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, Tsinghua NPR 3–4). So Panda is an independent pre-2000 dataset, on a third nozzle (D = 25.4 mm, NASA Lewis), consistent with A2 and C closing on a shorter-than-average shock wavelength. Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 5, 11) already states the reconciliation in general terms. The match is approximate: Panda's L averages 5–6 shock terminations rather than giving a spectral peak, and the G-JM is slightly subsonic, so the closure k_s is slightly above k_sw computed with c_∞. Panda has no A1 case, so his data cannot show the jump of k_sw from k_s1 to k_s2. Table 2's jets (L_sw > L) do not fit the picture. The PyFR LES at NPR 2.10/2.25 could measure near-field crest spacing on both sides of the switch and test the jump directly.

## gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets (queued 2026-10-22)

The paper's Fig. 3 (p. 2920) is the first band map across all stages. Over M_j 1–2, with the Powell et al. 1992 and Ponton & Seiner 1992 (t = 0.625D) tones overlaid, A1, A2 and Powell's secondary u lie near the lower limit of the (0, 2) band (S2), so they are axisymmetric. C and c fall on the lower limit of the first helical band (H1), so they are helical. B, D, b and d fall outside every band, which the authors put down to "more complex flapping/sinuous" oscillation (p. 2919–2920). This explains the azimuthal shape of each stage, but not staging: A1 and A2 share S2, so the jump needs the k_s1/k_s2 explanation of Nogueira 2022 and Edgington-Mitchell 2022. Like EM 2018, the paper contradicts Shen & Tam's split closure (free-stream for A1/B, guided for A2/C; p. 2918). Its "u on S2" agrees with Mancinelli 2021's weak U tone above C, which Powell et al. suggested extends A2. The note doubts the B/D explanation. Flapping and helical waves share the |m| = 1 vortex-sheet relation in a round jet, and streak-induced splitting moves the flapping band up, not down ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) p. 11), so thin-sheet band edges are the likelier cause. Ponton & Seiner group their high-M_j "D" with B (p. 537), while Fig. 3b labels it D. The LES has two helical C tones 0.025 apart (St 0.28, 0.305) plus an axisymmetric u (0.38) in one 500 D_j/u_j record (p. 2920). Whether they coexist or alternate is not addressed, which adds a case to the open coexistence-or-alternation question. The paper's f–k diagnostic, applied to the PyFR A1 and A2 states, would test whether the guided-mode band position changes across staging or only the shock wavenumber it pairs with.

## henderson-1993-tones-choked-jet-impinging-flat-plates (queued 2026-10-22)

This is an impinging-jet paper, but it adds five points to this thread.
1. Impinging-tone staging whose cycle increment depends on NPR (L1 jumps 4 → 5 cycles at NPR 2.36 but 4 → 6 at 2.70). The authors attribute this to a change in the preferred free-jet screech mode between these NPR (p. 312–313). The stages cluster around, and at large spacing merge into, choked-jet screech (p. 312).
2. Direct, if crude, evidence of random switching between two loops at one condition, with random intervals (p. 318). This is the impinging counterpart of the mutual exclusion near A1/A2 in mancinelli-2019 (p. 6–7), and of ho-1981. No dwell times are given.
3. In this rig's free-jet screech runs, the helical mode's **spin direction reversed at random** during continuous running, unlike Umeda et al., where it was stable once started (p. 324). This chirality switching is not yet on the page, and it matters for any m = ±1 analysis (and for the PyFR LES): a two-microphone phase averages over it.
4. No screech above NPR 3.79 on this rig (p. 316), which supports claim 4 that staging limits belong to the rig.
5. At NPR 2.36 the tone map carries both a "symmetrical" and a "helical" screech line (λ/d ≈ 1.63 and 2.18; St ≈ 0.59 and 0.44 by my estimate, Fig. 4). The authors also hypothesise that some tones are nonlinear sum/difference products rather than modes (p. 324), which relates to the quadratic B-tone of mancinelli-2021.

It agrees with the page's "selection, hysteresis and switching" open question and adds no hysteresis test.

## powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets (queued 2026-10-22)

This is the primary round-jet staging map (D = 10 mm, t/D = 0.8, NPR 2.0–5.84): A1 and A2 varicose, B and D sinuous, C helical, identified by two-microphone azimuthal phase over 18 restarts per angle (Figs 2, 11–12, p. 2825, 2829–2830). It contradicts the page's statement that "hysteresis has not been tested". The C ↔ D jump is hysteretic: at NPR 4.48 C is at 11.00 kHz with pressure rising and D at 9.125 kHz with pressure falling (Fig. 3, p. 2826), over a band of ≈ NPR 4.34–4.71 (M_j ≈ 1.62–1.67, read from Figs 2 and 17). In the up and down OASPL sweeps the A1/A2 (≈ 2.3), A2/B (≈ 2.5) and B/C (≈ 3.2) boundaries coincide at the ≈ 0.1 NPR point spacing (Fig. 17, read), so any A1/A2 bistable band is below that resolution. At NPR 2.45 A2 and B are both dominant and the jet "switches rapidly between them" (p. 2825, 2835), an early report of the mutually exclusive switching of [mancinelli-2019] and [mazharmanesh-2026]. Secondary tones about 25 dB down continue each dominant branch across every jump (Figs 2, 18–19, p. 2825, 2833). They are the averaged-spectrum ancestor of Bell 2021's coexisting "processes" and Edgington-Mitchell 2022's multimodal peaks, but averaged spectra cannot tell weak continuous loops from rare episodes. The A1 → A2 boundary here (NPR ≈ 2.3, M_j ≈ 1.16) is above the 2.17–2.27 of the page's facility table, with a very thick lip; add a row: Kyoto, D = 10 mm, t/D 0.8, NPR ≈ 2.26–2.35 (Fig. 4 photos and Fig. 17). The C/B frequency ratio (1.14–1.23, Fig. 20, p. 2834) is explained by a change in N + p (6/5 or 7/6). My derivation in the note: the β angle (Fig. 21) implies B obeys Powell's formula with the second-cell spacing to about 2 % while C sits about 24 % above it, which supports the B ↔ k_s1, C ↔ k_s2 assignment of edgington-mitchell-2022 and nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech. A useful suggestion for the PyFR driver: C ↔ D is a documented hysteretic transition, so it is a positive control for the saddle-node continuation method.

## weightman-2019-nozzle-external-geometry-azimuthal-mode-impinging-jet (queued 2026-10-22)

The paper is an impinging analogue of the receptivity test the page proposes (open question 5: change only the lip or exterior and see whether limits move while frequencies do not).
- **Frequencies fixed, selection moves.** At fixed NPR 3.4, changing only the nozzle exterior changes which azimuthal loop dominates:
  - t/D 0.1: helical St 0.255;
  - t/D 7: toroidal 0.385 plus helical 0.241;
  - t/D 0.33: toroidal 0.383 plus helical 0.253 (p. 430–438).

  Yet across nine configurations the tones stay at St ≈ 0.25 and ≈ 0.39 (Fig. 18, p. 441–442). The jet sets the frequencies and the boundary (the acoustic path) sets which loop wins. That is the signature the page asks for, seen here at the level of mode selection.
- **Claim 4, "the staging NPR belongs to the rig".** A surface 3D upstream of the exit (the outer nozzle base), and foam on it, decides whether the helical loop exists at all (p. 440–441). The paper also hedges "for this facility" (p. 441).
- **Direction of the effect.** Thickening the lip brings in a toroidal mode here. In free jets, [ponton-1992](../notes/ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) instead finds a thick lip widens helical C and revives B (p. 433 here). It parallels Vinoth et al. 2011 (reflectors give simultaneous toroidal and helical screech; p. 433), and the upstream plate of [edgington-mitchell-2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) that produced m = 0 multimodality (p. 6, 16).
- **Claim 3, mutual exclusion vs coexistence.** The infinite and intermediate lips sustain two inharmonic loops at once, with a strong difference tone (0.144 or ≈ 0.13; Figs 5 and 9). This suggests coexistence in time, not alternation, though it is untested with time-resolved data. It is a closed-geometry counterexample to single-jet mutual exclusion, not a free-jet one.
- **Consequence for the PyFR LES.** Nothing here changes the free-jet consensus, but it supports the page's advice to record lip and exterior geometry. The PyFR LES should model the nozzle exterior and upstream surfaces as built. A thin-lip vs flange run at the same NPR would test whether the A1/A2 limits move.
