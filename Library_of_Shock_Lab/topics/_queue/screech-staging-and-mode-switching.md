## panda-1999-experimental-investigation-screech-noise-generation (queued 2026-10-21)

Panda's two jets, M_j 1.19 axisymmetric (A2 per Mercier 2017, Table 1, p. 204) and M_j 1.42 helical (C), are both stages that [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) (p. 11–12) and [Nogueira 2022](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) (p. 9–13) assign to the suboptimal shock peak k_s2. His "unexplained" L_sw < L (p. 94) is what that model predicts. Since the G-JM sits at k_kh − k_s, the closing shock wavenumber is k_kh + k_up ≈ k_sw. The note's arithmetic gives k_sw D = 9.5 against 2π/L = 8.2 at M_j 1.19, and 6.1 against 4.9 at M_j 1.42. The ratios, 1.17 and 1.24, are close to k_s2/k_s1 ≈ 1.25 (Nogueira 2022, M_j 1.16–1.22) and ≈ 1.27 (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, Tsinghua NPR 3–4). So Panda is an independent pre-2000 dataset, on a third nozzle (D = 25.4 mm, NASA Lewis), consistent with A2 and C closing on a shorter-than-average shock wavelength. Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 5, 11) already states the reconciliation in general terms. The match is approximate: Panda's L averages 5–6 shock terminations rather than giving a spectral peak, and the G-JM is slightly subsonic, so the closure k_s is slightly above k_sw computed with c_∞. Panda has no A1 case, so his data cannot show the jump of k_sw from k_s1 to k_s2. Table 2's jets (L_sw > L) do not fit the picture. The PyFR LES at NPR 2.10/2.25 could measure near-field crest spacing on both sides of the switch and test the jump directly.
