## andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets (queued 2026-10-22)

- This is direct evidence for the claim on the page that "PNS-type solvers all miss the rapid decay in strongly screeching jets" ([tam-1995](notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 32): the rapid decay is caused by screech itself. With screech removed by lip notches, the mean shock train at M_j 1.35 persists to x/D ≈ 11 instead of collapsing after x/D ≈ 5.3, at essentially the same spacing (≈ 1.2D) and first-cell strength (Figs 3–5, p. 667–668; read).
- Linear, multiple-scales or zero-frequency PSE shock models (Ray 2005; Nogueira 2022 PFE) should therefore be compared with screech-free data, or given a screech-induced mean-flow correction. The harmonic resolvent's St = 0 component ([farghadan-2026](notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md)) is one such correction.
- Mean shock spacings read from the static-pressure surveys agree with [mercier-2017](notes/mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md): ≈ 0.54D at M_j 1.10 (baseline) and ≈ 0.53D (notched, first 10 shocks), and ≈ 1.2D at 1.35 (Fig. 5, Fig. 11; read), against Mercier's 0.57 and 1.20. The notches shorten only the second cell at M_j 1.35 (p. 668).
- For BBSAN source models built from measured shock cells ([wong-2021](notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), PIV of a screeching M_j 1.45 jet): a screeching jet's shock train under-represents the downstream cells, which near-field data show are the strongest, highest-frequency BBSAN sources (Figs 11–12, p. 670–671).
- On the "Which shock wavenumber closes which stage?" thread, the baseline BBSAN Sr_p jumps with every screech stage (Fig. 8, p. 669). So the time-averaged shock spacing or U_c changes with the stage, a far-field signature of the two-way coupling on the page.

## tan-2019-correlation-analysis-piv-screeching-jets (queued 2026-10-22)

The paper's link to this page is the **shock-cell time scale** τ̂₂ ∝ |∂V̄₁/∂x₁|⁻¹ in the Kalyan & Karabasov mixed-scale BBSAN model (eqs 9–11, p. 10–11). It is a mean-flow-gradient stand-in for shock strength, in contrast to the explicit Pack or PSE shock modes × wavepackets of [ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound] and [wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan].
- **Shock time scale:** on the lip line the shock-scale contribution is subdominant (f < 1 except right at the first cell; Fig. 15, p. 33, 40), so the random-turbulence time scale controls the modelled source there.
- **Shock modulation of the length scales:** the total-field second-order integral scales are modulated at the shock-cell period, out of phase with the functional scales (p. 24). After screech filtering, L₁₁ has local maxima at the reflection points (Fig. 13, p. 33). This is the length-scale counterpart of the shock modulation of coherent and random stresses in [edgington-mitchell-2014-coherent-structure-helical-screech-mode] (p. 836–837) and of the coherent–random anti-phase in Fig. 3 here (p. 22).
- **Spacing:** reflection points from ∂V̄₁/∂x = 0 (Fig. 2, my reading) give mean spacing over the first three cells of 1.47D (M_j 1.45, equal to Prandtl–Pack) and 1.67D (M_j 1.59, about 7% below Pack, with a Mach disk). The spacing shrinks downstream, from 1.51 to 0.99D (M_j 1.45) and from 1.70 to 1.34D (M_j 1.59). This is consistent with the shrinkage already on the page.
- **Does it change the page?** No change to the open questions. It adds a second, statistical model family to the "wavepacket × shock-cell sources of BBSAN" section.

## tan-2019-equivalent-bbsan-source-reconstruction-screeching-jets (queued 2026-10-22)

The paper adds the statistical (RANS/PIV) counterpart to the wavepacket × shock-cell BBSAN sources of ray-2005 and wong-2021. The shock part is the measured shock pressure p_s = p − p∞, reconstructed from mean PIV (isentropic core with P₀ from the NPR, adiabatic temperature, and integration of the van Oudheusden mean-momentum pressure gradient across the shear layer; eqs. 13–20, p. 17–18). It is then mirrored about the exit, Hann-windowed and Fourier transformed in x (p. 11). PIV and RANS shock pressures agree in peak location and amplitude except for x/D < 1.5 (Fig. 8, p. 32–33). RANS shock spacing drifts for M_j 1.59 beyond x/D ≈ 5, where a Mach disk at x/D ≈ 1.2 adds an internal shear layer (p. 30). The two-time-scale (mixed) model of Kalyan & Karabasov, which lets strong shocks lengthen the source time scale through f = (τ̂₁|∂V̄₁/∂x₁|)², is inactive here except at the first reflection (Fig. 1, p. 20–21): shock cells set where the source sits but not its time scale. Spacings read from Fig. 10 (mean of the first four cells 1.37D at M_j 1.45, matching edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets' 1.366D for the same data, and 1.60D at M_j 1.59) shrink downstream to about 1.0D and 1.3D, consistent with the shrinkage noted on the page. At M_j 1.59 the first cells are about 11% shorter than Pack (my comparison). Sources sit just upstream of each reflection point, where k and ε also peak (Figs 6–7) and where the coherent stress peaks in edgington-mitchell-2014-coherent-structure-helical-screech-mode. This supports the product picture's localisation at the shock tips but uses an isotropic closure, not wavepackets. Nothing here changes the open questions about k_s1/k_s2 closure.

## wong-2019-coherence-decay-wavepacket-bbsan (queued 2026-10-22)

- The shock side is Prandtl–Pack's uniform, non-decaying vortex-sheet train. It uses k_sn = 2σ_n/(D_jβ) and amplitudes c_sn = 2Δp/(σ_np_∞), the Tam & Tanna coefficient without the 1/J₁(σ_n) radial factor (eqs. 2.5–2.7, p. 974). On the axis the n = 2 to n = 1 ratio would be −0.66 rather than +0.44 (note's check).
- The model needs about ten modes before the far-field spectrum converges. Tam, Jackson & Seiner (1985) argued for four and Ray & Lele (2007) for one (p. 984).
- The modelled "n = 2" sits at Pack's k_s2 = 2.30k_s1. The page says the measured secondary shock wavenumbers come from cells that shrink downstream and are not Pack's higher modes ([nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md)). So this paper's higher-mode BBSAN content is an idealised Fourier description, which [wong-2021](../notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md) later corrected with PIV (k_mod = 0.79k + 1.02, plus axial decay).
- It supports Current-understanding item 6 (BBSAN needs the envelope and coherence, not only k_s):
  - neither L nor L_c moves the peak frequency;
  - a longer envelope raises the peak and sharpens the spurious peaks (App. A, Fig. 8, p. 988–989);
  - short L or L_c destroys the peak-frequency directivity (Fig. 9, p. 990).
- With [nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets](../notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md) (shocks lengthen the envelope), this suggests the need for jitter grows in real shock-containing jets. That is our inference.
- It adds an earlier, model-only data point to the open question "Jitter or shock unsteadiness between the BBSAN peaks?". In the model, coherence decay alone smooths the gaps; shocks are steady.

## wong-2020-azimuthal-decomposition-radiated-noise-shock-containing-jets (queued 2026-10-22)

This paper's contribution to the shock side of BBSAN is small.
- **What eq. 1 fits.** With the Pack–Prandtl vortex-sheet L₁ and u_c = 0.7u_j, eq. 1 fits the n = 1 and n = 2 lobes at NPR 3.10 and 3.67 in every azimuthal mode. At NPR 2.25 (M_j 1.14, β 0.55) it under-predicts the peak frequency, which the authors attribute to the Pack spacing (p. 2019–2020). In a screeching jet the real train is probably shorter and shrinking (andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets; mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes), which would raise the peak, as observed.
- **The higher-order curves are harmonics.** The note's pixel check of Fig. 3 shows them at 2.0× and 2.9× the n = 1 St at θ = 90°, i.e. L_n = L₁/n. Pack's Bessel-zero modes would give 2.30× and 3.60×. The "favourable" agreement of the secondary lobes (p. 2020) therefore supports a harmonic-like second wavenumber more than Pack's k_s2. That is consistent with the page's claim 3 that secondary shock peaks are not Pack's higher modes (nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech, p. 7–8), but the paper does not test it.
- **Convection speed across modes.** Using one u_c for all m implies that the wavepackets of all azimuthal orders convect alike (p. 2020).
- **Where the azimuthal content comes from.** In the authors' model picture the shocks are axisymmetric and stationary, so all azimuthal content of BBSAN comes from the wavepacket (p. 2022). That fits the page's KH × shock product picture. It also means wavepacket models need m up to about 4 at St ≳ 0.7 (p. 2023), while shock-cell models need only m = 0.
- **No measured spacing.** The paper gives no measured shock spacing. Mean schlieren of the 8 mm SJAF nozzle would settle the NPR 2.25 mismatch.
