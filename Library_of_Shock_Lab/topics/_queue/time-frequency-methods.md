## bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets (queued 2026-10-20)

- **New row for the intermittency-tracking table:** opposing microphones, a 200 Hz band-pass and a Hilbert transform per process. The outputs are:
  - per-NPR JPDFs of envelope and inter-microphone phase (Fig. 6);
  - the fraction of time below a μ − 5 dB threshold (Figs 7, 23);
  - event durations in screech cycles: 5–15 cycles interrupted and 20–30 cycles coupled at NPR ≥ 3.5 (Fig. 8);
  - joint amplitude histograms between microphones and between processes (Figs 9, 10, 12) (p. 10–19).
- **Exclusivity.** The joint histogram is the same exclusivity test as the JPDF of [Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), but the answer is "uncorrelated and coexisting": process 3 is steady at NPR 5.0 while processes 1 and 2 come and go (p. 16). This contrasts with the exclusive A1/A2 switching of [Mancinelli 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) (p. 6–7) and Mazharmanesh 2026 (ρ = −0.82).
- **Method caveats.**
  - The filter type and order are not given. Events of 5–15 cycles (0.5–1.5 ms at ≈ 10 kHz) are shorter than the ≈ 5 ms envelope time scale of an ideal 200 Hz band.
  - There is no beat test, although processes 1 and 2 are only ≈ 0.02 apart in St (≈ 800 Hz).
  - Each microphone is assumed to hear only its nearer jet (p. 14). Cross-talk combined with a wandering inter-jet phase could produce the corner and fan shapes on its own.
- **Null model for the pipeline checklist** (step 5, the note's derivation). A narrow-band Gaussian signal has a Rayleigh envelope with σ = 5.57 dB in dB. It spends 16–22% of the time below μ − 5 dB, and 13–27% for thresholds of 4–6 dB.
  - Process 2 has σ ≈ 5.5–6.0 dB and spends ≈ 24/20/16% below μ − 4/−5/−6 dB (Figs 23, 27), which matches.
  - So threshold-based "interruption" statistics need this null, the envelope analogue of Torrence's χ² background.
- **Without time resolution.** The paper shows how far conditional POD on POD-pair magnitudes can go (p. 23–28). But the empty corners of Fig. 21 follow partly from the selection, since |S|² + |A|² = |L|² + |U|². The real information is the distribution of inter-jet phase along the arc. HPOD_t on symmetry-split SPOD reconstructions of time-resolved schlieren would measure it directly.
- **Choice of tool.** Bell chose Hilbert over wavelets, citing Huang (2014) (p. 10), after narrow-banding each process. This is consistent with the page's point 1.

## research-question: hpod-instantaneous-frequency-uncertainty (queued 2026-10-21)

- **Current understanding, new point (after point 6): instantaneous frequency does not beat the time–frequency trade-off; it moves it into the narrow-banding step.** Hilbert IF is a time-local centroid of frequency (Cohen's identities, eq. 3.9–3.11 in [Huang 1998](../../notes/huang-1998-empirical-mode-decomposition-hilbert-spectrum.md), p. 11–12), meaningful only for one slowly modulated component (p. 12–15). The bandwidth B of whatever makes the signal single-component (Horel's W(ω), SPOD reconstruction band, Sieber's N_f, Bell's 200 Hz band) caps how fast IF can change: Sieber states the filter low-passes frequency changes ([Sieber 2016](../../notes/sieber-2016-spectral-proper-orthogonal-decomposition.md), p. 8–9), a single-bin reconstruction changes only block to block ([Nekkanti 2021](../../notes/nekkanti-2021-spod-reconstruction-denoising-frequency-time.md) note), and Raiola reads HPOD modulation from STFT spectrograms, not from dφ/dt ([Raiola 2025](../../notes/raiola-2025-hilbert-pod.md), p. 19). IF can still be very *precise* for an isolated component (SNR-limited), which is a different thing from resolution.
- **The two-tone limit reappears inside Hilbert methods:** Huang's unresolved pair cos(2πt/30) + cos(2πt/34) over 512 s (p. 45–47) has Δf ≈ 2/T, i.e. sits at the Fourier resolution limit (arithmetic in the question file). Could go in "The analytic signal" section.
- **Labelled derivations from the question (own reasoning, to be checked against Boashash 1992 / Cohen 1989 once indexed):** (a) for two tones in one band with amplitude ratio r < 1, the time-mean IF equals the *stronger* tone's frequency and the weaker one appears only as a ripple at Δf, with spikes as r → 1; this gives an exclusivity test for A1/A2 complementary to the JPDF of [Mazharmanesh 2026](../../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) (p. 11) — plateaus vs ripple; (b) a Carson-type cap, frequency excursion + modulation rate ≲ B/2. Suggested home: "Relevance to our work" checklist step 3–4.
- **Hypothesis for "Disagreements and open questions" (own reasoning):** for advecting waves, the field-of-view length L substitutes for record length (effective window ≈ L/c), via Raiola's reduction to SPOD for ω = ck (p. 11–13); consistent with Sieber's best N_f ≈ one convective transit (p. 17) and Horel's poor capture of pulses short compared with the transit time ([Horel 1984](../../notes/horel-1984-complex-principal-component-analysis.md), p. 1668–1670). Fails for multi-speed feedback flows.
- **Sharpen the open question "How fast a switch can be resolved?"** with the concrete benchmark: two advecting wavepackets 10% and 0.5% apart with prescribed switches and noise, through SPOD → band-B reconstruction → HPOD_t vs STFT, CWT and synchrosqueezing, measuring detection delay, IF bias and variance against B, SNR and L.

Full argument: [hpod-instantaneous-frequency-uncertainty](../../../Research_Questions/hpod-instantaneous-frequency-uncertainty.md).

## kriegseis-2021-hilbert-transform-revisited-hpod (queued 2026-10-21)

The "Kriegseis et al. 2021 (cited by Raiola; not in the library)" step in the **Origin** paragraph of "Hilbert POD in time and in space" can now be a link. The paper is a two-page ISPIV abstract applying HPOD_t: each point and component is made analytic along the snapshot sequence, then snapshot POD of the complex data (p. 1). It states that the analytic signal adds no information, so HPOD only regroups what POD contains (p. 1); this supports point 2. The Hilbert transform is applied to raw data with no narrow-banding (p. 1–2), but all three test flows (candle schlieren, plasma-actuator PIV, side-channel pump stereo PIV) are dominated by one periodic process, and IF is never computed, so it does not contradict point 1. No edge effects are discussed. No change to consensus or open questions.

## rilling-2008-one-or-two-frequencies-emd-two-tones (queued 2026-10-21)

Quantitative resolution limit for EMD; belongs in "The analytic signal" section next to Huang's close-tone failure, and in the Methods table (EMD row: "close tones: relative cutoff f_c ≈ 0.66 at 10 siftings"). For x = cos 2πt + a cos(2πft + φ) (f = f_LF/f_HF), ten siftings split the tones only if f < f_c ≈ 0.66–0.67, independent of a while af < 1; above f ≈ 0.75 the sum stays one waveform (Figs 2, 7, p. 6, 17). For af < 1 EMD is an equivalent linear high-pass filter, (1 − I(f))ⁿ, with cutoff f_c(n) ≈ [1 + (ln 2/n)^{1/4}]⁻¹, about 0.78 at n = 100 and 0.89 at n = 3000 (eq. 8–9, p. 11–12); record length does not enter (p. 4–5). **This explains [Huang 1998](../../notes/huang-1998-empirical-mode-decomposition-hilbert-spectrum.md)'s failure (p. 45–47)**: f = 30/34 = 0.88 needs about 2200 siftings for half separation (indexing agent's calculation), matching Huang's "up to 3000"; the cause is the frequency *ratio*, not Δf·T ≈ 2. When the low tone dominates the curvature (af² > 1) EMD never separates, and near f = 1/(2k) extrema sampling creates a spurious component at f_a = 2k_f f − 1 with its own IF (eq. 12, p. 13–15), a pitfall for Hilbert–Huang analysis of tonal jet signals. EMD's resolution is constant-Q and quasi-dyadic for noise (p. 3, 17), far too coarse for A1/A2 (f ≈ 0.91) or twin-jet symmetries; SPOD reconstruction ([Nekkanti 2021](../../notes/nekkanti-2021-spod-reconstruction-denoising-frequency-time.md)) is the right narrow-banding step. For "Harmonics: physical modes or intrawave modulation?": in the af² > 1 domain EMD keeps a fundamental plus odd harmonics as one IMF and the authors present this as a feature (p. 8, 14, 18), siding with Huang.

## daubechies-2011-synchrosqueezed-wavelet-transforms-emd (queued 2026-10-21)

First proof that an EMD-like decomposition recovers each component's IF and amplitude. Synchrosqueezing reassigns CWT energy along frequency only, using ω(a, b) = −i∂_bW/W (eq. 2.2–2.7, p. 6–7). Theorem 3.3 (p. 8–9): for sums of AM–FM components with slow modulation (|A′|, |φ″| ≤ εφ′) and relative gap d, with a wavelet of Fourier half-width Δ < d/(1 + d), the frequency estimate is within ε^{1/3} of φ′_k at every time. Supports point 1 formally: Lemma 3.6 (p. 11) is "at most one component per passband", and ω(a, b) is the analytic-signal IF of the band-passed signal averaged over the wavelet (eq. 2.11, 2.14, p. 7), so synchrosqueezing is a filter bank followed by Hilbert IF, the formal analogue of SPOD reconstruction → HPOD. Supports point 6: the wavelet's relative bandwidth must be below the tones' relative gap; synchrosqueezing does not move energy in time (p. 6), so it sharpens frequency, not time, and fails where components cross (Fig. 8–9, p. 17–18). A default Morlet (≈ 17% bandwidth, [Torrence 1998](../../notes/torrence-1998-practical-guide-wavelet-analysis.md) note) would not split A1/A2. New Methods-table row: "Synchrosqueezed CWT (Daubechies 2011) | one time-resolved series or temporal coefficient | sharp IF ridges, per-component reconstruction | relative gap must exceed wavelet bandwidth; time resolution still the wavelet's; fails at crossings". The non-uniqueness example (eq. 1.3, p. 4: three tones = one AM tone, "no universal solution") supports treating the Huang and BMD readings of harmonics as alternative decompositions. Add synchrosqueezing to the switch-benchmark open question.
