## rilling-2008-one-or-two-frequencies-emd-two-tones (queued 2026-10-21)

## boashash-1992-instantaneous-frequency-fundamentals (queued 2026-10-21)

This is the signal-processing source behind Current understanding point 1 and the section "The analytic signal: when instantaneous frequency means something", which so far rest on Huang 1998. It defines IF as Ville's (1/2π) d arg z/dt of Gabor's analytic signal (eq. 18, p. 522). It shows that IF is the first conditional frequency moment of the Wigner–Ville distribution and of every Cohen-class TFD whose kernel satisfies g(ν, 0) = 1 and ∂g/∂τ|₀ = 0 (eq. 22, 74–75, p. 522, 533), and gives an instantaneous bandwidth |a′/a|/2π about it (eq. 81, p. 534). Its bandwidth decomposition (Mandel, eq. 82–83, p. 534) is the same identity as Huang's eq. 3.11. It supplies the exact conditions that Huang gives only qualitatively:
- amplitude and phase are separable only if the spectra of a(t) and cos φ(t) do not overlap (Bedrosian, p. 525, 536–537);
- the Hilbert transform approximates the quadrature signal as the carrier f₀ → ∞ (Nuttall) or as BT → ∞ for a monotonic positive IF (Jones) (p. 529);
- IF is a physical time–frequency law, the inverse of the group delay, only for BT ≫ 1 (BT > 10 is assumed) and a monotonic IF (p. 526–528; Fig. 6: BT = 3.2 vs 125; Figs. 10–11: BT = 0.4 vs 25.6).

"Monocomponent" is defined strictly as an invertible IF law, and a non-monotonic law is drawn as multicomponent (Def. 1–2, Fig. 3, p. 527). This is stricter than Huang's IMF criterion. The decomposition into components is non-unique and application dependent (p. 527, 535). Mandel's two-tone example (eq. 28–30, p. 523) is the closed-form version of point 8 (beats): the IF stays on the stronger tone's side of the midpoint and is "outside the scope" of the definition. Vakman's paradox (IF is local but needs the whole record through the HT, p. 536) is the root of the end effects in point 5. It does not overturn the consensus; it puts point 1 on a formal footing and adds two things:
- the BT ≫ 1 requirement;
- an IF-versus-group-delay consistency check.

For the open question "how fast a switch can be resolved", it offers the BT condition and Rihaczek's relaxation time T_r = |df_i/dt|^{−1/2} (eq. 77, p. 533), but no benchmark. It also notes that a positive TFD (STFT, and by extension a scalogram) gives only a smeared IF (p. 534), which qualifies how wavelet ridges are read.

Papers line (1992, after Horel 1984): "[Boashash 1992](../notes/boashash-1992-instantaneous-frequency-fundamentals.md): IF tutorial. Carson–Fry to Ville definitions; Bedrosian/Nuttall conditions for the analytic signal; IF as first moment of TFDs and inverse of group delay only for BT ≫ 1; mono- vs multicomponent; instantaneous bandwidth (synthesis pending)."

## cohen-1989-time-frequency-distributions-review (queued 2026-10-21)

Cohen 1989 is the formal source behind point 1 and the queued research-question point: every bilinear time–frequency distribution is the Wigner kernel weighted by φ(θ, τ), and each property is a kernel constraint (marginals need φ(θ, 0) = φ(0, τ) = 1, reality φ = φ*(−θ, −τ); Table 1–2, p. 943, 952–955). The instantaneous frequency is the conditional mean frequency, ⟨ω⟩_t = φ′(t) for every kernel with φ(θ, 0) = 1 and ∂φ/∂τ|₀ = 0 (Wigner, Choi–Williams, Cohen–Lee; eq. 4.30, p. 954), and it equals the Hilbert IF only when the analytic signal is used; group delay is the dual conditional mean time (eq. 4.47, p. 955). The identities that [Huang 1998](../notes/huang-1998-empirical-mode-decomposition-hilbert-spectrum.md) quotes as "Cohen's" (Huang eq. 3.9–3.11) are Cohen's eq. 8.7–8.9 (p. 968–969): the spectral bandwidth² equals the energy-weighted spread of the IF plus ∫A′²dt, and the second term is the time average of the only positive local spread available, (A′/A)² for kernels with φ″(0) = ¼ (eq. 4.39, p. 955); this agrees with [Boashash 1992](../notes/boashash-1992-instantaneous-frequency-fundamentals.md) (p. 532–534). Cohen states that the uncertainty principle concerns only the spreads of the two marginals and has no bearing on joint distributions or on measurements at small times and frequencies (eq. 8.10–8.13, p. 970–971); the trade-off in a spectrogram is the *window's* (p. 963), since a spectrogram is the signal's distribution smoothed by the window's (eq. 6.29–6.30, p. 965). That supports rewording point 6 as "each method's window parameter sets its tile", not as a property of the signal, and agrees with [Torrence 1998](../notes/torrence-1998-practical-guide-wavelet-analysis.md) and [Daubechies 2011](../notes/daubechies-2011-synchrosqueezed-wavelet-transforms-emd.md). For multicomponent signals every bilinear distribution has cross terms, even with the analytic signal (p. 969): the Wigner puts 2A₁A₂cos Δωt at the midpoint frequency (eq. 5.23, p. 960), and Choi–Williams spreads it over a width ∝ |Δω|/√σ (eq. 3.89–3.91, p. 951). Feeding either into the conditional mean reproduces the two-tone IF ripple of point 8 and of the research question (the note's algebra). Practical additions: narrow-band before any Wigner or IF step in noise, because Nuttall shows the Wigner variance in white noise is infinite otherwise (p. 962–963); and for separating two components, Jones & Parks found a spectrogram with an optimally matched window better than the Wigner or smoothed Wigner (p. 977). This changes no consensus point. It supplies the citation for point 1, tightens point 6, and suggests a Methods-table row: "Wigner–Ville / Choi–Williams (Cohen 1989) | time-resolved series or temporal coefficients (analytic signal, or 2× Nyquist if real) | exact conditional-mean IF and group delay; reduced cross terms with CW | cross terms, negative values, nonlocal noise spreading, uninterpretable local spread".

## huang-2009-on-instantaneous-frequency (queued 2026-10-21)

This paper belongs in "The analytic signal: when instantaneous frequency means something", as the Hilbert–Huang group's own account of IF and its limits. It argues that the uncertainty principle (stated as Δω ΔT ≥ 1/2, p. 184) binds only integral transforms, so a phase derivative computed without one is not limited by it (p. 178). It also separates the two conditions behind point 1 of "Current understanding":
- *Bedrosian* (envelope and carrier spectra disjoint, eq. 24, p. 189), which IMFs violate in general unless band-passed (p. 189, 205);
- *Nuttall* (H{cos φ} = sin φ, eq. 25–28, p. 190), which depends on the phase function's structure and survives normalisation.

Its fix is a spline-based empirical AM–FM split of an IMF, iterated two or three times until |F| ≤ 1 (eq. 29–33, p. 191–195). IF then comes either from the HT of the unit-amplitude carrier (NHT), with a local error index [|z| − 1]² (eq. 36, p. 198), or from a "direct quadrature" √(1 − F²) with no transform (eq. 34–35, p. 196).

On a damped chirp Duffing-type model with known IF (eq. 42):
- DQ matches the truth;
- NHT removes negative IF but under-modulates the intrawave swing (≈ 0.018–0.037 vs 0.011–0.046 Hz, Fig. 17, p. 208);
- the raw HT goes negative (≈ −0.06 Hz);
- TEO collapses wherever the waveform is distorted;
- GZC gives only a quarter-period mean (p. 205–211).

This extends [Huang 1998](../notes/huang-1998-empirical-mode-decomposition-hilbert-spectrum.md) (the α + sin t picture, eq. 21–23 here). It also withdraws Huang 1998's 3000-sifting two-tone split as "unnecessary and unsatisfactory" (p. 219). Instead it treats beating tones as one AM–FM component, carrier (a+b)/2 with the envelope at (a−b)/2 (eq. 44, p. 220), with the second frequency recovered from an "AM spectrum" (p. 221–222). That agrees with [Rilling 2008](../notes/rilling-2008-one-or-two-frequencies-emd-two-tones.md) on what EMD does, while disagreeing on whether one should separate. It also fits the page's beat test (point 8).

For the pipeline, two points matter:
- The paper says band-passed signals satisfy Bedrosian and Nuttall automatically (p. 191), which supports SPOD reconstruction before HPOD.
- HPOD's IF is the analytic-signal IF, whose locality the paper leaves unquantified (p. 224). Its "fully local" claims apply to DQ, not to HPOD.

Suggested additions:
- a Methods-table row: "NHT / direct quadrature (Huang 2009) | one IMF or narrow-band time-resolved series | IF with sub-period detail, local error index | needs a clean IMF; DQ unstable with sparse samples (≲ 20 per period) and noise; NHT under-modulates intrawave IF";
- GZC as a cheap reference: any IF that differs grossly from it in the mean "cannot be correct" (p. 202);
- TEO as a nonlinearity detector (p. 201, 213).

It does not overturn the consensus. It sharpens the open question "Harmonics: physical modes or intrawave modulation?": the authors themselves call the harmonic and intrawave descriptions "the same data from two different viewpoints" (p. 223), chosen for familiarity, not correctness (p. 221–222).

## rife-1974-single-tone-parameter-estimation-discrete-time (queued 2026-10-21)

Rife & Boorstyn give the noise-limited **precision** benchmark that the page so far lacks. For one complex tone in white Gaussian noise, the Cramér–Rao bound with unknown phase is var{ω̂} ≥ 12σ²/[b₀²T²N(N²−1)] (T = sampling interval, eq. 17, p. 592). Rearranged, σ_f·T_rec ≈ 0.39/√(ρN), with ρ = b₀²/2σ² (eq. 47): frequency can be known to a small fraction of the Fourier bin 1/T_rec, while the resolution (the ±1-bin Dirichlet mainlobe, eq. 41–43, p. 595) does not change with SNR. The ML estimator is the peak of the zero-padded periodogram |A(ω)| (M = 2–4N), refined by a secant search (p. 593–595), so careful FFT-peak interpolation is near-optimal (p. 598). The precision holds only above a threshold: below a bin SNR Nρ of about 9–14 dB (q = 10⁻¹–10⁻³ from eq. 60; Fig. 3–4, p. 596–597), noise peaks win and the error jumps to a uniform-guess level. The per-sample threshold falls by about 2.5–2.9 dB per doubling of N. The single-tone model is the same monocomponent condition that [Huang 1998](../huang-1998-empirical-mode-decomposition-hilbert-spectrum.md) (p. 12–15) and Boashash 1992 impose on Hilbert IF. The two-tone and real-tone cases are deferred to Rife's thesis (p. 591, 598), so the *resolution* side stays with [Rilling 2008](../rilling-2008-one-or-two-frequencies-emd-two-tones.md) and [Daubechies 2011](../daubechies-2011-synchrosqueezed-wavelet-transforms-emd.md). Suggested changes to the page: (a) add to "Current understanding" that frequency precision (SNR × record-length limited) and resolution (1/T_rec or filter bandwidth limited) are different quantities, so a precise IF trace does not beat the uncertainty principle; (b) add to the pipeline checklist that a frequency estimate from an HPOD or SPOD-reconstructed phase should be a phase fit over a window whose bin SNR is above about 14 dB, attributed to the window centre (eq. 21, p. 592, 597). A plain average of dθ/dt is about √(N/6) worse than the bound (my derivation). It does not overturn anything on the page. It sharpens the open question "How fast a switch can be resolved?": for *exclusive* states, the window only needs σ_f ≪ Δf, which can be far shorter than 1/Δf.

## bedrosian-1963-product-theorem-hilbert-transforms (queued 2026-10-21)

Bedrosian 1963 is the exact, primary statement behind Current understanding point 1, which the page now rests on Huang 1998's qualitative "amplitude slowly varying compared with the phase" (Huang p. 15). The theorem (eq. 1, p. 868) says H[fg] = f·H[g] when f is low-pass and g high-pass with non-overlapping spectra, or when both are analytic. For a carrier, H[r cos(ω₀t + φ)] = r sin(ω₀t + φ), so the analytic signal returns the envelope r and the phase exactly, if the spectrum of r lies below ω₀ (p. 869). This sharpens rather than contradicts Huang: the requirement is spectral separation, not slowness. In my numerical check, an AM envelope modulated at any rate up to the carrier frequency is returned to round-off, with IF exactly f₀. The theorem says nothing about the number of components: a sum of two positive-frequency tones is analytic, so its Hilbert envelope (the beat of point 8) is exact. The beat is a property of the signal, not a transform error, and the monocomponent requirement stays a separate condition (Huang 1998, p. 12–14; Boashash 1992, p. 527). The 1963 letter covers only constant φ; for phase modulation its footnote (p. 869) claims no restriction, which holds only for r and φ defined through the analytic signal. Nuttall 1966 gives the exact condition for imposed AM and PM. The page's "Sign conventions differ" paragraph can add that eq. 3 here uses H[e^{ibx}] = +i sgn(b)e^{ibx} (Erdélyi's convention, H[cos] = −sin), opposite to Nuttall 1966 and MATLAB, though the AM paragraph of the same letter uses H[cos] = +sin. Suggested wording change for point 1: "needs a single component, whose modulation spectrum does not reach below zero frequency; narrow band is sufficient, not necessary". It does not overturn the page's consensus.

Papers line (1963, first in the list, before Horel 1984): "[Bedrosian 1963](../notes/bedrosian-1963-product-theorem-hilbert-transforms.md): product theorem H[fg] = f·H[g] for low-pass × high-pass factors with disjoint spectra, or two analytic factors; exact Hilbert envelope and phase of an AM carrier when the envelope's band lies below the carrier (synthesis pending)."

## nuttall-1966-quadrature-approximation-hilbert-transform-modulated-signals (queued 2026-10-21)

Nuttall 1966 gives the necessary and sufficient condition and the exact error for reading an imposed amplitude r(t) and phase φ(t) off the analytic signal. The Hilbert transform of r cos(2πf₀t + φ) equals r sin(2πf₀t + φ) iff the spectrum F of r e^{iφ} vanishes below −f₀, and otherwise the error energy is 2∫_{−∞}^{−f₀}|F|² df (p. 1459). The letter states that x need *not* be narrow band; F must be single-sided. Bedrosian's reply calls narrow-band signals "merely a special case" and generalises his 1963 product theorem to one-sided spectra (p. 1459). This corrects the wording of Current understanding point 1 ("needs a narrow-band signal"): narrow-banding is needed so that each band holds one component (Huang 1998, p. 12–14), not for the transform to be exact. The error spectrum is concentrated near zero frequency (p. 1459). My first-order derivation shows it appears as a carrier-rate ripple of relative size |d|/r in envelope and IF, largest where the envelope dips. That ties it to Huang's offset-circle picture (α + sin t, p. 12–13) and to Horel's warning that the transform emphasises spikes and edges (p. 1662). For the open question "how fast a switch can be resolved", my checks show the Hilbert step alone tolerates modulation up to about one carrier period: AM exact up to f_m = f₀; FM error small while Δf + f_m ≲ f₀; Gaussian bursts essentially exact for σ_t ≳ 0.5 T₀. So the reconstruction or filter band, not the Hilbert transform, sets the time resolution, consistent with Sieber 2016 (p. 8–9, 16–17) and the Nekkanti note. Nuttall's E is also an exact error metric for the synthetic switch benchmark the page proposes, where the imposed r and φ are known. Boashash 1992 restates the result as his Theorem 1 (p. 529).

## gabor-1946-theory-of-communication (queued 2026-10-21)

Gabor 1946 is the primary source for both foundations of the page: the analytic signal and the time–frequency uncertainty relation. The analytic signal is built by suppressing negative-frequency amplitudes and doubling positive ones; σ is the Hilbert transform of s (eq. 1.6–1.8, p. 432–433). The relation is Δt·Δf ≥ ½, with Δt and Δf defined as √(2π) times the rms spreads of |ψ|² and |φ|² about their centroids (eq. 1.21–1.26, p. 434), i.e. σ_t σ_f ≥ 1/4π. Equality holds only for the Gaussian logon (p. 435, 440–441).

It sharpens Current understanding point 1. Gabor introduced the complex signal to make the frequency centroid non-zero (with a real signal it is always zero, p. 433–434), not to define instantaneous frequency (IF). He comments on IF only in a footnote: Carson and Fry's IF is "useful for slowly-varying frequencies" but not general (p. 431). He also declines to draw an FM path as a sharp curve: it "has to be thick and blurred", and he represents it by logons of area ½ whose aspect ratio is a convention of the analysis (Fig. 1.12, p. 438). The phase-derivative IF is Ville's (1948; [boashash-1992](../notes/boashash-1992-instantaneous-frequency-fundamentals.md)).

Gabor's centroid formula (eq. 1.18) and second-moment formula (eq. 1.20) lead directly to the Cohen identities quoted by [Huang 1998](../notes/huang-1998-empirical-mode-decomposition-hilbert-spectrum.md) (eq. 3.9–3.11). The mean frequency is the amplitude-weighted mean of the phase derivative, and Δf² is an amplitude-modulation term plus the spread of the phase derivative (derivation in the note). So the page can trace the "IF is a conditional mean" reading back to Gabor's moments.

For point 6 (one parameter sets each method's trade-off), Gabor gives the counting rule. A band B over time T carries 2BT real data, one per area ½ (p. 430, 434–435), i.e. one complex amplitude per 1/B per band in the lattice (Gabor) expansion (eq. 1.29, p. 435–436). The shortest signal an ideal band B can pass has Δt ≈ 1.25/B and lasts about 2/B (eq. 1.37, p. 437–438).

Part 3's Gaussian-window frequency convertor anticipates the windowed-Fourier side of the page. The Gaussian window gives the smallest product of linked uncertainties (p. 447), and the time-resolution vs frequency-reproduction compromise "is a matter for experiment" (T ≈ 100 ms for speech, p. 448).

Part 2 offers a precedent for the "precision vs resolution" distinction in the research-question section. To explain pitch thresholds far finer than the ear's resonance width, Gabor posits a mechanism that "locates the centre of the resonance region with a precision increasing with the duration of the stimulus" (p. 444). Yet the measured thresholds never fall below 2.3 elementary areas (p. 443).

Sign conventions: the text maps cos → sin (MATLAB's convention). As read from the scan, however, the printed kernel of eq. 1.7 (dτ/(τ − t)) gives cos → −sin. This adds to the sign-convention caveats already on the page.

None of this changes the page's consensus. It supplies the primary citations for the queued research-question contribution (IF vs the uncertainty principle) and for the analytic-signal construction.

## research-question: hpod-instantaneous-frequency-uncertainty (queued 2026-10-21)

- **Current understanding, new point (after point 6): instantaneous frequency does not beat the time–frequency trade-off; it moves it into the narrow-banding step.** The uncertainty principle bounds the marginals and any windowed estimate, not a centroid or the local spread of one component ([Cohen 1989](../../notes/cohen-1989-time-frequency-distributions-review.md), eq. 8.10–8.13, p. 970–971; spectrogram limit eq. 6.5–6.7, p. 963). IF is the conditional mean frequency (Cohen eq. 4.24–4.30, p. 954; [Boashash 1992](../../notes/boashash-1992-instantaneous-frequency-fundamentals.md), eq. 73–75, p. 533), meaningful only for one component (Boashash p. 527; [Huang 2009](../../notes/huang-2009-on-instantaneous-frequency.md), p. 185, 224). The narrow-banding bandwidth sets separation: relative gap > relative filter bandwidth ([Daubechies 2011](../../notes/daubechies-2011-synchrosqueezed-wavelet-transforms-emd.md), Theorem 3.3, Lemma 3.6, p. 8–11), and the RMS IF excursion and envelope rate satisfy σ_IF² + ⟨(A′/A)²⟩ ≤ (B/2)² (Cohen eq. 8.9, p. 969; Boashash eq. 82–83, p. 534). The Hilbert step's own floor is about one carrier period ([Nuttall & Bedrosian 1966](../../notes/nuttall-1966-quadrature-approximation-hilbert-transform-modulated-signals.md), p. 1459), so the filter binds first. IF *precision* for an isolated tone is far finer than 1/T above an SNR threshold ([Rife & Boorstyn 1974](../../notes/rife-1974-single-tone-parameter-estimation-discrete-time.md), eq. 17, 60, p. 592, 596–597).
- **Point 1 refinement:** separate *exactness* (analytic signal returns a and θ iff the complex modulation is one-sided: [Bedrosian 1963](../../notes/bedrosian-1963-product-theorem-hilbert-transforms.md), Nuttall 1966) from *meaningfulness* (one component). A two-tone sum passes exactness, so narrow-banding is needed for meaning, not exactness.
- **Two tones in one band:** IF = θ′ = ω₁ + Δω(r² + r cos Δωt)/(1 + r² + 2r cos Δωt) (Mandel, Boashash eq. 30, p. 523; Cohen's two-tone Wigner conditional mean, eq. 5.23). Labelled derivations from the question: unweighted mean IF = stronger tone; negative IF only if r·ω_weak > ω_strong. Gives an A1/A2 exclusivity test (plateaus vs ripple at Δf) complementary to the JPDF of [Mazharmanesh 2026](../../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) (p. 11). Exclusive switching is detectable faster than 1/Δf (precision), coexistence is not.
