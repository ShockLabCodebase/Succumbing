# Axis switching and the evolving mean flow

_Last updated: 2026-10-21 · 12 papers_

## Scope
How the mean flow of rectangular and elliptical jets evolves downstream, above all axis switching (the jet's major and minor axes trading places), and how it interacts with the large-scale oscillation. The page covers where the switch sits and what moves it (NPR, screech, forcing, heating, nozzle edges); the streamwise vortices, streaks and corner vortices invoked to explain it; the modes that appear across the switch (bi-axial flapping, a "new flapping" mode, the quasi-helical pair); and the two-way coupling in which each screech tone holds its own mean flow.

Mode shapes upstream of the switch, the symmetry-breaking argument for flapping, single-jet symmetry decompositions, frequency models and trailing-edge control are on [non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md); twin jets are on [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md); stage jumps and mode switching on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md); mean-flow deformation and triadic interactions in round screeching jets on [screech](screech.md). Local stability on measured mean flows is on [linear-models](linear-models.md), and resolvent, global and OWNS analyses on [linear-operator-methods](linear-operator-methods.md).

**Conventions.** *Flapping* is oscillation across the nozzle's minor axis (se₁; AS in the D2 labels of [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md)) and *wagging* the same motion in the major-axis plane (ce₁; SA), as on [non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md). The labels are fixed to the nozzle, so after the switch nozzle-fixed flapping is wagging relative to the local mean flow (p. 11). Papers locate the switch differently: equal half-widths, which come several diameters before a full 90° rotation (p. 6; [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md)), a fitted flow aspect ratio of 1 ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)), the crossing of fitted spreading lines ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md); [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md)), the mean shock cells ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md)) or a fitted shock-free mean flow ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md)). Compare trends rather than positions across metrics.

## Current understanding
1. **Axis switching follows the large-scale oscillation.** The switch point moves downstream as NPR rises, but strong screech or forcing pulls it upstream, and shock-free, unforced jets switch late or not at all ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 133; [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 135–136; [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4216; [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 6); heating pushes it downstream in LES ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 13).
2. **The action is in the minor-axis plane.** Screech or forcing speeds minor-axis spreading while the major-axis width barely changes or contracts ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 123; [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 136), and the contraction is an inward shift of the shear layer, not a thinning ([Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 8).
3. **The mechanism is inferred, not demonstrated.** Stationary counter-rotating streamwise vortex pairs, grown from deformed screech-driven rolls, are the proposed driver ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 136–140, 144). Trailing-edge cutouts that make streamwise vortices switch the jet only off-design ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 6–7), and linear resolvent analysis yields dipole streaks but probably not corner vortices ([Marshall 2024](../notes/marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets.md), p. 13–17).
4. **The switched jet supports different modes.** Downstream of the switch, wagging or slower antisymmetric structures appear that the upstream jet does not support ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2745–2747; [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 11–14; [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 13–15). Because the symmetry families are orthogonal, nozzle-fixed flapping cannot turn into wagging linearly (p. 13–14), so a "bi-axial" mode may be two structures.
5. **Mean flow and tone select each other.** Mutually exclusive tones each hold their own short-time mean ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 14–15), and the evolving mean flow decides where the closing guided mode can exist ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 22–24). Heated LES moves the switch downstream while its tone grows ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 13, 18–19), so screech amplitude alone does not fix the switch point (my reading).

## Where the switch sits and what moves it
Screech reshapes these jets. At peak screech (NPR 3.8) the AR 16.7 jet spreads at 36° in the minor-axis plane, against 20–25° at other NPRs, and its axis crossover moves well upstream; otherwise the crossover moves downstream as NPR rises, and the jet is still not axisymmetric at 160 D ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 123, 133, 140–142). Without strong tones the minor-axis half-width grows at k = 0.105 at NPR 2.7 but 0.08 at NPR 5.4, a drop the authors put down to density ratio, while the major-axis half-width shrinks out to about 80 D before growing (p. 132–133). In an AR 4 jet at M_j 1.69, screech raises the minor-axis spreading rate from 0.065 (ideally expanded, no switch) to 0.11 over 5–20h and 0.33 beyond 21h, with the axes crossing at 16h ≈ 4AR, as in incompressible rectangular jets ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 135–136); the major-axis half-width hardly varies, so the dynamics are set in the minor-axis plane (p. 136). B-mode (flapping/helical) screech raises mass flux by about 60 % and the other stages do not (Zaman, cited by [Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md), p. 94, 97). AR 3 Mach 2 jets with trailing-edge cutouts switch by 8 D_eq only off-design, at M_j 2.5, and not at all up to 15 D_eq without an expansion mismatch ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 6–7); even the baseline nozzle shows corner-vortex deformation only at M_j 2.5 (p. 4, 6).

In AR 2 jets the switch lies inside the screech region. The group's ellipse has equal half-widths at x/D ≈ 4.3 at NPR 2.2 ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2745), and its fitted flow AR (about 1.7 at the nozzle, not 2) reaches 1 near x/D ≈ 5 at NPR 2.6 and farther downstream at 3.4 ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 10–11); the metrics differ, so the trend is the note's comparison. In the sharp-throat rectangular jet, the switch point of a fitted shock-free mean flow moves from 3.1 to 5.4 D_eq between NPR 2.5 and 3.7 (ideal expansion, where every tone is weakest), then back to 4.8 D_eq at NPR 4.5 ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4216), a retreat that fits Krothapalli's link between strong screech and early switching (the note's reading). Heating moves the switch downstream in LES of an AR 2 C-D jet, from 7.5h to 11h (4.7–6.9 D_eq), although the simulated tone grows with heating; the measured tone falls ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 13, 18–19).

[Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md) isolate the effect of the oscillation in a shock-free, low-Re elliptical-jet DNS forced near the nozzle at three levels. Stronger forcing moves the switch point upstream: none, then x/D_eq ≈ 12, then ≈ 8 (p. 6). The most strongly forced jet ends as an ellipse of higher aspect ratio than the nozzle, rotated by 90°, and starts spreading in the major axis again near the end of the domain, so jets can switch more than once (p. 6). Upstream of x/D_eq ≈ 5 the vorticity thickness is the same in both planes and grows as √x; forcing breaks that scaling, with the minor-axis layer growing faster while the major-axis one levels off, so the major-axis contraction is a shift of the shear layer, not a thinning (p. 7–8).

## Streamwise vortices, streaks and corner vortices
[Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md) give the mechanism most often cited, from cross-plane stereo PIV of the AR 4 jet. Streamwise vorticity at x/h = 6 is put down to nozzle imperfections (p. 136). It grows quickly in the curved shear layer, and by x/h = 16 two counter-rotating streamwise pairs dominate, with traces of the corner vortices; the authors identify them with the "ribs" that form as screech-driven spanwise rolls deform (p. 136–140). The pairs' self-induced outward velocity in the flapping plane pushes the major-axis sides out and raises minor-axis spreading (p. 144), and the authors attribute the switch to these stationary pairs (p. 136–140, 144). Two caveats: the pairs are an order of magnitude weaker than the spanwise structures (p. 144), and the causal chain is taken from earlier elliptic-jet work rather than shown dynamically (the note's reading). Streamwise vortices are also visible in the major-axis schlieren view of the AR 16.7 jet for x/D < 10, their spacing growing downstream ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 123–124). Trailing-edge cutouts generate such vortices on purpose, but only off-design, where they raise mixing by up to about 50 % ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 7–8); their vortices are inferred from the deformation of the marked mixing region rather than measured (p. 3).

Linear theory has been asked whether these structures are streaks amplified by the mean shear. Cross-plane resolvent analysis ([Marshall 2024](../notes/marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets.md), preprint, not validated) predicts AS/SA dipole streaks in rectangular jets. Sharpening the corners changes the gains by no more than 2×, so corner vortices are probably not linear streaks (p. 13–17). In round jets the influence runs the other way: weak |m| = 2 streaks fix the flapping axis, and as they rotate slowly the flapping plane precesses with them ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 10, 12, 20–24; the symmetry-breaking argument is on [non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md)). Streamwise structures can therefore be both a product of the oscillation and a selector of it.

## Modes across the switch
In the AR 2 rectangular jet at NPR 4.0, the mean shock cells switch axes at x/D_e ≈ 6.5, the fourth cell. The St 0.27 "bi-axial flapping" mode's envelope peaks at 6.5 D_e in the minor-axis view and at 8.1 D_e in the major-axis view, and sub-domain SPOD shows flapping upstream of the switch and wagging downstream ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 11–14); two other tones at the same NPR are unaffected (p. 15–17). The sharp-throat jet's wagging tone likewise sits at x/D_eq ≈ 5–8, downstream of its switch point ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4215–4216); the parallel is the note's, and the paper does not cite Mazharmanesh.

The forced DNS shows what happens to each symmetry family ([Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md)). Nozzle-fixed flapping saturates earlier and decays more steeply with forcing, sharply near the switch point, where it becomes wagging relative to the local mean flow and LSA predicts slower growth (p. 11). The effects are strongest at low St, whose wavepackets are longest (p. 12). At low St a "new flapping" mode in the SA family is born at the switch point with no upstream support (p. 13–15), and AS flapping cannot become SA linearly, because the symmetry families are orthogonal (p. 13–14). Sub-domain SPOD separates it from the ordinary wagging wavepacket, which keeps its coherence across the switch while its wavenumber rises; the authors read the new mode as an instability of the switched mean flow, not re-energised wagging (p. 15). The bi-axial mode may therefore be two coexisting structures rather than one wavepacket that rotates.

The helical-looking thread starts here too. In the AR 2 ellipse at NPR 2.2, [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) find a second antisymmetric POD pair in both planes, uncorrelated with the flapping pair and of twice its wavelength (2.4D against 1.2D); it saturates near x/D ≈ 7 and is linked to the shock-driven axis switch (p. 2745–2747). They call it helical, against the axis-locked modes of Morris & Bhat's stability analysis for a non-switching base flow (p. 2740, 2745), and explain its lack of a tone by its growing only after the shock train ends (p. 2747–2748); its St 0.27 is inferred from the wavelength and an assumed U_c (p. 2747). The planes were measured separately, so the π/2 phase lock of a helix is untested, and the major-axis pair grows only after x/D ≈ 3.5, which fits Suzuki's mode born at the switch point just as well. Where the flow AR passes through 1, se₁ and ce₁ become nearly degenerate again, so flapping and wagging could combine into helix-like motion or coexist as separate structures (both the note's reading). Later papers trace the quasi-helical thread to this pair ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 3, 20–21); Mazharmanesh's elliptical "quasi-helical" mode is antisymmetric from every view with a flat λ_s, and confirming that it is helical needs cross-plane data (p. 16–19).

## Each tone its own mean flow
The coupling runs both ways. In the AR 2 elliptical jet at NPR 3.6, two flapping tones (St 0.269 and 0.314) are mutually exclusive (ρ = −0.92), and each holds its own short-time mean, with shock cells contracted under one tone and extended under the other ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 14–15). The loop closes on k_s1 up to NPR 3.3 and on k_s2 from NPR 3.4 to 4.9 (p. 21–22), matching a stage jump in an AR 2 elliptical jet that keeps the dominant symmetry ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 18; mode switching in general is on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md)).

The evolving mean flow also limits the linear models. In the elliptical jet the guided-mode band shifts with shear-layer thickness, and at the screech St the mode is neutral only over a short streamwise stretch, where the measured guided-mode amplitude peaks ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 22–24). In the rectangular jet a weakest-link model works on a fitted shock-free mean flow that spreads, but a constant-profile base flow works only at low NPR ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4220). Both are locally parallel, so neither can follow a mode through the switch; that needs OWNS or global analysis on the switched mean flow ([linear-operator-methods](linear-operator-methods.md)).

## Disagreements and open questions
- **Bi-axial and quasi-helical modes: one wavepacket that changes plane, or two structures?** The helicity of the original pair was never tested across planes ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md)). Suzuki's orthogonality argument favours two structures, but in a shock-free, low-Re jet, and the downstream wagging tone of [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md) fits the same picture (the note's reading); whether that jet's four loops coexist or alternate is untested, because its analysis is block-averaged. The tone frequencies at elliptical NPR 3.6 also differ between [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) (0.28, 0.32) and [Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) (0.269, 0.273, 0.314). *To settle:* cross-plane or volumetric data, simultaneous two-view schlieren with sub-domain SPOD, and OWNS or global analysis on the axis-switched mean flow.
- **Cause and effect with the mean flow.** Does screech move the axis switch and pin the streaks, or do they select the mode ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md); [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md); [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 25)? [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md) argue the first and predict that exciting an ideally expanded jet should make it switch and that suppressing screech should remove the crossover (p. 140, 153). Suzuki's forced DNS supports the first prediction, but heated LES moves the switch downstream while its tone grows ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md)). *To settle:* forced LES with energy-flux budgets.
- **Corner vortices.** They are not linear streaks according to [Marshall 2024](../notes/marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets.md) (p. 17); a resolvent analysis on an LES mean flow is the proposed test. The matching measurement is cross-plane stereo PIV, which in the AR 4 jet shows counter-rotating streamwise pairs with traces of the corner vortices at x/h = 16 ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 136–140).
- **Where is the switch?** Half-widths, fitted flow aspect ratio, spreading-line crossings, shock cells and fitted shock-free mean flows give different positions for the same jet, and the half-width definition leads the full rotation by several diameters ([Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 6), so trends across the group's ellipse and rectangular jets are comparisons between metrics (the Ivelja note). *To settle:* apply one or two metrics to all the group's PIV mean flows.

## Methods and data
- **Time-resolved data** (high-speed schlieren, LES, DNS): sub-domain SPOD across the switch ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 11–14), done in DNS by giving zero weight to the region before or after the switch point, after a D2 split ([Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 5, 14); forcing at several levels to vary the oscillation independently of the nozzle (p. 6), and vorticity thickness to tell shear-layer growth from shear-layer shift (p. 7–8); short-time means conditioned on the active tone, with BMD for the triads ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md)).
- **Non-time-resolved data** (PIV, spark or 1 Hz schlieren): half-width growth in both planes ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 132–133; [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 135–136); cross-plane stereo PIV for streamwise vortices (p. 136–140); a fitted flow aspect ratio ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 10–11) or a fitted shock-free 3-D mean flow, which doubles as an LSA base flow ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4215–4216). Two planes measured separately lose the cross-plane phase that a helix test needs ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md)), and the mean image of a flapping jet looks artificially thick ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 7).

## Relevance to our work
- **Group rigs.** In the group's AR 2 ellipse and rectangular jets the switch sits within roughly 3–8 equivalent diameters, inside the usual schlieren and PIV fields ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md); [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md); [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md)). Fields of view should span both sides of it, and base flows for stability models must include the spreading.
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Reconstruct each tone in sub-domains either side of the switch, and per symmetry, then compare their Hilbert envelopes and phases: one bi-axial wavepacket should show a locked phase and a shared envelope, two structures independent ones (my suggestion). Conditioning short-time means on the instantaneous envelope, as [Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) did per tone, would test whether the switch point moves with screech amplitude.
- **PyFR staging LES.** Round jets do not switch axes, but the same lesson applies: the mean flow depends on which state the jet is in (Mazharmanesh 2026 show this for A1/A2 staging too; see [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md)), and slowly rotating streaks can fix a flapping axis at the |m| = 1 stages ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md)). Condition means on the state before using them as base flows.
