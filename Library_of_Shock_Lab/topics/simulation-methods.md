# Simulation methods

_Last updated: 2026-10-20 · 8 papers_

## Scope
Solvers and numerics for compressible flow (PyFR flux reconstruction, OpenFOAM `rhoCentralFoam` central schemes, the Charles/charLES finite-volume LES, cut-cell Euler), how the jet LES used across the library were set up, shock-dynamics computations (Euler, geometrical shock dynamics, the transient-oblique-shock model), far-field propagation from simulations (FW-H, CAA), machine learning for fluids, and the public simulation databases. The last section sets this against the user's PyFR A1/A2 staging LES ([README](../../Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/README.md)). Linear operators (stability, resolvent, PSE, OWNS) are in [linear-models](linear-models.md); SPOD/BMD/DMD algorithms in [modal-decomposition](modal-decomposition.md); screech physics in [screech](screech.md); SP-8072-type prediction in [rocket-noise-models](rocket-noise-models.md); schlieren and BOS practice in [schlieren-and-density-imaging](schlieren-and-density-imaging.md); PIV practice and facilities in [diagnostics-and-facilities](diagnostics-and-facilities.md).

## Current understanding
1. **Wall-modelled unstructured LES with the nozzle inside the domain predicts screech blind.** The Charles LES of the OSU twin rectangular jets matched near-field screech frequency and amplitude up to St ≈ 1 and OASPL to about 1 dB, without seeing the data ([Brès et al. 2021](../notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 9–13). The same recipe (nozzle included, turbulent exit boundary layer from synthetic turbulence or a wall model, sponges, small co-flow, permeable FW-H) underlies every jet LES in the library ([Schmidt et al. 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 3; [Towne et al. 2022](../notes/towne-2022-database-reduced-complexity-modeling.md), p. 4; [Nogueira et al. 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 13).
2. **Nozzle details are part of the physics, not decoration.** A lip groove makes the exit boundary layer turbulent and supports its own cavity waves ([Brès et al. 2021](../notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 9, 13); the nozzle-internal boundary layer seeds high-m streaks ([Nogueira et al. 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 15); lip thickness and nearby reflectors move staging boundaries (see Relevance).
3. **Second-order shock-capturing finite volume gets mean shock structure right but not dissipation-sensitive detail.** `rhoCentralFoam` puts the Ladenburg Mach-disc triple point within about 3% ([Greenshields et al. 2010](../notes/greenshields-2010-central-schemes-polyhedral-fv.md), p. 18), and cut-cell Euler converges in shock position from 23 cells per duct width ([Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2022](../notes/rezay-haghdoost-2022-shock-dividers.md), p. 6–7). But the limiter sets separation length ([Greenshields et al. 2010](../notes/greenshields-2010-central-schemes-polyhedral-fv.md), p. 19–21) and inviscid shear layers roll up too strongly ([Thethy et al. 2022](../notes/thethy-2022-shock-diffraction-non-quiescent-medium.md), p. 19).
4. **Front-tracking shock models are cheap and good near the source only.** GSD matches leading-front shapes but under-predicts branch separation and misses the wall shock and post-shock flow ([Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2022](../notes/rezay-haghdoost-2022-shock-dividers.md), p. 7, 9; [Thethy et al. 2022](../notes/thethy-2022-shock-diffraction-non-quiescent-medium.md), p. 20–21, 38).
5. **PyFR's flux reconstruction is accurate and GPU-efficient on paper, but nothing in the library validates it for jets, shocks or ILES.** The only PyFR paper is v0.1.0, which had no shock capturing, SGS model or absorbing boundaries ([Witherden et al. 2014](../notes/witherden-2014-pyfr-flux-reconstruction-framework.md), p. 10, 24–25).
6. **ML helps most when physics is built in, and interpolates rather than extrapolates** ([Brunton et al. 2020](../notes/brunton-2020-machine-learning-fluid-mechanics.md), p. 491, 493); a physics-informed BOS reconstruction roughly halves density error in a shocked flow ([Molnar et al. 2023](../notes/molnar-2023-physics-informed-bos-supersonic.md), p. 13).
7. **One LES family (Brès, Charles) underpins most of the library's SPOD, resolvent and HPOD method papers;** only its M_j 0.9 jet is documented as public ([Towne et al. 2022](../notes/towne-2022-database-reduced-complexity-modeling.md), p. 3–5).

## Solvers and numerics: high-order FR versus finite volume
PyFR recasts flux reconstruction (which unifies nodal DG and spectral difference) as dense matrix multiplies plus generated point-wise kernels, so explicit FR runs near the hardware's bandwidth limit on GPUs ([Witherden et al. 2014](../notes/witherden-2014-pyfr-flux-reconstruction-framework.md), p. 3–5, 9–15). It reaches design order ℘ + 1 on mixed and unstructured meshes (Couette, ℘ = 3: 3.97 ± 0.05 in 2D, 4.16 ± 0.44 on unstructured hexes; Tables 2, 4, p. 22–24) and scales weakly from 1 to 104 GPUs with runtime 1.00 → 1.01 (p. 27–28). Its viscous validation cases all use Rusanov fluxes with LDG β = ½, τ = 0.1 (p. 21, 24), the choices in the staging config. Its only unsteady 3D case, a Re 3900 cylinder at ℘ = 4 with no SGS model, has no quantitative check (p. 24–25).

`rhoCentralFoam` takes the opposite route: Kurganov–Tadmor and Kurganov–Noelle–Petrova central fluxes need no Riemann solver and only owner–neighbour interpolation, so they suit colocated polyhedral finite volumes ([Greenshields et al. 2010](../notes/greenshields-2010-central-schemes-polyhedral-fv.md), p. 4, 6–7). The paper's lessons are about dissipation. The limiter matters more than the flux (van Leer beats Minmod whatever the flux; KNP + van Leer is recommended, p. 13, 21), and on the double cone KNP is about as dissipative as Roe + van Leer (separation 47.5 vs 49.1 mm against a converged 56.1 mm, p. 19–21). Its validation is laminar, mostly steady, with first-order Euler time stepping (p. 6), so it says nothing about resonance or acoustics.

The JAXA lift-off LES sits between the two: sixth-order compact differencing with a tenth-order filter, switching to SLAU + MUSCL only where a shock sensor fires, inside a zonal RANS/LES ([Tsutsumi et al. 2015](../notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 11–12). That is the same "high order away from shocks, local capturing at shocks" idea as PyFR's entropy filter. The library compares none of these schemes on one jet.

Three practices recur. First, the nozzle is always meshed and its exit boundary layer is deliberately set turbulent to match tripped or high-Re experiments ([Schmidt et al. 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 3); a DNS that drops the nozzle says so and cites Brès et al. (2018) on why it matters ([Suzuki et al. 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 3). Second, sponge layers with damping plus a weak co-flow (M 0.009–0.1) to stop spurious recirculation ([Brès et al. 2021](../notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 7). Third, far-field sound by frequency-domain permeable FW-H with outflow end-caps, evaluated at the exact microphone positions ([Brès et al. 2021](../notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 7, 9), or on a 100D sphere ([Nogueira et al. 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 13, 15). Grid checks are coarse: a 2× refinement for the M 0.9 jet ([Towne et al. 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md), p. 4–5) and a plasma-actuator sub-study ([Brès et al. 2021](../notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 13–14).

Records are short: a few hundred tone cycles. That is enough to see intermittent coupling ([Jeun et al. 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), p. 9) and converged screech SPOD ([Nogueira et al. 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 25), but not slow dynamics: the streaks there rotate only π/2 over the record, so a record at least 4× longer would be needed to average them out (p. 14). [Brès et al. 2021](../notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md) judge a short LES against a long experiment by the block-to-block spread of short-time experimental spectra (±0.5–1.5 dB, p. 11), a transferable test.

For propagation through installations, CAA on a RANS mean flow is the cheaper alternative to LES + FW-H. Casalino's GFD solver of Pierce's equation (3–4 points per wavelength) captured pad-cavity confinement but still over-predicted by 2–3 dB, and a shock-containing mean flow made it too "stiff", so an equivalent fully expanded plume was used ([Casalino et al. 2009](../notes/casalino-2009-hybrid-empirical-caa-rocket-noise.md), p. 6, 12, 15). Both rocket studies blame linear propagation for part of their error ([Tsutsumi et al. 2015](../notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 13).

## Shock-dynamics computations: cut-cell Euler, GSD and TOS
The group's TU Berlin collaboration uses one solver family across three papers: second-order MUSCL with a van Leer limiter on Cartesian cut cells, with AMR and an exact Riemann solver in 3D ([Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2020](../notes/rezay-haghdoost-2020-transient-supersonic-trailing-jet-shocklet.md), p. 6–7), and dimensionally split in 2D and axisymmetric ([Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2022](../notes/rezay-haghdoost-2022-shock-dividers.md), p. 5–6; [Thethy et al. 2022](../notes/thethy-2022-shock-diffraction-non-quiescent-medium.md), p. 6–8). The work moved from simulation as a matched companion to schlieren, to simulation as a controlled experiment, to reduced models:
- **2020:** inflow calibrated by a 1D reactive model against a measured total-pressure trace; numerical schlieren matches the experiment qualitatively; a constant-inflow re-run shows the second triple point is intrinsic, not a supply transient (p. 7–12). The transient-oblique-shock model (Rankine–Hugoniot in a frame moving with each shock element) then explains the shocklet from reflected-shock rotation (p. 12–19).
- **2022 (dividers):** a grid study (4–6 cells per shock at any N_D; converged from N_D = 23) and GSD alongside. Shock separation at the exit is 0.55D measured, 0.53 CFD, 0.39 GSD; both models run fast enough (about 5–10 min) for design iteration (p. 6–9, 15).
- **2022 (diffraction):** Euler with geometry and background flow varied separately rules out nozzle focusing as the cause of the front distortion (p. 14–19). A GSD with an added background velocity reproduces the measured fronts from M₀ and M_b alone, and closed-form results follow (generalised Skews angle, eq. 5.8, p. 23; weak-shock angle, eq. 5.13, p. 26).

The shared limitation is inviscid shear layers: roll-up comes from numerical dissipation ([Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2020](../notes/rezay-haghdoost-2020-transient-supersonic-trailing-jet-shocklet.md), p. 6–7) and KH vortices are "significantly overestimated" ([Thethy et al. 2022](../notes/thethy-2022-shock-diffraction-non-quiescent-medium.md), p. 19). Grid spacing is not reported in the 2020 paper.

## Machine learning for fluids
[Brunton et al. 2020](../notes/brunton-2020-machine-learning-fluid-mechanics.md) is the map. POD is a linear autoencoder, but autoencoder modes are neither orthogonal nor ordered (p. 485, 490), which matters for POD-pair phase recovery. Cluster-based ROMs give a Markov view of switching between states (p. 491). Superresolution and CNN PIV exist but are bounded by their training data (p. 491–493), and the authors call for curated benchmark data (p. 499–500). [Molnar et al. 2023](../notes/molnar-2023-physics-informed-bos-supersonic.md) is the one worked case: a PINN fitted to raw BOS image differences with a steady Euler + irrotationality loss gives density at 3.75% NRMSE (against 6.12% for Tikhonov unified BOS and 8.34% for Abel inversion), plus velocity and pressure (≤ 3.09%) (p. 13–14). Its practical lessons match the review's cautions: non-dimensional residuals, fixed loss weights, excluding the corner singularity, and 2–3 h of GPU training against 10 min for the CFD it replaces (p. 10, 14–16). The Euler loss excludes shear layers, so it fits shock cores, not jets as a whole (note's Relevance). [Taira et al. 2020](../notes/taira-2020-modal-analysis-applications-outlook.md) (not tagged) covers ML and SINDy in its outlook.

## Public databases
[Towne et al. 2022](../notes/towne-2022-database-reduced-complexity-modeling.md) release about 8 TB in six time-resolved sets with MATLAB readers (p. 3). The jet is the Brès M_j 0.9 LES: 10,000 snapshots every 0.2 c∞/D on a cylindrical grid to 30D, plus azimuthal modes and the mean flow (p. 4–6). There is also a wall-resolved NACA 0012 LES (35M cells, p. 35). The same jet, or its M 0.4 and M 1.5 siblings, is the test data in [Schmidt et al. 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), [Towne et al. 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md), [Schmidt et al. 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), [Pickering et al. 2020](../notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md), [Nekkanti & Schmidt 2021](../notes/nekkanti-2021-spod-reconstruction-denoising-frequency-time.md), [Yeung & Schmidt 2024](../notes/yeung-2024-adaptive-spod-broadband-tonal.md) and [Raiola & Kriegseis 2025](../notes/raiola-2025-hilbert-pod.md). It is subsonic with broadband wavepackets plus trapped-wave tones, so it is a clean test for the SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD chain, but it has no shocks or screech. The screeching heated M_j 1.35 and twin-rectangular databases are not described as public in the notes.

## Disagreements and open questions
**Is ILES at reduced Re enough for staging?** Every validated screech LES is wall-modelled at lab Re with an SGS model. The PyFR run has neither and proposes Re_D ≈ 10⁵ with a laminar exit layer ([M3 setup](../../Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/M3_SETUP.md), §5). Indirect support: trapped modes in a mean-flow operator are Re-independent above about 5 × 10⁴ ([Schmidt et al. 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 25–27), and the Shock Lab nozzle's exit layer is laminar and thin ([Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 8). Against: the A1 cut-off near M_j 1.12 moves with shear-layer thickness ([Nogueira et al. 2022](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 12). Settle it by repeating one bracket at two Re and two exit-layer thicknesses.

**Does shock-capturing dissipation bias resonance?** The library shows dissipation setting separation and roll-up (claim 3) but no test of a shock-capturing scheme on a screech tone. At NPR 2.10 the shock-induced transverse velocity is below 3% of U_e ([Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 9), so the entropy filter may barely act; logging where it acts and checking that the tone is unchanged between ℘ = 3 and 4 would settle it.

**Bistable edges or one noisy state?** The driver assumes two saddle-node edges with hysteresis. The evidence is consistent but indirect: tones never coexist ([Mancinelli et al. 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md), p. 7; [Mazharmanesh et al. 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 11–12), and both stages are absolutely unstable at M_j 1.12 ([Nogueira et al. 2022](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 13). Tam's rule that the largest total growth wins ([Tam 1995](../notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 39) is single-valued instead. No note reports up-and-down NPR ramps. Slow ramps in the 16 mm rig with TR schlieren would settle it, and so would LES branches that fail to overlap.

**Lab switching may be noise-driven.** At NPR 2.19 a slow drift of tone strength was put down to supply-pressure fluctuation ([Mazharmanesh et al. 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 26–28). An LES with a steady inlet has none of this, so measured dwell times need not follow from the LES edges.

**Euler versus viscous for transient shock flows.** [Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2022](../notes/rezay-haghdoost-2022-shock-dividers.md) argue viscosity is marginal for front propagation (p. 7, 11); [Thethy et al. 2022](../notes/thethy-2022-shock-diffraction-non-quiescent-medium.md) show it is not marginal for shock–vortex interaction (p. 19). A viscous run of one case would bound the error.

## Methods and data
All simulation output here is time-resolved, so it suits SPOD, BMD, k–ω maps ([Towne et al. 2022](../notes/towne-2022-database-reduced-complexity-modeling.md), p. 7) and time-direction HPOD. Decimating snapshots gives a PIV-like ensemble on which to test POD-pair and space-only HPOD routes on a known flow. For comparison with schlieren, path-integrate ∂ρ/∂x into a numerical schlieren image ([Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2020](../notes/rezay-haghdoost-2020-transient-supersonic-trailing-jet-shocklet.md), p. 8), or for BOS, ray-trace synthetic images through the flow ([Molnar et al. 2023](../notes/molnar-2023-physics-informed-bos-supersonic.md), p. 11–12). For 3D fields, a D2 split before SPOD cuts memory 4× ([Yeung et al. 2022](../notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md)), and streaming SPOD avoids storing snapshots ([Schmidt & Towne 2019](../notes/schmidt-2019-streaming-spod.md)). Single transient shock events don't suit block-averaged methods; front tracking and x–t diagrams are what these papers use. RANS mean flows are a cheaper base for LSA but give narrower shear layers and drift downstream ([Ivelja et al. 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 18, 23).

## Relevance to our work
**Practices the PyFR staging LES should follow** (setup in the [README](../../Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/README.md) and [base.example.ini](../../Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/base.example.ini)):
- **Match the rig's nozzle exterior and lip.** Lip thickness shifts staging boundaries and A2 amplitude through reflection off the exit face ([Ponton & Seiner 1992](../notes/ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md), p. 538, 548), and flanges and plates change staging ([Knast et al. 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md), p. 3–4; [Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 16). The mesh keeps the outer body to 4.7D upstream, which is right. But the CAD has a 1 mm flat lip (about 0.06D), while the 16 mm Shock Lab nozzle in [Mazharmanesh et al. 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) is listed with mean lip thickness l/D_e = 0.31 (p. 5). Confirm that the two are the same nozzle and what "mean lip thickness" measures.
- **Set and report the exit boundary layer.** It, more than Re, moves the A1 cut-off ([Nogueira et al. 2022](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 12), and Charles practice is to match it deliberately.
- **Boundaries:** keep the absorbing-layer test (A1 tone unchanged when `xsp`/`rsp` move). Consider a weak co-flow (M ≈ 0.01) as all Charles jets use ([Brès et al. 2021](../notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 7).
- **Acoustics:** the lip ring suffices for the driver, but comparison with far-field or exit-plane array data ([Mancinelli et al. 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md)) needs an FW-H surface or probes at the experimental positions.
- **A control visit:** re-run the anchor with no NPR step to measure the step transient in r(t), as [Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2020](../notes/rezay-haghdoost-2020-transient-supersonic-trailing-jet-shocklet.md) separated supply transients from intrinsic dynamics (p. 12). *(My suggestion.)*
- **Treat each converged state's mean separately.** A1 and A2 each carry their own short-time mean, and the long-time mean is a state the jet never occupies ([Mazharmanesh et al. 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 11–13). Compute LSA base flows and shock spectra per anchor.
- **Sampling and length:** published screech LES sample at fD/c∞ ≈ 10–13 and run 150–400 cycles. For a cold M_j 1.12 jet U_j/c∞ ≈ 1.0 (my estimate), so fD/c∞ = 10 gives about 15 samples per A1 cycle.

**Validation data in the library:**
- *Same diameter, Shock Lab:* 16 mm nozzle, 150 kHz schlieren, 400,000 frames per case. A1 alone at NPR 2.19 (St 0.607), A2 alone at 2.25 (St 0.665), mutually exclusive switching at 2.20 (St 0.606 / 0.694, ρ = −0.82), with per-state mean shock cells ([Mazharmanesh et al. 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 4–12).
- *Anchor NPRs 2.10 and 2.25:* PIV of the 15 mm, 5 mm-lip nozzle. Gives guided-mode structure and wave decomposition, St 0.67 and 0.65, and global LSA on the mean at St 0.667 ([Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md); [Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 8–12).
- *Staging point across facilities:* Pprime switching between M_j 1.115 and 1.13 (NPR ≈ 2.18–2.22, my isentropic conversion), dwell times 1–4 s, St_d ≈ 0.069 modulation ([Mancinelli et al. 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md), p. 6–7). Tsinghua switches at NPR 2.25 with a k_kh − k_s2 precursor ([Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 11–13). This spread of about 0.05 in NPR is ten times the driver's bracket width, so the LES bracket is only meaningful against the matched nozzle.
- *Models to compare against:* linear growth rates of A1 vs A2 (ω0i 0.223 vs 0.057 at M_j 1.12; [Nogueira et al. 2022](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 13) and the ω_i ≥ 0 regions of [Mancinelli et al. 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md). Global modes at St 0.66/0.72/0.77 on an M_j 1.12 PIV mean ([Farghadan et al. 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 11).
- *Mean shock cells:* NASA static-pressure surveys for a Mach 1.0 nozzle at M_j 1.02–1.28 ([Norum & Seiner 1982](../notes/norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md), App. A). The Ladenburg triple point covers higher NPR ([Greenshields et al. 2010](../notes/greenshields-2010-central-schemes-polyhedral-fv.md), p. 18).

**Gaps:** no paper on PyFR's entropy filter, ILES of jets, prisms or single-precision accuracy. The later PyFR papers that [Witherden et al. 2014](../notes/witherden-2014-pyfr-flux-reconstruction-framework.md) lists as follow-ups would fill them.

**Elsewhere:** cut-cell Euler + GSD is the group's toolkit for transient shock flows. The background-flow GSD may bear on ignition overpressure leaving into moving gas (an inference in the [Thethy et al. 2022](../notes/thethy-2022-shock-diffraction-non-quiescent-medium.md) note). For rocket noise, LES + FW-H and RANS-based CAA both still over-predict by 2–4 dB; see [rocket-noise-models](rocket-noise-models.md).
