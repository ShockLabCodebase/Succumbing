# Screech

_Last updated: 2026-10-20 · 37 papers_

## Scope

Screech in free, shock-containing jets: what closes the feedback loop, where shock leakage radiates the tone, how the frequency is predicted, A1/A2/B/C staging and mode switching, amplitude and nozzle receptivity, the absolute-instability and global-mode views, triadic interactions and mean-flow deformation, and how LES and linear models compare with experiment. Twin-jet coupling and flapping or axis switching in rectangular and elliptical nozzles are covered in [non-axisymmetric-and-twin-jets](non-axisymmetric-and-twin-jets.md); here they appear only where they test the screech mechanism. The model machinery (vortex sheet, Floquet LSA, resolvent) is in [linear-models](linear-models.md), BBSAN and mixing noise in [jet-noise-mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md), impinging-jet and edge tones in [impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md), and SPOD/BMD/HPOD practice in [modal-decomposition](modal-decomposition.md) and [time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md).

## Current understanding

1. **The loop has four links**: a KH wavepacket grows from the lip, interacts with the shock cells, an upstream wave returns to the lip, and the lip turns it into new KH waves ([Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) p. 47–49; [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) p. 2). Apart from the KH–shock interaction, the frequency-selecting links appear linear (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves, p. 24; [Mancinelli 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) p. 8).
2. **The upstream link is the guided jet mode (G-JM), not free-stream sound.** PIV finds an upstream wave with core support matching the (0, 2) vortex-sheet eigenfunction, and every A1/A2 tone lies in that mode's propagative band ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) p. 9–11). Models closed by free-stream sound predict tones where none exist (Mancinelli 2019, p. 6; [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) p. 15). Theory says why: sound generated inside the jet is totally reflected by the shear layer, while sound from outside cannot enter the core when travelling upstream ([Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) p. 14–17, 32).
3. **The G-JM is energised by a triad between the KH wave and the shock cells** at k_kh − k_s, the Tam & Tanna difference wavenumber ([Tam 1995](../notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) p. 32–34; [Tam & Tanna 1982](../notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md)). PIV confirmed it, plus a downstream duct-like wave at k_kh + k_s (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves, p. 14–17), and so did schlieren, LES and global models ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) p. 12–13; [Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md) p. 13–14; [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) p. 15–17).
4. **Staging comes from the shock train having several wavenumbers.** Shock spacing shrinks downstream, so the mean-flow spectrum has a primary peak k_s1 and suboptimal peaks k_s2, k_s3. A1 and B close on k_s1, A2 and C on k_s2 ([Nogueira 2022](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) p. 7–13; Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 11–13).
5. **Frequency is predicted to 1–10 % by linear models; no model in the library predicts amplitude** (Raman 1998, p. 48, 101; Tam 1995, p. 40).
6. **Near a staging point the tones are mutually exclusive.** They switch with dwell times of seconds (Mancinelli 2019, p. 6–7), and each loop sits on its own short-time mean flow ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) p. 11–13).
7. **The nozzle and its surroundings set amplitude and mode ranges far more than frequency**: lip thickness, reflectors and upstream plates add or remove modes and change levels by tens of dB ([Ponton 1992](../notes/ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) p. 537–548; Raman 1998, p. 75–78).
8. **The far-field tone is not the loop.** In round and low-AR jets the radiated sound comes from acoustically matched wavenumbers and from shock leakage, now seen directly ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) p. 13–19), both by-products of the same triad (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 20–22; Nogueira 2024, p. 32).

## Closure: from Powell's loop to the guided jet mode

Powell's model, as reviewed by Raman (1998, p. 48–49), treats the shock cells as a phased array of monopoles and gives f = U_c/[s(1 + M_c)] from either a phase criterion or an upstream-directivity criterion. [Tam 1986](../notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md) (p. 7–10) recast screech as the θ → 180° limit of BBSAN: the inverse BBSAN peak frequency is linear in cos θ and extrapolates onto the tone, and only the narrow band radiated straight at the lip can sustain the loop (the "weakest link"), so the loop phase does not choose the frequency. [Tam 1988](../notes/tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) (p. 10–12) carried this to rectangular jets, with spacing set by the minor dimension. All of these close the loop with free-stream sound outside the jet (Tam 1995, p. 37).

The guided-mode picture came from subsonic jets. [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) (p. 14–19) showed that trapped duct-like waves and an upstream "discrete free-stream" mode propagate only between neutral saddle points, and that these bands bound the near-nozzle tones. Edgington-Mitchell 2018 (p. 6–11) split the leading POD pair of A1 and A2 jets (NPR 2.10, 2.25) by the sign of k_x and found the upstream wave at about a fifth of the KH amplitude. Shape alone could not exclude the acoustic continuum; the band test did (p. 10). This contradicted Shen & Tam's separate closures for A1 and A2 (p. 10–11). Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves (p. 14–23) added the downstream duct-like wave and showed that the near-field standing wave and core modulation come mostly from G-JM–KH interference, not from shocks modulating KH growth. Nogueira 2024 (p. 19–33) supplies the physics: the G-JM is in-jet sound in a duct whose wall impedance runs from hard at the sonic line to soft at large |α|, and the evanescent outer field carries net energy upstream. Because the triad sits inside the shear layer, screech always needs the G-JM; impingement tones need it only when their source is in the core (p. 31–33; contrast the wall-jet shocklet in [Weightman 2019](../notes/weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md) and [Edgington-Mitchell 2025](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md), both not tagged).

The closure generalises, with refinements. In an AR 2 elliptical jet the se₁ G-JM is neutral at the screech frequency only over a short x band, and the measured G-JM amplitude peaks inside it ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) p. 22–24). In twin round jets the G-JM band, not KH growth, selects the coupling symmetry ([Nogueira 2021, twin jets](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md) p. 16–19; [Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) p. 18–19). In LES of twin rectangular jets the G-JM gain (10.1) dwarfs that of free-stream sound (1.16), and its points of return sit at peaks of shock–KH interaction (Jeun 2024, p. 20–21). Negative k_x does not mean upstream group velocity: the k_kh − k_s2 wave in an A1 jet is duct-like and cannot close the loop (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 13; Towne 2017, p. 8).

**Shock leakage and source location.** Raman's "feedback shock" from one dominant source in AR ≳ 9 rectangular jets was unexplained in 1998 (p. 70–73), and his source localisation (3rd shock at M_j 1.45; p. 71–73) assumes one source. Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, saw leakage in k_x < 0-filtered 1 Mfps schlieren: the shock tip overshoots the sonic line as a KH vortex passes and sheds a front that is sharp from birth (p. 3–4, 13–19). Ray tracing on phase-reconstructed PIV puts the escape at the saddle between vortices (p. 26–27), first at the 3rd cell for A1 and C (M_j 1.45, as in Raman) and the 4th for A2, moving between the 6th and 3rd with vortex strength, close to the 3rd–5th scatter in the schlieren (p. 19–30). That fits Mancinelli's best-fit 4th-cell reflection (Mancinelli 2019, p. 4–5), not a source at the instability-wave peak (Tam 1986, p. 4; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 26). One shock-like, upstream-directed source explains the feedback shock (p. 21–23), though several cells emit (p. 28). Closure was not tested; Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 20–22) treats leakage as a correlated sound source in round and low-AR jets that may close the loop only at high AR.

The frameworks are closer than they look. Mancinelli 2021 (p. 24) shows Powell's phase criterion and the Tam & Tanna relation coincide, with k_s = (2πp − φ)/L_s for irregular spacing, so the integer p of a resonance model stands in for the choice of shock peak; Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 4) objects that changing p is not a mechanism. Nogueira 2022 (p. 14–15) shows Tam's weakest-link condition is essentially the saddle-point condition. Fitted parameters are rig-specific: the Monash twin-jet rig reproduces Mancinelli's finite-thickness fit (s = 4, φ = 0, p = 4, 5) but needs a different vortex-sheet φ, put down to lip thickness of 15 % against 3 % (Stavropoulos 2023, p. 16–17). In all models shear-layer thickness matters more than shock amplitude: it moves the G-JM band edges and the absolute growth rate (Nogueira 2021, absolute instability, p. 16; Mancinelli 2021, p. 16–17; Ivelja 2024, p. 23). Convection speed cannot explain staging: U_c/U_j stays at 0.57–0.60 across all coupling modes of twin jets, while the standing-wavelength-to-shock-spacing ratio changes from mode to mode ([Knast 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) p. 10–11). Tam's closed-form formula is a poor check in the A1/A2 range (0.92 against 0.67 at M_j 1.09, by the note author's calculation; Tam 1995 note).

## Staging, switching and multimodality

Raman (1998, p. 49, 52, 101) lists Merle's A1, A2, B, C, D stages (A axisymmetric, B flapping, C helical) and calls the jumps unexplained. Tam (1995, p. 39) explained the toroidal-to-helical switch near M_j 1.3 by which azimuthal mode has the largest total instability growth, but that cannot explain A1 → A2: both are m = 0 and both close on the same G-JM (Nogueira 2022, p. 2).

The present explanation is the multi-peak shock spectrum. Nogueira 2022 (p. 7–13) fitted the centreline mean pressure as a chirp, placed A1 in the near-nozzle region and A2 downstream where spacing shrinks, and found both loops absolutely unstable at M_j 1.12, with ω0i 0.223 (A1) against 0.057 (A2) at δ = 0.2, matching the stronger measured A1. Edgington-Mitchell 2022 extended this to B, C and k_s3: the lipline standing wavenumber follows k_s1 or k_s2 by stage even at rapidly switching conditions (p. 11); k_s2 is a local quasi-periodicity starting at the 5th–8th shock cell (p. 17); energy at k_kh − k_s2 grows before the A1 → A2 switch (Tsinghua NPR 2.15–2.2, switch at 2.25; p. 13); and jets of AR ≥ 4 do not stage because flapping breaks the shocks down before a suboptimal peak forms (p. 18–19). Farghadan 2026 (p. 11–17) found separate lightly damped global modes at St 0.66, 0.72 and 0.77, each tied to a different shock wavenumber, with the observed tone on k_s2 hidden inside the broad primary peak. Mazharmanesh 2026 (p. 19–22) measured a loop-specific k_S from BMD interaction maps that matches k_kh − k_GJM as well as or better than k_s1, k_s2; in an AR 2 elliptical jet a single loop on k_s1 (NPR 2.3–3.3) gives way to multimodal loops on k_s2 (NPR 3.4–4.9). Stavropoulos 2023 (p. 17) reaches the same A1/k_s1, A2/k_s2 split through the p/s ratios of its fits.

**Switching dynamics.** At Pprime, M_j ≤ 1.115 gives only A1, 1.12 jumps between A1 and A2, and ≥ 1.13 only A2, with no coexistence, dwell times of 1–4 s and an A1 envelope modulated at St ≈ 0.069 ≈ St₂ − St₁ only where switching occurs (Mancinelli 2019, p. 6–7). At NPR 2.20 the A1 (St 0.606) and A2 (0.694) amplitudes have ρ = −0.82, and the St = 0 SPOD coefficient correlates at 0.96 and −0.82 with them, so the long-time mean is a state the jet never occupies (Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 8–13). The A1 → A2 NPR varies by rig: about 2.19 at Pprime (M_j ≈ 1.12, my isentropic conversion), 2.17–2.22 for a Monash D = 10 mm nozzle ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md) p. 9–10), 2.20 for a D_e = 16 mm nozzle (Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 8–10) and 2.25 at Tsinghua (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 13); AR 2 rectangular and elliptical jets stage near 2.2 ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) p. 7). Older evidence is ambiguous on coexistence: the B → C switch is abrupt with a thin lip but the tones overlap over M_j 1.38–1.41 with a thick lip, which averaged spectra cannot separate from alternation (Ponton 1992, p. 538), and Raman (1998, p. 55–56) cites both switching and genuinely coexisting tones in rectangular jets. At higher NPR several loops appear in one block-averaged SPOD: four non-harmonic tones at NPR 3.3, none on k_s1 (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 15–17), and three loops at NPR 4.0 in an AR 2 rectangular jet, including a bi-axial flapping mode (Mazharmanesh 2025, p. 11–17). Twin jets add symmetry competition, most intermittent at the A1 → A2 staging (Wong 2023, p. 9–12).

## Amplitude and receptivity

Raman (1998, p. 49) writes the limit cycle as growth × shock interaction × feedback efficiency × receptivity = 1, and shows that shock strength keeps rising with M_j while amplitude plateaus at 154–155 dB over M_j 1.3–1.65 and then collapses (p. 64). Thickening the lip from t/D 0.015 to 0.625 lets screech persist to M_j ≈ 1.95 instead of stopping near 1.6–1.7, revives the B mode above M_j 1.7 and shifts B and C wavelengths slightly in opposite directions (Ponton 1992, p. 537–541). A thick lip raised screech by 10 dB, a flapping plane rotated about 90° between nominally identical tests, and heating makes jets quieter (Tam 1995, p. 23, 36, 39–40). Reflectors change both amplitude and frequency by detuning the loop phase (Raman 1998, p. 77–78), and the Monash upstream plate produced an unusual dominant m = 0 mode at NPR 3.3 (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 16). A small tab removes the tones, lowering peak narrowband levels by about 16–21 dB ([Norum 1982](../notes/norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md) p. 149, 162), and in rectangular jets asymmetric trailing-edge cutouts suppress overexpanded screech while symmetric ones amplify it ([Kerechanin 2001](../notes/kerechanin-2001-trailing-edge-rectangular-jet-acoustics.md) p. 4–5).

The models give partial handles only. In the absolute-instability view the nozzle sets amplification, not frequency (Nogueira 2021, absolute instability, p. 22); ω0i falls from about 0.44 to 0.07 as δ grows from 0.15 to 0.22 (p. 16) and rises with M_j like the measured amplitude (Nogueira 2022, p. 13). That fits Raman's shock-strength result, since the saddle is insensitive to shock amplitude. The resonance models need |R₁R₂| of 0.1–0.3 to sustain A1/A2, still fitted, with a Wiener–Hopf model proposed (Mancinelli 2021, p. 19–22). Screech also changes the jet: at peak screech an AR 16.7 rectangular jet spreads at 36° rather than 20–25° ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md) p. 123), and strong screech adds about 3 dB to mixing noise ([Tam 1996](../notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), not tagged).

## Absolute instability and global modes

Nogueira 2021, absolute instability (p. 13–16, 21–22), showed that a weak sinusoidal shock train barely moves the eigenvalues but creates a KH–G-JM saddle with positive absolute growth, even in a cold jet. Shear-layer thickening downstream makes it a pocket of absolute instability, the nozzle is not needed to select the frequency, and the model gives no reason for B–C–D switching. Ivelja's streamwise-limited G-JM band (p. 24–25) fits this pocket. Global analyses of measured mean flows agree on the frequency, but Farghadan 2026 finds the least-stable mode slightly stable, so screech must be forced (p. 11, 14). The same operator also holds an St 0.31 duct resonance of the Towne 2017 type (Farghadan 2026, p. 13–14).

## Triads, harmonics and mean-flow deformation

Beyond the KH–shock triad, three kinds of interaction matter. **Between tones:** A1–A2 modulation at St₂ − St₁ during switching (Mancinelli 2019, p. 7); and above M_j ≈ 1.25 the B tone appears in the m = 0 spectrum as a quadratic m = ±1 product (cross-bicoherence ≈ 1 at St 0.34), not as an axisymmetric loop (Mancinelli 2021, p. 24–25). **Harmonics:** a bilinear harmonic resolvent on the PIV mean plus the screech mode reproduces the St = 0 shock-cell correction and the 2St_s and 3St_s sideline beams; the harmonic radiation comes from k_kh − k_s1 although the loop closes on k_s2, and forcing the operator with the screech self-interaction matches the optimal harmonic responses (inner products > 0.95 per frequency) without appeal to turbulence (Farghadan 2026, p. 20–25). **Mean flow:** the St_s − St_s = 0 self-interaction distorts the mean, each loop has its own short-time mean, and shock spacing both selects the loop and is changed by it (Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 3, 16–19), as Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 12) inferred from jumps in the shock spectrum. Krothapalli 1986 is the long-time-average precursor of this. For |m| = 1 screech in round jets, the (±1, St_s) ↔ (2, 0) triad lets weak |m| = 2 streaks split the m = ±1 eigenvalues into flapping and wagging modes and fix the axis, and in LES the flapping axis precesses with the streaks by about π/2 over about 100 cycles ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) p. 7–12, 20); the helical C mode reverses its helicity often ([Edgington-Mitchell 2023](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2023-screeching-jet-seen-from-all-sides.md) p. 4).

## LES and linear models against experiment

LES now matches screech frequency and amplitude (Edgington-Mitchell 2023, p. 2): the blind OSU twin-rectangular LES matches spectra to St ≈ 1 including the screech tones ([Brès 2021](../notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md)), and LES resolves intermittent twin-jet coupling (Jeun 2024) and flapping-axis precession (Nogueira 2026). An early Euler simulation already got 5043 Hz and 156.4 dB against 5248 Hz and 157 dB (Raman 1998, p. 98). No LES of round-jet A1/A2 staging is in the library. [Edgington-Mitchell 2014](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2014-underexpanded-jet-mach-disk-shear-layer.md) (p. 16–17) gives a validation target for strongly underexpanded LES: no recirculation behind the Mach disk in 200,000 PIV samples.

Linear models match frequencies to 1–10 % and G-JM shapes very closely (Edgington-Mitchell 2018, p. 9–10; Nogueira 2021, twin jets, p. 19–21, "every peak, node and decay rate"; Ivelja 2024, p. 21–22). KH shapes come out too narrow without spreading or eddy viscosity (Nogueira 2021, twin jets, p. 19–20), and growth rates are not quantitative (Nogueira 2021, absolute instability, p. 11–12). The resolvent–POD inner product is 0.89 (Farghadan 2026, p. 17). G-JM bands are sensitive to the mean flow: RANS shear layers narrow them (Ivelja 2024, p. 23), and R/θ falls from about 18 to 6 along the loop although one value is used (Mancinelli 2021, p. 22–23). Parabolised models cannot carry the upstream wave at all ([Nogueira 2022, wavepacket modulation](../notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md), not tagged).

## Disagreements and open questions

**What selects the frequency.** Phase-criterion resonance (Mancinelli 2019, 2021), the weakest link (Tam 1986), the absolute-instability saddle (Nogueira 2021, 2022) and global modes (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves; Farghadan 2026) all hit the tone. They differ on whether nozzle reflection matters: the saddle view says no, the resonance models need R₁R₂. Ponton's small but systematic lip-induced frequency shifts (p. 538) sit between the two. Running all four on one mean flow, and changing only the nozzle exterior in an LES, would settle it.

**Self-excited or forced.** The model-base-flow saddle is absolutely unstable (Nogueira 2021, absolute instability), but the measured mean flow's least-damped global mode is slightly stable (Farghadan 2026, p. 14). Global stability on the short-time means of each loop, rather than the long-time mean, would test whether each state is marginally stable on its own mean (Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 19).

**One reflection point or two shock wavenumbers.** Mancinelli's models produce A1/A2 by changing p at a fixed 4th-cell reflection; Nogueira 2022 and Edgington-Mitchell 2022 attribute A2 to k_s2 from downstream cells. Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves, matched its own A2 case (NPR 2.25) with the leading k_s, with a larger mismatch than for A1 (Nogueira 2022 note), and Farghadan 2026 finds the k_s2 peak buried in the primary one. Long-field (> 10D) mean shock spectra plus BMD k_S of A1 and A2 on one rig would settle the labelling.

**Selection, hysteresis and switching.** Linear models allow both A1 and A2 over a band (Nogueira 2022, p. 13; Mancinelli 2021), yet experiments switch over only ΔM_j ≈ 0.01–0.015. Whether staging is bistable, whether switching is stochastic between attractors or driven by supply-pressure drift (Wong 2023, p. 39–41), and whether multimodal tones coexist or alternate (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 7) are open. Long time-resolved records with HPOD and JPDFs, and LES continuation, are the tests.

**Leakage as closure.** Round-jet evidence is against it: leaked fronts are born outside the jet, which cannot feed the G-JM (Nogueira 2024, p. 14, 32), and at NPR 2.10 the first leak (x/D ≈ 1.8; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 23) lies upstream of the G-JM peak (≈ 3.3; Edgington-Mitchell 2018, p. 6), though at NPR 2.25 both sit near x/D ≈ 3. Yet totally reflected rays stay in the core like a G-JM (the note's reading), so one event might feed both. Correlating each cycle's leakage with G-JM amplitude at the lip would separate them; at high AR (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 21–22), tones outside the G-JM band would show leakage closure.

**Directivity.** In the ray model one leakage source already radiates along Norum's 160° peak, so superposition is not needed (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 21–23); Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 20) gets the upstream/downstream balance from a distributed Mach-wave-type source. Both are qualitative, and the leakage paper's microphones stop at 120°, though they show the C-mode first harmonic peaking at 90° (p. 6), as in Tam 1995 (p. 21–23), whereas Krothapalli 1986 found the fundamental dominating there (p. 125). Far-field angles to 160° and LES would decide.

**Amplitude.** No predictive model exists; |R₁R₂| is fitted and the link between ω0i and saturated amplitude is only suggested (Nogueira 2022, p. 13).

**Flapping, helical and precession.** Whether streaks anchor screech or screech drives streaks is open (Nogueira 2026, p. 25), as is whether stage C corresponds to weak streaks; forced LES or multi-view time-resolved schlieren would test it.

**Full scale.** Screech is absent from rocket and afterburning-engine data ([Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 9, 16; [Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 6). Heating weakens it (Tam 1995, p. 39–40), but whether the G-JM band simply misses hot, highly overexpanded plumes is untested.

## Methods and data

- **Mean fields** (any schlieren, BOS or PIV mean, or probe pressure): centreline FFT for k_s1, k_s2, k_s3, with a field of view of 8–10D or more and a sliding window to locate each peak (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 7, 17; Nogueira 2022, p. 7–8). Acoustic PSDs need fine steps (ΔM_j = 0.005) and azimuthal arrays to see staging and m (Mancinelli 2019, p. 5).
- **Non-time-resolved PIV**: POD of transverse velocity, ring check on the pair, complex mode, FFT in x, split by the sign of k_x (Edgington-Mitchell 2018; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves, p. 9–17; Ivelja 2024). The reconstructed cycle also feeds leakage ray tracing (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 11–13). It needs a single steady tone, and it hides helicity sign (Edgington-Mitchell 2023) and switching.
- **Time-resolved schlieren**: SPOD at the tone and the standing-wave wavenumber (Edgington-Mitchell 2022), SPOD frequency–time, JPDFs and BMD interaction maps (Mazharmanesh 2026), rotated views and sub-domain SPOD (Mazharmanesh 2025), wavelets (Mancinelli 2019; Wong 2023), Hilbert phase (Jeun 2024) and k_x < 0 filtering for single, intermittent leakage events (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 10, 19).
- **Pitfalls**: phase and group velocity can differ in sign; beating between uncoupled tones mimics coupling (Wong 2023, p. 32–38); check bicoherence before modelling an m = 0 tone (Mancinelli 2021); precession splits a tone across SPOD modes 1 and 2 (Nogueira 2026, p. 22); shocks inside a PIV window smear over ≈ 0.2D (Edgington-Mitchell 2014, p. 16–17).
- **Data tables**: tone frequencies and shock spacings are collected in `data/screech_tones.csv` and `data/shock_cell_spacing.csv`.

## Relevance to our work

**Lab experiments.** Record lip thickness, outer nozzle shape and upstream surfaces in every campaign, and sweep NPR in steps of 0.01 or finer around 2.15–2.30. Records must cover many switches (seconds), not just many cycles.

**SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Rebuild the A1 and A2 bands and the St = 0 mode separately and track their instantaneous amplitudes: mutual exclusion should appear as anti-correlation, and the lag between tone amplitude and the St = 0 mode tests two-way coupling (Mazharmanesh 2026). Use the Hilbert transform in time, or along −x for the G-JM, since a Hilbert transform along +x discards the k_x < 0 G-JM (Edgington-Mitchell 2022 note). Conditioning the k_x < 0 field on each cycle's KH amplitude would replace the static r̄ ± 2σ rescaling of the leakage source (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 28–30).

**What the PyFR staging LES** (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`, 16 mm converging nozzle, A1 seeded at NPR 2.10 and A2 at 2.25) **could settle:**
1. *Bistability.* The up-branch limit of A1 and the down-branch limit of A2 bound a hysteresis band. Linear models allow both loops over a band near M_j 1.12; if the two LES limits coincide, staging is a sharp exchange rather than bistability, and the experimental 50 % point should lie between them.
2. *Mean-flow-mediated selection.* Converged A1 and A2 states at the same NPR give two mean flows. Their shock spectra test whether the loop changes k_s2 strength (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 12), and local, Floquet or global analysis of each tests whether each state damps the other loop. Farghadan 2026 already finds an St 0.66 mode next to the 0.72 tone at M_j 1.12.
3. *Rates against linear growth.* Compare 1/t_switch and post-step growth or decay with ω0i (0.223 against 0.057; Nogueira 2022, p. 13) and with the ω_i of Mancinelli 2021.
4. *Precursors and triads.* In forced switches, check whether energy at k_kh − k_s2 grows before the tone switches (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 13), and use BMD to test whether the St₂ − St₁ modulation (Mancinelli 2019, p. 7) is a driving triad.
5. *Receptivity.* Changing only the lip or outer geometry tests whether limits shift while frequencies do not (Ponton 1992; Nogueira 2021, absolute instability, p. 22), and projecting the near-lip field onto KH and G-JM eigenfunctions would estimate the R₁ and R₂ that Mancinelli 2021 (p. 22) needs.
6. *Leakage.* At the leakage-PIV NPRs, check whether the first leak moves from the 3rd to the 4th cell on staging, and test leakage as closure (above) with the sound speed the ray model lacked (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 12, 19–23).

The LES cannot give dwell-time statistics (1–4 s, about 10⁴–10⁵ D/U_j). Its nozzle lip must match the comparison rig: the README describes a 1 mm flat lip, whereas the 16 mm nozzle of Mazharmanesh 2026 is listed with l/D_e = 0.31 and the leakage nozzle with t/D = 0.33 (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 4).

**Rocket noise.** Lab screech tones do not carry over to launch vehicles (Lubert 2022), but the Tam & Tanna triad underlying the G-JM is the same machinery used for BBSAN ([Wong 2021](../notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), not tagged).
