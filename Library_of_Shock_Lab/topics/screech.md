# Screech

_Last updated: 2026-10-21 · 38 papers_

## Scope

Screech in free, shock-containing jets: how the feedback loop closes, where shock leakage radiates the tone and where the source sits, amplitude and nozzle receptivity, and triadic interactions and mean-flow deformation. Frequency prediction, absolute instability and global modes, and the comparison of LES and linear models with experiment are on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md); staging, mode switching and multimodal loops on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md). Rectangular and elliptical jets ([non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md)) and twin-jet coupling ([twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md)) appear here only where they test the mechanism. Local and guided-jet-wave models are in [linear-models](linear-models.md), shock-cell models and Floquet-in-x analysis in [shock-cell-models](shock-cell-models.md), global stability and resolvent analysis in [linear-operator-methods](linear-operator-methods.md), BBSAN in [jet-noise-mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md), impinging and edge tones in [impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md), and SPOD/BMD/HPOD practice in [modal-decomposition](modal-decomposition.md) and [time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md).

## Current understanding

1. **The loop has four links**: KH growth from the lip, interaction with the shock cells, an upstream wave, and receptivity at the lip ([Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) p. 47–49; [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) p. 2). Apart from the KH–shock interaction, the frequency-selecting links appear linear (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves, p. 24; [Mancinelli 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) p. 8), and an integer number of periods spans the lip to one dominant source at the 3rd or 4th shock tip ([Mercier 2017](../notes/mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) p. 219–224).
2. **The upstream wave is the guided jet mode (G-JM), not free-stream sound.** Its core support and propagative band match A1/A2 PIV ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) p. 9–11) and cold rectangular-jet tones ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md) p. 26); free-stream closure predicts tones where none exist (Mancinelli 2019, p. 6; [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) p. 15); and in-jet sound is totally reflected by the shear layer, while outside sound cannot enter the core travelling upstream ([Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) p. 14–17, 32).
3. **A KH–shock triad at the Tam & Tanna difference wavenumber k_kh − k_s energises the G-JM** ([Tam 1995](../notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) p. 32–34; [Tam & Tanna 1982](../notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md)), as PIV (with a duct-like k_kh + k_s wave; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves, p. 14–17), schlieren, LES and global models confirm ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) p. 12–13; [Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md) p. 13–14; [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) p. 15–17).
4. **Stages close on different shock wavenumbers and exclude each other near a single-jet staging point** ([Nogueira 2022](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) p. 7–13; Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 11–13; Mancinelli 2019, p. 6–7).
5. **Frequency is predictable to a few per cent; amplitude is not** (see "Frequency prediction and global modes" below).
6. **The nozzle and its surroundings set amplitude and mode ranges, by tens of dB, far more than frequency** ([Ponton 1992](../notes/ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) p. 537–548; Raman 1998, p. 75–78).
7. **The far-field tone is not the loop.** Round and low-AR jets radiate through acoustically matched wavenumbers and through shock leakage, now seen directly ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) p. 13–19), both by-products of the triad (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 20–22; Nogueira 2024, p. 32).

## Closure: from Powell's loop to the guided jet mode

Powell's model (Raman 1998, p. 48–49) treats the shock cells as a phased array of monopoles, giving f = U_c/[s(1 + M_c)]. [Tam 1986](../notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md) (p. 7–10) made screech the θ → 180° limit of BBSAN: the inverse BBSAN peak frequency, linear in cos θ, extrapolates onto the tone, and only the band radiated straight at the lip sustains the loop (the "weakest link"), so the loop phase does not choose the frequency. [Tam 1988](../notes/tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) (p. 10–12) extended this to rectangular jets, with spacing set by the minor dimension. All of these close the loop with free-stream sound (Tam 1995, p. 37).

The guided-mode picture came from subsonic jets, whose trapped duct-like waves and upstream "discrete free-stream" mode propagate only between neutral saddle points, bounding the near-nozzle tones ([Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) p. 14–19). Edgington-Mitchell 2018 (p. 6–11) split the leading POD pair of A1 and A2 jets (NPR 2.10, 2.25) by the sign of k_x and found an upstream wave at about a fifth of the KH amplitude, which only the band test could separate from the acoustic continuum (p. 10). Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves (p. 14–23) added the downstream duct-like wave and traced the standing wave and core modulation mostly to G-JM–KH interference, not to shocks modulating KH growth. Nogueira 2024 (p. 19–33) supplies the physics: the G-JM is in-jet sound in a duct whose wall impedance runs from hard at the sonic line to soft at large |α|, and the evanescent outer field carries net energy upstream. Because the triad sits inside the shear layer, screech always needs the G-JM; impingement tones need it only when their source is in the core (p. 31–33; contrast the wall-jet shocklet in [Weightman 2019](../notes/weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md) and [Edgington-Mitchell 2025](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md), both not tagged).

The closure generalises. An AR 2 elliptical jet's se₁ G-JM is neutral at the tone only over a short x band, where its measured amplitude peaks ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) p. 22–24). In twin round jets the G-JM band, not KH growth, selects the coupling symmetry ([Nogueira 2021, twin jets](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md) p. 16–19; [Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) p. 18–19). In twin rectangular LES the G-JM gain (10.1) dwarfs that of free-stream sound (1.16), and its points of return sit at peaks of shock–KH interaction (Jeun 2024, p. 20–21). Single AR 2 rectangular LES (TR 1–3) put the NPR 3 tones only in the first antisymmetric planar band, with in-jet spectra peaking on k = −ω/a₀ (Gojon 2019, p. 24–31; branch points as in Nogueira 2024, p. 23). That is band membership only, with no eigenfunction match, and a 10h window (Δkh_j ≈ 0.6) cannot separate in-jet sound from a nearby A1 wave (Gojon 2019 note). Negative k_x is not upstream group velocity: the k_kh − k_s2 wave in an A1 jet is duct-like and cannot close the loop (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 13; Towne 2017, p. 8).

**Shock leakage and source location.** Raman's "feedback shock" from one dominant source in AR ≳ 9 rectangular jets was unexplained (p. 70–73), and his localisation (3rd shock at M_j 1.45; p. 71–73) assumes one source. So does Mercier 2017: near-field phase reversal across ten round jets puts the source at the 4th shock tip for A1 and A2 and the 3rd or 4th for flapping B, to one cell (p. 215, 219–222, Table 5), where Mancinelli fixed his reflection (Mancinelli 2019, p. 4–5; Mancinelli 2021, p. 7). Its return wave, known only to c₀ ± 10 %, cannot tell the G-JM from sound (p. 213, 226). Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, saw leakage in k_x < 0-filtered 1 Mfps schlieren: the shock tip overshoots the sonic line as a KH vortex passes and sheds a front that is sharp from birth (p. 3–4, 13–19). Ray tracing on phase-reconstructed PIV puts the escape at the saddle between vortices (p. 26–27), first at the 3rd cell for A1 and C (M_j 1.45, as in Raman) and the 4th for A2, moving between the 6th and 3rd with vortex strength, close to the 3rd–5th scatter in the schlieren (p. 19–30): Mercier's cell for A2 and one upstream for A1, within both methods' resolution. In an AR 2 elliptic jet the flapping mode's coherent vorticity, a leakage proxy, peaks just before the 3rd shock ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) p. 2747). None of this supports a source at the instability-wave peak (Tam 1986, p. 4; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 26). One shock-like, upstream-directed source explains the feedback shock (p. 21–23), though several cells emit (p. 28). Closure was not tested; Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 20–22) treats leakage as a correlated sound source that may close the loop only at high AR.

## Frequency prediction and global modes

Linear models predict the tone to 1–10 % and LES to about 0.01 in St. The successful models, G-JM resonance loops (Mancinelli 2019, 2021), the Floquet saddle ([Nogueira 2021, absolute instability](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md)), local stability on PIV means (Nogueira 2022) and global or resolvent analysis (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves; Farghadan 2026), largely coincide, and all are more sensitive to shear-layer thickness than to shock strength. A model base flow is absolutely unstable while the measured mean's least-damped global mode is slightly stable, so whether screech is a self-excited global mode is open. U_c rises along the jet, so Powell-type formulas need the source position (Mercier 2017). LES matches frequency but not yet the heating trend of amplitude (Gojon 2019). The prediction table, convection speed, the global-mode question, what the models say about amplitude and how LES and linear models compare with experiment are on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md).

## Staging and mode switching

Screech jumps between Merle's A1 and A2 (axisymmetric), B (flapping), C (helical) and D stages (Raman 1998, p. 49, 52, 101). A1 and B close on the primary shock wavenumber k_s1, A2 and C on a suboptimal k_s2 (Nogueira 2022, p. 7–13; Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 11–13), seen in Mercier's data as one extra loop period at a fixed 4th-shock source (Mercier 2017, p. 222–224). Near a single-jet staging point the tones exclude each other, each on its own short-time mean ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) p. 11–13). Selection, hysteresis, rig dependence, multimodal and twin-jet loops, and the PyFR continuation LES that traces the A1/A2 limits are on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md).

## Amplitude and receptivity

Raman (1998, p. 49) writes the limit cycle as growth × shock interaction × feedback efficiency × receptivity = 1; shock strength keeps rising with M_j while amplitude plateaus at 154–155 dB over M_j 1.3–1.65 and then collapses (p. 64). Thickening the lip from t/D 0.015 to 0.625 keeps screech going to M_j ≈ 1.95 rather than 1.6–1.7, revives the B mode above M_j 1.7 and shifts B and C wavelengths slightly in opposite directions (Ponton 1992, p. 537–541). A thick lip raised screech by 10 dB, a flapping plane rotated about 90° between nominally identical tests, and heating makes jets quieter (Tam 1995, p. 23, 36, 39–40). Reflectors change amplitude and frequency by detuning the loop phase (Raman 1998, p. 77–78); the Monash upstream plate produced an unusual dominant m = 0 mode at NPR 3.3 (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 16); and identical twin nozzles on two Monash rigs, one with hard enclosure walls and a flush plenum face, shift the onset of the lower tone, while OSU's facility changes the staging most at NPR 3.0–4.0 (Bell 2021, p. 6, 17–18, 35). A small tab removes the tones, lowering peak narrowband levels by about 16–21 dB ([Norum 1982](../notes/norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md) p. 149, 162), and asymmetric trailing-edge cutouts suppress overexpanded rectangular-jet screech while symmetric ones amplify it ([Kerechanin 2001](../notes/kerechanin-2001-trailing-edge-rectangular-jet-acoustics.md) p. 4–5). An untripped rectangular LES with every tone frequency right has screech rising from 151 to 177 dB/St over TR 1 → 3 where the experiment falls (Gojon 2019, p. 18–19, 21–22), probably because of its weakly turbulent exit boundary layer (1.75 % peak, p. 8–10) and the lower Re of the hot cases (Gojon 2019 note).

What the absolute-instability and resonance models can say about amplitude is on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md). Screech also changes the jet: at peak screech an AR 16.7 rectangular jet spreads at 36° rather than 20–25° ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md) p. 123), and strong screech adds about 3 dB to mixing noise ([Tam 1996](../notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), not tagged).

## Triads, harmonics and mean-flow deformation

Beyond the KH–shock triad, three kinds of interaction matter. **Between tones:** A1–A2 modulation at St₂ − St₁ during switching (Mancinelli 2019, p. 7); and above M_j ≈ 1.25 the B tone appears in the m = 0 spectrum as a quadratic m = ±1 product (cross-bicoherence ≈ 1 at St 0.34), not as an axisymmetric loop (Mancinelli 2021, p. 24–25). **Harmonics:** a bilinear harmonic resolvent on the PIV mean plus the screech mode reproduces the St = 0 shock-cell correction and the 2St_s and 3St_s sideline beams; the harmonics radiate from k_kh − k_s1 although the loop closes on k_s2, and forcing with the screech self-interaction matches the optimal harmonic responses (inner products > 0.95 per frequency) without appeal to turbulence (Farghadan 2026, p. 20–25). **Mean flow:** the St_s − St_s = 0 self-interaction distorts the mean, so each loop has its own short-time mean and shock spacing both selects the loop and is changed by it (Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 3, 16–19), as Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 12) inferred from jumps in the shock spectrum; Krothapalli 1986 is the long-time-average precursor. In |m| = 1 round-jet screech the (±1, St_s) ↔ (2, 0) triad lets weak |m| = 2 streaks split the m = ±1 eigenvalues into flapping and wagging modes and fix the axis, which in LES precesses with the streaks by about π/2 over about 100 cycles ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) p. 7–12, 20); the helical C mode reverses its helicity often ([Edgington-Mitchell 2023](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2023-screeching-jet-seen-from-all-sides.md) p. 4).

## Disagreements and open questions

Questions on staging (labelling A2, hysteresis, which instability screeches) are on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md), and those on what selects the frequency, self-excitation and amplitude prediction on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md).

**Leakage as closure.** Round-jet evidence is against it: leaked fronts are born outside the jet, which cannot feed the G-JM (Nogueira 2024, p. 14, 32), and at NPR 2.10 the first leak (x/D ≈ 1.8; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 23) lies upstream of the G-JM peak (≈ 3.3; Edgington-Mitchell 2018, p. 6), though at NPR 2.25 both sit near x/D ≈ 3. Yet totally reflected rays stay in the core like a G-JM (the note's reading), so one event might feed both. Correlating each cycle's leakage with G-JM amplitude at the lip would separate them; at high AR (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 21–22), tones outside the G-JM band would show leakage closure.

**Directivity.** In the ray model one leakage source already radiates along Norum's 160° peak (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 21–23); Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 20) gets the upstream/downstream balance from a distributed Mach-wave-type source. Both are qualitative. The leakage paper's microphones stop at 120° but show the C-mode first harmonic peaking at 90° (p. 6), as in Tam 1995 (p. 21–23), whereas Krothapalli 1986 found the fundamental dominating there (p. 125). Far-field angles to 160° and LES would decide.

**Flapping, helical and precession.** Whether streaks anchor screech or screech drives streaks is open (Nogueira 2026, p. 25), as is whether stage C corresponds to weak streaks; forced LES or multi-view time-resolved schlieren would test it.

**Full scale.** Screech is absent from rocket and afterburning-engine data ([Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 9, 16; [Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 6). Heating weakens it (Tam 1995, p. 39–40), though not in the one heated LES (above). In a planar vortex sheet heating lowers the G-JM bands (Gojon 2019, Fig. 20, p. 25), and the hot underexpanded tones (TR 2 and 3 at NPR 4) already fall 8–31 % below the A1 band (Gojon 2019 note's arithmetic), which the authors blame on the thin-shear-layer idealisation (p. 26). A finite-thickness or LES-mean-flow band calculation for hot, strongly off-design jets would show whether the band misses hot plumes.

## Methods and data

Staging-specific methods (shock-spectrum peaks, switch tracking, loop counts) are on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md), and imaging and PIV practice on [schlieren-and-density-imaging](schlieren-and-density-imaging.md) and [diagnostics-and-facilities](diagnostics-and-facilities.md).
- **Non-time-resolved PIV**: POD of transverse velocity, ring check on the pair, complex mode, FFT in x, split by the sign of k_x (Edgington-Mitchell 2018; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves, p. 9–17; Ivelja 2024). The reconstructed cycle feeds leakage ray tracing (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 11–13), and a POD-pair rebuild gives coherent vorticity at the shock tips as a source proxy (Edgington-Mitchell 2015, p. 2747). The route needs a single steady tone and hides helicity sign (Edgington-Mitchell 2023).
- **Time-resolved schlieren**: SPOD at the tone and the standing-wave wavenumber (Edgington-Mitchell 2022), Hilbert phase (Jeun 2024), and k_x < 0 filtering for single, intermittent leakage events (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 10, 19).
- **Microphones**: phase-averaged axial surveys against a fixed reference locate the dominant source; radial surveys cannot, and beyond about 5D reflections take over even in an anechoic room (Mercier 2017, p. 216–221).
- **Pitfalls**: phase and group velocity can differ in sign; precession splits a tone across SPOD modes 1 and 2 (Nogueira 2026, p. 22); shocks inside a PIV window smear over ≈ 0.2D ([Edgington-Mitchell 2014](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2014-underexpanded-jet-mach-disk-shear-layer.md) p. 16–17). Model inputs and validation data are discussed on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md).
- **Data tables**: tones and shock spacings in `data/screech_tones.csv` and `data/shock_cell_spacing.csv`.

## Relevance to our work

**SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Take the Hilbert transform in time, or along −x for the G-JM, since along +x it discards the k_x < 0 G-JM (Edgington-Mitchell 2022 note). Conditioning the k_x < 0 field on each cycle's KH amplitude would replace the static r̄ ± 2σ rescaling of the leakage source (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 28–30).

**PyFR staging LES** (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`). Its tests of staging limits, hysteresis, receptivity, leakage location and loop count are on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md). It also offers the conclusive self-excitation test, an NPR step ([screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md)), and a check of how amplitude depends on the exit boundary layer (Gojon 2019).

**Rocket noise.** Lab screech tones do not carry over to launch vehicles (Lubert 2022), but the Tam & Tanna triad behind the G-JM is the same machinery used for BBSAN ([Wong 2021](../notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), not tagged).
