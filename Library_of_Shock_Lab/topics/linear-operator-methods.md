# Linear operator methods: global stability, resolvent and marching

_Last updated: 2026-10-21 · 23 papers_

## Scope

Methods built on the discretised linearised Navier–Stokes operator about a two-dimensional base flow, usually the turbulent mean: global stability (is the jet an amplifier or an oscillator?), resolvent analysis and the problem of what forcing and eddy viscosity to use, parabolised and one-way marching (PSE, PFE, OWNS) as cheaper routes to the same responses, and the harmonic resolvent, which puts triadic interactions back into the operator. The page records what each method explains (KH wavepackets, Orr packets, lift-up streaks, trapped acoustic modes, screech modes and their harmonics), how its modes compare with SPOD of experiments and LES, and what it costs.

The models these methods build on (why linearising about the mean works, eddy viscosity in general, local stability and vortex sheets, absolute instability, guided-jet waves) are on [linear models](linear-models.md), and shock-cell models, Floquet models in x and wavepacket × shock-cell sources on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md). Applications are linked: [screech](screech.md) and [screech staging and mode switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md) for screech global modes, [non-axisymmetric jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md) for cross-plane resolvent streaks, [jet-noise mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md) for Mach-wave radiation, and SPOD practice in [modal decomposition](modal-decomposition.md). Five non-jet papers underpin the methods: pipe ([McKeon & Sharma 2010](../notes/mckeon-2010-critical-layer-framework-turbulent-pipe-flow.md)), channel ([Morra 2021](../notes/morra-2021-colour-of-forcing-resolvent-channel.md)), airfoil ([Rolandi 2025](../notes/rolandi-2025-biglobal-resolvent-naca0012.md)) and two reviews ([Taira 2017](../notes/taira-2017-modal-analysis-fluid-flows-overview.md); [Taira 2020](../notes/taira-2020-modal-analysis-applications-outlook.md)). The local-to-global framework is [Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md).

## Current understanding

1. **Free jets are amplifiers.** No isolated, weakly damped global mode exists. The "preferred mode" (St ≈ 0.43–0.46) is a pseudo-resonance of a non-normal operator ([Garnaud 2013](../notes/garnaud-2013-preferred-mode-incompressible-jets.md), p. 5, 8, 11), not the weakly damped global mode once proposed ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 501–502). Compressible jets add discrete trapped-wave branches, all still stable ([Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 8).
2. **The resolvent is low rank where KH dominates.** σ₁ ≫ σ₂ for roughly 0.2–0.3 ≲ St ≲ 2 at m = 0 and 1, where the optimal response is the KH wavepacket and matches the leading SPOD mode ([Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 6, 13, 16–17; [Towne 2018](../notes/towne-2018-spod-relationship-dmd-resolvent.md), p. 35; [Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 19–20).
3. **Three linear mechanisms organise broadband jet turbulence.** KH dominates at moderate St and low m (c_ph ≈ 0.8U_j). Orr takes over for m = 0 below St ≈ 0.3 and in suboptimal modes (c_ph ≈ 0.4, downstream of the core). Lift-up streaks dominate for St/m ≲ 0.1 ([Pickering 2020](../notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md), p. 15–21; [Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 8–13; [Nogueira 2019](../notes/nogueira-2019-large-scale-streaky-structures-jets.md), p. 13–19).
4. **Most failures come from the forcing, not the operator.** Real nonlinear forcing is coloured and projects onto suboptimal modes ([Towne 2018](../notes/towne-2018-spod-relationship-dmd-resolvent.md), p. 18–21; [Morra 2021](../notes/morra-2021-colour-of-forcing-resolvent-channel.md), p. 20–22). An eddy viscosity repairs much of this ([Pickering 2021](../notes/pickering-2021-optimal-eddy-viscosity-resolvent-jets.md), p. 14–17), but the forcing amplitudes are still not uniform (p. 26–27).
5. **With the shocks in the mean flow, the operator finds screech.** The least-stable global mode falls within about 1% of the screech frequency ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 11–17; [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 11), but it is slightly stable, so screech must be sustained by forcing (Farghadan, p. 14). The harmonic resolvent then recovers the shock-cell correction to the mean and the harmonics (p. 20–23).
6. **Marching methods trade upstream waves for cost.** PSE keep one downstream mode and miss low-St Orr packets ([Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 23–24); OWNS-R keeps every downstream mode ([Zhu & Towne 2021](../notes/zhu-2021-recursive-one-way-navier-stokes.md), p. 24–26); none carries the upstream waves that close screech ([Nogueira 2022, PFE](../notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 9, 13).
7. **Mean-flow eigenvalues are models, not stability statements** ([Taira 2017](../notes/taira-2017-modal-analysis-fluid-flows-overview.md), p. 27–28; [Rolandi 2025](../notes/rolandi-2025-biglobal-resolvent-naca0012.md), p. 17).

## What a mean-flow operator means

[McKeon & Sharma 2010](../notes/mckeon-2010-critical-layer-framework-turbulent-pipe-flow.md) gave the resolvent framework: the nonlinear terms act as forcing of the operator linearised about the measured mean (p. 8), and the response is low rank (p. 13). Why linearising about a mean works at all, and how eddy viscosity enters, is on [linear models](linear-models.md). The reviews add caveats. A mean flow is not an equilibrium, so its eigenvalues are a model rather than a stability statement ([Taira 2017](../notes/taira-2017-modal-analysis-fluid-flows-overview.md), p. 27–28; [Taira 2020](../notes/taira-2020-modal-analysis-applications-outlook.md), p. 7–8). [Rolandi 2025](../notes/rolandi-2025-biglobal-resolvent-naca0012.md) quantifies the risk: mean-flow eigenvalues miss the airfoil shedding frequency by 5.5% and 13.4%, worse at stronger nonlinearity (p. 17), and some are stable while the flow oscillates (p. 9–10). Weak-shock screech does far better, at about 1% ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 11–12).

## Global stability: amplifier or oscillator

[Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md) built the theory for slowly developing flows on the local absolute/convective criterion (on [linear models](linear-models.md)). Such flows fall into four classes: locally stable, convective amplifiers, marginally stable flows with weakly damped global modes, and flows with an absolute pocket (p. 488–489). The pocket is necessary but not sufficient for a global mode, whose frequency is set to leading order at a saddle in the *complex* X-plane, with ω_G,i ≤ ω₀,ᵢ(X_s) ≤ ω₀,ᵢ|max (p. 492–495). The inner turning-point problem is a Ginzburg–Landau equation, with a ladder of global modes O(ε) apart (p. 496). The Ginzburg–Landau model of [Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md) shows the gap: absolute at the inlet for A > 1.25, globally unstable only above A ≈ 1.6 (p. 19–21). Pressure feedback from surfaces, as in edge and cavity tones, is another route (p. 498).

For incompressible model jets every global mode is stable and none stands out ([Garnaud 2013](../notes/garnaud-2013-preferred-mode-incompressible-jets.md), p. 5). The compressible M 0.9 jet adds discrete trapped branches, still all stable ([Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 8); what those waves are is on [linear models](linear-models.md).

On a PIV mean flow of a screeching jet, the least-stable mode sits at St = 0.667 against 0.67 measured, with wavenumbers about 0.5/D too high ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 11–12, 16, 19). [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) found St = 0.72 against 0.714. That eigenvalue is slightly stable, so screech must be sustained by forcing (p. 11, 14). Farghadan also found screech-like modes at St 0.66 and 0.77 that use different shock wavenumbers, and a duct/guided resonance at St 0.31 (p. 11–17). The PIV-derived mean flow limits the analysis to weak shocks (NPR 2.10 only in Edgington-Mitchell 2021, p. 10).

**Numerical choices.** Both screech studies used Re = 10³ as an eddy-viscosity surrogate, although that value removes trapped modes in subsonic jets ([Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 25–27). When the mean flow has unstable eigenvalues, a discounted resolvent with a finite time horizon is the usual workaround ([Rolandi 2025](../notes/rolandi-2025-biglobal-resolvent-naca0012.md), p. 10; [Taira 2020](../notes/taira-2020-modal-analysis-applications-outlook.md), p. 19). Truncated domains can add a spurious outflow-to-inflow "box" resonance (Huerre & Monkewitz, p. 498–499; cf. the outflow-spoiled Ginzburg–Landau eigenvalues of [Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 20–21).

## Resolvent analysis and the forcing problem

**What it explains.** With the nozzle included, as local theory had anticipated ([linear models](linear-models.md)), the resolvent's optimal response is KH fed by Orr-type forcing inside the nozzle boundary layer, and the suboptimals are free-jet Orr packets ([Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 9–15; cf. [Garnaud 2013](../notes/garnaud-2013-preferred-mode-incompressible-jets.md), p. 8–9). Lesshafft's local projection contradicts Garnaud's reading of the downstream wavepacket as a jet-column mode (p. 13). [Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md) separates KH (c_ph ≈ 0.8U_j) from Orr (≈ 0.4U_j, on the critical layer) at M_j 0.4–1.5 and recovers Mach-wave radiation at M_j 1.5 (p. 9–13, 21–23). Orr is the mechanism that PSE wavepacket models had missed below St ≈ 0.2.

Lift-up is the third mechanism. A local resolvent at ω = 0 reproduces the radial shape of m = 7 and 10 streak SPOD modes, but only the peak position for m = 3 ([Nogueira 2019](../notes/nogueira-2019-large-scale-streaky-structures-jets.md), p. 17–18). A global eddy-viscosity resolvent maps all three mechanisms, and the dominant streak m falls roughly as 1/x ([Pickering 2020](../notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md), p. 21–22). For rectangular jets a cross-plane resolvent predicts dipole AS/SA streaks and suggests corner vortices are not linear streaks (p. 13–17), but its thickness trend flips with the forcing weighting and is unvalidated ([Marshall 2024](../notes/marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets.md), p. 17–19, 23–24).

**How well it matches.**
- SPOD and resolvent modes coincide only when the resolvent expansion coefficients are uncorrelated; with deterministic coefficients the model is rank one, whatever the number of modes kept ([Towne 2018](../notes/towne-2018-spod-relationship-dmd-resolvent.md), p. 18–21). Under coherent forcing, only the leading mode survives when σ₁ ≫ σ₂ ([Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 22).
- Against experiment, the leading SPOD mode is matched for St 0.4–0.7 by the optimal response alone (p. 19–22). SPOD mode 2 is missed at every St, and the model's eigenvalue spectrum peaks at St 0.6 instead of 0.7 ([Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 20–21).
- Projections of resolvent onto SPOD modes are 0.94 for KH but 0.23 for low-St lift-up at (m, St) = (3, 0.05) ([Pickering 2020](../notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md), p. 17). The public M 0.9 database shows the same pattern: agreement at St 0.4–1, failure at 0.2 ([Towne 2022](../notes/towne-2022-database-reduced-complexity-modeling.md), not tagged, p. 6–7).
- Resolvent analysis amplifies trapped and Tam & Hu waves more strongly than they appear in SPOD of free jets ([Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 21–25), and slightly over-predicts the trapped-mode spike at St ≈ 0.4 ([Pickering 2020](../notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md), p. 29; cf. [Taira 2017](../notes/taira-2017-modal-analysis-fluid-flows-overview.md), p. 34).

**Forcing colour and eddy viscosity.**
- [Morra 2021](../notes/morra-2021-colour-of-forcing-resolvent-channel.md) computed the true forcing in channel DNS. Passed through the resolvent it recovers the response exactly. The forcing is rank two, projects mainly onto suboptimal input modes, and its components cancel one another. An eddy viscosity helps because its equivalent forcing roughly reproduces those projections, not because it models the physics (p. 11–24).
- For jets, an adjoint-optimised eddy-viscosity field raises low-St alignment from about 10% to 80% and KH alignment to 96%. A RANS k–ε viscosity scaled by c = 0.2 comes close for St 0.05–1, m = 0–5 and M_j 0.4–1.5 (p. 14–17, 23). Alignment is worst where SPOD eigenvalues overlap within their confidence bands, and at M_j 1.5 (p. 24–25). Uniform linear damping does clearly worse ([Pickering 2021](../notes/pickering-2021-optimal-eddy-viscosity-resolvent-jets.md), p. 28).
- The other jet studies used a uniform Re: 3 × 10⁴ in [Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), where suboptimal gains scale as Re^1.2 and so Re decides where Orr overtakes KH (p. 14–15), and 10³ in the screech studies above. A non-smooth PIV-derived base flow can create spurious suboptimal modes ([Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 11–12).

## Marching methods: PSE, PFE and OWNS

PSE march downstream and keep a single mode, so they cannot carry upstream waves. They miss Orr packets at low St because the most unstable local wave is only a subdominant resolvent mode there ([Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 23–24). Volume forcing in the free shear layer recovers the low-St growth near the nozzle that inflow-forced PSE miss ([Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 24–25). PSE wavepackets remain good enough to drive BBSAN source models ([shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md)).

The parabolised Floquet equations (PFE) of [Nogueira 2022, PFE](../notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md) add shock harmonics α ± jα_s to PSE, which lengthens the envelope in shock-containing jets (p. 18–19; consequences on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md)). PFE cannot carry upstream waves, so screech frequencies are excluded (p. 9, 13), and their minimum step grows as M_j rises (p. 6–7). [Zhu & Towne 2021](../notes/zhu-2021-recursive-one-way-navier-stokes.md) (OWNS-R) keeps every downstream mode, including acoustics and Orr waves, at about 3× the per-step cost of PSE, but removes upstream modes by construction (p. 24–26). A Floquet OWNS model that carries upstream waves is the test proposed for whether shocks modulate the KH envelope directly ([shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md)).

## Harmonic resolvent and triads

The standard resolvent lumps all triadic interactions into one forcing term ([Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 9). For screech the key triad, KH × shock giving k_kh ± k_s, is already in a global analysis on a shock-containing mean ([shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md)); the harmonic resolvent goes after the rest. [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) first showed that the resolvent screech mode matches the PIV POD pair (inner product 0.89; p. 17). Adding that mode to the mean flow as a periodic base flow, a bilinear harmonic resolvent reproduces the shock-cell correction to the mean and the sideline beams at 2St_s and 3St_s, which the standard resolvent misses (p. 20–23). The screech mode's self-interaction forcing gives responses aligned above 0.95 frequency by frequency, but the concatenated alignment is only 0.36, because the SVD picks inter-frequency phases that the real self-interaction does not have (p. 24–25). Steady streaks enter the same way, as a zero-frequency partner that couples m = ±1 ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 7–8).

## Disagreements and open questions

**Oscillator or not?** The Floquet model gives ω₀,ᵢ > 0 ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md)), while the global mean-flow screech mode is slightly stable ([Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 14). These need not conflict, since a local absolute pocket does not guarantee a global mode ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 492–495). The base flows also differ (model vs measured mean), and mean flows of saturated oscillators should be close to marginal (cf. [Rolandi 2025](../notes/rolandi-2025-biglobal-resolvent-naca0012.md), p. 9–10). *To settle:* run SP-LSA on the measured mean, and apply Huerre & Monkewitz's ranked tests with NPR as the control parameter (p. 516–518): lock-in, response against forcing amplitude (slope 1 for an amplifier, flat for an oscillator), saturation, and transient Landau fits, as [Oberleithner 2011](../notes/oberleithner-2011-swirling-jet-vortex-breakdown-stability.md) did (p. 9–11).

**What forcing model, and what eddy viscosity, for screeching jets?** Even an optimised eddy viscosity leaves the forcing amplitudes non-uniform ([Pickering 2021](../notes/pickering-2021-optimal-eddy-viscosity-resolvent-jets.md), p. 26–27), and alignment is worst at M_j 1.5 (p. 24–25). Shock-containing jets are untested: Re = 10³ is used routinely, although it removes trapped modes in subsonic jets ([Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 25–27), and the RANS model of Pickering 2021 has not been tried on them. *To settle:* repeat the screech global and resolvent analyses with a c ≈ 0.2 RANS viscosity and compare the damping of the guided and duct modes.

**Can a harmonic resolvent stand in for the real triads?** Frequency by frequency it can, but the concatenated response aligns at only 0.36 because the SVD chooses inter-frequency phases freely ([Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 24–25). BMD of time-resolved schlieren or LES measures those phases directly, so it is the natural check (our inference).

## Methods and data

- **Inputs.** A smooth two-dimensional mean flow, for which non-time-resolved PIV is enough. Recipes: tanh fits with Crocco–Busemann temperature ([Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 3–4), since a non-smooth PIV base flow creates spurious suboptimals; or extrapolated PIV with constant stagnation density checked against BOS ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 10), which limits the analysis to weak shocks. Local-model inputs are on [linear models](linear-models.md).
- **Validation data.** *Time-resolved* (schlieren, microphones, LES): SPOD modes, with their confidence intervals checked before comparing. Oscillator tests (lock-in, forcing-amplitude response, transient Landau fits) also need time series ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 516–518). *Snapshot PIV*: the POD pair can calibrate the amplitude of a periodic base flow ([Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 18).
- **Comparison metrics.** Alignment |ψ₁*Wu₁|; values above about 0.4 mean qualitative agreement ([Pickering 2021](../notes/pickering-2021-optimal-eddy-viscosity-resolvent-jets.md), p. 14). The direct–adjoint product isolates trapped waves ([Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 18–19).
- **Cost.** The harmonic resolvent with RSVD-Δt takes under 2 h on 200 cores ([Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 18–19). OWNS-R costs about 3× PSE per step ([Zhu & Towne 2021](../notes/zhu-2021-recursive-one-way-navier-stokes.md), p. 26), against minutes for local SP-LSA ([linear models](linear-models.md)).

## Relevance to our work

- **SPOD of lab data.** Resolvent modes are the natural model for the lab's SPOD modes, but the two coincide only for uncorrelated forcing coefficients ([Towne 2018](../notes/towne-2018-spod-relationship-dmd-resolvent.md)), so a match is expected only where the SPOD spectrum is low rank ([Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md)). Screech is the easy case: the resolvent screech mode matches a PIV POD pair ([Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 17), so a resolvent mode computed on the lab's own PIV mean can be checked against its POD pairs and SPOD modes with the alignment metric (our inference).
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Global and resolvent models are stationary and say nothing about intermittency. The coexisting screech-like eigenvalues on k_s1, k_s2 and k_s3 ([Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md)) are candidates for the modes whose amplitudes HPOD tracks near a staging boundary, and HPOD amplitudes are what the transient Landau test needs ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 518).
- **PyFR staging LES.** The LES mean flow can feed global and resolvent analyses without the weak-shock limit of PIV-derived means (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, p. 10), and converged A1 and A2 states at one NPR give two mean flows whose global spectra can be compared (our inference). The LES screech mode can supply the periodic base flow of a harmonic resolvent, at under 2 h on 200 cores (Farghadan 2026, p. 18–19). The same mean is the place to test a RANS eddy viscosity against Re = 10³.
- **Rocket noise.** The M_j 1.5 resolvent reproduces Mach-wave radiation ([Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 21–23), but for a cold or isothermal jet. Heated cases and wavepacket source models are on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md); see also [rocket-noise models](rocket-noise-models.md).
