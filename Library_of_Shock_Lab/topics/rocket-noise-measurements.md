# Rocket-noise measurements

_Last updated: 2026-10-20 · 14 papers_

## Scope

Full-scale measurements of rocket and high-power jet noise: horizontal static firings (Shuttle RSRM, GEM-60, SLS QM-1/QM-2, Antares stage 1), launches (Antares, Falcon 9, SLS Artemis I, Atlas V, Vulcan, Firefly Alpha, Starship), one small subscale motor, and the F-22A and F404 aircraft data that first showed nonlinear propagation and full-scale BBSAN. It covers levels, spectra, directivity, apparent source location, sound power, ignition overpressure, pad and terrain effects, nonlinear propagation, crackle and sonic booms, and what each campaign revealed about the models. The models themselves (SP-8072, DSM-2, SATURN, CAA/LES) are on [rocket-noise-models](rocket-noise-models.md); source physics in lab jets on [jet-noise-mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md); impingement feedback on [impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md). The numbers are collected in [rocket_noise.csv](../data/rocket_noise.csv). Almost all the work is from BYU (Gee's group) with NASA, Blue Ridge Research and JAXA; none is Shock Lab group work.

## Current understanding

1. **Rocket noise is broadband mixing noise dominated by Mach-wave radiation.** No screech, BBSAN, impingement tones or spatiospectral lobes have been found in full-scale rocket data ([Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 9, 12–16). RSRM spectra are a single low-frequency hump ([Kenny 2009](../notes/kenny-2009-space-shuttle-srm-plume-acoustics.md), p. 13–14), and a subscale Epsilon free jet shows no BBSAN at 90–120° ([Tsutsumi 2015](../notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 13). A full-scale afterburning F404 does show BBSAN at discrete shock cells ([Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 32), so the absence is a property of hot Mach 3–4 rocket plumes, not of scale.
2. **Acoustic efficiency is 0.3–0.4%**, below the classic 0.5% and SP-8072's 1%: RSRM 0.43%, Falcon 9 0.34% ([Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 8), revised to 0.30% from near-pad data ([Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 14); Atlas V 0.33%, but Vulcan Centaur needs 0.17% ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 375).
3. **The dominant source sits 17–22 D_e downstream and moves upstream with frequency** ([Gee 2012](../notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 4; [Gee 2016](../notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 1, 5; [Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 12–14). SP-8072's source-location curve inherits a plotting error from Potter (1968) (Lubert, p. 12–13).
4. **Peak directivity is 60–72° from the plume axis once angles are referred to that source**, not SP-8072's ~50° ([James 2012](../notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md), p. 8; [Mathews 2021](../notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 625; [Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 11–12; [Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 82). An Oertel convective Mach number predicts it ([Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 14).
5. **Spectra peak at Sr = fD/U_e ≈ 0.01–0.025**, about ten times lower than lab and military jets, for reasons not yet understood ([Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 10; [Mathews 2021](../notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 628–629; [Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 15–16).
6. **Waveforms carry acoustic shocks at every range measured**, to ~65 km ([Mathews 2021](../notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 624). Nonlinear steepening replaces high-frequency energy that linear absorption would remove ([Gee 2008](../notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 9–10; [Reichman 2018](../notes/reichman-2018-qm2-srm-far-field-acoustics.md), p. 6; [Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 84–85) but barely changes OASPL ([Gee 2008](../notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 12).
7. **The pad controls the first seconds.** Ignition overpressure is aimed along the flame-trench exit ([Gee 2023](../notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 4; [Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 51; [Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 5), and duct and deck sources dominate until the free plume clears the pad ([Panda 2013](../notes/panda-2013-noise-sources-rocket-test-firings-launch-phased-array.md), p. 19–20).
8. **Legacy environmental-assessment models miss by 10–20 dB** for SLS and Starship ([Gee 2023](../notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 3–4; [Gee 2024](../notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 5), while the data-built SATURN model is within ~1 dB inside 20 km ([Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 91, 95).

## Static firings: from the Shuttle RSRM to the SLS booster

NASA's first full-scale solid-motor database came from three horizontal RSRM firings (TEM-13, FVM-2, FSM-15; D_e 3.88 m) at Promontory, Utah, gathered so SP-8072 would no longer be extrapolated from 5–50 kN subscale motors to 13 MN ([Kenny 2009](../notes/kenny-2009-space-shuttle-srm-plume-acoustics.md), p. 7). The first look found positively skewed waveforms, a near-field OASPL peak of ≈156.5 dB at ≈23 D_n on a line 67 m off-axis, a far-field peak near 50° on a 304 m arc, and OASPL that tracked nozzle vectoring more than the 27% thrust decay (Kenny, p. 11–16). Haynes & Kenny turned the same arc into Ares I directivity indices ([Haynes 2009](../notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md), p. 12–13). Two reanalyses changed how those arc data are read. Gee et al. mapped OASPL and skewness between the 18D line and the 80D arc: peak radiation at ~56° traces back to a source at 17–18D; the source moves upstream and the directivity sideline as frequency rises; Sk{∂p/∂t} exceeds 30 and grows with distance; and beyond 40D, where the plume hits a hillside "bowl", high-frequency content and skewness collapse ([Gee 2012](../notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 4–11). They warned that the 80D arc is not in the geometric far field at low frequency (p. 6). James et al. made this quantitative: the 10 Hz source sits ≈31 D_e downstream, and referring each band to its apparent source rotates the DSM peak from 51° to 65°, matching the Oertel estimate; DSM-1 and DSM-2 agree only beyond 200–300 D_e, about 1 km ([James 2012](../notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md), p. 5–11). Lubert et al. restate the result as 53° → 65° ([Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 14–15).

The five-segment SLS booster (QM-2, 2016) was measured only in the far field: five stations at 80–120°, 2.40–2.64 km away, with OASPL falling from 118 to 106 dB, peaks at 10–20 Hz that move lower towards the aft angles (opposite to lab jets), energy out to ~10 kHz, and Sk{∂p/∂t} of 21–32 at 80–100° but under 3 at 110–120° ([Reichman 2018](../notes/reichman-2018-qm2-srm-far-field-acoustics.md), p. 6–10). QM-1, at similar levels a year earlier, peaked near Sk ≈ 3; the authors favour the atmosphere over motor temperature as the cause (p. 9). Horizontal firings into terrain are not clean free jets, which is why SATURN rejects the James source curve ([Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 79).

## Near-field intensity and source location

Vector intensity became the main source-location tool because far-field beamforming assumes straight rays. On a 12.7 cm solid motor, tetrahedral probes within ~6D of the shear layer showed the source contracting and moving upstream with frequency, radiation at 44–51°, and refraction near the plume; the OASPL maximum lay no further downstream than 10–12D, while the strongest single band (500 Hz) came from ~20D ([Gee 2010](../notes/gee-2010-near-field-vector-intensity-solid-rocket-motor.md), p. 3–6). On the 1.09 m GEM-60, twelve probes were processed with the phase-and-amplitude-gradient estimator (PAGE), which stays accurate far past the spatial Nyquist limit where finite-difference intensity fails ([Gee 2016](../notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 4; [Reichman 2018](../notes/reichman-2018-qm2-srm-far-field-acoustics.md), p. 7). Ray tracing put the 3 dB region at 17–32D at 50 Hz and 10–20D above 600 Hz, with radiation angles rising from 65–70° to 80–85°; the true source is at least 30D long and contracts less, and lies further downstream, than an afterburning F-22A source ([Gee 2016](../notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 1, 4–5). These intensity locations underlie SATURN's s(Sr) = 9.85 Sr^−0.21 D_eff ([Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 80) and Lubert's ~22 D_e at Sr 0.025 ([Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 13–14).

Aircraft sources sit much closer to the nozzle: holography places afterburning-fighter sources at ~6–15 D_e (Lubert, p. 12), and on the installed F404 at afterburner the dominant lipline maximum lies between the potential-core end (~7 D_j) and the supersonic-core end (~12–13 D_j), with BBSAN sources at shock cells II–VIII, cell I silent and VI–VII loudest ([Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 18, 32).

Beamforming has mapped pads rather than plumes. A 70-microphone array 400 ft from Wallops pad 0A found the water-quenched flame-duct exit to be the dominant Antares static-fire source at all frequencies, with the engine mount 16 dB weaker, contrary to SP-8072's distributed plume. At launch the source moved from mount openings to the duct, then over the deck when hot plume spilled during the TEL-avoidance tilt before the rainbirds reached full flow, and only after ~7 s to the free plume ([Panda 2013](../notes/panda-2013-noise-sources-rocket-test-firings-launch-phased-array.md), p. 17–23). Its SEM deconvolution broke distributed plume sources into spurious spots (p. 16).

## Directivity, convective Mach number and flight

Measured peak angles agree once the origin is right: RSRM 65° ([James 2012](../notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md), p. 8), GEM-63 ~65° ([Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 15), and Falcon 9 64° both at 7–14 km (1 dB-down 58–70°; [Mathews 2021](../notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 625) and from stations at 240–750 m (1 dB band 58–72°, 3 dB band 48–79°; [Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 11–12), with SLS at 66° and Antares 230 at 60–70° as cited by Kellison (p. 12). The static Oertel number gives 69° for Falcon 9; subtracting the vehicle Mach number at peak radiation (M_v ≈ 0.55) gives 64°, and the convective-velocity form needs κ ≈ 0.31, half the lab range 0.6–0.85 ([Mathews 2021](../notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 625–627). Kellison et al. compared five convective Mach numbers: the supersonic instability-wave value (66°) fits best and the KH form (72°) is too far from the axis. Their frequency-resolved Q(θ, Sr) peaks at 10–30° below Sr 0.004 and settles at 60–75° above 0.0125 ([Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 12–13). The Atlas V map behind SATURN settles at ≈72° above Sr 0.03, 15–20° further from the axis than SP-8072's indices ([Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 82), and near-field vectors at high frequency (80–85°) point upstream of every far-field index ([Gee 2016](../notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 5). The 3 dB width is ≈30–35°, against 56–57° in the Sutherland and Eldred models ([Mathews 2021](../notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 627).

## Sound power, spectra and clustering

Sound power has been measured by integrating a far-field directivity (Falcon 9, 196 dB re 1 pW, η 0.34%; [Mathews 2021](../notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 627–628) and by sweeping one microphone through angle along the trajectory (195.4 ± 0.7 dB, η 0.30%; [Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 14); Kellison et al. say the earlier value lacked a ground-reflection correction worth 2–3 dB (p. 15). Scaled to 100 D_eff, OASPL collapses at 141–144 dB for Falcon 9, GEM-63 and RSRM, well below the Greska et al. convective-Mach fit at Falcon 9's M_O = 2.81, which they read as η saturating at rocket conditions (p. 16–17). The McInerny-type OASPL_max model fits Falcon 9 within 1 dB at 100 D_e ([Mathews 2021](../notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 628), all eleven Atlas V stations within ±2.5 dB ([Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 53), and two Atlas V launches to 20 km with RMSE 1.8 dB once a ground factor δ = 1.9 is included ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 375–376).

Peak Strouhal numbers range from 0.010 to 0.025 across RSRM, Falcon 9, Atlas V and SATURN (values and bases in the CSV; see open questions). Exit-sound-speed and critical-diameter scalings collapse rockets with other jets better than fD/U_e does ([Mathews 2021](../notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 628–629).

Clustering matters. The Atlas V's two RD-180 nozzles (S/D ≈ 1.14) give a lower peak frequency broadside (32 Hz) than end-on (43 Hz); both collapse to Sr ≈ 0.019 if the broadside view uses the merged diameter D√2, and OASPL shows no asymmetry beyond ±1.9 dB ([Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 53–55). Starship (74.4 MN) is 5–10.5 dB louder than SLS and 9–17 dB louder than Falcon 9, more than thrust alone suggests ([Gee 2024](../notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 7), while Vulcan's separated plumes radiate at half the Atlas V efficiency ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 375).

## Nonlinear propagation and crackle

The effect was established at full scale with F-22A run-ups: a generalized-Burgers propagator fed with 23 m waveforms matched 305 m one-third-octave spectra within 2–7 dB to 20 kHz, while a linear model was 7–12 dB low at 4 kHz and 120–140 dB low at 20 kHz, at every angle from 90° to 145° from the inlet; OASPL changed by about 1 dB or less ([Gee 2008](../notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 9–12). This contradicts an earlier F/A-18E bispectral result of no nonlinearity at 90° (p. 11). The rocket data agree with the F-22A picture. Sk{∂p/∂t} exceeds 30 near the RSRM peak direction and grows with distance ([Gee 2012](../notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 8); QM-2 still shows 21–32 at 2.5 km ([Reichman 2018](../notes/reichman-2018-qm2-srm-far-field-acoustics.md), p. 8); SLS keeps 80–95 dB at 10 kHz at 1.5–5 km ([Gee 2023](../notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 6); Falcon 9 launch and boom spectra hold the weak-shock ≈10 dB/decade roll-off to 10 kHz ([Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 6). Atlas V spectra lose ≈15 dB at 1 kHz over 7 km against 26 dB from linear absorption, and 30–40 dB at 10 kHz against more than 1200 dB ([Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 84–85). The atmosphere matters: a foggy Atlas V launch decayed at the weak-shock rate r^−1.1, a clear one at r^−1 ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 375), and QM-1's skewness was about a tenth of QM-2's (Reichman, p. 9). Crackle is reported but not quantified at SLS, Starship and Falcon 9 launches ([Gee 2023](../notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 5; [Gee 2024](../notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 4–5; Kellison, p. 5).

## Ignition overpressure, pads and terrain

Ignition overpressure is a directional transient set by the trench. At Artemis I the SRB rarefaction reached ≈400 Pa (146 dB) at 1.78 km on the trench side, while post-lift-off radiation was north–south symmetric ([Gee 2023](../notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 4–5). Distance-scaled Atlas V levels spread by ~30 dB with azimuth, peaking at the trench exit, with an unusual "whooshing" waveform shared with Antares ([Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 50–51); Falcon 9 shows ~6 dB between trench and opposite side, with energy at 2–3 Hz ([Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 5). SATURN omits the deflected plume, so its comparisons start 5–8 s after ignition (Kellison, p. 9; Mathews 2025, p. 96–97).

Terrain and ground also shape the data. Terrain shielded one Falcon 9 site by ≥10 dB until line of sight, which a thin-barrier model reproduces ([Mathews 2021](../notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 630–631). Pole microphones show ground-interference nulls that wind can move ([Kenny 2009](../notes/kenny-2009-space-shuttle-srm-plume-acoustics.md), p. 14; [Gee 2010](../notes/gee-2010-near-field-vector-intensity-solid-rocket-motor.md), p. 4), and ground-plate microphones need a pressure factor δ ≈ 1.9 ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 373). In subscale Epsilon tests at an intermediate vehicle height (H/D_e = 14), the fairing load came from free-jet Mach waves reflected off the ground in the open flame path, not from deflector impingement; a front cover cut it by 3 dB ([Tsutsumi 2015](../notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 15–16).

## Launch campaigns against community-noise models and SATURN

SLS Artemis I gave 127–136 dB maximum OASPL at 1.5–5.2 km, nearly 20 dB above the Ares-V-based environmental assessment (<110 dB at 4.5 km), and 105.6 dBA at 5.15 km ([Gee 2023](../notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 3–7). For Starship Flight 5 the RNOISE-based assessment underpredicted L_Z,max by 2–9 dB from 10 to 35.5 km but overpredicted SEL_A by 10–18 dBA, so it mishandles how spectrum and level change during ascent; infrasound kept L_Z above ambient for 16–17 min, and the landing burn was as loud as lift-off ([Gee 2024](../notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 5). SATURN, built from three Atlas V stations within 1 km plus an empirical propagation-loss filter, predicted Firefly Alpha and Starship Flight 6 maximum levels within ~1 dB inside 20 km, against 2.2–2.7 dB for SP-8072 ([Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 72, 91, 95). On Falcon 9 it matched running OASPL within 1 dB around the peak at 1.1 and 2.6 km but put the 1.1 km spectral peak at 12 Hz against 20–30 Hz measured ([Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 8–10). Beyond 20 km its Starship error grows to ≈12 dB at 35.5 km (SP-8072 ≈18 dB), put down to launch-day atmospheric conditions (Mathews 2025, p. 95).

## Sonic booms

Booster flyback produces the largest-amplitude event: for Starship a triple-shock boom of 7.1 psf at 10.5 km and near or above 10 psf at the nearest stations, above the updated assessment ([Gee 2024](../notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 5–6); for Falcon 9 ~5 psf at 750 m and above 8 psf at 1–2 km from the landing zone, not decaying monotonically ([Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 6, 8). Ascent booms over Ventura County from six Falcon 9 launches were Friedlander-type single shocks near the acceleration focus (maximum 1.90 psf, 133 dB peak) and multi-arrival rumbles downrange (69 of 132 recordings below 0.5 psf). Low-level wind moved the carpet off land for one launch and a 155° azimuth for another, and the PCBoom-based assessment placed the focus near Santa Barbara, where no booms were measured ([Gee 2026](../notes/gee-2026-falcon-9-ascent-sonic-boom.md), p. 4–7).

## Disagreements and open questions

**Falcon 9 peak angle: 64° or 71°?** Mathews & Gee revised the Falcon 9 value from 64° to 71° after a 3-D angle correction and used 71° for Atlas V ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 375, 378), yet Kellison et al. measured 64° again from near-pad stations, while SATURN's Atlas V map peaks at 72° ([Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 11–12; [Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 82). Kellison et al. note that straight rays, a fixed 18 D_eff source and no moving-source correction could bias their angle by 2–3° (p. 12–13). Ray-traced, moving-source-corrected directivity from the same near-pad array on several vehicles would settle it.

**Where is the source, and are static firings biased?** Intensity puts the peak at 17–32 D_e ([Gee 2016](../notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 1), whereas Varnier-type core lengths put peak power near 9–10 D_e ([Haynes 2009](../notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md), p. 9; [Tsutsumi 2015](../notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 9), and horizontal firings impinge on hillsides beyond ~40D ([Gee 2012](../notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 10–11). Near-field intensity on a launch, or on a firing with an unobstructed plume, is the missing measurement.

**Why Sr ≈ 0.01–0.02, and which diameter for clusters?** The order-of-magnitude gap to lab jets is "still unclear" ([Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 10). Published values mix single-nozzle and equivalent diameters, and peak-angle SPL with sound-power spectra, so Falcon 9's 0.010 ([Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 15–16), 0.019 ([Mathews 2021](../notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 629) and SLS's 0.015 (Kellison, p. 15) are not directly comparable. Values on one basis, plus hot high-Mach lab jets with known D and U, would test whether temperature belongs in the scaling, as Mathews suggests ([Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 105).

**Is η constant?** Kellison et al. read the plateau of scaled OASPL against M_O as saturation and SLS as low-efficiency ([Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 17); Vulcan needs half the Atlas V value ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 375), while clustered Starship and Saturn V seem louder than thrust predicts ([Gee 2024](../notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 7). Near-pad sound power with one ground treatment across many vehicles is needed.

**Shocks: radiated or formed?** Whether far-field shocks leave the source as shocks or form by steepening is unresolved ([Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 10). Nonlinear propagation is offered for the near- versus far-field directivity mismatch ([Gee 2016](../notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 5) and for the high-frequency narrowing of the RSRM indices ([James 2012](../notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md), p. 6), which Haynes & Kenny put down to terrain ([Haynes 2009](../notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md), p. 13); the F-22A and F/A-18E disagree on sideline nonlinearity ([Gee 2008](../notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 11). Simultaneous near- and far-field arrays with meteorology would separate the effects, using one sampling rate, since the Gee 2012 note points out that Sk{∂p/∂t} depends on it and the paper mixed 48 and 96 kHz.

**Why no tones?** Lubert et al. suggest masking by mixing noise and pad geometry for the missing impingement tones (p. 16–17); this is untested. Merlin 1D plumes are overexpanded, so shock cells exist ([Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 4), and the F404 shows full-scale BBSAN, so whether the guided-jet-mode loop fails or is merely masked in rocket plumes is open (see [screech](screech.md)).

**Propagation beyond 20 km and boom carpets.** Long-range Starship levels, SEL_A errors and the ascent-boom focus all point to weather and terrain that the models leave out ([Gee 2024](../notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 5; [Gee 2026](../notes/gee-2026-falcon-9-ascent-sonic-boom.md), p. 6–7). Ray tracing with the as-flown trajectory and atmosphere is the planned test.

## Methods and data

Every result here comes from time-resolved pressure sampled at 48–204.8 kHz; none of the methods applies to non-time-resolved data such as PIV. Recurring practice:
- **Mounting:** ground-plate microphones with δ ≈ 1.9 ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 373) against pole mounts with wind-shifted interference nulls ([Kenny 2009](../notes/kenny-2009-space-shuttle-srm-plume-acoustics.md), p. 14); pole-shift filtering to recover sub-Hz booms ([Gee 2026](../notes/gee-2026-falcon-9-ascent-sonic-boom.md), p. 3).
- **Block statistics:** 1 s running OASPL, spectra averaged over the 3 dB-down window, and 4 s blocks for Q(θ, Sr), with the moving vehicle turning time into angle ([Gee 2023](../notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 3; [Kellison 2026](../notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 11–13). This is a crude treatment of non-stationarity.
- **Waveform statistics:** Sk{p} and Sk{∂p/∂t}, with Sk{∂p/∂t} ≥ 5 as the shock threshold ([Reichman 2018](../notes/reichman-2018-qm2-srm-far-field-acoustics.md), p. 8).
- **Arrays:** PAGE intensity probes ([Gee 2016](../notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 4); beamforming of pads, where SEM fragments distributed sources ([Panda 2013](../notes/panda-2013-noise-sources-rocket-test-firings-launch-phased-array.md), p. 16); holography with OLVR partial fields for aircraft ([Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 13–16).
- **Geometry:** polar arcs must be centred on the apparent source, not the nozzle ([James 2012](../notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md), p. 5; [Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 14).

No paper uses SPOD, wavelets or Hilbert envelopes, so intermittent features (crackle, and the L_Z,max scatter the Starship letter attributes to short, high-amplitude events; [Gee 2024](../notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 5) are averaged away.

## Relevance to our work

- **Feedback is absent at full scale.** The lab screech and impinging-jet loops do not appear in rocket data ([Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 16–17), yet installation still dominates the near field (Panda's duct exit, Tsutsumi's reflected Mach waves). A hot or high-NPR lab jet on an inclined plate, with time-resolved schlieren and microphones, would ask when the loop survives; Mathews calls for exactly such synchronised schlieren and acoustics ([Mathews 2025](../notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 104–105).
- **Cheap metrics for our microphones.** Add Sk{∂p/∂t} and the one-third-octave slope test (−3 dB/octave holding with distance signals shocks; [Gee 2008](../notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 6) to the basic-statistics step; recentre arcs on the apparent source; small PAGE probes could locate where screech energy leaves the jet.
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Launch records are non-stationary and intermittent. Hilbert envelopes of high-pass microphone signals, or SPOD-filtered reconstructions on arrays, would track shock activity and source changes that block averages hide.
- **Rocket-noise modelling and simulation.** The measured η, source curves, directivity and propagation losses are the inputs for SATURN-type models (see [rocket_noise.csv](../data/rocket_noise.csv)). LES/CAA comparisons need nonlinear propagation: Tsutsumi's hybrid RANS/LES with linear FW-H was up to 4 dB high ([Tsutsumi 2015](../notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 12–13). The PyFR staging LES is not directly affected.
