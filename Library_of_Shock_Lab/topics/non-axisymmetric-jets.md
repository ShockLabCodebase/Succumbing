# Non-axisymmetric jets: rectangular and elliptical

_Last updated: 2026-10-21 · 18 papers_

## Scope
Single supersonic, mostly screeching, jets from rectangular and elliptical nozzles: the oscillation shapes they select, how axis switching and the evolving mean flow change them, symmetry decompositions on single jets, passive control by nozzle shape, and how linear models (vortex sheets, Floquet-in-azimuth stability, cross-plane resolvent) and LES of single non-round jets compare with experiment. Round jets whose flapping axis is fixed by weak streaks pose the same symmetry-breaking problem and are included.

Twin jets, with the full D2 (SS/SA/AS/AA) treatment and the table mapping each paper's labels, are on [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md). The screech loop is on [screech](screech.md) and staging and mode switching on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md); this page says only what changes with geometry. See also [linear-models](linear-models.md) (formulations), [modal-decomposition](modal-decomposition.md) (SPOD/POD practice), [time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md) (Hilbert and wavelet tracking), [impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md) (non-axisymmetric impinging jets) and [jet-noise-mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md) (mixing noise, BBSAN).

**Naming.** *Flapping* is the transverse oscillation across the minor axis (Mathieu mode se₁ of an elliptic jet), *wagging* the same motion in the major-axis plane (ce₁), *varicose* ce₀ ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md)). In the single-jet D2 labels of [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md) (first letter about y = 0, second about z = 0), AS = flapping and SA = wagging, and the diagonal flapping of [Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md) (p. 21–24) is AA by the note's mapping. Twin-jet papers use the same letters for other planes (table on [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md)).

## Current understanding
1. **Breaking axisymmetry turns helices into flapping.** In a round jet the m = ±1 modes are degenerate, so flapping and helical motions are equivalent. Any π-periodic distortion of the cross-section (an ellipse, a second jet, weak |m| = 2 streaks) splits them into a flapping and a wagging eigenmode with different growth rates, and one wins ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 8–12; [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 7; [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 7–10). Experiments, LES and DNS agree that minor-axis flapping dominates rectangular and elliptical jets ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 138–139; [Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md), p. 54; [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 131–132, 140; [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2743–2746; [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 15; [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4214, 4217; [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 10). The exception is cold LES of an AR 2 C-D nozzle, which flaps along the diagonal until it is heated ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 21–24).
2. **AR ≈ 2 jets stage and are multimodal; AR ≥ 4 jets do not stage.** AR 2 rectangular and elliptical jets switch from varicose to flapping at NPR ≈ 2.2 ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 7), then run weaker varicose, second-flapping and wagging loops alongside the flapping tone, more of them when underexpanded (p. 15–17; [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4214–4215). At AR 4, strong flapping breaks up the shock cells before a suboptimal shock peak can form, so there is no staging ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 18–19).
3. **Axis switching follows the large-scale oscillation and changes the modes downstream.** The switch point moves downstream as NPR rises, but strong screech or forcing pulls it upstream, and shock-free, unforced jets switch late or not at all ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 133; [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 135–136; [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4216; [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 6); heating pushes it downstream in LES ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 13). Beyond it, wagging or slower antisymmetric structures appear that the upstream jet does not support ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2745–2747; [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 11–14; [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 13–15).
4. **The upstream guided jet mode closes the loop.** Its existence band explains symmetry selection better than KH growth does ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 25–27; [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 22–24), as in twin jets. On a measured mean flow, a weakest-link model built on it predicts the flapping tone of an AR 2 rectangular jet within a few per cent ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4218–4220). Nothing yet predicts which flapping plane or coexisting loop wins, or the amplitude.
5. **A single view or average misleads** (see symmetry decompositions and methods below): views hide or fake symmetries, mirrored phase-locked cycles impose them, and a mean or a whole-record transform blurs alternating states.

## Mode shapes: flapping, wagging, varicose, helical
[Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md) gave the classic evidence: in an AR 16.7 jet, phase-locked schlieren show the two shear layers exactly out of phase (p. 138–139), with minor-axis flapping near x/D ≈ 10 and a major-axis oscillation beyond 60 D, each at St ≈ 0.12 on its own dimension, read from one station and one peak through Crighton's elliptic vortex sheet (p. 139–140).

In [Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md)'s pre-2000 picture, rectangular jets are antisymmetric; symmetric modes appear near choking (Gutmark et al. saw a switch at M_j 1.15) but stay weak, their feedback efficiency being low (p. 54–55), and bevelled AR 5 nozzles select higher-order spanwise waveguide modes (p. 85–87). Phase-locked stereo PIV shows an AR 4 C-D jet flapping over the whole sweep, M_j 1.2–1.8, with only the flapping frequency in the near field ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 131–132, 140). The authors put the Strouhal jump at the design Mach number, usually read as varicose → flapping, down to the convection speed falling from 0.63 to 0.50 U_j (p. 132). The jump crosses a toneless gap at M_j 1.42–1.46 rather than a hysteretic change of stage, as expected if AR ≥ 4 jets do not stage ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 18–19; the Alkislar note's reading), but no overexpanded PIV is shown and the mirrored cycle imposes the antisymmetry being measured (p. 130). Flapping does not need screech: an ideally expanded multi-stream rectangular jet has flapping POD modes at St 0.46–0.70 ([Berry 2017](../notes/berry-2017-pod-time-resolved-schlieren-multi-stream-rectangular-jet.md), p. 8).

[Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) rotate the nozzle through 19 views: the screech SPOD eigenvalue λ_s(θ) is flat for varicose modes and v-shaped, lowest in the major-axis view, for flapping (p. 9–11). In the group's AR 2 ellipse the minor-axis POD pair holds 15 % of the transverse-velocity energy at NPR 2.2 ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2745–2746), 23.6 % at 2.6 and 35 % at 3.4, against 6.8 % and 10.3 % on the major axis ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 15). Two-view schlieren SPOD of a sharp-throat AR 2 C-D rectangular jet (the OSU twin design at about 0.45 scale) finds four tones at NPR 4.0: minor-axis flapping (St 0.265), varicose (0.337, present from NPR 2.7), a second flapping tone (0.308) and wagging seen only across the major axis (0.272), the last two only near and above ideal expansion; the far field and the PIV POD register only the first ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4214–4217). In forced subsonic DNS, AS flapping dominates at every forcing level and SA wagging decays faster ([Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 8, 10).

**The nozzle alone does not fix the flapping plane.** LES of the Cincinnati AR 2 C-D nozzle at NPR 3 flaps along the diagonal when cold (AA, with 180° phase jumps across both planes), is mixed at TR 2 and flaps purely across the minor axis at TR 2.5 and 3 ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 21–24), concentrating the hot jets' Mach-wave radiation in that plane (p. 16). One DFT over the record cannot say whether the diagonal pattern is steady or an average of alternating AS and AA states (the note's reading); the twin version of this nozzle does alternate ([Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), p. 9–10). The major-axis-view signature of the dominant tone in [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), put down by the authors to misalignment or poor convergence (p. 4214), could be the same diagonal component (the note's suggestion).

**Why flapping wins.** In a single round jet the m = 1 eigenvalue appears twice, in AS and SA, and a helix is SA1 + i AS1; a second jet splits the two into four eigenvalues whose small differences in k_i grow exponentially with x, so a helix at s/D 2.2 becomes SA1 flapping within a few diameters ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 8–9, 12). [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) apply the same N = 2 Floquet argument to a single round jet: a |m| = 2 streak of amplitude 0.01 already fixes the flapping axis (p. 10, 12), and in a heated M_j 1.35 LES the flapping plane precesses with slowly rotating streaks, drifting about π/2 over about 100 screech cycles (p. 20–24). An ellipse supplies the N = 2 periodicity itself ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 7). Treating ellipses, twin jets and streaky round jets as one symmetry-breaking problem is my reading; each paper treats only its own case.

**Helical-looking motion** rests on weak evidence. In the AR 2 ellipse at NPR 2.2, [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) find a second antisymmetric POD pair in both planes, uncorrelated with the flapping pair and of twice its wavelength (2.4D against 1.2D); it saturates near x/D ≈ 7 and is linked to the shock-driven axis switch (p. 2745–2747). They call it helical, against the axis-locked modes of Morris & Bhat's stability analysis for a non-switching base flow (p. 2740, 2745), and explain its lack of a tone by its growing only after the shock train ends (p. 2747–2748); its St 0.27 is inferred from the wavelength and an assumed U_c (p. 2747). The planes were measured separately, so the π/2 phase lock of a helix is untested, and the major-axis pair grows only after x/D ≈ 3.5, which fits Suzuki's mode born at the switch point just as well. Where the flow AR passes through 1, se₁ and ce₁ become nearly degenerate again, so flapping and wagging could combine into helix-like motion or coexist as separate structures (both the note's reading). Later papers trace the quasi-helical thread to this pair ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 3, 20–21); Mazharmanesh's elliptical "quasi-helical" mode is antisymmetric from every view with a flat λ_s, and confirming that it is helical needs cross-plane data (p. 16–19). The "helical" coupling once reported for round twins has been recast as a mixture of coupling states ([twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md)).

## Axis switching and the evolving mean flow
Screech reshapes these jets. At peak screech (NPR 3.8) the AR 16.7 jet spreads at 36° in the minor-axis plane, against 20–25° at other NPRs, and its axis crossover moves well upstream; otherwise the crossover moves downstream as NPR rises, and the jet is still not axisymmetric at 160 D ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 123, 133, 140–142). In an AR 4 jet at M_j 1.69, screech raises the minor-axis spreading rate from 0.065 (ideally expanded, no switch) to 0.11 over 5–20h and 0.33 beyond 21h, with the axes crossing at 16h ≈ 4AR ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 135–136). The authors attribute the switch to stationary counter-rotating streamwise vortex pairs formed as the screech-driven spanwise rolls deform (p. 136–140, 144), which is inferred, not shown; the streamwise vortices at x/h = 6 they put down to nozzle imperfections (p. 136). B-mode (flapping/helical) screech raises mass flux by about 60 % and the other stages do not (Zaman, cited by [Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md), p. 94, 97). AR 3 Mach 2 jets with trailing-edge cutouts switch by 8 D_eq only off-design, at M_j 2.5, and not at all up to 15 D_eq without an expansion mismatch ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 6–7).

In AR 2 jets the switch lies inside the screech region. The group's ellipse has equal half-widths at x/D ≈ 4.3 at NPR 2.2 ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2745), and its fitted flow AR (about 1.7 at the nozzle, not 2) reaches 1 near x/D ≈ 5 at NPR 2.6 and farther downstream at 3.4 ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 10–11); the metrics differ, so the trend is the note's comparison. In the sharp-throat rectangular jet, the switch point of a fitted shock-free mean flow moves from 3.1 to 5.4 D_eq between NPR 2.5 and 3.7 (ideal expansion, where every tone is weakest), then back to 4.8 D_eq at NPR 4.5 ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4216), a retreat that fits Krothapalli's link between strong screech and early switching (the note's reading). Heating moves the switch downstream in LES, from 7.5h to 11h (4.7–6.9 D_eq), although the simulated tone grows with heating; the measured tone falls ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 13, 18–19).

In the AR 2 rectangular jet at NPR 4.0, the mean shock cells switch axes at x/D_e ≈ 6.5, the fourth cell. The St 0.27 "bi-axial flapping" mode's envelope peaks at 6.5 D_e in the minor-axis view and at 8.1 D_e in the major-axis view, and sub-domain SPOD shows flapping upstream of the switch and wagging downstream ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 11–14); two other tones at the same NPR are unaffected (p. 15–17). The sharp-throat jet's wagging tone likewise sits at x/D_eq ≈ 5–8, downstream of its switch point ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4215–4216); the parallel is the note's, and the paper does not cite Mazharmanesh.

[Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md) isolate the effect in a shock-free, low-Re DNS. Stronger near-nozzle forcing moves the switch point upstream (none, then x/D_eq ≈ 12, then ≈ 8; p. 6), and the major-axis contraction is a shift of the shear layer, not a thinning (p. 8). At low St a "new flapping" mode in the SA family is born at the switch point with no upstream support (p. 13–15), and AS flapping cannot become SA linearly, because the symmetry families are orthogonal (p. 13–14). The bi-axial mode may therefore be two coexisting structures rather than one wavepacket that rotates.

The coupling runs both ways. In the AR 2 elliptical jet at NPR 3.6, two flapping tones (St 0.269 and 0.314) are mutually exclusive (ρ = −0.92), and each holds its own short-time mean, with shock cells contracted under one tone and extended under the other ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 14–15). The loop closes on k_s1 up to NPR 3.3 and on k_s2 from NPR 3.4 to 4.9 (p. 21–22), matching a stage jump in an AR 2 elliptical jet that keeps the dominant symmetry ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 18; see [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md)).

## Symmetry decompositions for single jets
The general machinery, built mostly on twin jets, is on [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md): the exact, orthogonal D2 split that cuts 3-D SPOD memory 4× ([Yeung 2022](../notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md)), symmetry-imposed POD of planar PIV, and sub-domain, conditional and half-domain POD. For single jets:
- **D2 and even–odd splits.** Applied before pressure SPOD of DNS, with zero-weight sub-domains, the D2 split separates the wagging and new-flapping structures ([Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 5, 14). Even and odd PIV fields slightly improve POD convergence for an ellipse ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 15).
- **Path-integrated views.** Two orthogonal views give a symmetry fingerprint (flapping only in the minor-axis view, wagging only in the major-axis view, varicose in both), but an antisymmetric mode need not vanish from the orthogonal view: the dominant flapping tone of the sharp-throat jet also shows in the major-axis view, which the authors put down to misalignment or poor convergence ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4214). In an elliptic jet the barrel shock shows only in the major-axis view, and minor-axis expansion-wave reflections look like shocks there ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2741–2742). The mean image of a flapping jet looks artificially thick ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 7).
- **Nozzle rotation.** Comparing modes from mirror-image views with an inner product β stands in for a D2 split ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 8–9).

## Linear models against experiment and LES
**Shock cells and frequency.** [Tam 1988](../notes/tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) extends the Prandtl–Pack vortex sheet to any cross-section. The minor dimension sets the spacing, the aspect-ratio correction is under 3 % for AR > 4 (p. 6), measured spacing for M_j 1.1–1.7 agrees (p. 9–10), and the screech formula with u_c = 0.7 u_j sits slightly above the data, untested below AR 4 (p. 12). LES of an AR 2 C-D jet ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md)) fills that gap: Tam's first-cell length is within 1–5 %, the cells shrink linearly downstream (mean ΔL/L_s 5.5 %, as in round jets), heating cuts the visible count from 10 to 4 (p. 15), and the screech formula with the LES u_c is within about 10 % (p. 19). In the AR 4 jet, Powell's formula with the measured U_c and L_s ≈ 3.2h reproduces St ≈ 0.09 to about 1 %, while Tam's spacing (≈ 3.0h) is about 6 % short (the Alkislar note's check). Measured convection speeds sit below 0.7 u_j: about 0.5 u_j, vorticity-weighted and constant in x, in the AR 4 jet ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 143–144), and 0.60–0.67 u_j when Tam's formula is inverted for the AR 2 LES tones (the Gojon note's arithmetic). u_c = 0.55 u_j fits rectangular data better, and finite shear-layer thickness shortens the spacing by about 20 % ([Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md), p. 59, 62). The area-equivalent diameter collapses screech St for AR 2–5 but fails for AR ≥ 9.6 ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 7–8). On the small dimension, St at peak screech is 0.11–0.14 for rectangular jets against 0.24–0.28 for round ones ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 140). The nozzle barely moves the AR 2 tone: at M_j ≈ 1.55 the cold Cincinnati LES (St 0.24–0.25), the sharp-throat jet (0.265), the OSU twins (0.27) and the group's convergent AR 2 rectangular jet (flapping at 0.23, NPR 4.0) agree within about 0.04 (the Gojon and Nogueira notes' comparisons; [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 15).

**KH and guided modes on measured mean flows.**
- *Elliptical jet, Floquet LSA* ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)). On fitted PIV and RANS mean flows the se₁ KH wavenumbers fall within the POD range, whichever mean flow is used (p. 18–20), and the se₁ guided-mode centreline peak matches POD "almost perfectly" (p. 21–22). The guided-mode band is at most 0.09 wide in St and shifts with shear-layer thickness, so RANS gives narrower bands (p. 22–23). At the screech St the mode is neutral only over a short streamwise stretch, where the measured guided-mode amplitude peaks (p. 22–24).
- *Rectangular jet, planar vortex sheet* ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md)). The NPR 3 LES tones fall only in the first antisymmetric band of Tam & Norum's upstream neutral modes, which the authors take as the reason for flapping, and the LES pressure shows upstream energy near that band's lower limit (p. 24–27). They place cold AR 4–5 experimental tones, including [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md)'s, in or just below the band, and blame those below it on the infinitely thin vortex sheet (p. 26). The model ignores the major dimension, so it cannot separate minor-axis from diagonal flapping. By the note's arithmetic the hot underexpanded tones fall 8 % and 31 % below the band, between the S1 and A1 bands, as by eye do many cold points above M_j ≈ 1.45, so band membership is a weaker test of closure than the authors suggest (my reading).
- *Rectangular jet, Floquet LSA* ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md)). The k_x spectrum of the PIV flapping mode has a subsonic upstream peak near k_KH − k_sh1, so the dominant loop closes on the first shock peak (p. 4218). On a fitted shock-free 3-D mean flow, a weakest-link model (α_GJM = α_KH − k_sh) predicts the flapping tone within a few per cent at all six PIV NPRs, near the POD-mode peak (p. 4219–4220); a constant-profile base flow works only at low NPR, so the spreading must be modelled (p. 4220). The k_sh2 loop offered for the second flapping tone sits as close to the varicose tone on frequency alone (the note's reading), and the varicose and wagging tones are not modelled.
- *Streaky round jet, Floquet LSA* ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md)). The guided-mode band moves up in frequency for flapping and down for wagging (p. 11).

**Cross-plane resolvent.** [Marshall 2024](../notes/marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets.md) (preprint, not validated) predict AS/SA dipole streaks in rectangular jets. Sharpening the corners changes gains by no more than 2×, so corner vortices are probably not linear streaks (p. 13–17).

**LES.** Implicit LES of the single Cincinnati nozzle matches the measured tone within 0.01 in St at six conditions, along with the PIV mean flow and the OASPL directivity, but its screech level rises by 26 dB from TR 1 to 3 while the measured level falls ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 18–19, 21–22, 24). Twin-jet LES is assessed on [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md).

**Verdict.** Local models get wavenumbers and mode shapes right, the guided mode better than KH, existence bands bound the tones, and on a measured mean flow the weakest-link model predicts the dominant tone. LES still gets the amplitude trend with temperature wrong, and all the models are locally parallel.

## Passive control by nozzle shape
Trailing-edge cutouts on AR 3 Mach 2 nozzles make streamwise vortices only off-design, raising mixing by up to about 50 % ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 7–8). They lower noise at 30° by up to 12 dB when the jet is underexpanded ([Kerechanin 2001](../notes/kerechanin-2001-trailing-edge-rectangular-jet-acoustics.md), p. 3). On overexpanded jets, single-sided cuts remove screech, while symmetric two-sided cuts amplify it and raise its frequency, which the authors attribute to breaking or keeping the symmetry of the shock cells about the major axis (p. 4–5). A thick lip revives weak screech ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 8), as Raman documents for rectangular jets ([Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md), p. 75–77).

## Twin jets
A second jet breaks axisymmetry as an elliptical nozzle does, and adds coupling. Round and rectangular twins couple in symmetric or antisymmetric states out to large spacings, but steady coupling, switching between symmetries, one jet oscillating alone and uncoupling all occur ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md); [Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md); [Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md)). Symmetric coupling sits slightly below antisymmetric in frequency, as twin-jet stability models predict ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md)), each jet flaps as a single jet would, and coupling barely moves the tone. All of this, with the twin-jet models, is on [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md).

## Disagreements and open questions
- **What fixes the flapping plane of a single jet?** For the cold diagonal flapping that heating turns minor-axis ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md)), candidates are heating shifting the guided-mode bands, a time average of competing AS and AA states as in the twin jets ([Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md)), and streaks pinning or slowly rotating the plane as in round jets ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 20–24). The planar band model cannot tell AS from AA. *To settle:* D2-split SPOD of the LES at the tone with per-symmetry Hilbert tracking, and rotated-view schlieren of a cold C-D jet, which may already show a diagonal component ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md)).
- **Bi-axial and quasi-helical modes: one wavepacket that changes plane, or two structures?** The helicity of the original pair was never tested across planes ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md)). Suzuki's orthogonality argument favours two structures, but in a shock-free, low-Re jet, and the downstream wagging tone of [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md) fits the same picture (the note's reading); whether that jet's four loops coexist or alternate is untested, because its analysis is block-averaged. The tone frequencies at elliptical NPR 3.6 also differ between [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) (0.28, 0.32) and [Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) (0.269, 0.273, 0.314). *To settle:* cross-plane or volumetric data, simultaneous two-view schlieren with sub-domain SPOD, and OWNS or global analysis on the axis-switched mean flow.
- **Cause and effect with the mean flow.** Does screech move the axis switch and pin the streaks, or do they select the mode ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md); [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md); [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 25)? [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md) argue the first and predict that exciting an ideally expanded jet should make it switch and that suppressing screech should remove the crossover (p. 140, 153). Suzuki's forced DNS supports the first prediction, but heated LES moves the switch downstream while its tone grows ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md)). *To settle:* forced LES with energy-flux budgets.
- **What sets the tone amplitude?** The authors of the Cincinnati LES explain its wrong level trend only by an initially disturbed LES jet against a fully turbulent experiment ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 18–19, 27). [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md) argue that coherent circulation at the end of the third shock cell, with shock strength, sets the amplitude (p. 142–144, 153), and the AR 2 ellipse also produces its tone where coherent vorticity peaks before the third shock ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2747). But Alkislar's evidence is one phase at two conditions, and the quieter jet is only 1–3 dB quieter with less than half the circulation (the note's reading). *To settle:* Hilbert-tracked coherent amplitude at the radiating cell against the microphone envelope, and LES with matched exit turbulence at each TR.
- **Corner vortices.** They are not linear streaks according to [Marshall 2024](../notes/marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets.md) (p. 17); a resolvent analysis on an LES mean flow is the proposed test. The matching measurement is cross-plane stereo PIV, which in the AR 4 jet shows counter-rotating streamwise pairs with traces of the corner vortices at x/h = 16 ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 136–140).
- Coupling questions (what selects the symmetry, whether intermittency is intrinsic, which wave closes the cross-excitation loop) are on [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md).

## Methods and data
- **Time-resolved data** (high-speed schlieren, LES, DNS, microphones): SPOD with nozzle rotation and sub-domains ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md)) or with two views, plus a k–ω dispersion relation along one line that shows the KH, guided and duct branches ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4213–4214); a Fourier transform of the 3-D LES field at the tone and k–ω spectra inside the jet for upstream waves ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 21–27), which cannot tell a steady pattern from alternating states (the note's reading); D2-split SPOD with sub-domains ([Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md)); BMD and short-time means that tie each tone to its own mean flow ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md)).
- **Non-time-resolved data** (PIV, 1 Hz schlieren): symmetry-imposed POD pairs, k_x filtering and LSA ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)) on a fitted shock-free 3-D mean flow ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4215–4216); microphone-triggered phase-locked stereo PIV when the tone is clean, without mirroring half the cycle ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 130); two-point correlation of schlieren ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2743).
- **Aliasing.** Check the tone against Nyquist when snapshot POD runs on decimated schlieren ([Berry 2017](../notes/berry-2017-pod-time-resolved-schlieren-multi-stream-rectangular-jet.md), p. 8).

## Relevance to our work
- **Group rigs.** The AR 2 elliptical nozzles ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md); [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md)) and the AR 2 convergent and sharp-throat C-D rectangular nozzles ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md); [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md)) are the data this page is about; the twin round nozzle plates are on [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md). Record nozzle orientation against the camera, since the view decides which symmetries a schlieren sees.
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Reconstruct each tone band separately for each symmetry (even and odd fields, or D2 components) and for sub-domains either side of the axis switch, then track instantaneous amplitude and frequency. First targets: whether the loops of the multimodal AR 2 jets coexist or alternate ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md); [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md)), whether the bi-axial mode is one structure or two, and diagonal against minor-axis flapping ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md)). Test for beats before calling anything switching ([twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md)).
- **PyFR staging LES.** For 3-D output the D2 split (or an azimuthal split) cuts memory 4×. If runs reach the |m| = 1 stages, round-jet LES can carry slowly rotating |m| = 2 streaks that fix a flapping axis, and averaging them out needs records several times longer than one streak rotation ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 14).
