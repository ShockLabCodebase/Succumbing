# Screech staging and mode switching

_Last updated: 2026-10-21 · 26 papers_

## Scope

Staging in free, shock-containing jets: Merle's A1, A2, B, C and D stages, which shock wavenumber each loop closes on (k_s1 against k_s2), how a jet moves between stages (mutual exclusion, dwell times, intermittency, possible hysteresis), multimodal loops at higher NPR, and how staging limits might be predicted or computed. The loop itself and amplitude are in [screech](screech.md), and frequency prediction and the absolute-instability and global-mode view in [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md). Staging in rectangular and elliptical jets is treated in [non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md) and coupling-state switching in [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md); here they appear where they test the single-jet picture. Switch-tracking tools are in [time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md), local and Floquet models in [linear-models](linear-models.md), global modes in [linear-operator-methods](linear-operator-methods.md), and impinging and edge-tone stages in [impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md).

## Current understanding

1. **The stages are mapped, but the jumps were long unexplained.** A1 and A2 are axisymmetric, B flapping and C helical (Raman 1998, p. 49, 52, 101). Instability growth explains the toroidal-to-helical switch (Tam 1995, p. 39), not A1 → A2, which share m = 0 and the guided jet mode (G-JM) ([Nogueira 2022](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) p. 2).
2. **Stages close on different peaks of the shock spectrum.** Spacing shrinks downstream, giving a primary peak k_s1 and suboptimal k_s2, k_s3: A1 and B close on k_s1, A2 and C on k_s2 (Nogueira 2022, p. 7–13; [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) p. 11–13). Mercier's loop count, one extra period at a fixed 4th-shock source, carries the same signature ([Mercier 2017](../notes/mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) p. 214, 222–224), so the two descriptions coincide at present resolution.
3. **Near a single-jet staging point the tones are mutually exclusive**, with dwell times of seconds ([Mancinelli 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) p. 6–7) and a short-time mean flow for each loop ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) p. 11–13). Between twin-jet coupling states the tones instead coexist, uncorrelated and intermittent ([Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md) p. 16, 29).
4. **The staging NPR belongs to the rig as much as to the jet.** A1 → A2 falls between NPR 2.17 and 2.27 across five facilities; lip thickness, reflectors and upstream plates add or remove modes ([Ponton 1992](../notes/ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) p. 537–548; [Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) p. 75–78), and identical nozzles in two rigs stage at different NPRs (Bell 2021, p. 17–18, 35).
5. **Linear models allow both loops over a band, so selection is nonlinear** (Nogueira 2022, p. 13; [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md)). No model predicts the staging limits, and hysteresis has not been tested.
6. **At higher NPR several loops share one block-averaged spectrum**, each on its own shock wavenumber (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 15–17; [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) p. 11–17; [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) p. 11–17). Whether they coexist or alternate is unresolved.

## Stages and early explanations

Raman (1998, p. 49, 52, 101) lists Merle's stages and calls the jumps unexplained. [Tam 1995](../notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) (p. 39) picks the azimuthal mode with the largest total instability growth, which cannot separate A1 from A2: both are m = 0 and both close on the same G-JM (Nogueira 2022, p. 2), not on separate closures as Shen & Tam proposed ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) p. 10–11). The absolute-instability saddle fixes the frequency but gives no reason for B–C–D switching ([Nogueira 2021, absolute instability](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md)). Nor does convection speed: U_c/U_j stays at 0.57–0.60 across all coupling modes of twin jets, while the ratio of standing wavelength to shock spacing changes from mode to mode ([Knast 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) p. 10–11).

Not every tone change is a stage. Above M_j ≈ 1.25 the B tone appears in the m = 0 spectrum as a quadratic m = ±1 product (cross-bicoherence ≈ 1 at St 0.34), not as an axisymmetric loop (Mancinelli 2021, p. 24–25). In heated rectangular LES the tone shape changes from diagonal (TR 1) to minor-axis flapping (TR ≥ 2), which the authors link to Raman's mode II → I jump ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md) p. 21–24); four TR points cannot show whether that change is discrete.

## Which shock wavenumber closes each loop

The present explanation is the multi-peak shock spectrum. Nogueira 2022 (p. 7–13) fitted the centreline mean pressure as a chirp, placed A1 in the near-nozzle region and A2 downstream where spacing shrinks, and found both loops absolutely unstable at M_j 1.12, with ω0i 0.223 (A1) against 0.057 (A2) at δ = 0.2, matching the stronger measured A1. Edgington-Mitchell 2022 extended this to B, C and k_s3. The lipline standing wavenumber follows k_s1 or k_s2 by stage, even at rapidly switching conditions (p. 11); k_s2 is a local quasi-periodicity starting at the 5th–8th shock cell (p. 17); energy at k_kh − k_s2 grows before the A1 → A2 switch (Tsinghua NPR 2.15–2.2, switch at 2.25; p. 13); and jets of AR ≥ 4 do not stage, because flapping breaks the shocks down before a suboptimal peak forms (p. 18–19).

Other methods agree. Farghadan 2026 (p. 11–17) found separate lightly damped global modes at St 0.66, 0.72 and 0.77, each tied to a different shock wavenumber, with the observed tone on k_s2 hidden inside the broad primary peak. Mazharmanesh 2026 (p. 19–22) measured a loop-specific k_S from BMD interaction maps that matches k_kh − k_GJM as well as or better than k_s1, k_s2; in an AR 2 elliptical jet a single loop on k_s1 (NPR 2.3–3.3) gives way to multimodal loops on k_s2 (NPR 3.4–4.9). [Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) (p. 17) reaches the same A1/k_s1, A2/k_s2 split for twin jets through the p/s ratios of its resonance fits.

Mercier 2017 gives the loop view of the same jump. Between M_j 1.13 and 1.15 (St 0.59 → 0.64) the loop gains one period while the source stays at the 4th shock (z_N 2.7D → 2.8D; p. 222–224, Tables 2, 5). Its test of Tam's k_h − k_sc = −k_s gives (k_h + k_s)/k̄_sc = 1.01–1.05, except 1.10 at M_j 1.13 and 1.28 at A2 (Table 4, p. 214). The integer-loop criterion makes this ratio m/N, so 1.28 ≈ 5/4 is the k_sw ≈ k_s2 signature of Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 11), seen five years earlier, and the 1.10 just below the jump may be the growing k_kh − k_s2 energy (Mercier 2017 note's derivation and speculation).

At Pprime, M_j ≤ 1.115 gives only A1, 1.12 jumps between A1 and A2, and ≥ 1.13 only A2, with no coexistence, dwell times of 1–4 s and an A1 envelope modulated at St ≈ 0.069 ≈ St₂ − St₁ only where switching occurs (Mancinelli 2019, p. 6–7). At NPR 2.20 the A1 (St 0.606) and A2 (0.694) amplitudes have ρ = −0.82, and the St = 0 SPOD coefficient correlates at 0.96 and −0.82 with them, so the long-time mean is a state the jet never occupies (Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 8–13). How joint statistics separate switching from coexistence is set out in [time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md).

Older evidence is ambiguous on coexistence. The B → C switch is abrupt with a thin lip, but the tones overlap over M_j 1.38–1.41 with a thick lip, which averaged spectra cannot separate from alternation (Ponton 1992, p. 538). Raman (1998, p. 55–56) cites both switching and genuinely coexisting tones in rectangular jets, and Mercier could hold a single A2 tone only at M_j 1.15, finding it shared with another mode on either side (p. 205).

## Multimodal loops and non-round jets

At higher NPR several loops appear in one block-averaged SPOD: four non-harmonic tones at NPR 3.3, none on k_s1 (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 15–17), and three loops at NPR 4.0 in an AR 2 rectangular jet, including a bi-axial flapping mode (Mazharmanesh 2025, p. 11–17). Non-round jets stage early and nozzle by nozzle. AR 2 rectangular and elliptical jets jump from varicose to flapping near NPR 2.2 (Mazharmanesh 2025, p. 7), but another Monash ellipse, with a thicker, azimuthally varying lip (Edgington-Mitchell 2015 note), already flaps at St 0.52 at NPR 2.2 with no varicose tone ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) p. 2743), falling to 0.38 and 0.27 at NPR 2.6 and 3.4 ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) p. 23–24). Some stage changes may not show in spectra: Mercier's flapping cases split into N = m = 3 and N = m = 4 with no tone jump (St 0.31 → 0.30 at M_j 1.35 → 1.37; p. 224, Table 2), though that rests on N alone and is tentative.

A second instability need not screech: the Monash ellipse at NPR 2.2 carries a slower, silent antisymmetric pair (see "Which instability screeches" below). Staging maps of non-round nozzles are on [non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md).

## Twin jets

Twin jets add symmetry competition, most intermittent at the A1 → A2 staging (Wong 2023, p. 9–12). Bell 2021 (p. 9) lists three untested staging mechanisms: a change of azimuthal mode or coupling, of the number of structures in the loop, or from free-stream to guided return. At s/D = 3, coherence reveals weaker continuous "processes" (one loop each, with fixed azimuthal modes, coupling and source) that coexist across the acoustic jumps at NPR 3.4 and 4.4 (p. 7–9). Between antisymmetric and symmetric coupling the jets couple either way or one oscillates alone; the coupled states never occur together, and the tones are uncorrelated rather than exclusive (p. 16, 23–29), which the authors read as competition between two global modes (p. 29). The loudest process is a clean limit cycle (σ ≈ 1 dB and 2–6°, about 170 dB) only at NPR 2.9–3.4 and noise-like just after its onset at NPR 2.3 (Fig. 27, p. 34; Fig. 6b, read), like Wong's intermittency near a stage boundary. Coupling can also change with no frequency jump (Knast 2018, p. 5, 11). LES resolves intermittent coupling in twin rectangular jets ([Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md)), and some apparent intermittency is only a beat between detuned tones ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md) p. 10–11; Wong 2023, p. 32–38). The coupling physics is on [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md).

## Disagreements and open questions

**One reflection point or two shock wavenumbers.** Mancinelli's models produce A1/A2 by changing p at a fixed 4th-cell reflection; Nogueira 2022 and Edgington-Mitchell 2022 attribute A2 to k_s2 from downstream cells. Mercier's data, the basis of the first view, fit both: the source stays at the 4th shock while the loop gains a period, and the same jump appears as (k_h + k_s)/k̄_sc = 1.28 ≈ m/N = 5/4 (p. 214, 222–224), so over the first four cells the two descriptions are one statement (Mercier 2017 note; cf. Mancinelli 2021, p. 24). Still open is where k_s2 comes from if the source does not move, since Nogueira 2022 places A2 downstream. [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) matched its own A2 case (NPR 2.25) with the leading k_s, though with a larger mismatch than for A1 (Nogueira 2022 note), and Farghadan 2026 finds the k_s2 peak buried in the primary one. Mercier's counts also assume a return at c₀; a slower G-JM return would push its +0.5π cases towards half-integers (Mercier 2017 note). Long-field (> 10D) mean shock spectra, BMD k_S and the loop count (N, m) of A1 and A2 on one rig would settle the labelling.

**Selection, hysteresis and switching.** Linear models allow both A1 and A2 over a band (Nogueira 2022, p. 13; Mancinelli 2021), yet experiments switch over only ΔM_j ≈ 0.01–0.015. Open: whether staging is bistable, whether switching is stochastic between attractors or driven by supply-pressure drift (Wong 2023, p. 39–41), and whether multimodal tones coexist or alternate (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 7). The coexisting, uncorrelated twin-jet tones (Bell 2021, p. 16, 29) have envelope spreads of 5–6 dB and ≈ 20 % "interruptions", as a narrow-band Gaussian (Rayleigh) envelope would, so they may be the fading of damped, noise-driven resonances rather than switching between limit cycles (Bell 2021 note). Classical global-mode theory treats only supercritical Hopf bifurcations; subcritical ones were much more complicated, with no known example ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md) p. 517), so A1/A2 bistability lies outside it. Three of its results still bear on competition: global modes come in families O(ε) apart in frequency (eq. 46, p. 496); a self-excited hot jet carries two modes with separate onsets (Fig. 18b, p. 513); and feedback that suppresses one global mode tends to destabilise another (p. 518–519). Global stability on each loop's short-time mean would test whether each state damps the other loop (Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 19). Other resonators give no precedent for hysteresis: jet–edge switches between two guided-wave loops repeat as M_j rises and falls ([Stavropoulos 2026](../notes/stavropoulos-2026-jet-edge-interaction.md) p. 14–18), and the "hysteresis" of impinging-jet stages is intermittent switching at one stand-off ([Ho 1981](../notes/ho-1981-dynamics-impinging-jet-feedback-phenomenon.md) p. 134–135). Long time-resolved records with HPOD and JPDFs, checked against a Rayleigh null, and LES continuation are the tests.

**Which instability screeches.** At NPR 2.2 the Monash ellipse carries a slower antisymmetric, helical-like POD pair (λ ≈ 2.4D, uncorrelated with the flapping pair) that makes no tone (Edgington-Mitchell 2015, p. 2745–2748). The authors argue that it grows only after the shock train has ended, so it cannot leak (p. 2747–2748). The paper predates G-JM closure, though, and the alternative is that no upstream wave exists for that pair near St 0.27: in this nozzle even the flapping (se₁) G-JM is neutral only over a short x band (Ivelja 2024, p. 22–24, NPR 2.6 and 3.4), and no band has been computed for the helical-like family. Quasi-helical tones do appear at higher NPR in another ellipse (St 0.28 at NPR 3.6, Mazharmanesh 2025, p. 16; 0.273, weak and intermittent, Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 13, 15), which fits the first view without excluding the second. Filtering that pair's PIV POD modes by the sign of k_x would separate the two.

## Methods and data

- **Acoustics**: fine NPR steps (ΔM_j = 0.005), azimuthal arrays to see m (Mancinelli 2019, p. 5), and records covering many switches (seconds). Wavelets show switching in time (Mancinelli 2019; Wong 2023); coherence at all frequencies finds weak "processes", and band-pass Hilbert envelopes give per-process JPDFs and dropout statistics (Bell 2021, p. 8–14).
- **Mean fields** (any schlieren, BOS, PIV or probe mean): centreline FFT for k_s1, k_s2, k_s3, with a field of view of 8–10D or more and a sliding window to locate each peak (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 7, 17; Nogueira 2022, p. 7–8).
- **Time-resolved schlieren**: SPOD frequency–time maps, JPDFs of tone amplitudes and BMD interaction maps for k_S (Mazharmanesh 2026); rotated views and sub-domain SPOD for non-round modes (Mazharmanesh 2025); lip-line phase from a thin strip (16–24 px at 220–430 kHz) gives the loop count and U_c(x) (Mercier 2017, p. 206–208, 222–225).
- **Non-time-resolved PIV**: conditional POD on each jet's pair amplitude separates coupled and single-jet states (Bell 2021, p. 23–27); otherwise the POD-pair route needs one steady tone and hides switching.
- **Pitfalls**: beating mimics coupling (Wong 2023, p. 32–38); envelope-threshold dropouts need a Rayleigh null, a stated filter and a beat test (Bell 2021 note); check bicoherence before counting an m = 0 tone as a stage (Mancinelli 2021); block-averaged spectra cannot tell coexistence from alternation.
- **Data tables**: tone frequencies by stage are in `data/screech_tones.csv`.

## Relevance to our work

**Lab experiments.** Record lip thickness, outer nozzle shape, upstream surfaces and enclosure walls in every campaign (Bell 2021, p. 6, 17–18), and sweep NPR in steps of 0.01 or finer around 2.15–2.30. The existing Monash A1 (NPR 2.10) and A2 (2.25) PIV could give Mercier's loop count from the POD-pair phase along the lip line, testing m = 4 → 5 at fixed N on a thick-lip nozzle (Mercier 2017 note).

**SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Rebuild the A1 and A2 bands and the St = 0 mode separately and track their instantaneous amplitudes: mutual exclusion should appear as anti-correlation, and the lag between tone amplitude and the St = 0 mode tests two-way coupling (Mazharmanesh 2026). Test dropouts against a Rayleigh null and a beat before calling them switches.

**The PyFR staging-continuation LES** (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`; 16 mm converging nozzle, A1 seeded at NPR 2.10, A2 at 2.25) cannot wait out 1–4 s dwell times (about 10⁴–10⁵ D/U_j). It steps NPR up from converged A1 and down from converged A2, and extrapolates each limit from a saddle-node fit of 1/t_switch: an assumption about the bifurcation, in territory classical theory leaves out (Huerre 1990 note's inference). What it could settle:
1. *Bistability.* The A1 up-branch and A2 down-branch limits bound any hysteresis band. If they coincide, staging is a sharp exchange; either way the experimental 50 % point should lie between them.
2. *Mean-flow-mediated selection.* Converged A1 and A2 states at one NPR give two mean flows. Their shock spectra test whether the loop changes k_s2 strength (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 12), and local, Floquet or global analysis tests whether each state damps the other loop; Farghadan 2026 already finds an St 0.66 mode beside the 0.72 tone at M_j 1.12.
3. *Rates.* Compare 1/t_switch and post-step growth or decay with ω0i (0.223 against 0.057; Nogueira 2022, p. 13) and the ω_i of Mancinelli 2021. Each NPR step is Huerre & Monkewitz's conclusive transient test ([screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md)): Landau fits to Hilbert envelopes at several points should agree (Huerre 1990, p. 497, 518). Their theory is supercritical only (p. 517).
4. *Precursors.* Check whether energy at k_kh − k_s2 grows before a forced switch (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 13), and use BMD to test whether the St₂ − St₁ modulation (Mancinelli 2019, p. 7) is a driving triad.
5. *Receptivity.* Changing only the lip or outer geometry tests whether limits shift while frequencies do not (Ponton 1992; Nogueira 2021, absolute instability, p. 22); projecting the near-lip field onto KH and G-JM eigenfunctions would estimate the R₁ and R₂ that Mancinelli 2021 (p. 22) needs.
6. *Leakage.* Check whether the first leak moves from the 3rd to the 4th cell on staging, and test leakage as closure ([screech](screech.md)) with the sound speed the ray model lacked ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) p. 12, 19–23).
7. *Loop count.* Mercier's lip-line phase analysis (y/D ≈ 0.55) should give N = 4 in both states and m = 4 → 5 (p. 222–224), a discrete state label; the core field then shows whether the upstream wave near −ω/c₀ is the G-JM or sound (p. 213).

The limits and switch times must not depend on domain length, sponge or outflow treatment, since outflow-to-inflow feedback can self-excite spatial simulations (Huerre 1990, p. 498). Their sensitivity to the exit boundary layer also needs testing: it is the likely reason an LES can get frequencies right and amplitudes wrong (Gojon 2019, p. 8–10, 18–19). The README's 1 mm flat lip differs from the 16 mm nozzle of Mazharmanesh 2026 (l/D_e = 0.31) and the leakage nozzle (t/D = 0.33; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 4).
