# Non-axisymmetric and twin jets

_Last updated: 2026-10-21 · 27 papers_

## Scope
Supersonic, mostly screeching, jets from rectangular and elliptical nozzles, and pairs of round or rectangular jets. The page covers which oscillation shapes these jets select (varicose, flapping, wagging, helical), how axis switching and other changes in the mean flow alter those shapes, the symmetry decompositions used to separate them, how twin jets couple and why the coupling is often intermittent, and how well the linear models built for these geometries (vortex sheets, Floquet-in-azimuth stability, cross-plane resolvent) match experiment and LES.

The screech loop itself (guided-jet-mode closure, k_kh − k_s triads, staging in round jets) is on [screech](screech.md); this page only says what changes with geometry. Model formulations in general are on [linear-models](linear-models.md), SPOD/POD practice on [modal-decomposition](modal-decomposition.md), Hilbert and wavelet tracking on [time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md), non-axisymmetric impinging jets on [impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md), mixing noise and BBSAN on [jet-noise-mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md), and clustered rocket nozzles on [rocket-noise-models](rocket-noise-models.md).

**Naming.** *Flapping* is the transverse oscillation across the minor axis (Mathieu mode se₁ of an elliptic jet); *wagging* is the same motion in the major-axis plane (ce₁); *varicose* is ce₀ ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md)). For twin jets and D2-symmetric flows the papers use two letters, XY, but they do not all mean the same planes:

## Current understanding
1. **Breaking axisymmetry turns helices into flapping.** In a round jet the m = +1 and m = −1 modes are degenerate, so flapping and helical motions are equivalent. Any π-periodic distortion of the cross-section (an ellipse, a second jet, weak |m| = 2 streaks) couples them and splits the pair into a flapping and a wagging eigenmode with different growth rates and wavenumbers. One of the pair then wins ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 8–12; [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 7; [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 7–10). Experiments, LES and DNS agree that minor-axis flapping dominates rectangular and elliptical jets ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 138–139; [Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md), p. 54; [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 131–132, 140; [Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 9; [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2743–2746; [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 15; [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4214, 4217; [Yeung 2022](../notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md), p. 8; [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 10). The exception is cold LES of an AR 2 C-D nozzle, which flaps along the diagonal until it is heated ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 21–24).
2. **AR ≈ 2 jets stage and are multimodal; AR ≥ 4 jets do not stage.** AR 2 rectangular and elliptical jets switch from varicose to flapping at NPR ≈ 2.2 ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 7). Weaker varicose, second-flapping and wagging loops then run alongside the dominant flapping tone, more of them when underexpanded ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 15–17; [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4214–4215). At AR 4, strong flapping breaks up the shock cells so quickly that there is no suboptimal shock peak, and so no staging ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 18–19). The frequency jump at the design Mach number of an AR 4 C-D jet is no stage jump either: it crosses a gap with no tone (the note's reading), and its authors put it down to a drop in convection speed with the jet flapping on both sides, though the overexpanded PIV behind that is not shown ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 131–132).
3. **Axis switching follows the large-scale oscillation, and changes which modes the jet supports downstream.** The switch point moves downstream as NPR rises, but strong screech or forcing pulls it upstream, and shock-free, unforced jets switch late or not at all ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 133; [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 135–136; [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4216; [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 6). Heating moves it downstream in LES ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 13). Downstream of the switch, wagging or slower antisymmetric structures appear that the upstream jet does not support ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2745–2747; [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 11–14; [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 13–15).
4. **Twin jets are coupled out to large spacings, but the coupling is often unsteady.** Steady coupling, switching between symmetries, one jet oscillating alone, and uncoupling all occur in round twins, at both the A1/A2 and the m = 1 stages ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 9–12; [Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md), p. 23–29; [Knast 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md), p. 4–5). In rectangular twins, AA coupling is broken by bursts of AA/AS competition ([Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), p. 9–10). Symmetric coupling sits at a lower frequency than antisymmetric, as twin-jet stability models predict, but the split is 0.3–0.6 % at A1/A2 and 5–15 % at m = 1 ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 19; [Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md), p. 27; [Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 20, 22). The NPR of each state differs between rigs ([Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md), p. 6, 17–18, 35).
5. **In every geometry studied, the upstream guided jet mode closes the loop.** Its existence band explains symmetry selection better than KH growth does ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), p. 15–19; [Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 18–19; [Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 25–27; [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 22–24; [Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), p. 20–21). On a measured mean flow, a weakest-link model built on it predicts the flapping tone of an AR 2 rectangular jet within a few per cent ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4218–4220).
6. **Split the data by symmetry before decomposing it.** The split is exact and orthogonal, and it saves memory and speeds convergence ([Yeung 2022](../notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md), p. 5, 8; [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), p. 6–8).
7. **Five diagnostic traps recur.** Two slightly detuned jets beat, which looks like intermittency or "coupling" in both symmetries ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 10–11; [Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 32–38). When two tones coexist, a cross-spectral phase read at one peak is spurious despite high coherence ([Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md), p. 9–10). Line-of-sight integration cancels out-of-phase content ([Esfahani 2021, control](../notes/esfahani-2021-control-twin-rectangular-jet-coupling.md), p. 15), yet an antisymmetric mode need not vanish from the orthogonal view ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4214), and in an elliptic jet reflected expansion waves look like shocks in the major-axis view ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2741–2742). Building a phase-locked cycle by mirroring half of it imposes the symmetry being measured ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 130). The mean image of a flapping jet looks artificially thick ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 7).

## Mode shapes: flapping, wagging, varicose, helical
The classic evidence is [Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md). In an AR 16.7 rectangular jet, phase-locked schlieren show the two shear layers exactly out of phase (p. 138–139). Minor-axis flapping appears near x/D ≈ 10, and a sideways major-axis oscillation appears beyond 60 D. Each has St ≈ 0.12 on its own nozzle dimension, which the authors read, from one station and one spectral peak, through Crighton's elliptic vortex sheet (p. 139–140).

[Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) summarises the pre-2000 picture. Rectangular jets are antisymmetric, except near choking, where symmetric modes appear (Gutmark et al. saw a switch at M_j 1.15) but stay weak because their feedback efficiency is low (p. 54–55). Bevelled AR 5 nozzles select higher-order spanwise waveguide modes (p. 85–87). Phase-locked stereo PIV of an AR 4 C-D jet qualifies the first point. The jet flaps over the whole sweep, M_j 1.2–1.8, and the near-field microphone hears only the flapping frequency ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 131–132, 140). The authors put the Strouhal jump at the design Mach number, usually read as varicose → flapping, down to the convection speed falling from 0.63 to 0.50 U_j in Tam's formula (p. 132). The jump crosses a gap with no tone at M_j 1.42–1.46 rather than a hysteretic change of stage, which fits AR ≥ 4 jets not staging ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 18–19; the Alkislar note's reading). No overexpanded PIV is shown, though, and building the cycle by mirroring half of it imposes the antisymmetry being measured (p. 130). In the AR 2 C-D twin jets of [Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), each jet flaps at every M_j from 1.20 to 1.85, and no symmetric or lateral mode is seen (p. 9). Flapping is not only a product of screech: [Berry 2017](../notes/berry-2017-pod-time-resolved-schlieren-multi-stream-rectangular-jet.md) finds flapping POD modes at St 0.46–0.70 in an ideally expanded multi-stream rectangular jet with no screech (p. 8).

Modern work classifies the modes quantitatively. [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) rotates the nozzle through 19 views. The screech SPOD eigenvalue λ_s(θ) is flat for varicose modes, and for flapping modes it is v-shaped with its minimum at the major-axis view (p. 9–11). In the group's AR 2 elliptical nozzle the minor-axis POD pair holds 15 % of the transverse-velocity energy at NPR 2.2 ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2745–2746), then 23.6 % (NPR 2.6) and 35 % (NPR 3.4), against 6.8 % and 10.3 % on the major axis ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 15): se₁ flapping that strengthens with NPR. Two-view schlieren SPOD of a single sharp-throat AR 2 C-D rectangular jet (the OSU twin design at about 0.45 scale) finds four tones at NPR 4.0: minor-axis flapping (St 0.265), varicose (0.337), a second minor-axis flapping tone (0.308) and wagging seen only across the major axis (0.272). The varicose tone appears from NPR 2.7, the other two only near and above ideal expansion, and the far field and the PIV POD register only the first ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4214–4217). In LES of the OSU twin jets, the St 0.3 tone appears only in AA and AS, the components antisymmetric about the plane of the nozzles ([Yeung 2022](../notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md), p. 8). In forced subsonic DNS, AS flapping dominates at every forcing level, and SA wagging decays faster ([Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 8, 10).

**The flapping plane is not fixed by the nozzle alone.** In LES of the Cincinnati AR 2 C-D nozzle at NPR 3, the cold jet flaps along the diagonal, with 180° phase jumps across both planes (AA). At TR 2 the pattern is mixed, and at TR 2.5 and 3 the jet flaps purely across the minor axis ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 21–24), which concentrates the hot jets' Mach-wave radiation in the minor-axis plane (p. 16). One DFT over the record cannot say whether the diagonal pattern is steady or an average of alternating AS and AA states (the note's reading); the twin version of this nozzle does alternate ([Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), p. 9–10). The major-axis-view signature of the dominant tone in [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), which the authors put down to misalignment or poor convergence (p. 4214), could be the same diagonal component (the note's suggestion).

**Why flapping wins.** [Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md) show that in a single round jet the m = 1 eigenvalue appears twice, in AS and SA, and that a helix is SA1 + i AS1 (p. 8–9). A second jet splits the two into four eigenvalues. Small differences in k_i then grow exponentially with x, so a helix at s/D 2.2 becomes SA1 flapping within a few diameters (p. 12). [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) (not tagged) apply the same N = 2 Floquet argument to a single round jet. A |m| = 2 streak of amplitude 0.01 already fixes the flapping axis (p. 10, 12). In a heated M_j 1.35 LES, the flapping plane precesses with slowly rotating streaks, drifting about π/2 over about 100 screech cycles (p. 20–24). For an ellipse the nozzle itself provides the N = 2 periodicity ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 7). So ellipses, twin jets and streaky round jets are one symmetry-breaking problem. This unification is my reading; each paper treats only its own case.

**Helical-looking motion** survives in a few places, each on weak evidence. In the AR 2 ellipse at NPR 2.2, [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) find a second antisymmetric POD pair, uncorrelated with the flapping pair and of twice its wavelength (2.4D against 1.2D). It appears in both planes, saturates near x/D ≈ 7, and is linked to the shock-driven axis switch (p. 2745–2747). The authors call it helical, against the axis-locked modes of Morris & Bhat's stability analysis, which assumed a non-switching base flow (p. 2740, 2745). It makes no tone, which they explain by its growing only after the shock train has ended (p. 2747–2748); its St 0.27 is inferred from the wavelength and an assumed U_c, not measured (p. 2747). The two planes were measured separately, so the π/2 phase lock a helix needs is untested, and the major-axis pair grows only after x/D ≈ 3.5, which fits Suzuki's mode born at the switch point just as well (the note's reading). The symmetry-breaking argument above offers a mechanism for either reading: where the flow AR passes through 1, se₁ and ce₁ become nearly degenerate again, so flapping and wagging can combine into helix-like motion or coexist as separate structures (the Edgington-Mitchell 2015 note's reading). Later papers trace the quasi-helical thread to this pair ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 3, 20–21). Mazharmanesh's elliptical "quasi-helical" mode is antisymmetric from every view and has a flat λ_s; confirming that it really is helical needs cross-plane data (p. 16–19). Knast's co- and counter-rotating "helical" coupling of round twins rests on a two-point correlation that cannot rigorously tell a helix from flapping ([Knast 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md), p. 6–9), and [Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md) recast the counter-rotating case as a mixture of coupling states (below).

## Axis switching and the evolving mean flow
Screech reshapes these jets. At peak screech (NPR 3.8) the AR 16.7 jet spreads at 36° in the minor-axis plane, against 20–25° at other NPRs, and its axis crossover moves well upstream. At other NPRs the crossover moves downstream as NPR rises, and the jet is still not axisymmetric at 160 D ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 123, 133, 140–142). Stereo PIV of an AR 4 jet at M_j 1.69 quantifies this. Screech raises the minor-axis spreading rate from 0.065 in the ideally expanded jet, which does not switch, to 0.11 over 5–20h and 0.33 beyond 21h, and the axes cross at 16h ≈ 4AR ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 135–136). The authors attribute the switch to stationary counter-rotating streamwise vortex pairs that form as the screech-driven spanwise rolls deform (p. 136–140, 144). That mechanism is inferred, not shown, and the streamwise vortices at x/h = 6 are put down to nozzle imperfections instead (p. 136). [Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) cites Zaman: B-mode (flapping/helical) screech raises mass flux by about 60 %, while the other stages do not (p. 94, 97). In AR 3 Mach 2 jets with trailing-edge cutouts, axis switching by 8 D_eq occurs only off-design, at M_j 2.5; with no expansion mismatch there is none up to 15 D_eq ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 6–7).

In AR 2 jets the switch sits inside the screech region and moves downstream as NPR rises. In the group's ellipse the half-widths are equal at x/D ≈ 4.3 at NPR 2.2 ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2745). The fitted flow AR (about 1.7 at the nozzle, not 2) reaches 1 near x/D ≈ 5 at NPR 2.6 and farther downstream at 3.4 ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 10–11); the metrics differ, so the trend is the note's comparison. In the sharp-throat rectangular jet, the switch point of a fitted shock-free mean flow moves from 3.1 to 5.4 D_eq between NPR 2.5 and 3.7 (ideal expansion, where every tone is weakest), then back to 4.8 D_eq at NPR 4.5 ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4216). The retreat fits Krothapalli's link between strong screech and early switching (the note's reading). Heating moves the switch downstream in LES, from 7.5h to 11h (4.7–6.9 D_eq), even though the simulated tone grows with heating; the measured tone falls ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 13, 18–19).

In the AR 2 rectangular jet at NPR 4.0, the mean shock cells switch axes at x/D_e ≈ 6.5, the fourth cell. The St 0.27 "bi-axial flapping" mode's envelope peaks at 6.5 D_e in the minor-axis view and at 8.1 D_e in the major-axis view. Sub-domain SPOD shows flapping upstream of the switch and wagging downstream ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 11–14). Two other tones present at the same NPR are unaffected by the switch (p. 15–17). The wagging tone of the sharp-throat jet likewise sits at x/D_eq ≈ 5–8, downstream of its switch point ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4215–4216); that this is the same behaviour is the note's reading, and the paper does not cite Mazharmanesh.

[Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md) isolates the effect in a shock-free, low-Re DNS:
- Stronger near-nozzle forcing moves the switch point upstream: none, then x/D_eq ≈ 12, then ≈ 8 (p. 6).
- The major-axis contraction is a shift of the shear layer, not a thinning (p. 8).
- At low St, a "new flapping" mode in the SA family is born at the switch point and has no upstream support (p. 13–15).
- AS flapping cannot become SA linearly, because the symmetry families are orthogonal (p. 13–14).

This is a real constraint on the bi-axial mode: it may be two coexisting structures rather than one wavepacket that rotates.

The coupling runs both ways. In the AR 2 elliptical jet at NPR 3.6, two flapping tones (St 0.269 and 0.314) are mutually exclusive (ρ = −0.92), and each holds its own short-time mean, with shock cells contracted under one tone and extended under the other ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), not tagged, p. 14–15). The loop closes on k_s1 up to NPR 3.3 and on k_s2 from NPR 3.4 to 4.9 (p. 21–22). This matches a stage jump in an AR 2 elliptical jet that does not change the dominant symmetry ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 18).

## Symmetry decompositions
- **D2 split (3-D data).** Four orthogonal components (SS, SA, AS, AA) sum exactly to the field, and SPOD of one quadrant per component saves 4× memory. Full-field SPOD does recover the components eventually, but its fourth mode was unconverged ([Yeung 2022](../notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md), p. 5, 8). Applied to near-lip pressure, the split gives instantaneous AA and AS amplitudes ([Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), p. 10). Applied before pressure SPOD of DNS, with zero-weight sub-domains, it separates the wagging and new-flapping structures ([Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 5, 14).
- **Symmetry-imposed POD (planar PIV).** Splitting into even and odd fields about the midplane works without time resolution, but the measurement plane cannot see two of the four symmetries (1AA, 1AS) ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), p. 6–8, Table 1 p. 7). The split also slightly improves convergence for an ellipse ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 15).
- **Sub-domain and conditional POD (planar PIV).** POD of each jet's half of the field, then POD of only the snapshots in which one or both jets oscillate strongly, separates single-jet, symmetric and antisymmetric states without time resolution ([Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md), p. 22–27). Requiring both jets to be strong confines the symmetric and antisymmetric amplitudes to an arc, so the empty corners of the mutual-exclusivity plot are partly built in; the different wavelengths of the two coupled pairs are the firmer evidence (the note's analysis).
- **Half-domain and symmetry-imposed POD with CWT (TR schlieren).** One field per jet (HD) and even and odd fields (SI) are each passed through POD and a Morlet transform. This tracks each jet's frequency and the dominant symmetry over time ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 8–10).
- **Path-integrated views.** Schlieren through two out-of-phase jets cancels their lobes ([Esfahani 2021, control](../notes/esfahani-2021-control-twin-rectangular-jet-coupling.md), p. 15). Two orthogonal views give a symmetry fingerprint (flapping only in the minor-axis view, wagging only in the major-axis view, varicose in both), but the dominant flapping tone of the sharp-throat jet also shows in the major-axis view, which the authors put down to misalignment or poor convergence ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4214). In an elliptic jet the barrel shock shows only in the major-axis view, and minor-axis expansion-wave reflections look like shocks there ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2741–2742). Rotating the nozzle and comparing modes from mirror-image views with an inner product β stands in for a D2 split ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 8–9).

## Twin-jet coupling states and intermittency
**Round twins, m = 0 (A1/A2).** Staging NPRs barely differ from those of a single jet. Three states occur ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 1, 9–19):
- steady coupling in one symmetry;
- SS↔SA competition, with switches every O(10²–10³) cycles and a frequency shift of 0.30–0.63 %;
- uncoupling, seen only at s/D 6, with the jets about 0.5–0.6 % apart in frequency.

Every spacing changes state at the A1→A2 jump (p. 12). SS sits at a lower frequency than SA (p. 19), as twin-jet stability models predict ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 20, 22). This is the clearest model–experiment agreement on coupling. Fine NPR sweeps show that supply-pressure drift explains the switching at one condition but not at another, which points to stochastic switching between competing global modes ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 39–41).

**Round twins, m = 1 (higher NPR).** At S/D 3, [Knast 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) find antisymmetric flapping, then counter-rotating helical motion, then symmetric flapping as NPR rises from 2.3 to 5.5. At S/D 6 they find co-rotating helical motion and symmetric flapping (Table 2, p. 9). The inter-plume phase jumps without any jump in frequency, coupling still dominates at S/D 6 (p. 4–5), and U_c/U_j stays at 0.57–0.60 across all the modes (p. 10). Knast attributed regions of scattered phase to unsteady coupling but could not test it (p. 5).

[Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md) test it, on the same records and on PIV from a second Monash rig:
- In the s/D 3 "phase anomaly" (NPR 3.4–4.4), coherence at all frequencies reveals two or three coexisting tones of continuously varying frequency. In their wide states the Hilbert envelopes dip more than 5 dB below the mean about 20 % of the time (p. 8–14). The dips are sometimes anti-correlated between the two jets and sometimes shared (p. 14–16), and the tones of different processes are uncorrelated rather than mutually exclusive (p. 16), unlike the two flapping tones of the AR 2 ellipse ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 14–15).
- Conditional POD of about 9500 PIV fields at NPR 4.6 finds three states: one jet oscillating alone, symmetric coupling, and antisymmetric coupling, never both coupled forms at once (p. 23–28). The schlieren clips show either jet alone, both, or neither (p. 28–29).
- The symmetric tone again lies below the antisymmetric one: St 0.22 against 0.23 at NPR 4.6, matching POD wavelengths of 3.1D and 2.8D (Table 2, p. 27), and about 0.04 lower on the first rig (figure reading).
- The antisymmetric → indeterminate → symmetric sequence holds on both Monash rigs, which have identical nozzles, and at OSU, but at different NPRs, traced to enclosure reflections and the plenum face (p. 6, 17–18, 35–36).

So Knast's counter-rotating "helical" region is a mixture of states: the m = 1 counterpart of Wong's three A1/A2 states, with uncoupling already at s/D 3. The 5–15 % frequency split is an order of magnitude larger than Wong's. Together with the different wavelengths, it suggests a different loop closure per symmetry rather than a change in phase speed (the note's inference). At NPR 5 the coupling is steady and symmetric; this is the 1SS coupling that [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md) find in the same PIV data (p. 9–12, 20–21).

**Rectangular twins.** Raman and Taghavi's AR 5 jets flap out of phase and switch to in phase at M_j ≈ 1.5, taking a new shared frequency. The out-of-phase mode is about 15 dB quieter midway between the nozzles ([Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md), p. 88–91; AR from [Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 3). The OSU AR 2 jets ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 8–11):
- couple out of phase at M_j 1.40–1.50;
- lose coherence just above design;
- couple in phase from M_j 1.65;
- gain only about 5 dB at the switch, against 15–20 dB in earlier rigs;
- at M_j 1.20–1.35 keep their own frequencies (140 Hz apart, falling to 33 Hz) and beat. This contradicts the shared-frequency claim.

In LES of the Cincinnati geometry (NPR 3, M_j ≈ 1.36), plateaus of AA coupling are broken by bursts in which AA and AS compete ([Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), p. 9–10). Their tone, St 0.37 (p. 5), equals the 0.36–0.37 of the cold single jet from the same nozzle family ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), Table 4, p. 24). A single jet of the OSU design tracks the OSU twins within about 0.02 in St ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), the note's comparison with [Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md) Fig. 6, p. 8). Coupling therefore barely moves the tone, and neither does the nozzle: at M_j ≈ 1.55 the cold Cincinnati LES (St 0.24–0.25), the sharp-throat jet (0.265), the OSU twins (0.27) and the group's convergent AR 2 rectangular jet (flapping at 0.23, NPR 4.0) agree within about 0.04 (the Gojon and Nogueira notes' comparisons; [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 15). In the OSU geometry at M_j 1.5 LES has both AA and AS, with AA the stronger by figure reading ([Yeung 2022](../notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md), p. 8). The minor-axis tone adds about 5 dB to the minor-axis OASPL ([Brès 2021](../notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 11).

Forcing with plasma actuators at the screech frequency moves the coupling ([Esfahani 2021, control](../notes/esfahani-2021-control-twin-rectangular-jet-coupling.md), p. 13–16):
- Symmetric, in-phase forcing removes the intermittency and raises the tone by 8 dB.
- Antisymmetric, out-of-phase forcing decouples the jets and lowers the tone by 7 dB, but raises the first harmonic by 14 dB.

Decoupling coincides with screech being disrupted on one jet, so the effect of loop timing is not isolated. The same rig's regime boundaries move with stagnation and ambient temperature (p. 9).

## Linear models against experiment and LES
**Shock cells and frequency.** [Tam 1988](../notes/tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) extends the Prandtl–Pack vortex sheet to any cross-section. The minor dimension sets the shock spacing, and the aspect-ratio correction is under 3 % for AR > 4 (p. 6). Measured spacing for M_j 1.1–1.7 agrees (p. 9–10). The screech formula with u_c = 0.7 u_j sits slightly above the data, and the AR dependence could not be tested below AR 4 (p. 12). LES of an AR 2 C-D jet fills that gap: Tam's first-cell length is within 1–5 %, the cells shrink linearly downstream (mean ΔL/L_s 5.5 %, as in round jets) and heating cuts the visible count from 10 to 4 (p. 15), and his screech formula with the LES u_c is within about 10 % ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 19). In the AR 4 jet, Powell's formula with the measured U_c and L_s ≈ 3.2h reproduces St ≈ 0.09 to about 1 %, while Tam's vortex-sheet spacing (≈ 3.0h) is about 6 % short (the Alkislar note's check). Measured convection speeds sit below 0.7 u_j: about 0.5 u_j, vorticity-weighted and constant in x, in the AR 4 jet ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 143–144), and 0.60–0.67 u_j when Tam's formula is inverted for the AR 2 LES tones (the Gojon note's arithmetic). [Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) reports that u_c = 0.55 u_j fits rectangular data better, and that finite shear-layer thickness shortens the spacing by about 20 % (p. 59, 62). The area-equivalent diameter collapses screech St for AR 2–5 but fails for AR ≥ 9.6 ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 7–8). Based on the small dimension, St at peak screech is 0.11–0.14 for rectangular jets, against 0.24–0.28 for round jets ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 140).

**KH and guided modes on measured mean flows.**
- *Elliptical jet, Floquet LSA* ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)). On fitted PIV and on RANS mean flows, the se₁ KH wavenumbers fall within the POD range, and the KH result barely depends on which mean flow is used (p. 18–20). The se₁ guided-mode centreline peak matches POD "almost perfectly" (p. 21–22). The guided-mode band is at most 0.09 wide in St, and shifts with shear-layer thickness, so RANS gives narrower bands (p. 22–23). At the screech St the mode is neutral only over a short streamwise stretch, and the measured guided-mode amplitude peaks inside it (p. 22–24).
- *Twin round jets, Floquet LSA* ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md)). The KH wavenumber is 1.80 from POD against 1.90 from LSA. The upstream wavenumber is −1.93 in both, and KH shapes come out too narrow (p. 19–20). The four KH symmetries share one phase speed and differ only slightly in growth (p. 15–17). Only 1SS and 1AS have usable guided-mode bands near the tone (p. 18).
- *Rectangular jet, planar vortex sheet* ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md)). Tam & Norum's planar bands of upstream neutral modes hold the NPR 3 LES tones only in the first antisymmetric band, which the authors take as the reason for flapping. Wavenumber–frequency spectra of the LES pressure show upstream energy near that band's lower limit (p. 24–27). The authors place cold experimental tones from AR 4–5 jets, including [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md)'s, in or just below the same band, and blame the points below it on the infinitely thin vortex sheet (p. 26). The model ignores the major dimension, so it cannot separate minor-axis from diagonal flapping. By the note's arithmetic the hot underexpanded tones fall 8 % and 31 % below the band, in the gap between the S1 and A1 bands, and so by eye do many cold experimental points above M_j ≈ 1.45. Band membership is therefore a weaker test of closure here than the authors suggest (my reading).
- *Rectangular jet, Floquet LSA* ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md)). The k_x spectrum of the PIV flapping mode has a subsonic upstream peak near k_KH − k_sh1, so the dominant loop closes on the first shock peak (p. 4218). On a fitted shock-free 3-D mean flow, LSA wavenumbers in a weakest-link model (α_GJM = α_KH − k_sh) predict the flapping tone within a few per cent at all six PIV NPRs, near the POD-mode peak (p. 4219–4220). A constant-profile base flow works only at low NPR, so the spreading must be modelled (p. 4220). A k_sh2 loop is offered for the second flapping tone, but on frequency alone it sits as close to the varicose tone (the note's reading), and the varicose and wagging tones are not modelled.

**Twin-jet vortex sheets and spacing.** [Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) couple two vortex sheets through the Bessel addition theorem and also solve a finite-thickness model. Branch points of the SS (0, 2) mode bound A1 and A2 sharply from below (p. 18–19). The best-fit finite-thickness parameters are identical to the single jet's (p. 19). The SA band narrows at small spacing, and the SA branch point comes within 1 % of the single-jet value only at S ≈ 50 (p. 11–12). The model still cannot pick SS or SA (p. 22). [Stavropoulos 2024](../notes/stavropoulos-2024-upstream-waves-merging-twin-jets.md) explain the spacing dependence geometrically. As twin jets merge, the (0, 2) mode becomes a higher-order mode of a double-width jet (p. 7), and touching round twins behave like an AR 2 ellipse at long wavelengths (p. 11–12). The SA branch point rises as the jets approach, while SS barely moves (p. 9).

**KH-only maps.** [Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md) map the preferred mode for perfectly expanded jets. It falls in stripes of constant St·s/D, mostly SA1, and coupling strengthens with M_j and T_R (p. 12–19). The authors defer to guided-mode closure for screeching jets (p. 19–20), and the note reads their thin-shear-layer map as favouring SA1 where Nogueira observed 1SS. In the streak model of [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), the guided-mode band moves up in frequency for flapping and down for wagging (p. 11).

**Cross-plane resolvent.** [Marshall 2024](../notes/marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets.md) (preprint, not validated) predict AS/SA dipole streaks in rectangular jets. Sharpening the corners changes gains by no more than 2×, so corner vortices are probably not linear streaks (p. 13–17).

**LES.** A blind wall-modelled LES matches near-field spectra to St ≈ 1 and OASPL to within about 1 dB, but is 2–4 dB low on the minor axis at sideline and upstream angles ([Brès 2021](../notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 9–13). Implicit LES of the single Cincinnati nozzle matches the measured tone within 0.01 in St at six conditions, along with the PIV mean flow and the OASPL directivity. But its screech level rises by 26 dB from TR 1 to 3, while the measured level falls ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 18–19, 21–22, 24). [Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md) run no LSA, but find that at the tone the guided-mode gain (10.10) far exceeds the free-stream acoustic gain (1.16), with a phase speed of −0.71 U_j (p. 18–20). Their cross-excitation return points line up with guided-mode points (p. 21).

**Verdict.** Local models get wavenumbers and mode shapes right, the guided mode better than KH, and existence bands bound the tones. On a measured mean flow, the weakest-link model also predicts the dominant tone ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md)). No model yet predicts which coupling state, flapping plane or coexisting loop wins, or the amplitude, and LES still gets the amplitude trend with temperature wrong. All the models are locally parallel, and the twin-jet versions superpose single-jet mean flows, which holds for about 5 D in close jets ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 8).

## Passive control by nozzle shape
Trailing-edge cutouts on AR 3 Mach 2 nozzles make streamwise vortices only off-design, raising mixing by up to about 50 % ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 7–8). They lower noise at 30° by up to 12 dB when the jet is underexpanded ([Kerechanin 2001](../notes/kerechanin-2001-trailing-edge-rectangular-jet-acoustics.md), p. 3). On overexpanded jets, single-sided cuts remove screech, while symmetric two-sided cuts amplify it and raise its frequency. The authors attribute this to breaking or keeping the symmetry of the shock cells about the major axis (p. 4–5). A thick lip revives weak screech ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 8), as Raman documents for rectangular jets ([Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md), p. 75–77).

## Disagreements and open questions
- **What selects the coupling symmetry?** Three candidates are on the table: KH growth ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md)), guided-mode existence bands ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md); [Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md)), and loop gain between competing global modes ([Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md), p. 29; [Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 26). Bands bound the tones but admit both SS and SA. Bell's 5–15 % split between the symmetric and antisymmetric tones, with different wavelengths, hints that each symmetry closes its own loop (the note's inference). *To settle:* a complex-valued (gain-bearing) resonance model or Floquet-in-x/global analysis of twin-jet mean flows, as suggested in [Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) (p. 22), tested against cross-plane PIV that can separate 1SS from 1AS ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), p. 21). A cheap first test is Nogueira's Floquet LSA on Bell's NPR 4.6 mean flow: do the 1SS and 1SA guided-mode bands both reach St 0.22–0.23?
- **Is intermittency intrinsic or driven?** Candidates are stochastic switching between competing global modes, a reading that starts with [Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md) (p. 29) and that [Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md) test against supply drift (p. 39–41); frequency detuning between nominally identical nozzles ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 10–11); and AA/AS competition ([Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md)). A fourth comes from re-reading Bell's statistics. The wide states have envelope spreads of 5.5–6 dB, close to the 5.6 dB of narrow-band Gaussian noise, and interruption fractions that track the threshold as noise would. That favours weakly damped, noise-driven modes over switching between saturated limit cycles (the note's analysis); Bell tested neither supply drift nor beating. The two OSU papers disagree at M_j 1.35. One reports beating with a drifting phase. The other reports two tones 150 Hz apart, with in-phase coupling interrupted at about 123 Hz and no phase drift ([Esfahani 2021, control](../notes/esfahani-2021-control-twin-rectangular-jet-coupling.md), p. 10). *To settle:* long records with logged plenum pressure and temperature, per-jet and per-symmetry Hilbert tracking, the beat test (beat frequency equal to the frequency difference), and a Rayleigh null for every envelope statistic.
- **What fixes the flapping plane of a single jet?** Minor-axis flapping is the norm, but cold LES of an AR 2 C-D nozzle flaps along the diagonal and turns to minor-axis flapping only when heated ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 21–24). Candidates are heating shifting the guided-mode bands, a time average of competing AS and AA states as in the twin jets ([Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), p. 9–10), and streaks pinning or slowly rotating the plane as in round jets ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 20–24). The planar band model cannot tell AS from AA. The major-axis-view signature of the dominant tone in a cold jet of the same nozzle type may be the experimental trace of a diagonal component ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4214; the note's suggestion). *To settle:* D2-split SPOD of the LES at the tone with per-symmetry Hilbert tracking, and rotated-view schlieren of a cold C-D jet.
- **Bi-axial and quasi-helical modes.** It is open whether each is one wavepacket that changes plane or two structures. The thread starts with a helical-like pair whose helicity was never tested across planes ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2745–2747). Suzuki's orthogonality argument favours two structures, but in a shock-free, low-Re jet; the wagging tone downstream of the switch in [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md) fits the same picture (the note's reading). Whether that jet's four loops coexist or alternate is untested, because its analysis is block-averaged. The tone frequencies at elliptical NPR 3.6 also differ between [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) (0.28, 0.32) and [Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) (0.269, 0.273, 0.314). *To settle:* cross-plane or volumetric data, rotated or simultaneous two-view schlieren with sub-domain SPOD, and OWNS or global analysis on the axis-switched mean flow.
- **Cause and effect with the mean flow.** Does screech move the axis switch and pin the streaks, or do they select the mode ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md); [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md); [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 25)? [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md) argue the first, through screech-driven streamwise vortices, and predict that exciting an ideally expanded jet should make it switch and that suppressing screech should remove the crossover (p. 140, 153). Suzuki's forced DNS supports the first prediction. Heating, however, moves the switch downstream in LES while the simulated tone grows ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 13, 18–19). *To settle:* forced LES with energy-flux budgets.
- **What sets the tone amplitude?** LES of the Cincinnati nozzle gets the tone frequency within 0.01 in St but its level rises by 26 dB from TR 1 to 3 while the measured level falls; the authors note only that the LES jet is initially disturbed and the experiment fully turbulent ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 18–19, 27). [Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md) argue that coherent circulation at the end of the third shock cell, together with shock strength, sets the amplitude (p. 142–144, 153), and the AR 2 ellipse also produces its tone where coherent vorticity peaks before the third shock ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2747). But Alkislar's evidence is one phase at two conditions, and the quieter jet is only 1–3 dB quieter while its circulation is less than half (the note's reading). *To settle:* Hilbert-tracked coherent amplitude at the radiating cell against the microphone envelope, and LES with matched exit turbulence at each TR.
- **Which wave closes the cross-excitation loop?** [Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md) says the guided mode; [Esfahani 2021, control](../notes/esfahani-2021-control-twin-rectangular-jet-coupling.md) frames it as free-stream acoustics (p. 11). Guided-mode bands have been computed for a single rectangular jet ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4219) but not yet for rectangular twins.
- **Lateral coupling.** [Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md) see none in the major-axis plane (p. 9). The companion paper's regime map lists in-phase coupling in both planes when the jets are underexpanded ([Esfahani 2021, control](../notes/esfahani-2021-control-twin-rectangular-jet-coupling.md), p. 9).
- **Corner vortices.** They are not linear streaks according to [Marshall 2024](../notes/marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets.md) (p. 17). A resolvent analysis on an LES mean flow is the proposed test. Cross-plane stereo PIV is the matching measurement; in the AR 4 jet it shows counter-rotating streamwise pairs with traces of the corner vortices at x/h = 16 ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 136–140).

## Methods and data
- **Time-resolved data** (high-speed schlieren, LES, microphones) supports:
  - SPOD with nozzle rotation and sub-domains ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md)), or with two views, plus a k–ω dispersion relation along one line that shows the KH, guided and duct branches ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4213–4214);
  - a Fourier transform of the 3-D LES field at the tone for the oscillation shape, and k–ω spectra inside the jet for upstream waves ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 21–27), though one transform over the record cannot tell a steady pattern from alternating states;
  - D2-split SPOD ([Yeung 2022](../notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md));
  - CWT of POD coefficients and conditional sampling ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md));
  - coherence at all frequencies to find coexisting tones, then band-pass Hilbert envelopes and phases per tone ([Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md), p. 8–14), which need a stated filter, a beat test and a Rayleigh null (the note's analysis);
  - wavelet coherence between microphone pairs, one pair across a jet for its mode and one across the jets for coupling ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 5–9);
  - Hilbert phase difference and two-probe gain maps ([Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md));
  - BMD ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md)).

  The frequency shifts between A1/A2 states are 0.3–0.6 %, so SPOD needs long blocks and Morlet wavelets need hundreds of cycles ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md)). The m = 1 tones are 5–15 % apart and much easier to separate ([Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md)).
- **Non-time-resolved data** (PIV, 1 Hz schlieren) supports:
  - symmetry-imposed POD pairs followed by k_x filtering and LSA ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)), with a fitted shock-free 3-D mean flow as the base flow ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4215–4216);
  - sub-domain and conditional POD on each jet's pair amplitude ([Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md), p. 22–27);
  - microphone-triggered phase-locked stereo PIV when the tone is clean, though mirroring half the cycle imposes antisymmetry ([Alkislar 2003](../notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 130);
  - two-point correlation of schlieren ([Knast 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md); [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), p. 2743);
  - log σ maps for standing waves ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 15–17).

  POD pairs fail when the coupling switches ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md)).
- **Aliasing.** Check the tone against Nyquist when snapshot POD runs on decimated schlieren ([Berry 2017](../notes/berry-2017-pod-time-resolved-schlieren-multi-stream-rectangular-jet.md), p. 8).

## Relevance to our work
- **Group rigs.** The group's twin round nozzle plates ([Knast 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md); [Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md); [Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md); [Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md)), the AR 2 elliptical nozzles ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md); [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md)) and the AR 2 convergent and sharp-throat C-D rectangular nozzles ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md); [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md)) are the data this page is about. Fitted resonance parameters differ between rigs, and lip thickness (15 % against 3 %) is the suspected cause ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 16). Identical nozzles on two Monash rigs also give different state NPRs, because of the enclosure walls and the plenum face ([Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md), p. 6, 17–18). Record lip, flange, enclosure and upstream-surface geometry.
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** The pipeline fits the open intermittency questions above. Reconstruct the tone band separately from even and odd fields (or D2 components), and from each half-domain, then track instantaneous amplitude and frequency per symmetry and per jet. That replaces Bell's assumption of one microphone per jet with direct envelopes and gives the inter-jet phase that conditional POD can only infer (the Bell note). Choose band widths with the 0.3–0.6 % (A1/A2) or 5–15 % (m = 1) shifts in mind, test for beats before calling anything switching, and compare envelope statistics with a Rayleigh null. First targets: round twins at s/D 3, NPR 3.4–4.4 ([Bell 2021](../notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md)); whether the loops of the multimodal AR 2 jets coexist or alternate ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md); [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md)); and diagonal against minor-axis flapping ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md)).
- **PyFR staging LES.** For 3-D output the D2 split (or an azimuthal split) cuts memory 4×. If runs reach the |m| = 1 stages, round-jet LES can carry slowly rotating |m| = 2 streaks that fix a flapping axis, and averaging them out needs records several times longer than one streak rotation ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 14). If envelope statistics near a staging boundary look Rayleigh-like, switching is noise-driven, and dwell times follow escape-rate statistics rather than the deterministic 1/t_switch fit of the continuation driver (the Bell note's suggestion).
- **Rocket noise.** Screech is absent at full scale ([Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md)). Coupling still sets near-field loads in the lab: about 15 dB between coupling modes midway between the jets ([Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md), p. 90–91). The "equivalent geometry" of merging jets ([Stavropoulos 2024](../notes/stavropoulos-2024-upstream-waves-merging-twin-jets.md)) parallels the merged equivalent jet in Eldred-type clustered-nozzle models ([Kandula 2008](../notes/kandula-2008-near-field-acoustics-clustered-rocket-engines.md)); the link is mine.
