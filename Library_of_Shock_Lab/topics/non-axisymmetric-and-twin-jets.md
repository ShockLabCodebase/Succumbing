# Non-axisymmetric and twin jets

_Last updated: 2026-10-20 · 22 papers_

## Scope
Supersonic, mostly screeching, jets from rectangular and elliptical nozzles, and pairs of round or rectangular jets. The page covers which oscillation shapes these jets select (varicose, flapping, wagging, helical), how axis switching and other changes in the mean flow alter those shapes, the symmetry decompositions used to separate them, how twin jets couple and why the coupling is often intermittent, and how well the linear models built for these geometries (vortex sheets, Floquet-in-azimuth stability, cross-plane resolvent) match experiment and LES.

The screech loop itself (guided-jet-mode closure, k_kh − k_s triads, staging in round jets) is on [screech](screech.md); this page only says what changes with geometry. Model formulations in general are on [linear-models](linear-models.md), SPOD/POD practice on [modal-decomposition](modal-decomposition.md), Hilbert and wavelet tracking on [time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md), non-axisymmetric impinging jets on [impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md), mixing noise and BBSAN on [jet-noise-mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md), and clustered rocket nozzles on [rocket-noise-models](rocket-noise-models.md).

**Naming.** *Flapping* is the transverse oscillation across the minor axis (Mathieu mode se₁ of an elliptic jet); *wagging* is the same motion in the major-axis plane (ce₁); *varicose* is ce₀ ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md)). For twin jets and D2-symmetric flows the papers use two letters, XY, but they do not all mean the same planes:

## Current understanding
1. **Breaking axisymmetry turns helices into flapping.** In a round jet the m = +1 and m = −1 modes are degenerate, so flapping and helical motions are equivalent. Any π-periodic distortion of the cross-section (an ellipse, a second jet, weak |m| = 2 streaks) couples them and splits the pair into a flapping and a wagging eigenmode with different growth rates and wavenumbers. One of the pair then wins ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 8–12; [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 7; [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 7–10). Experiments, LES and DNS agree that minor-axis flapping dominates rectangular and elliptical jets ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 138–139; [Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md), p. 54; [Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 9; [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 15; [Yeung 2022](../notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md), p. 8; [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 10).
2. **AR ≈ 2 jets stage and become multimodal; AR ≥ 4 jets do not stage.** AR 2 rectangular and elliptical jets switch from varicose to flapping at NPR ≈ 2.2 ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 7). At AR 4, strong flapping breaks up the shock cells so quickly that there is no suboptimal shock peak, and so no staging ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 18–19).
3. **Axis switching changes which modes the jet can support downstream** ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md); [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)).
4. **Twin jets are coupled out to large spacings, but the coupling is often unsteady.** Steady coupling, switching between symmetries, and uncoupling all occur ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 9–12; [Knast 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md), p. 4–5; [Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), p. 9–10).
5. **In every geometry studied, the upstream guided jet mode closes the loop.** Its existence band explains symmetry selection better than KH growth does ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), p. 15–19; [Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 18–19; [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 22–24; [Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), p. 20–21).
6. **Split the data by symmetry before decomposing it.** The split is exact and orthogonal, and it saves memory and speeds convergence ([Yeung 2022](../notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md), p. 5, 8; [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), p. 6–8).
7. **Three diagnostic traps recur.** Two slightly detuned jets beat, which looks like intermittency or "coupling" in both symmetries ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 10–11; [Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 32–38). Line-of-sight integration cancels out-of-phase content ([Esfahani 2021, control](../notes/esfahani-2021-control-twin-rectangular-jet-coupling.md), p. 15). The mean image of a flapping jet looks artificially thick ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 7).

## Mode shapes: flapping, wagging, varicose, helical
The classic evidence is [Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md). In an AR 16.7 rectangular jet, phase-locked schlieren show the two shear layers exactly out of phase (p. 138–139). Minor-axis flapping appears near x/D ≈ 10, and a sideways major-axis oscillation appears beyond 60 D. Each has St ≈ 0.12 on its own nozzle dimension, which the authors read through Crighton's elliptic vortex sheet (p. 139–140). That reading rests on one station and one spectral peak.

[Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) summarises the pre-2000 picture. Rectangular jets are antisymmetric, except near choking, where symmetric modes appear (Gutmark et al. saw a switch at M_j 1.15) but stay weak because their feedback efficiency is low (p. 54–55). Bevelled AR 5 nozzles select higher-order spanwise waveguide modes (p. 85–87). In the AR 2 C-D twin jets of [Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), each jet flaps at every M_j from 1.20 to 1.85, and no symmetric or lateral mode is seen (p. 9). Flapping is not only a product of screech: [Berry 2017](../notes/berry-2017-pod-time-resolved-schlieren-multi-stream-rectangular-jet.md) finds flapping POD modes at St 0.46–0.70 in an ideally expanded multi-stream rectangular jet with no screech (p. 8).

Modern work classifies the modes quantitatively. [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) rotates the nozzle through 19 views. The screech SPOD eigenvalue λ_s(θ) is flat for varicose modes, and for flapping modes it is v-shaped with its minimum at the major-axis view (p. 9–11). In an AR 2 elliptical jet, [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) find that the minor-axis POD pair holds 23.6 % (NPR 2.6) and 35 % (NPR 3.4) of the fluctuation energy, against 6.8 % and 10.3 % on the major axis (p. 15). The jet is therefore se₁ flapping. In LES of the OSU twin jets, the St 0.3 tone appears only in AA and AS, the components antisymmetric about the plane of the nozzles ([Yeung 2022](../notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md), p. 8). In forced subsonic DNS, AS flapping dominates at every forcing level, and SA wagging decays faster ([Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 8, 10).

**Why flapping wins.** [Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md) show that in a single round jet the m = 1 eigenvalue appears twice, in AS and SA, and that a helix is SA1 + i AS1 (p. 8–9). A second jet splits the two into four eigenvalues. Small differences in k_i then grow exponentially with x, so a helix at s/D 2.2 becomes SA1 flapping within a few diameters (p. 12). [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) (not tagged) apply the same N = 2 Floquet argument to a single round jet. A |m| = 2 streak of amplitude 0.01 already fixes the flapping axis (p. 10, 12). In a heated M_j 1.35 LES, the flapping plane precesses with slowly rotating streaks, drifting about π/2 over about 100 screech cycles (p. 20–24). For an ellipse the nozzle itself provides the N = 2 periodicity ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 7). So ellipses, twin jets and streaky round jets are one symmetry-breaking problem. This unification is my reading; each paper treats only its own case.

Helical-looking motion survives in a few places. Knast's round twin jets show co- and counter-rotating "helical" coupling, but the two-point correlation they rely on cannot rigorously tell a helix from flapping ([Knast 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md), p. 6–9). The elliptical "quasi-helical" mode is antisymmetric from every view and has a flat λ_s; confirming that it really is helical needs cross-plane data ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 16–19).

## Axis switching and the evolving mean flow
Screech reshapes these jets. At peak screech (NPR 3.8) the AR 16.7 jet spreads at 36° in the minor-axis plane, against 20–25° at other NPRs, and its axis crossover moves well upstream. At other NPRs the crossover moves downstream as NPR rises, and the jet is still not axisymmetric at 160 D ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 123, 133, 140–142). [Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) cites Zaman: B-mode (flapping/helical) screech raises mass flux by about 60 %, while the other stages do not (p. 94, 97). In AR 3 Mach 2 jets with trailing-edge cutouts, axis switching by 8 D_eq occurs only off-design, at M_j 2.5; with no expansion mismatch there is none up to 15 D_eq ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 6–7).

The AR 2 work puts the switch inside the screech region. The fitted flow AR of Ivelja's ellipse is about 1.7 at the nozzle, not 2, and it reaches 1 near x/D ≈ 5 at NPR 2.6, farther downstream at NPR 3.4 ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 10–11).

In the AR 2 rectangular jet at NPR 4.0, the mean shock cells switch axes at x/D_e ≈ 6.5, the fourth cell. The St 0.27 "bi-axial flapping" mode's envelope peaks at 6.5 D_e in the minor-axis view and at 8.1 D_e in the major-axis view. Sub-domain SPOD shows flapping upstream of the switch and wagging downstream ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 11–14). Two other tones present at the same NPR are unaffected by the switch (p. 15–17).

[Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md) isolates the effect in a shock-free, low-Re DNS:
- Stronger near-nozzle forcing moves the switch point upstream: none, then x/D_eq ≈ 12, then ≈ 8 (p. 6).
- The major-axis contraction is a shift of the shear layer, not a thinning (p. 8).
- At low St, a "new flapping" mode in the SA family is born at the switch point and has no upstream support (p. 13–15).
- AS flapping cannot become SA linearly, because the symmetry families are orthogonal (p. 13–14).

This is a real constraint on the bi-axial mode: it may be two coexisting structures rather than one wavepacket that rotates.

The coupling runs both ways. In the AR 2 elliptical jet at NPR 3.6, two flapping tones (St 0.269 and 0.314) are mutually exclusive (ρ = −0.92), and each holds its own short-time mean, with shock cells contracted under one tone and extended under the other ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), not tagged, p. 14–15). The loop closes on k_s1 up to NPR 3.3 and on k_s2 from NPR 3.4 to 4.9 (p. 21–22). This matches a stage jump in an AR 2 elliptical jet that does not change the dominant symmetry ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 18).

## Symmetry decompositions
- **D2 split (3-D data).** Four orthogonal components (SS, SA, AS, AA) sum exactly to the field, and SPOD of one quadrant per component saves 4× memory. Full-field SPOD does recover the components eventually, but its fourth mode was unconverged ([Yeung 2022](../notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md), p. 5, 8). Applied to near-lip pressure, the split gives instantaneous AA and AS amplitudes ([Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), p. 10). Applied before pressure SPOD of DNS, with zero-weight sub-domains, it separates the wagging and new-flapping structures ([Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 5, 14).
- **Symmetry-imposed POD (planar PIV).** Splitting into even and odd fields about the midplane works without time resolution, but the measurement plane cannot see two of the four symmetries (1AA, 1AS) ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), p. 6–8, Table 1 p. 7). The split also slightly improves convergence for an ellipse ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 15).
- **Half-domain and symmetry-imposed POD with CWT (TR schlieren).** One field per jet (HD) and even and odd fields (SI) are each passed through POD and a Morlet transform. This tracks each jet's frequency and the dominant symmetry over time ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 8–10).
- **Path-integrated views.** Schlieren through two out-of-phase jets cancels their lobes ([Esfahani 2021, control](../notes/esfahani-2021-control-twin-rectangular-jet-coupling.md), p. 15). Rotating the nozzle and comparing modes from mirror-image views with an inner product β stands in for a D2 split ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 8–9).

## Twin-jet coupling states and intermittency
**Round twins, m = 0 (A1/A2).** Staging NPRs barely differ from those of a single jet. Three states occur ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 1, 9–19):
- steady coupling in one symmetry;
- SS↔SA competition, with switches every O(10²–10³) cycles and a frequency shift of 0.30–0.63 %;
- uncoupling, seen only at s/D 6, with the jets about 0.5–0.6 % apart in frequency.

Every spacing changes state at the A1→A2 jump (p. 12). SS sits at a lower frequency than SA (p. 19), as twin-jet stability models predict ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 20, 22). This is the clearest model–experiment agreement on coupling. Fine NPR sweeps show that supply-pressure drift explains the switching at one condition but not at another, which points to stochastic switching between competing global modes ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 39–41).

**Round twins, m = 1 (higher NPR).** At S/D 3, [Knast 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) find antisymmetric flapping, then counter-rotating helical motion, then symmetric flapping as NPR rises from 2.3 to 5.5. At S/D 6 they find co-rotating helical motion and symmetric flapping (Table 2, p. 9). The inter-plume phase jumps without any jump in frequency, and coupling still dominates at S/D 6 (p. 4–5). U_c/U_j stays at 0.57–0.60 across all the modes (p. 10). Regions of scattered phase are attributed to unsteady coupling, but the time-averaged method cannot test this (p. 5). At NPR 5 and s/D 3, [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md) find steady 1SS coupling in PIV (p. 9–12, 20–21). This appears consistent with Knast's symmetric flapping at the same spacing and NPR, once the labels are mapped.

**Rectangular twins.** Raman and Taghavi's AR 5 jets flap out of phase and switch to in phase at M_j ≈ 1.5, taking a new shared frequency. The out-of-phase mode is about 15 dB quieter midway between the nozzles ([Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md), p. 88–91; AR from [Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 3). The OSU AR 2 jets ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 8–11):
- couple out of phase at M_j 1.40–1.50;
- lose coherence just above design;
- couple in phase from M_j 1.65;
- gain only about 5 dB at the switch, against 15–20 dB in earlier rigs;
- at M_j 1.20–1.35 keep their own frequencies (140 Hz apart, falling to 33 Hz) and beat. This contradicts the shared-frequency claim.

In LES of the Cincinnati geometry (NPR 3, M_j ≈ 1.36), plateaus of AA coupling are broken by bursts in which AA and AS compete ([Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md), p. 9–10). In the OSU geometry at M_j 1.5 LES has both AA and AS, with AA the stronger by figure reading ([Yeung 2022](../notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md), p. 8). The minor-axis tone adds about 5 dB to the minor-axis OASPL ([Brès 2021](../notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 11).

Forcing with plasma actuators at the screech frequency moves the coupling ([Esfahani 2021, control](../notes/esfahani-2021-control-twin-rectangular-jet-coupling.md), p. 13–16):
- Symmetric, in-phase forcing removes the intermittency and raises the tone by 8 dB.
- Antisymmetric, out-of-phase forcing decouples the jets and lowers the tone by 7 dB, but raises the first harmonic by 14 dB.

Decoupling coincides with screech being disrupted on one jet, so the effect of loop timing is not isolated. The same rig's regime boundaries move with stagnation and ambient temperature (p. 9).

## Linear models against experiment and LES
**Shock cells and frequency.** [Tam 1988](../notes/tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) extends the Prandtl–Pack vortex sheet to any cross-section. The minor dimension sets the shock spacing, and the aspect-ratio correction is under 3 % for AR > 4 (p. 6). Measured spacing for M_j 1.1–1.7 agrees (p. 9–10). The screech formula with u_c = 0.7 u_j sits slightly above the data, and the AR dependence could not be tested below AR 4 (p. 12). [Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) reports that u_c = 0.55 u_j fits rectangular data better, and that finite shear-layer thickness shortens the spacing by about 20 % (p. 59, 62). The area-equivalent diameter collapses screech St for AR 2–5 but fails for AR ≥ 9.6 ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 7–8). Based on the small dimension, St at peak screech is 0.11–0.14 for rectangular jets, against 0.24–0.28 for round jets ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md), p. 140).

**KH and guided modes on measured mean flows.** Two results from the same Floquet-in-azimuth spatial LSA:
- *Elliptical jet* ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)). On fitted PIV and on RANS mean flows, the se₁ KH wavenumbers fall within the POD range, and the KH result barely depends on which mean flow is used (p. 18–20). The se₁ guided-mode centreline peak matches POD "almost perfectly" (p. 21–22). The guided-mode band is at most 0.09 wide in St, and shifts with shear-layer thickness, so RANS gives narrower bands (p. 22–23). At the screech St the mode is neutral only over a short streamwise stretch, and the measured guided-mode amplitude peaks inside it (p. 22–24).
- *Twin round jets* ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md)). The KH wavenumber is 1.80 from POD against 1.90 from LSA. The upstream wavenumber is −1.93 in both, and KH shapes come out too narrow (p. 19–20). The four KH symmetries share one phase speed and differ only slightly in growth (p. 15–17). Only 1SS and 1AS have usable guided-mode bands near the tone (p. 18).

**Twin-jet vortex sheets and spacing.** [Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) couple two vortex sheets through the Bessel addition theorem and also solve a finite-thickness model. Branch points of the SS (0, 2) mode bound A1 and A2 sharply from below (p. 18–19). The best-fit finite-thickness parameters are identical to the single jet's (p. 19). The SA band narrows at small spacing, and the SA branch point comes within 1 % of the single-jet value only at S ≈ 50 (p. 11–12). The model still cannot pick SS or SA (p. 22). [Stavropoulos 2024](../notes/stavropoulos-2024-upstream-waves-merging-twin-jets.md) explain the spacing dependence geometrically. As twin jets merge, the (0, 2) mode becomes a higher-order mode of a double-width jet (p. 7), and touching round twins behave like an AR 2 ellipse at long wavelengths (p. 11–12). The SA branch point rises as the jets approach, while SS barely moves (p. 9).

**KH-only maps.** [Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md) map the preferred mode for perfectly expanded jets. It falls in stripes of constant St·s/D, mostly SA1, and coupling strengthens with M_j and T_R (p. 12–19). The authors defer to guided-mode closure for screeching jets (p. 19–20), and the note reads their thin-shear-layer map as favouring SA1 where Nogueira observed 1SS. In the streak model of [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), the guided-mode band moves up in frequency for flapping and down for wagging (p. 11).

**Cross-plane resolvent.** [Marshall 2024](../notes/marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets.md) (preprint, not validated) predict AS/SA dipole streaks in rectangular jets. Sharpening the corners changes gains by no more than 2×, so corner vortices are probably not linear streaks (p. 13–17).

**LES.** A blind wall-modelled LES matches near-field spectra to St ≈ 1 and OASPL to within about 1 dB, but is 2–4 dB low on the minor axis at sideline and upstream angles ([Brès 2021](../notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 9–13). [Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md) run no LSA, but find that at the tone the guided-mode gain (10.10) far exceeds the free-stream acoustic gain (1.16), with a phase speed of −0.71 U_j (p. 18–20). Their cross-excitation return points line up with guided-mode points (p. 21).

**Verdict.** Local models get wavenumbers and mode shapes right, the guided mode better than KH, and existence bands bound the tones. No model yet predicts which coupling state or flapping axis wins, or the amplitude. All of them are locally parallel, and the twin-jet versions superpose single-jet mean flows, which holds for about 5 D in close jets ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 8).

## Passive control by nozzle shape
Trailing-edge cutouts on AR 3 Mach 2 nozzles make streamwise vortices only off-design, raising mixing by up to about 50 % ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 7–8). They lower noise at 30° by up to 12 dB when the jet is underexpanded ([Kerechanin 2001](../notes/kerechanin-2001-trailing-edge-rectangular-jet-acoustics.md), p. 3). On overexpanded jets, single-sided cuts remove screech, while symmetric two-sided cuts amplify it and raise its frequency. The authors attribute this to breaking or keeping the symmetry of the shock cells about the major axis (p. 4–5). A thick lip revives weak screech ([Samimy 1998](../notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md), p. 8), as Raman documents for rectangular jets ([Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md), p. 75–77).

## Disagreements and open questions
- **What selects the coupling symmetry?** Three candidates are on the table: KH growth ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md)), guided-mode existence bands ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md); [Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md)), and loop gain between competing global modes ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 26). Bands bound the tones but admit both SS and SA. *To settle:* a complex-valued (gain-bearing) resonance model or Floquet-in-x/global analysis of twin-jet mean flows, as suggested in [Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) (p. 22), tested against cross-plane PIV that can separate 1SS from 1AS ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), p. 21).
- **Is intermittency intrinsic or driven?** Candidates are stochastic switching ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 39–41), frequency detuning between nominally identical nozzles ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 10–11), and AA/AS competition ([Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md)). The two OSU papers disagree at M_j 1.35. One reports beating with a drifting phase. The other reports two tones 150 Hz apart, with in-phase coupling interrupted at about 123 Hz and no phase drift ([Esfahani 2021, control](../notes/esfahani-2021-control-twin-rectangular-jet-coupling.md), p. 10). *To settle:* long records with logged plenum pressure and temperature, per-jet and per-symmetry Hilbert tracking, and the beat test (beat frequency equal to the frequency difference).
- **Bi-axial and quasi-helical modes.** It is open whether each is one wavepacket that changes plane or two structures. Suzuki's orthogonality argument favours two, but that is in a shock-free, low-Re jet. The tone frequencies at elliptical NPR 3.6 also differ between [Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) (0.28, 0.32) and [Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) (0.269, 0.273, 0.314). *To settle:* cross-plane or volumetric data, and OWNS or global analysis on the axis-switched mean flow.
- **Cause and effect with the mean flow.** Does screech move the axis switch and pin the streaks, or do they select the mode ([Krothapalli 1986](../notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md); [Suzuki 2026](../notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md); [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 25)? *To settle:* forced LES with energy-flux budgets.
- **Which wave closes the cross-excitation loop?** [Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md) says the guided mode; [Esfahani 2021, control](../notes/esfahani-2021-control-twin-rectangular-jet-coupling.md) frames it as free-stream acoustics (p. 11). No one has computed guided-mode bands for rectangular twin jets yet.
- **Lateral coupling.** [Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md) see none in the major-axis plane (p. 9). The companion paper's regime map lists in-phase coupling in both planes when the jets are underexpanded ([Esfahani 2021, control](../notes/esfahani-2021-control-twin-rectangular-jet-coupling.md), p. 9).
- **Corner vortices.** They are not linear streaks according to [Marshall 2024](../notes/marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets.md) (p. 17). A resolvent analysis on an LES mean flow is the proposed test.

## Methods and data
- **Time-resolved data** (high-speed schlieren, LES, microphones) supports:
  - SPOD with nozzle rotation and sub-domains ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md));
  - D2-split SPOD ([Yeung 2022](../notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md));
  - CWT of POD coefficients and conditional sampling ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md));
  - wavelet coherence between microphone pairs, one pair across a jet for its mode and one across the jets for coupling ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 5–9);
  - Hilbert phase difference and two-probe gain maps ([Jeun 2024](../notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md));
  - BMD ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md)).

  The frequency shifts between states are 0.3–0.6 %, so SPOD needs long blocks and Morlet wavelets need hundreds of cycles ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md)).
- **Non-time-resolved data** (PIV, 1 Hz schlieren) supports:
  - symmetry-imposed POD pairs followed by k_x filtering and LSA ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md));
  - two-point correlation of schlieren ([Knast 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md));
  - log σ maps for standing waves ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 15–17).

  POD pairs fail when the coupling switches ([Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md)).
- **Aliasing.** Check the tone against Nyquist when snapshot POD runs on decimated schlieren ([Berry 2017](../notes/berry-2017-pod-time-resolved-schlieren-multi-stream-rectangular-jet.md), p. 8).

## Relevance to our work
- **Group rigs.** The group's twin round nozzle plate ([Knast 2018](../notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md); [Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md); [Wong 2023](../notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md)) and the AR 2 rectangular and elliptical nozzles ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)) are the data this page is about. Fitted resonance parameters differ between rigs, and lip thickness (15 % against 3 %) is the suspected cause ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 16), so record lip and flange geometry.
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** The pipeline fits the open intermittency questions above. Reconstruct the tone band separately from even and odd fields (or D2 components), and from each half-domain, then track instantaneous amplitude and frequency per symmetry and per jet. Choose band widths with the 0.3–0.6 % shifts in mind, and test for beats before calling anything switching. Multimodal AR 2 cases ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md)) are the obvious first target.
- **PyFR staging LES.** For 3-D output the D2 split (or an azimuthal split) cuts memory 4×. If runs reach the |m| = 1 stages, round-jet LES can carry slowly rotating |m| = 2 streaks that fix a flapping axis, and averaging them out needs records several times longer than one streak rotation ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 14).
- **Rocket noise.** Screech is absent at full scale ([Lubert 2022](../notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md)). Coupling still sets near-field loads in the lab: about 15 dB between coupling modes midway between the jets ([Raman 1998](../notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md), p. 90–91). The "equivalent geometry" of merging jets ([Stavropoulos 2024](../notes/stavropoulos-2024-upstream-waves-merging-twin-jets.md)) parallels the merged equivalent jet in Eldred-type clustered-nozzle models ([Kandula 2008](../notes/kandula-2008-near-field-acoustics-clustered-rocket-engines.md)); the link is mine.
