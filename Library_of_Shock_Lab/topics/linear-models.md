# Linear models of jet dynamics

_Last updated: 2026-10-20 · 38 papers_

## Scope

Linear models built on a jet's base flow, usually its turbulent mean: local spatial stability and vortex sheets, Floquet analyses (periodic in x or in azimuth), guided-jet-wave theory, global stability, resolvent analysis and its forcing and eddy-viscosity problem, parabolised and one-way marching (PSE, PFE, OWNS), and the harmonic resolvent. The page sorts them by model family and by what they explain: KH wavepackets, Orr packets, lift-up streaks, trapped and guided acoustic waves, and modulation by shock cells. It also records where they match experiment or LES and where they fail.

Applications are covered on other pages and only linked here. Closure, staging and frequency prediction are in [screech](screech.md); flapping and coupling in [non-axisymmetric and twin jets](non-axisymmetric-and-twin-jets.md); BBSAN and Mach-wave source models in [jet-noise mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md); the SPOD and POD used to test the models in [modal decomposition](modal-decomposition.md); edge and impingement loops in [impinging jets and edge tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md). Four non-jet papers are included because they underpin the methods: pipe ([McKeon & Sharma 2010](../notes/mckeon-2010-critical-layer-framework-turbulent-pipe-flow.md)), channel ([Morra 2021](../notes/morra-2021-colour-of-forcing-resolvent-channel.md)), airfoil ([Rolandi 2025](../notes/rolandi-2025-biglobal-resolvent-naca0012.md)) and the two Taira reviews.

## Current understanding

1. **Linearising about the turbulent mean works, but only statistically.** PSE on measured mean flows reproduce the averaged phase and envelope of wavepackets for St ≈ 0.2–0.9, m = 0–2, up to the end of the potential core, with one free amplitude per (m, ω) ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md), p. 109–113; [Cavalieri 2013](../notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), p. 574–585). Single realisations need not follow the model (Gudmundsson & Colonius, p. 122–123).
2. **Free jets are amplifiers.** No isolated, weakly damped global mode exists. The "preferred mode" (St ≈ 0.43–0.46) is a pseudo-resonance of a non-normal operator ([Garnaud 2013](../notes/garnaud-2013-preferred-mode-incompressible-jets.md), p. 5, 8, 11). The resolvent is low rank (σ₁ ≫ σ₂) for roughly 0.2–0.3 ≲ St ≲ 2 at m = 0 and 1. There the optimal response is the KH wavepacket and matches the leading SPOD mode ([Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 6, 13, 16–17; [Towne 2018](../notes/towne-2018-spod-relationship-dmd-resolvent.md), p. 35; [Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 19–20).
3. **Three linear mechanisms organise broadband jet turbulence.** KH dominates at moderate St and low m (c_ph ≈ 0.8U_j). Orr takes over for m = 0 below St ≈ 0.3 and in suboptimal modes (c_ph ≈ 0.4, downstream of the core). Lift-up streaks dominate for St/m ≲ 0.1 ([Pickering 2020](../notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md), p. 15–21; [Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 8–13; [Nogueira 2019](../notes/nogueira-2019-large-scale-streaky-structures-jets.md), p. 13–19).
4. **Most failures come from the forcing, not the operator.** Real nonlinear forcing is coloured and projects onto suboptimal modes ([Towne 2018](../notes/towne-2018-spod-relationship-dmd-resolvent.md), p. 18–21; [Morra 2021](../notes/morra-2021-colour-of-forcing-resolvent-channel.md), p. 20–22). An eddy viscosity repairs much of this ([Pickering 2021](../notes/pickering-2021-optimal-eddy-viscosity-resolvent-jets.md), p. 14–17), but the forcing amplitudes are still not uniform (p. 26–27).
5. **The jet is also an acoustic waveguide.** Upstream guided-jet waves and duct-like trapped waves are discrete, near-neutral modes of vortex-sheet, finite-thickness and global operators ([Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md), p. 14–19; [Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 9–11). They are sound generated inside the jet and totally reflected by the shear layer ([Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md), p. 19–22). The band in which they propagate brackets the observed screech, edge and near-nozzle tones ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), p. 10; [Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md), p. 10–14; [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md), p. 19).
6. **Shocks turn a linear operator into a resonator.** Interaction between the KH wave and the shock cells puts energy at k_kh ± k_s. Global analysis on a mean flow that contains the shocks recovers this and the screech frequency to about 1% ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 11–17; [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 11). A Floquet model shows that the shocks create a KH–guided-mode saddle (absolute instability) within 2–6% of the screech frequency ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 13–15).
7. **Weak azimuthal non-uniformity selects flapping.** A second jet, an elliptic cross-section or weak |m| = 2 streaks split the degenerate m = ±1 modes into flapping modes ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 10–12; [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 9–11; [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 17–18).

## Why mean-flow models work at all

The empirical case came first. [Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md) matched PSE to a POD-filtered near-field array (0.2 < St < 0.65, m = 0–2, cold and hot). [Cavalieri 2013](../notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md) matched it to time-resolved cross-plane PIV, with shape agreement |β| ≥ 0.9 between x/D ≈ 2 and the end of the core (p. 583). Both fail below St ≈ 0.2. Gudmundsson & Colonius blamed non-parallel effects (p. 110); Cavalieri could not choose between non-parallel and nonlinear causes (p. 575). The later answer, the Orr mechanism, is under *Resolvent* below.

[McKeon & Sharma 2010](../notes/mckeon-2010-critical-layer-framework-turbulent-pipe-flow.md) gave the formal basis. The nonlinear terms become forcing of the operator linearised about the measured mean, with no eddy viscosity (p. 8). The response is low rank, σ₁ ≈ 10σ₂ (p. 13), and peaks at the critical layer, where the phase speed equals the local mean velocity. [Oberleithner 2011](../notes/oberleithner-2011-swirling-jet-vortex-breakdown-stability.md) showed the same mean-flow LSA also reproduces a *self-excited* global mode, the St = 0.49 precessing helix of a swirling jet. Wavelength, radial shape and growth and decay all match, except at the wavemaker (p. 26–29).

The reviews add caveats. A mean flow is not an equilibrium, so its eigenvalues are a model rather than a stability statement ([Taira 2017](../notes/taira-2017-modal-analysis-fluid-flows-overview.md), p. 27–28; [Taira 2020](../notes/taira-2020-modal-analysis-applications-outlook.md), p. 7–8). [Rolandi 2025](../notes/rolandi-2025-biglobal-resolvent-naca0012.md) quantifies the risk: mean-flow eigenvalues miss the airfoil shedding frequency by 5.5% and 13.4%, worse at stronger nonlinearity (p. 17), and some are stable while the flow oscillates (p. 9–10). Weak-shock screech does far better, at about 1% ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 11–12).

## Local stability, vortex sheets and Floquet extensions

**Vortex sheets** are the cheapest model. They give guided-mode bands ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md); [Mancinelli 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md); [Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md)), twin-jet coupling ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md); [Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md)) and the convective-Mach-number collapse of compressible shear-layer growth ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md), p. 14–15). Their known faults:
- The vortex sheet stabilises completely above a critical M_c of about 1.5–2.1, whereas a finite-thickness layer never does. Vortex-sheet KH growth at high M_c is therefore unreliable ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md), p. 14–15, 22–23).
- Finite thickness lowers the KH phase speed and makes the wave more dispersive, so the fitted reflection phase changes ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), p. 9–11).
- Cooling the jet raises the guided-mode band and thickening the shear layer lowers it. The isothermal vortex sheet of Mancinelli 2019 fitted A1/A2 only because these effects cancel (p. 15–16, 25–26). Thickness matters below R/θ ≈ 30 (p. 16–17).

**Floquet in x** handles the periodicity of the shock cells. In [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) a 2% sinusoidal shock barely moves the eigenvalues (< 0.01%, p. 8) but creates a KH–guided-mode saddle. The saddle frequency is insensitive to shock amplitude, while its growth rate ω₀,ᵢ depends strongly on shear-layer thickness δ (p. 15–16). The growth rates are not quantitative: the KH growth had to be cut to 20% to compare mode shapes (p. 11–12). [Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) feeds in the two measured shock wavenumbers. Its saddles fall within 10% of A1 and A2 (p. 12), and ω₀,ᵢ ranks A1 above A2 at M_j = 1.12 (0.223 vs 0.057; p. 13). The model over-predicts A2 at M_j = 1.22, where the measured tone is weak (p. 14).

**Floquet in azimuth** covers cross-sections that are not round:
- *Twin jets.* The four m = 1 KH symmetries have nearly equal growth rates and phase speeds, so the upstream guided-mode bands decide the coupling (1SS). The upstream wavenumber matches PIV within 1%; KH matches within 5.6% and its shape comes out too narrow ([Nogueira & Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), p. 15–21).
- *Elliptic jets.* KH predictions hold whether the base flow is a PIV fit or RANS. The guided-mode band is narrow (ΔSt ≤ 0.09) and moves with shear-layer thickness. The mode is neutral at the screech frequency only over a short x band, and the measured upstream-wave peak sits inside it ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 18, 22–24).
- *Twin jets without shocks.* Preferred-mode maps show stripes at constant St·s/D, with SA1 sinuous flapping favoured ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 12–13). As spacing changes, the SA guided band moves and the SS band hardly does ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 11, 14–15). When the jets merge, the mode becomes a higher-order mode of an equivalent AR ≈ 2 jet ([Stavropoulos 2024](../notes/stavropoulos-2024-upstream-waves-merging-twin-jets.md), p. 6–13).

All of these are locally parallel. They give wavenumbers, shapes and bands, not growth integrated along x or receptivity ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 24–26).

## Guided-jet waves and trapped acoustic modes

The idea goes back to Tam & Hu (1989, not in the library). Two companion papers on an M_j = 0.9 LES made it concrete.
- [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) used linearised Euler, vortex-sheet and soft-duct models. They give duct-like k± modes and an upstream "discrete free-stream" k⁻ mode (p. 14–15). Two neutral saddles, S1 and S2, bound the band where k⁺ and k⁻ coexist (p. 16–18), and that band brackets the near-nozzle tones (p. 19). The three models agree on S1 within about 4% (p. 16–17). In a weakly non-parallel (WKB) view the saddles act as turning points, which gives five resonance loops (p. 25–28). Tones exist only for 0.82 < M < 1 in isothermal jets (p. 31–33).
- [Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md) found the same waves as lightly damped global modes labelled (m, n_r, n_x). The least damped is (0, 1, 1) at St ≈ 0.37 (p. 8). Rebuilding a dispersion relation from the global modes reproduces the LES frequency–wavenumber bands (p. 16–17), and the modes explain tones that had been blamed on the rig (p. 12–13).

The same family closes other loops:
- *Jet–edge tones.* KH plus k_TH⁻ (M ≤ 0.82) or k_p⁻ (M > 0.82) reproduce the "frequency squeezing" of the tones. Wave trapping sets the high-St cut-off (p. 9–14). The free-stream-sound loop fits neither the spacing nor the Mach trend ([Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md), p. 4–5). Above M ≈ 0.82, loops through k⁻_d and k⁻_p compete ([Stavropoulos 2026](../notes/stavropoulos-2026-jet-edge-interaction.md), not tagged, p. 15–18).
- *Screech.* The upstream wave extracted from PIV matches the (0, 2) vortex-sheet eigenfunction inside the core but decays too slowly outside it, and every A1/A2 tone lies inside its cut-on band ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), p. 9–10). The authors stress that the band test, not the shape match, is decisive, because free-stream sound has similar eigenfunctions (p. 10). Prediction models built on this follow in [Mancinelli 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) and [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md); see [screech](screech.md).

[Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) then explained what the wave is. Waves generated inside the jet are totally reflected by the shear layer. Fitting a standing wave between the two shear layers recovers the double-vortex-sheet dispersion relation exactly (p. 19–22). Sound from outside cannot excite the wave (p. 14). The net energy flux sets its upstream direction and roughly locates the saddles (p. 23–28). For m = 1 the branch point does not follow the hard-duct rule (p. 30). The paper also argues that screech always needs the guided mode, whereas impingement needs it only when the upstream source sits in the core (p. 31–33).

Phase-only resonance models over-predict. With finite thickness they produce a tone for every integer p from 1 to 7 (p. 17). A complex-ω model with a fitted |R₁R₂|(St, M_j) removes most spurious tones, but the fit uses the data it predicts ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), p. 17–21). The complex model of [Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md) is fragile near saddle points (p. 20–24). Resolvent analysis amplifies trapped and Tam & Hu waves more strongly than they appear in SPOD of free jets ([Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 21–25), and slightly over-predicts the trapped-mode spike at St ≈ 0.4 ([Pickering 2020](../notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md), p. 29; cf. [Taira 2017](../notes/taira-2017-modal-analysis-fluid-flows-overview.md), p. 34).

## Global stability: amplifier or oscillator

For incompressible model jets every global mode is stable and none stands out ([Garnaud 2013](../notes/garnaud-2013-preferred-mode-incompressible-jets.md), p. 5). The compressible M 0.9 jet adds discrete trapped branches, still all stable ([Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 8).

On a PIV mean flow of a screeching jet, the least-stable mode sits at St = 0.667 against 0.67 measured, with wavenumbers about 0.5/D too high ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 11–12, 16, 19). [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) found St = 0.72 against 0.714. That eigenvalue is slightly stable, so screech must be sustained by forcing (p. 11, 14). Farghadan also found screech-like modes at St 0.66 and 0.77 that use different shock wavenumbers, and a duct/guided resonance at St 0.31 (p. 11–17). The PIV-derived mean flow limits the analysis to weak shocks (NPR 2.10 only in Edgington-Mitchell 2021, p. 10).

Viscosity is a free parameter, and its value matters. In [Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md) the trapped modes disappear at Re = 10³ and converge only above about 5 × 10⁴ (p. 25–27). Yet both screech studies used Re = 10³ as an eddy-viscosity surrogate and still found guided and duct waves. When the mean flow has unstable eigenvalues, a discounted resolvent with a finite time horizon is the usual workaround ([Rolandi 2025](../notes/rolandi-2025-biglobal-resolvent-naca0012.md), p. 10; [Taira 2020](../notes/taira-2020-modal-analysis-applications-outlook.md), p. 19).

## Resolvent analysis and the forcing problem

**What it explains.** With the nozzle included, the optimal response is KH fed by Orr-type forcing inside the nozzle boundary layer, and the suboptimals are free-jet Orr packets ([Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 9–15; cf. [Garnaud 2013](../notes/garnaud-2013-preferred-mode-incompressible-jets.md), p. 8–9). Lesshafft's local projection contradicts Garnaud's reading of the downstream wavepacket as a jet-column mode (p. 13). [Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md) separates KH (c_ph ≈ 0.8U_j) from Orr (≈ 0.4U_j, on the critical layer) at M_j 0.4–1.5 and recovers Mach-wave radiation at M_j 1.5 (p. 9–13, 21–23).

Lift-up is the third mechanism. A local resolvent at ω = 0 reproduces the radial shape of m = 7 and 10 streak SPOD modes, but only the peak position for m = 3 ([Nogueira 2019](../notes/nogueira-2019-large-scale-streaky-structures-jets.md), p. 17–18). A global eddy-viscosity resolvent maps all three mechanisms, and the dominant streak m falls roughly as 1/x ([Pickering 2020](../notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md), p. 21–22). For rectangular jets, a cross-plane resolvent predicts dipole AS/SA streaks and indicates that corner vortices are not linear streaks (p. 13–17). Its shear-thickness trend flips with the forcing weighting, and nothing has yet been validated against data ([Marshall 2024](../notes/marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets.md), p. 17–19, 23–24).

**How well it matches.**
- SPOD and resolvent modes coincide only when the resolvent expansion coefficients are uncorrelated. With deterministic coefficients the model is rank one, whatever the number of modes kept ([Towne 2018](../notes/towne-2018-spod-relationship-dmd-resolvent.md), p. 18–21). Under coherent forcing, only the leading mode survives when σ₁ ≫ σ₂ ([Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 22).
- Against experiment, the leading SPOD mode is matched for St 0.4–0.7 and the optimal response alone is enough (p. 19–22). SPOD mode 2 is missed at every St, and the model's eigenvalue spectrum peaks at St 0.6 instead of 0.7 ([Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 20–21).
- Projections of resolvent onto SPOD modes are 0.94 for KH but 0.23 for low-St lift-up at (m, St) = (3, 0.05) ([Pickering 2020](../notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md), p. 17). The public M 0.9 database shows the same pattern: agreement at St 0.4–1, failure at 0.2 ([Towne 2022](../notes/towne-2022-database-reduced-complexity-modeling.md), not tagged, p. 6–7).

**Forcing colour and eddy viscosity.**
- [Morra 2021](../notes/morra-2021-colour-of-forcing-resolvent-channel.md) computed the true forcing in channel DNS. Passed through the resolvent it recovers the response exactly. The forcing is rank two, projects mainly onto suboptimal input modes, and its components cancel one another. An eddy viscosity helps because its equivalent forcing roughly reproduces those projections, not because it models the physics (p. 11–24).
- For jets, an adjoint-optimised eddy-viscosity field raises low-St alignment from about 10% to 80% and KH alignment to 96%. A RANS k–ε viscosity scaled by c = 0.2 comes close for St 0.05–1, m = 0–5 and M_j 0.4–1.5 (p. 14–17, 23). Alignment is worst where SPOD eigenvalues overlap within their confidence bands, and at M_j 1.5 (p. 24–25). Uniform linear damping does clearly worse ([Pickering 2021](../notes/pickering-2021-optimal-eddy-viscosity-resolvent-jets.md), p. 28).
- The other jet studies used a uniform Re: 3 × 10⁴ in [Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), where suboptimal gains scale as Re^1.2 and so Re decides where Orr overtakes KH (p. 14–15), and 10³ in the screech studies above. [Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md) shows that a non-smooth PIV-derived base flow can create spurious suboptimal modes (p. 11–12).

## Marching methods: PSE, PFE and OWNS

PSE march downstream and keep a single mode, so they cannot carry upstream waves. They miss Orr packets at low St because the most unstable local wave is only a subdominant resolvent mode there ([Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 23–24). Volume forcing in the free shear layer recovers the low-St growth near the nozzle that inflow-forced PSE miss ([Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 24–25). PSE wavepackets are still good enough for BBSAN. Fully coherent PSE sources put the n = 1 and n = 2 peaks within ±2 dB/St, and adding LES coherence decay (jitter) recovers up to 15 dB/St between the peaks ([Wong 2021](../notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), p. 20–25).

The parabolised Floquet equations (PFE) of [Nogueira 2022, PFE](../notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md) add shock harmonics α ± jα_s. In shock-containing jets they give a longer envelope than shock-free PSE, which decay too fast (p. 18–19). They cannot carry upstream waves, so screech frequencies are excluded (p. 9, 13), and their minimum step grows as M_j rises (p. 6–7). [Zhu & Towne 2021](../notes/zhu-2021-recursive-one-way-navier-stokes.md) (OWNS-R) keeps every downstream mode, including acoustics and Orr waves, at about 3× the per-step cost of PSE, but removes upstream modes by construction (p. 24–26).

## Shock cells, triads and the harmonic resolvent

Tam & Tanna proposed the product picture, in which KH × shock gives k ± λ_n ([Tam & Tanna 1982](../notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md), not tagged, p. 9). It was confirmed experimentally with a linear operator: once the shocks are part of the mean flow, the screech loop is linear except for the KH–shock interaction ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 15–19, 24).

[Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) goes beyond single-frequency models. The resolvent screech mode matches the PIV POD pair (inner product 0.89; p. 17). Adding that mode to the mean flow as a periodic base flow, a bilinear harmonic resolvent reproduces the shock-cell correction to the mean and the sideline beams at 2St_s and 3St_s, which the standard resolvent misses (p. 20–23). The screech mode's self-interaction forcing gives responses aligned above 0.95 frequency by frequency. The concatenated alignment is only 0.36, because the SVD picks inter-frequency phases that the real self-interaction does not have (p. 24–25). The harmonic resolvent thus treats the triads that the standard resolvent lumps into one forcing term ([Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 9). Steady streaks enter the same way, as a zero-frequency partner that couples m = ±1 ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 7–8).

## Disagreements and open questions

**What selects the screech frequency?** Four views compete:
- A phase-and-gain loop with reflection coefficients at the nozzle and the shock ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md)).
- An absolute instability that needs no nozzle; the nozzle sets only the amplitude ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 22).
- The "weakest link" of lip receptivity ([Tam 1986](../notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), not tagged, p. 9–10). [Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) argues this is essentially the saddle condition (p. 14–15).
- The least-stable global mode ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md)).

*To settle:* vary lip thickness at a fixed mean flow and check whether frequency or only amplitude moves, and measure R₁ and R₂ by projecting LES fields onto k± eigenfunctions (Mancinelli 2021, p. 22).

**Oscillator or not?** The Floquet model gives ω₀,ᵢ > 0, while the global mean-flow screech mode is slightly stable ([Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 14). The first uses a model base flow and the second a measured mean. Mean flows of saturated oscillators are expected to be close to marginal (not in the library; cf. [Rolandi 2025](../notes/rolandi-2025-biglobal-resolvent-naca0012.md), p. 9–10). *To settle:* run SP-LSA on the measured mean, or apply the Landau and lock-in tests of [Oberleithner 2011](../notes/oberleithner-2011-swirling-jet-vortex-breakdown-stability.md) (p. 9–11) to screech, using NPR as the control parameter.

**Which shock wavenumber closes which stage?** Four answers are on the table:
- A1 uses k_s1 and A2 uses a secondary k_s2 ([Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 7–14; [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), not tagged).
- The stages differ only by the integer p at one reflection point; App. B of [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) links this to the k_s view (p. 24).
- [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) argues the first suboptimal peak is hidden inside the primary peak (p. 16–17).
- A BMD-derived k_S matches k_kh − k_GJM better than either peak ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), not tagged, p. 20–22).

*To settle:* resolve the shock spectrum over a long field of view and run BMD on time-resolved schlieren across a staging sweep.

**Do shocks modulate the KH envelope directly?** PFE says yes: the harmonics are slaved to KH and lengthen the envelope ([Nogueira 2022, PFE](../notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 12, 18–19). [Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) (p. 20–23) and [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) (p. 16) see superposed separate waves instead. The PFE authors concede they cannot yet separate negative-wavenumber modulation from upstream waves (p. 21). *To settle:* a Floquet OWNS model that carries upstream waves, compared with k_x-filtered SPOD at frequencies away from screech.

**Eddy viscosity for screeching jets.** Re = 10³ is used routinely, although it removes trapped modes in subsonic jets ([Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 25–27). The RANS model of Pickering 2021 has not been tested on shock-containing flows. *To settle:* repeat the screech global and resolvent analyses with a c ≈ 0.2 RANS viscosity and compare the damping of the guided and duct modes.

**Flapping axis and helicity.** The LSA makes flapping the more unstable KH mode, but the LES matches the wagging orientation, which the authors attribute to closure. Whether energy flows from screech to the streaks or the reverse is open ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 21, 25). Likewise, KH growth maps favour SA1 near s/D = 3 ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md)), yet 1SS is observed there ([Nogueira & Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md)). *To settle:* forced LES, and cross-plane measurements that can tell the symmetries apart.

## Methods and data

- **Inputs.** The models need only a mean flow, so non-time-resolved PIV is enough. Recipes:
  - Gaussian or tanh fits with Crocco–Busemann temperature ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md); [Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 3–4).
  - Extrapolated PIV with constant stagnation density checked against BOS ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 10).

  Shear-layer thickness controls the guided band and the growth rates more than shock strength does ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md); [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md)).
- **Validation data.**
  - *Time-resolved* (schlieren, microphones, LES): SPOD modes and k–St maps ([Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md)).
  - *Snapshot PIV*: a POD-pair complex mode, split by the sign of k_x, at a tone fixed by a microphone ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)). The POD pair can also calibrate the amplitude of a periodic base flow ([Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 18).
- **Comparison metrics.**
  - The β projection ([Cavalieri 2013](../notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), eq. 4.11).
  - Alignment |ψ₁*Wu₁|; values above about 0.4 mean qualitative agreement ([Pickering 2021](../notes/pickering-2021-optimal-eddy-viscosity-resolvent-jets.md), p. 14).
  - SPOD confidence intervals, checked before comparing.
  - The direct–adjoint product, which isolates trapped waves ([Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 18–19).
- **Cost.** SP-LSA takes about 3 min on a laptop ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 6). The harmonic resolvent with RSVD-Δt takes under 2 h on 200 cores ([Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 18–19). OWNS-R costs about 3× PSE per step ([Zhu & Towne 2021](../notes/zhu-2021-recursive-one-way-navier-stokes.md), p. 26).

## Relevance to our work

- **Lab screech, impinging and non-axisymmetric data.**
  - Overlay finite-thickness guided-mode bands, built from measured R/θ and temperature, on St–NPR maps.
  - Split SPOD or POD-pair modes by the sign of k_x, and check the upstream peak against the band, since the mode exists only over a short x range ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)).
  - Record lip thickness: the fitted reflection phases differ between rigs with 15% and 3% lips ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 16).
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Every model here is stationary and single-frequency, so none says anything about intermittency or switching. Two testable links to instantaneous amplitudes from HPOD near a staging boundary: the ω₀,ᵢ ranking of A1 over A2 ([Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 13), and coexisting screech-like eigenvalues on k_s1, k_s2 and k_s3 ([Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md)). The linear models fix the spatial shapes; HPOD tracks their time variation.
- **PyFR staging LES.** The LES mean flow can supply θ_R(x), shock positions and the k_s spectrum for local, SP-LSA and global models. The k± projections could estimate the R₁R₂ that [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) had to fit (p. 22). Comparing ω₀,ᵢ or the loop ω_i with the measured 1/t_switch is our inference, not tested in any paper.
- **Rocket noise.** For supersonic jets one coherent wavepacket per frequency dominates Mach-wave radiation, and no volume forcing is needed ([Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 16, 26). The M_j 1.5 resolvent reproduces that radiation ([Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 21–23). This supports wavepacket source models, but every jet tested is cold or isothermal; see [rocket-noise models](rocket-noise-models.md).
