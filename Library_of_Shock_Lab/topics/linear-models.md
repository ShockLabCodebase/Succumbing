# Linear models of jet dynamics

_Last updated: 2026-10-21 · 43 papers_

## Scope

Linear models built on a jet's base flow, usually its turbulent mean: local spatial stability and vortex sheets, Floquet analyses (periodic in x or in azimuth), guided-jet-wave theory, global stability, resolvent analysis and its forcing and eddy-viscosity problem, parabolised and one-way marching (PSE, PFE, OWNS), and the harmonic resolvent. The page sorts them by model family and by what they explain (KH wavepackets, Orr packets, lift-up streaks, trapped and guided acoustic waves, shock-cell modulation) and records where they match experiment or LES and where they fail.

Applications are only linked here: closure, staging and frequency prediction in [screech](screech.md); flapping and coupling in [non-axisymmetric and twin jets](non-axisymmetric-and-twin-jets.md); BBSAN and Mach-wave source models in [jet-noise mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md); the SPOD and POD used to test the models in [modal decomposition](modal-decomposition.md); edge and impingement loops in [impinging jets and edge tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md). Two classic reviews supply the theory: local spatial stability ([Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md)) and absolute/convective, local/global instability ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md)). Five non-jet papers underpin the methods: pipe ([McKeon & Sharma 2010](../notes/mckeon-2010-critical-layer-framework-turbulent-pipe-flow.md)), channel ([Morra 2021](../notes/morra-2021-colour-of-forcing-resolvent-channel.md)), airfoil ([Rolandi 2025](../notes/rolandi-2025-biglobal-resolvent-naca0012.md)) and the two Taira reviews.

## Current understanding

1. **Linearising about the turbulent mean works, but only statistically.** PSE on measured mean flows reproduce the averaged phase and envelope of wavepackets for St ≈ 0.2–0.9, m = 0–2, up to the end of the potential core, with one free amplitude per (m, ω) ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md), p. 109–113; [Cavalieri 2013](../notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), p. 574–585). Single realisations need not follow the model (Gudmundsson & Colonius, p. 122–123). The envelope's saturation needs no nonlinearity: jet divergence alone caps the linear gain ([Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), p. 190–191).
2. **Free jets are amplifiers.** No isolated, weakly damped global mode exists. The "preferred mode" (St ≈ 0.43–0.46) is a pseudo-resonance of a non-normal operator ([Garnaud 2013](../notes/garnaud-2013-preferred-mode-incompressible-jets.md), p. 5, 8, 11), not the weakly damped global mode once proposed ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 501–502). Without shocks, only low-density jets go absolutely unstable (round below ρ_j/ρ∞ = 0.72, planar below 0.90), and only with thin laminar layers at low M; at higher Re and M they revert to amplifiers (p. 511–512), so heated turbulent jets stay convective ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md), p. 111). The resolvent is low rank (σ₁ ≫ σ₂) for roughly 0.2–0.3 ≲ St ≲ 2 at m = 0 and 1, where the optimal response is the KH wavepacket and matches the leading SPOD mode ([Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 6, 13, 16–17; [Towne 2018](../notes/towne-2018-spod-relationship-dmd-resolvent.md), p. 35; [Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 19–20).
3. **Three linear mechanisms organise broadband jet turbulence.** KH dominates at moderate St and low m (c_ph ≈ 0.8U_j). Orr takes over for m = 0 below St ≈ 0.3 and in suboptimal modes (c_ph ≈ 0.4, downstream of the core). Lift-up streaks dominate for St/m ≲ 0.1 ([Pickering 2020](../notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md), p. 15–21; [Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 8–13; [Nogueira 2019](../notes/nogueira-2019-large-scale-streaky-structures-jets.md), p. 13–19).
4. **Most failures come from the forcing, not the operator.** Real nonlinear forcing is coloured and projects onto suboptimal modes ([Towne 2018](../notes/towne-2018-spod-relationship-dmd-resolvent.md), p. 18–21; [Morra 2021](../notes/morra-2021-colour-of-forcing-resolvent-channel.md), p. 20–22). An eddy viscosity repairs much of this ([Pickering 2021](../notes/pickering-2021-optimal-eddy-viscosity-resolvent-jets.md), p. 14–17), but the forcing amplitudes are still not uniform (p. 26–27).
5. **The jet is also an acoustic waveguide.** Upstream guided-jet waves and duct-like trapped waves are discrete, near-neutral modes of vortex-sheet, finite-thickness and global operators ([Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md), p. 14–19; [Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 9–11): sound generated inside the jet and totally reflected by the shear layer ([Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md), p. 19–22). Their band brackets the observed screech, edge and near-nozzle tones ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), p. 10; [Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md), p. 10–14; [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md), p. 19). In rectangular jets the flapping tone's measured upstream peak sits near k_KH − k_sh1 ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4218), although the planar vortex-sheet band misses hot underexpanded tones ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 26). Shear-layer thickness and temperature move the band more than shock strength does ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), p. 15–17; [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4220).
6. **Shocks turn a linear operator into a resonator.** Interaction between the KH wave and the shock cells puts energy at k_kh ± k_s. Global analysis on a mean flow that contains the shocks recovers this and the screech frequency to about 1% ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 11–17; [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 11). A Floquet model shows that the shocks create a KH–guided-mode saddle (absolute instability) within 2–6% of the screech frequency ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 13–15). Such a local pocket is necessary but not sufficient for a self-excited global mode ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 492–495).
7. **Weak azimuthal non-uniformity selects flapping.** A second jet, an elliptic cross-section or weak |m| = 2 streaks split the degenerate m = ±1 modes into flapping modes ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 10–12; [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 9–11; [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 17–18).

## Why mean-flow models work at all

Local theory came first. [Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md) built inviscid spatial LSA of round jets with Crocco–Busemann temperature and the tanh "profile 2" that the group's LSA still uses (p. 161–164). As θ/R grows from 0.05 to 0.4, the most amplified St falls from about 0.75 to 0.21 (m = 0) and 0.11 (m = 1), and St < 0.35 stays unstable over the whole core (p. 169–170). Mach number stabilises m = 0 more than m = 1 in thick layers, and heating destabilises (p. 174–177). A flight stream acts as an axial stretch, and the curves collapse with σ = 1 + 1.4U∞/ΔU (p. 178–181). Slowly diverging theory caps the gain at St 0.5 at about 90, 57 and 26 for m = 0, 1 and 2, a saturation once blamed on nonlinearity (p. 190–191). In that theory the local phase speed depends on radius and on the variable used (p. 189–190), and whether locally parallel phase speeds mean anything was disputed between Crighton & Gaster and Morris & Tam (p. 192). PSE, which march the full eigenfunction, later made that dispute moot in practice (our reading).

The empirical case followed. [Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md) matched PSE to a POD-filtered near-field array (0.2 < St < 0.65, m = 0–2, cold and hot). [Cavalieri 2013](../notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md) matched it to time-resolved cross-plane PIV, with |β| ≥ 0.9 between x/D ≈ 2 and the end of the core (p. 583). Both fail below St ≈ 0.2, blamed on non-parallel effects (Gudmundsson & Colonius, p. 110) or left open between non-parallel and nonlinear causes (Cavalieri, p. 575). The later answer, the Orr mechanism, is under *Resolvent* below.

[McKeon & Sharma 2010](../notes/mckeon-2010-critical-layer-framework-turbulent-pipe-flow.md) gave the formal basis. The nonlinear terms become forcing of the operator linearised about the measured mean, with no eddy viscosity (p. 8). The response is low rank, σ₁ ≈ 10σ₂ (p. 13), and peaks at the critical layer. [Oberleithner 2011](../notes/oberleithner-2011-swirling-jet-vortex-breakdown-stability.md) showed the same mean-flow LSA also reproduces a *self-excited* global mode, the St = 0.49 precessing helix of a swirling jet: wavelength, radial shape and growth and decay all match, except at the wavemaker (p. 26–29).

The reviews add caveats. A mean flow is not an equilibrium, so its eigenvalues are a model rather than a stability statement ([Taira 2017](../notes/taira-2017-modal-analysis-fluid-flows-overview.md), p. 27–28; [Taira 2020](../notes/taira-2020-modal-analysis-applications-outlook.md), p. 7–8). [Rolandi 2025](../notes/rolandi-2025-biglobal-resolvent-naca0012.md) quantifies the risk: mean-flow eigenvalues miss the airfoil shedding frequency by 5.5% and 13.4%, worse at stronger nonlinearity (p. 17), and some are stable while the flow oscillates (p. 9–10). Weak-shock screech does far better, at about 1% ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 11–12).

## Local stability, vortex sheets and Floquet extensions

**Vortex sheets** are the cheapest model. They give guided-mode bands ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md); [Mancinelli 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md); [Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md); planar: [Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md)), twin-jet coupling ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md); [Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md)) and the convective-Mach-number collapse of compressible shear-layer growth ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md), p. 14–15). Their known faults:
- The vortex sheet stabilises completely above a critical M_c of about 1.5–2.1, whereas a finite-thickness layer never does, so vortex-sheet KH growth at high M_c is unreliable ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md), p. 14–15, 22–23).
- Finite thickness lowers the KH phase speed and makes the wave more dispersive, so the fitted reflection phase changes ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), p. 9–11).
- Cooling the jet raises the guided-mode band and thickening the shear layer lowers it. The isothermal vortex sheet of Mancinelli 2019 fitted A1/A2 only because these effects cancel (p. 15–16, 25–26). Thickness matters below R/θ ≈ 30 (p. 16–17), and so does which θ: Michalke calls the density-weighted θ_c unsuitable for the velocity layer (p. 162), yet Mancinelli 2021 used it (p. 22–23). For a cold M 1.2 jet the two differ by about 16% ([Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), note's calculation).

**Floquet in x** handles the periodicity of the shock cells. In [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) a 2% sinusoidal shock barely moves the eigenvalues (< 0.01%, p. 8) but creates a KH–guided-mode saddle that meets the Briggs–Bers pinch condition (p. 14). The saddle frequency is insensitive to shock amplitude, while its growth rate ω₀,ᵢ depends strongly on shear-layer thickness δ (p. 15–16). The growth rates are not quantitative: the KH growth had to be cut to 20% to compare mode shapes (p. 11–12). [Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) feeds in the two measured shock wavenumbers. Its saddles fall within 10% of A1 and A2 (p. 12), and ω₀,ᵢ ranks A1 above A2 at M_j = 1.12 (0.223 vs 0.057; p. 13). The model over-predicts A2 at M_j = 1.22, where the measured tone is weak (p. 14).

**Floquet in azimuth** covers cross-sections that are not round:
- *Twin jets.* The four m = 1 KH symmetries have nearly equal growth rates and phase speeds, so the upstream guided-mode bands decide the coupling (1SS). The upstream wavenumber matches PIV within 1%; KH matches within 5.6% and its shape comes out too narrow ([Nogueira & Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), p. 15–21).
- *Elliptic jets.* KH predictions hold whether the base flow is a PIV fit or RANS. The guided-mode band is narrow (ΔSt ≤ 0.09) and moves with shear-layer thickness. The mode is neutral at the screech frequency only over a short x band, and the measured upstream-wave peak sits inside it ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 18, 22–24).
- *Rectangular jets.* A shock-free four-tanh fit to minor- and major-axis PIV reproduces centreline decay, shear-layer growth and axis switching ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4216). On it, flapping is the most unstable KH mode, and the flapping guided mode has the same symmetry but decays far more slowly away from the jet (p. 4219). A weakest-link condition, α_GJM = α_KH − k_sh, with α_KH fixed near the lip, α_GJM varying in x and k_sh measured, predicts the flapping tone within a few per cent at NPR 2.5–4.5. The predicted St falls with x as the thickening layer lowers the guided band, and the predicted region usually lies near the POD-mode peak (p. 4219; NPR 2.9 is the exception, note's reading). A constant-thickness base flow fails above NPR 2.6 (p. 4220). Only flapping is modelled, so the varicose and wagging tones remain unexplained (p. 4219).
- *Twin jets without shocks.* Preferred-mode maps show stripes at constant St·s/D, with SA1 sinuous flapping favoured ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 12–13). As spacing changes, the SA guided band moves and the SS band hardly does ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 11, 14–15). When the jets merge, the mode becomes a higher-order mode of an equivalent AR ≈ 2 jet ([Stavropoulos 2024](../notes/stavropoulos-2024-upstream-waves-merging-twin-jets.md), p. 6–13).

All of these are locally parallel. They give wavenumbers, shapes and bands, not growth integrated along x, receptivity or loop selection ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 24–26; [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4220).

## Guided-jet waves and trapped acoustic modes

The idea goes back to Tam & Hu (1989, not in the library). [Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md) had already met non-KH "irregular" roots and upstream-travelling waves of uncertain meaning (p. 166, 177), which we read as early sightings of the guided family. Two companion papers on an M_j = 0.9 LES made it concrete.
- [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) used linearised Euler, vortex-sheet and soft-duct models. They give duct-like k± modes and an upstream "discrete free-stream" k⁻ mode (p. 14–15). Two neutral saddles, S1 and S2, bound the band where k⁺ and k⁻ coexist (p. 16–18), and that band brackets the near-nozzle tones (p. 19). The three models agree on S1 within about 4% (p. 16–17). In a weakly non-parallel (WKB) view the saddles act as turning points, which gives five resonance loops (p. 25–28). Tones exist only for 0.82 < M < 1 in isothermal jets (p. 31–33).
- [Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md) found the same waves as lightly damped global modes labelled (m, n_r, n_x). The least damped is (0, 1, 1) at St ≈ 0.37 (p. 8). Rebuilding a dispersion relation from the global modes reproduces the LES frequency–wavenumber bands (p. 16–17), and the modes explain tones that had been blamed on the rig (p. 12–13).

The same family closes other loops:
- *Jet–edge tones.* KH plus k_TH⁻ (M ≤ 0.82) or k_p⁻ (M > 0.82) reproduce the "frequency squeezing" of the tones, and wave trapping sets the high-St cut-off (p. 9–14). The free-stream-sound loop fits neither the spacing nor the Mach trend ([Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md), p. 4–5). Above M ≈ 0.82, loops through k⁻_d and k⁻_p compete ([Stavropoulos 2026](../notes/stavropoulos-2026-jet-edge-interaction.md), not tagged, p. 15–18).
- *Round-jet screech.* The upstream wave extracted from PIV matches the (0, 2) vortex-sheet eigenfunction inside the core but decays too slowly outside it, and every A1/A2 tone lies inside its cut-on band ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), p. 9–10). The band test, not the shape match, is decisive, because free-stream sound has similar eigenfunctions (p. 10). Prediction models built on this ([Mancinelli 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md); [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md)) are on the [screech](screech.md) page.
- *Rectangular-jet screech.* A planar vortex sheet gives closed-form lower band limits, equal to the planar branch points of Nogueira 2024. Heating lowers them, just as cooling raises the round-jet band ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 25). The overexpanded (NPR 3) LES tones sit 16–24% above the first antisymmetric (A1) limit at every temperature, and the cold underexpanded tone 2% above it. The hot underexpanded tones fall 8% and 31% below it, as do, by eye, cold experiments above M_j ≈ 1.45 (p. 26; note's arithmetic). The authors blame the infinitely thin sheet but do not compute a thick one (p. 26). The model gives bands only, with no growth or phase condition, and it ignores the major dimension. It therefore cannot tell minor-axis from diagonal flapping, or explain the switch from one to the other with heating (p. 21–24); the oblique-wave bands of [Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) (p. 28–30) are the natural 3-D extension (our reading). The PIV evidence is stronger: the flapping mode's upstream peak is subsonic and sits near k_KH − k_sh1, and the schlieren tone lies close to a faint negative-k guided arc ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4214, 4218).

[Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) then explained what the wave is. Waves generated inside the jet are totally reflected by the shear layer, and a standing wave between the two shear layers recovers the double-vortex-sheet dispersion relation exactly (p. 19–22). Sound from outside cannot excite the wave (p. 14). The net energy flux sets its upstream direction and roughly locates the saddles (p. 23–28). For m = 1 the branch point does not follow the hard-duct rule (p. 30). The paper argues that screech always needs the guided mode, whereas impingement needs it only when the upstream source sits in the core (p. 31–33).

Phase-only resonance models over-predict: with finite thickness they produce a tone for every integer p from 1 to 7 (p. 17). A complex-ω model with a fitted |R₁R₂|(St, M_j) removes most spurious tones, but the fit uses the data it predicts ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), p. 17–21). The complex model of [Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md) is fragile near saddle points (p. 20–24). Resolvent analysis amplifies trapped and Tam & Hu waves more strongly than they appear in SPOD of free jets ([Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 21–25), and slightly over-predicts the trapped-mode spike at St ≈ 0.4 ([Pickering 2020](../notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md), p. 29; cf. [Taira 2017](../notes/taira-2017-modal-analysis-fluid-flows-overview.md), p. 34).

## Global stability: amplifier or oscillator

The framework is [Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md). A parallel profile is absolutely unstable if ω₀,ᵢ > 0 at a saddle that pinches a k⁺ and a k⁻ branch; a saddle of two branches from the same half-plane does not count (p. 482–484). A purely spatial calculation sees the transition as k⁺ and k⁻ swapping (p. 486), which is how the review reads the hot-jet "mode mixing" of [Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md) (p. 177; Table 4, p. 530). Slowly developing flows fall into four classes: locally stable, convective amplifiers, marginally stable flows with weakly damped global modes, and flows with an absolute pocket (p. 488–489). The pocket is necessary but not sufficient for a global mode, whose frequency is set to leading order at a saddle in the *complex* X-plane, with ω_G,i ≤ ω₀,ᵢ(X_s) ≤ ω₀,ᵢ|max (p. 492–495). The inner turning-point problem is a Ginzburg–Landau equation, with a ladder of global modes O(ε) apart (p. 496). The Ginzburg–Landau model of [Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md) shows the gap: absolute at the inlet for A > 1.25, globally unstable only above A ≈ 1.6 (p. 19–21). Pressure feedback from surfaces, as in edge and cavity tones, is another route (p. 498). The turning points of [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) (p. 25–28) and the Floquet saddle of [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) (p. 14) use this machinery.

For incompressible model jets every global mode is stable and none stands out ([Garnaud 2013](../notes/garnaud-2013-preferred-mode-incompressible-jets.md), p. 5). The compressible M 0.9 jet adds discrete trapped branches, still all stable ([Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 8).

On a PIV mean flow of a screeching jet, the least-stable mode sits at St = 0.667 against 0.67 measured, with wavenumbers about 0.5/D too high ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 11–12, 16, 19). [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) found St = 0.72 against 0.714. That eigenvalue is slightly stable, so screech must be sustained by forcing (p. 11, 14). Farghadan also found screech-like modes at St 0.66 and 0.77 that use different shock wavenumbers, and a duct/guided resonance at St 0.31 (p. 11–17). The PIV-derived mean flow limits the analysis to weak shocks (NPR 2.10 only in Edgington-Mitchell 2021, p. 10).

Viscosity is a free parameter, and its value matters. In [Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md) the trapped modes disappear at Re = 10³ and converge only above about 5 × 10⁴ (p. 25–27). Yet both screech studies used Re = 10³ as an eddy-viscosity surrogate and still found guided and duct waves. When the mean flow has unstable eigenvalues, a discounted resolvent with a finite time horizon is the usual workaround ([Rolandi 2025](../notes/rolandi-2025-biglobal-resolvent-naca0012.md), p. 10; [Taira 2020](../notes/taira-2020-modal-analysis-applications-outlook.md), p. 19). Truncated domains can add a spurious outflow-to-inflow "box" resonance (Huerre & Monkewitz, p. 498–499; cf. the outflow-spoiled Ginzburg–Landau eigenvalues of [Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 20–21).

## Resolvent analysis and the forcing problem

**What it explains.** Michalke anticipated the nozzle's role: a bare edge keeps no memory of a passing wave, a lip wall could close a feedback loop, and feedback puts the natural shear-layer frequency (fθ/U_o ≈ 0.012–0.013) below the most amplified one (0.0164) ([Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), p. 194–196). With the nozzle included, the resolvent's optimal response is KH fed by Orr-type forcing inside the nozzle boundary layer, and the suboptimals are free-jet Orr packets ([Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 9–15; cf. [Garnaud 2013](../notes/garnaud-2013-preferred-mode-incompressible-jets.md), p. 8–9). Lesshafft's local projection contradicts Garnaud's reading of the downstream wavepacket as a jet-column mode (p. 13). [Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md) separates KH (c_ph ≈ 0.8U_j) from Orr (≈ 0.4U_j, on the critical layer) at M_j 0.4–1.5 and recovers Mach-wave radiation at M_j 1.5 (p. 9–13, 21–23).

Lift-up is the third mechanism. A local resolvent at ω = 0 reproduces the radial shape of m = 7 and 10 streak SPOD modes, but only the peak position for m = 3 ([Nogueira 2019](../notes/nogueira-2019-large-scale-streaky-structures-jets.md), p. 17–18). A global eddy-viscosity resolvent maps all three mechanisms, and the dominant streak m falls roughly as 1/x ([Pickering 2020](../notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md), p. 21–22). For rectangular jets a cross-plane resolvent predicts dipole AS/SA streaks and suggests corner vortices are not linear streaks (p. 13–17), but its thickness trend flips with the forcing weighting and is unvalidated ([Marshall 2024](../notes/marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets.md), p. 17–19, 23–24).

**How well it matches.**
- SPOD and resolvent modes coincide only when the resolvent expansion coefficients are uncorrelated; with deterministic coefficients the model is rank one, whatever the number of modes kept ([Towne 2018](../notes/towne-2018-spod-relationship-dmd-resolvent.md), p. 18–21). Under coherent forcing, only the leading mode survives when σ₁ ≫ σ₂ ([Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 22).
- Against experiment, the leading SPOD mode is matched for St 0.4–0.7 by the optimal response alone (p. 19–22). SPOD mode 2 is missed at every St, and the model's eigenvalue spectrum peaks at St 0.6 instead of 0.7 ([Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 20–21).
- Projections of resolvent onto SPOD modes are 0.94 for KH but 0.23 for low-St lift-up at (m, St) = (3, 0.05) ([Pickering 2020](../notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md), p. 17). The public M 0.9 database shows the same pattern: agreement at St 0.4–1, failure at 0.2 ([Towne 2022](../notes/towne-2022-database-reduced-complexity-modeling.md), not tagged, p. 6–7).

**Forcing colour and eddy viscosity.**
- [Morra 2021](../notes/morra-2021-colour-of-forcing-resolvent-channel.md) computed the true forcing in channel DNS. Passed through the resolvent it recovers the response exactly. The forcing is rank two, projects mainly onto suboptimal input modes, and its components cancel one another. An eddy viscosity helps because its equivalent forcing roughly reproduces those projections, not because it models the physics (p. 11–24).
- For jets, an adjoint-optimised eddy-viscosity field raises low-St alignment from about 10% to 80% and KH alignment to 96%. A RANS k–ε viscosity scaled by c = 0.2 comes close for St 0.05–1, m = 0–5 and M_j 0.4–1.5 (p. 14–17, 23). Alignment is worst where SPOD eigenvalues overlap within their confidence bands, and at M_j 1.5 (p. 24–25). Uniform linear damping does clearly worse ([Pickering 2021](../notes/pickering-2021-optimal-eddy-viscosity-resolvent-jets.md), p. 28).
- The other jet studies used a uniform Re: 3 × 10⁴ in [Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), where suboptimal gains scale as Re^1.2 and so Re decides where Orr overtakes KH (p. 14–15), and 10³ in the screech studies above. A non-smooth PIV-derived base flow can create spurious suboptimal modes ([Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 11–12).

## Marching methods: PSE, PFE and OWNS

PSE march downstream and keep a single mode, so they cannot carry upstream waves. They miss Orr packets at low St because the most unstable local wave is only a subdominant resolvent mode there ([Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 23–24). Volume forcing in the free shear layer recovers the low-St growth near the nozzle that inflow-forced PSE miss ([Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 24–25).

PSE wavepackets are still good enough for BBSAN. [Ray 2005](../notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md) first drove a linearised Euler solver with products of a PSE wave and zero-frequency shock modes on a RANS mean (p. 2–3). At St 0.6 the upstream lobe sits at the local Mach angle of k_t − k_s, and a locally parallel Green's function matches the solver in level and direction (p. 9). At St 0.9 the wave stays in the shear layer, the sources become rings, and interference splits the radiation into narrow lobes whose angles, but not amplitudes, the Green's function reproduces (p. 9–10, 12–13). Level and directivity thus depend on the wavepacket's radial shape and envelope length, not only on its wavenumber (p. 5–7). The forced solver also radiates Mach waves from KH waves it excites itself (p. 7). The study is preliminary and has no comparison with measurements (p. 13). Fully coherent PSE sources put the n = 1 and n = 2 peaks within ±2 dB/St, and adding LES coherence decay (jitter) recovers up to 15 dB/St between the peaks ([Wong 2021](../notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), p. 20–25).

The parabolised Floquet equations (PFE) of [Nogueira 2022, PFE](../notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md) add shock harmonics α ± jα_s. In shock-containing jets they give a longer envelope than shock-free PSE, which decay too fast (p. 18–19); given Ray's envelope dependence, this matters for BBSAN. PFE cannot carry upstream waves, so screech frequencies are excluded (p. 9, 13), and their minimum step grows as M_j rises (p. 6–7). [Zhu & Towne 2021](../notes/zhu-2021-recursive-one-way-navier-stokes.md) (OWNS-R) keeps every downstream mode, including acoustics and Orr waves, at about 3× the per-step cost of PSE, but removes upstream modes by construction (p. 24–26).

## Shock cells, triads and the harmonic resolvent

Tam & Tanna proposed the product picture, in which KH × shock gives k ± λ_n ([Tam & Tanna 1982](../notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md), not tagged, p. 9). It was confirmed experimentally with a linear operator: once the shocks are part of the mean flow, the screech loop is linear except for the KH–shock interaction ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 15–19, 24).

[Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) goes beyond single-frequency models. The resolvent screech mode matches the PIV POD pair (inner product 0.89; p. 17). Adding that mode to the mean flow as a periodic base flow, a bilinear harmonic resolvent reproduces the shock-cell correction to the mean and the sideline beams at 2St_s and 3St_s, which the standard resolvent misses (p. 20–23). The screech mode's self-interaction forcing gives responses aligned above 0.95 frequency by frequency, but the concatenated alignment is only 0.36, because the SVD picks inter-frequency phases that the real self-interaction does not have (p. 24–25). The harmonic resolvent thus treats the triads that the standard resolvent lumps into one forcing term ([Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 9). Steady streaks enter the same way, as a zero-frequency partner that couples m = ±1 ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 7–8).

## Disagreements and open questions

**What selects the screech frequency?** Four views compete:
- A phase-and-gain loop with reflection coefficients at the nozzle and the shock ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md)).
- An absolute instability that needs no nozzle; the nozzle sets only the amplitude ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 22).
- The "weakest link" of lip receptivity ([Tam 1986](../notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), not tagged, p. 9–10), which [Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) argues is essentially the saddle condition (p. 14–15). It also predicts rectangular flapping tones ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4219).
- The least-stable global mode ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md)).

Even linear theory disputes where in an absolute pocket the frequency is set: at the maximum ω₀,ᵢ (Pierrehumbert), at the absolute/convective boundary (Koch), or at the complex-X saddle. A wake simulation favours Koch's boundary, which the review's authors concede "appears to contradict" their saddle ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 493–495, 502). A local saddle frequency is thus a leading-order estimate. *To settle:* vary lip thickness at a fixed mean flow and check whether frequency or only amplitude moves, and measure R₁ and R₂ by projecting LES fields onto k± eigenfunctions (Mancinelli 2021, p. 22).

**Oscillator or not?** The Floquet model gives ω₀,ᵢ > 0, while the global mean-flow screech mode is slightly stable ([Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 14). These need not conflict, since a local absolute pocket does not guarantee a global mode (Huerre & Monkewitz, p. 492–495). The base flows also differ (model vs measured mean), and mean flows of saturated oscillators should be close to marginal (cf. [Rolandi 2025](../notes/rolandi-2025-biglobal-resolvent-naca0012.md), p. 9–10). *To settle:* run SP-LSA on the measured mean, and apply Huerre & Monkewitz's ranked tests with NPR as the control parameter (p. 516–518): lock-in, response against forcing amplitude (slope 1 for an amplifier, flat for an oscillator), saturation, and transient Landau fits, as [Oberleithner 2011](../notes/oberleithner-2011-swirling-jet-vortex-breakdown-stability.md) did (p. 9–11).

**Which shock wavenumber closes which stage?** Four answers are on the table:
- A1 uses k_s1 and A2 uses a secondary k_s2 ([Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 7–14; [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), not tagged). In an AR 2 rectangular jet the dominant flapping tone closes on k_sh1, and a k_sh2 loop is offered for the second flapping tone (p. 4218–4220), though its St also lies near the varicose tone ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), note's reading of Fig. 17).
- The stages differ only by the integer p at one reflection point; App. B of [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) links this to the k_s view (p. 24).
- [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) argues the first suboptimal peak is hidden inside the primary peak (p. 16–17).
- A BMD-derived k_S matches k_kh − k_GJM better than either peak ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), not tagged, p. 20–22).

*To settle:* resolve the shock spectrum over a long field of view and run BMD on time-resolved schlieren across a staging sweep. A cheaper check (our inference): map the local Mach angle of k_t − k_s along x, as [Ray 2005](../notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md) did (p. 9), from SPOD k_t(x) and mean k_s(x); 180° is Tam's closure condition.

**Do shocks modulate the KH envelope directly?** PFE says yes: the harmonics are slaved to KH and lengthen the envelope ([Nogueira 2022, PFE](../notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 12, 18–19). [Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) (p. 20–23) and [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) (p. 16) see superposed separate waves instead. The PFE authors concede they cannot yet separate negative-wavenumber modulation from upstream waves (p. 21). It matters for BBSAN, whose level and directivity depend strongly on envelope length (Ray 2005, p. 5). *To settle:* a Floquet OWNS model that carries upstream waves, compared with k_x-filtered SPOD at frequencies away from screech.

**Do guided-mode bands survive heating and thick layers?** The vortex-sheet band misses hot underexpanded rectangular tones ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 26). Thickening should lower the band (Mancinelli 2021, p. 15–17), as Gojon's authors argue without computing it (p. 26), and a spreading base flow rescues a rectangular prediction that a constant profile misses ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4220). But no finite-thickness model has been run for hot jets, and in round jets cooling and thickening cancel, so a fit can be right for the wrong reason (Mancinelli 2021, p. 15–16). *To settle:* finite-thickness or Floquet LSA on hot-jet LES or PIV means, using the velocity θ rather than θ_c (Michalke 1984, p. 162).

**Eddy viscosity for screeching jets.** Re = 10³ is used routinely, although it removes trapped modes in subsonic jets ([Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 25–27). The RANS model of Pickering 2021 has not been tested on shock-containing flows. *To settle:* repeat the screech global and resolvent analyses with a c ≈ 0.2 RANS viscosity and compare the damping of the guided and duct modes.

**Flapping axis and helicity.** The LSA makes flapping the more unstable KH mode, but the LES matches the wagging orientation, which the authors attribute to closure. Whether energy flows from screech to the streaks or the reverse is open ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 21, 25). Likewise, KH growth maps favour SA1 near s/D = 3 ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md)), yet 1SS is observed there ([Nogueira & Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md)). *To settle:* forced LES, and cross-plane measurements that can tell the symmetries apart.

## Methods and data

- **Inputs.** The models need only a mean flow, so non-time-resolved PIV is enough. Recipes:
  - Gaussian or tanh (Michalke profile 2) fits with Crocco–Busemann temperature ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md); [Lesshafft 2019](../notes/lesshafft-2019-resolvent-modelling-wavepackets-turbulent-jet.md), p. 3–4).
  - Extrapolated PIV with constant stagnation density checked against BOS ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 10).
  - For rectangular jets, a shock-free product of four tanh profiles fitted to both PIV planes ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4212–4213).

  Shear-layer thickness, measured as the velocity θ rather than θ_c, controls the guided band and the growth rates more than shock strength does ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md); [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md); [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md)).
- **Validation data.**
  - *Time-resolved* (schlieren, microphones, LES): SPOD modes and k–St maps ([Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md)). Oscillator tests (lock-in, forcing-amplitude response, transient Landau fits) also need time series ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 516–518).
  - *Snapshot PIV*: a POD-pair complex mode, split by the sign of k_x, at a tone fixed by a microphone ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)). The POD pair can also calibrate the amplitude of a periodic base flow ([Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 18).
- **Comparison metrics.**
  - The β projection ([Cavalieri 2013](../notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), eq. 4.11).
  - Alignment |ψ₁*Wu₁|; values above about 0.4 mean qualitative agreement ([Pickering 2021](../notes/pickering-2021-optimal-eddy-viscosity-resolvent-jets.md), p. 14).
  - SPOD confidence intervals, checked before comparing.
  - The direct–adjoint product, which isolates trapped waves ([Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 18–19).
- **Cost.** SP-LSA takes about 3 min on a laptop ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 6). The harmonic resolvent with RSVD-Δt takes under 2 h on 200 cores ([Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 18–19). OWNS-R costs about 3× PSE per step ([Zhu & Towne 2021](../notes/zhu-2021-recursive-one-way-navier-stokes.md), p. 26).

## Relevance to our work

- **Lab screech, impinging and non-axisymmetric data.**
  - Overlay finite-thickness guided-mode bands, built from measured R/θ and temperature, on St–NPR maps. For AR 2 rectangular jets, Gojon's closed-form limit (eq. 16, p. 25) is a quick first overlay and the PIV-fitted model of Nogueira 2025 the fuller one.
  - Split SPOD or POD-pair modes by the sign of k_x, and check the upstream peak against the band, since the mode exists only over a short x range ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)).
  - Record lip thickness: the fitted reflection phases differ between rigs with 15% and 3% lips ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 16).
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Every model here is stationary and single-frequency, so none says anything about intermittency or switching. Two testable links to instantaneous amplitudes from HPOD near a staging boundary: the ω₀,ᵢ ranking of A1 over A2 ([Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 13), and coexisting screech-like eigenvalues on k_s1, k_s2 and k_s3 ([Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md)). HPOD amplitudes are also what the transient Landau test needs (Huerre & Monkewitz, p. 518). The linear models fix the spatial shapes; HPOD tracks their time variation.
- **PyFR staging LES.** The LES mean flow can supply θ_R(x), shock positions and the k_s spectrum for local, SP-LSA and global models. The k± projections could estimate the R₁R₂ that [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) had to fit (p. 22). The weakest-link recipe of [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md) (fitted spreading mean, α_KH near the lip, α_GJM(x), measured k_s1 and k_s2; p. 4213, 4219) is cheap enough to run at every continuation step and would test whether A1 closes on k_s1 and A2 on k_s2 (our inference). Comparing ω₀,ᵢ or the loop ω_i with the measured 1/t_switch is our inference, not tested in any paper.
- **Rocket noise.** For supersonic jets one coherent wavepacket per frequency dominates Mach-wave radiation, and no volume forcing is needed ([Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 16, 26). The M_j 1.5 resolvent reproduces that radiation ([Schmidt 2018](../notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 21–23). These jets are cold or isothermal. The one heated supersonic case on this page is a BBSAN source model: at T_j/T∞ = 2 the first shock mode barely changes, so heating acts through the instability waves, whose gains it raises ([Ray 2005](../notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md), p. 10). See [rocket-noise models](rocket-noise-models.md).
