# Linear models: local stability and wave models

_Last updated: 2026-10-22 · 39 papers_

## Scope

Linear models that treat a jet's coherent structures as waves on its mean flow: why linearising about the turbulent mean works and how eddy viscosity enters; local spatial stability, vortex sheets and absolute instability; Floquet models periodic in azimuth (non-round and twin jets); guided-jet waves, trapped acoustic modes and the resonance models built on them; wavepackets as models of the near field and of Mach-wave radiation; and the ray (geometrical-acoustics) limit of the KH–shock interaction. The page records where they match experiment or LES and where they fail.

Global stability, resolvent analysis, PSE/PFE/OWNS marching and the harmonic resolvent are on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md). Shock cells and their interaction with wavepackets (Prandtl–Pack, the product picture, Floquet in x, PFE, BBSAN sources) are on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md). Applications are only linked: [screech](screech.md); [screech frequency and global modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md); [screech staging and mode switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md); [non-axisymmetric jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md); [twin-jet coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md); [jet-noise mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md); [impinging jets and edge tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md); SPOD and POD validation in [modal decomposition](modal-decomposition.md). The theory comes from two classic reviews, local spatial stability ([Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md)) and absolute/convective instability ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md)), and the guided-wave family from [Tam & Hu 1989](../notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md).

## Current understanding

1. **Linearising about the turbulent mean works, but only statistically.** PSE on measured mean flows reproduce the averaged phase and envelope of wavepackets for St ≈ 0.2–0.9, m = 0–2, up to the end of the potential core, with one free amplitude per (m, ω) ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md), p. 109–113; [Cavalieri 2013](../notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), p. 574–585). Single realisations need not follow the model (Gudmundsson & Colonius, p. 122–123). The envelope's saturation needs no nonlinearity: jet divergence alone caps the linear gain ([Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), p. 190–191). Locally parallel LSA is cruder near the nozzle of a shock-containing jet: on LES means it gets the fall of the most unstable St within one diameter but not its values ([Karami 2020](../notes/karami-2020-acoustic-hydrodynamic-waves-underexpanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 22–25).
2. **Shock-free jets are convectively unstable.** Only low-density jets go absolutely unstable (round below ρ_j/ρ∞ = 0.72, planar below 0.90), and only with thin laminar layers at low M; at higher Re and M they revert to amplifiers ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 511–512), so heated turbulent jets stay convective ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md), p. 111). Global operators agree ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)).
3. **The jet is also an acoustic waveguide.** Besides KH, a jet carries trapped subsonic waves, and in supersonic jets some of them travel upstream, neutral, in narrow bands along ω/k ≈ −a∞ ([Tam & Hu 1989](../notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 462–463, 475–476). These guided-jet waves and the duct-like trapped waves are discrete, near-neutral modes of vortex-sheet, finite-thickness and global operators ([Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md), p. 14–19; [Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 9–11): sound generated inside the jet and totally reflected by the shear layer ([Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md), p. 19–22). Their bands bracket the observed screech, edge, impingement and near-nozzle tones ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), p. 10; [Gojon 2018](../notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md), p. 2919–2920; [Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md), p. 10–14; [Jaunet 2019](../notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md), p. 7; [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md), p. 19), and LES frequency–wavenumber spectra show them inside supersonic cores ([Bogey & Gojon 2017](../notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 583–585; [Gojon 2018](../notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md), p. 2921). In a rectangular jet the flapping tone's upstream peak sits near k_KH − k_sh1 ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4218). The band moves more with shear-layer thickness and temperature than with shock strength ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), p. 15–17; [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4220). Tones sit mostly near the lower band edge, where the wave travels at about a∞, so frequencies alone barely separate guided from free-stream closure ([Gojon 2018](../notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md), p. 2919–2920; [Bogey & Gojon 2017](../notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 582).
4. **Shocks turn the jet into a resonator.** KH–shock interaction puts energy at k_kh ± k_s, and global analysis on a mean flow that contains the shocks recovers the screech frequency to about 1% ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 11–17; [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 11). A Floquet model shows that the shocks create a KH–guided-mode saddle (absolute instability) within 2–6% of the screech frequency ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 13–15). Such a local pocket is necessary but not sufficient for a self-excited global mode ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 492–495). The shock-cell side is on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md).
5. **Weak azimuthal non-uniformity selects flapping.** A second jet, an elliptic cross-section or weak |m| = 2 streaks split the degenerate m = ±1 modes into flapping modes ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 10–12; [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 9–11; [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 17–18).
6. **Wavepackets are good sound sources.** One coherent wavepacket per frequency dominates supersonic Mach-wave radiation ([Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 16, 26); BBSAN models built on wavepacket × shock-cell products also need the envelope and coherence ([Ray 2005](../notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md); [Wong 2021](../notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md); [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md)).

## Why mean-flow models work at all

Local theory came first. [Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md) built inviscid spatial LSA of round jets with Crocco–Busemann temperature and the tanh "profile 2" that the group's LSA still uses (p. 161–164). As θ/R grows from 0.05 to 0.4, the most amplified St falls from about 0.75 to 0.21 (m = 0) and 0.11 (m = 1), and St < 0.35 stays unstable over the whole core (p. 169–170). Mach number stabilises m = 0 more than m = 1 in thick layers, and heating destabilises (p. 174–177). A flight stream acts as an axial stretch, and the curves collapse with σ = 1 + 1.4U∞/ΔU (p. 178–181). Slowly diverging theory caps the gain at St 0.5 at about 90, 57 and 26 for m = 0, 1 and 2, a saturation once blamed on nonlinearity (p. 190–191). The local phase speed then depends on radius and on the variable used (p. 189–190), and whether locally parallel phase speeds mean anything was disputed between Crighton & Gaster and Morris & Tam (p. 192); PSE, which march the full eigenfunction, made the dispute moot in practice (our reading). Michalke also anticipated the nozzle's role: a bare edge keeps no memory of a passing wave, a lip wall could close a feedback loop, and feedback puts the natural shear-layer frequency (fθ/U_o ≈ 0.012–0.013) below the most amplified one (0.0164) (p. 194–196), as resolvent analyses with the nozzle later confirmed ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)). An impinging-jet LES makes the same point: the local most unstable St falls with x identically for plate distances 2d and 5d, yet the tones differ by a factor of two (fd/a∞ = 0.91 and 0.45), so local stability cannot pick the tone and the loop must ([Karami 2020](../notes/karami-2020-acoustic-hydrodynamic-waves-underexpanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 18, 22–23; note's reading).

The empirical case followed. [Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md) matched PSE to a POD-filtered near-field array (0.2 < St < 0.65, m = 0–2, cold and hot), and [Cavalieri 2013](../notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md) to time-resolved cross-plane PIV, with |β| ≥ 0.9 between x/D ≈ 2 and the end of the core (p. 583). Both fail below St ≈ 0.2, blamed on non-parallel effects (Gudmundsson & Colonius, p. 110) or left open between non-parallel and nonlinear causes (Cavalieri, p. 575). The later answer, the Orr mechanism, came from resolvent analysis ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)).

[McKeon & Sharma 2010](../notes/mckeon-2010-critical-layer-framework-turbulent-pipe-flow.md) gave the formal basis. The nonlinear terms become forcing of the operator linearised about the measured mean, with no eddy viscosity (p. 8). The response is low rank, σ₁ ≈ 10σ₂ (p. 13), and peaks at the critical layer. [Oberleithner 2011](../notes/oberleithner-2011-swirling-jet-vortex-breakdown-stability.md) showed that mean-flow LSA also reproduces a *self-excited* global mode, the St = 0.49 precessing helix of a swirling jet: wavelength, radial shape and growth and decay all match, except at the wavemaker (p. 26–29). A mean flow is still not an equilibrium; what that does to eigenvalues computed on it is on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md).

**Eddy viscosity.** The turbulence hidden in the mean still damps and forces the waves, and most models stand in for it with a uniform Reynolds number whose value matters. The trapped acoustic modes of a subsonic jet vanish at Re = 10³ and converge only above about 5 × 10⁴ ([Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 25–27), yet the screech global analyses used Re = 10³ as a surrogate and still found guided and duct waves ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md); [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md)). A RANS-based eddy viscosity beats uniform damping in the jet resolvent ([Pickering 2021](../notes/pickering-2021-optimal-eddy-viscosity-resolvent-jets.md), p. 28), because its equivalent forcing roughly reproduces the projections of the true forcing, not because it models the physics ([Morra 2021](../notes/morra-2021-colour-of-forcing-resolvent-channel.md), p. 11–24). Michalke's theory and vortex sheets are inviscid; for them the open choice is the base flow, above all the shear-layer thickness.

## Local stability, vortex sheets and absolute instability

**Vortex sheets** are the cheapest model. They give guided-mode bands ([Tam & Hu 1989](../notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md); [Tam & Ahuja 1990](../notes/tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md); [Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md); [Gojon 2018](../notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md); [Mancinelli 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md); [Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md); [Jaunet 2019](../notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md); planar: [Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md)), twin-jet coupling ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md); [Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md)) and the convective-Mach-number collapse of compressible shear-layer growth ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md), p. 14–15). Their known faults:
- KH phase speeds and wavefront angles come out well, growth rates poorly ([Tam & Hu 1989](../notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 467).
- The vortex sheet stabilises completely above a critical M_c of about 1.5–2.1, whereas a finite-thickness layer never does, so vortex-sheet KH growth at high M_c is unreliable ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md), p. 14–15, 22–23). Relatedly, above a critical M_j that falls with jet temperature (≈ 2.8 isothermal, ≈ 2.0 at T_j/T∞ = 3, read from figure) the KH zero merges into the family of supersonic instability waves, trapped Mach waves that exist only for u_j > a_j + a∞ and then dominate ([Tam & Hu 1989](../notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 456–459, 480–481).
- Finite thickness lowers the KH phase speed and makes the wave more dispersive, so the fitted reflection phase changes ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), p. 9–11).
- Cooling the jet raises the guided-mode band and thickening the shear layer lowers it. The isothermal vortex sheet of Mancinelli 2019 fitted A1/A2 only because these effects cancel (p. 15–16, 25–26). Thickness matters below R/θ ≈ 30 (p. 16–17), and so does which θ: Michalke calls the density-weighted θ_c unsuitable for the velocity layer (p. 162), yet Mancinelli 2021 used it (p. 22–23). For a cold M 1.2 jet the two differ by about 16% ([Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), note's calculation).
- Near the nozzle of an underexpanded jet the mean profile is far from tanh, with an off-axis velocity overshoot from the expansion, and the authors of the one local LSA there warn against tanh and vortex-sheet base flows ([Karami 2020](../notes/karami-2020-acoustic-hydrodynamic-waves-underexpanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 22, 26).

**Local LSA on shock-containing means.** [Karami 2020](../notes/karami-2020-acoustic-hydrodynamic-waves-underexpanded-impinging-jets.md) is the library's only local stability analysis inside the first shock cell: an inviscid compressible-Rayleigh, m = 0 calculation (companion-matrix method) on LES means of an NPR 3.4 impinging jet at x = 0.1–0.9d (p. 7–8, 21–23). The most unstable fd/a∞ falls from ≈ 4 (peak −α_i ≈ 18–23) at x = 0.1d to ≈ 0.5 at 0.9d, the same for both plate distances (p. 22–23): Michalke's θ/R trend (p. 169–170) compressed into one diameter. The LES energy-norm spectrum follows the trend but not the values, which the authors blame on non-parallelism, recommending PSE (p. 24–26). The note adds caveats: the comparison sets a local growth-rate peak against energy accumulated from the lip, which by itself pushes the LES peak upward; the abstract says the LSA "overpredicts" where p. 24 says "underpredicts"; the printed cubic coefficients misplace the factor 3 and the shear term; and the paper does not say how it separated the KH branch from Tam & Hu's other families. It makes no guided-mode or absolute-instability analysis.

**Absolute instability** is the local criterion that the Floquet and guided-wave models test. A parallel profile is absolutely unstable if ω₀,ᵢ > 0 at a saddle that pinches a k⁺ and a k⁻ branch; a saddle of two branches from the same half-plane does not count ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 482–484). A purely spatial calculation sees the transition as k⁺ and k⁻ swapping (p. 486), which is how the review reads the hot-jet "mode mixing" of [Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md) (p. 177; Table 4, p. 530). The WKB turning points of [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) (p. 25–28) and the Floquet saddle of [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) (p. 14) use this machinery. Whether a local pocket yields a global mode is on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md).

## Floquet models

**Periodic in x.** The shock train is periodic, and KH × shock puts energy at k ± k_s ([Tam & Tanna 1982](../notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md), not tagged). A Floquet model in x shows that a weak shock train creates a KH–guided-mode saddle, an absolute instability, near the screech frequency ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md)), and with the two measured shock wavenumbers the saddles fall near A1 and A2 ([Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md)). These models, the shock-cell solutions they rest on and the experimental confirmation of the product waves are on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md). A reading from the Tam & Hu note, not a result of any paper: at ω → 0 each (0, m) subsonic branch of the vortex sheet ends at the Pack shock-cell wavenumber, kR_j = j_{0,s}/(M_j² − 1)^{1/2}, so one dispersion branch runs from the stationary shock cell through the downstream subsonic waves to the double zero and the upstream guided wave. That is a compact way to see why k_kh − k_s lands on the guided branch near the saddle ([Tam & Hu 1989](../notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), note's derivation).

**Floquet in azimuth** covers cross-sections that are not round:
- *Twin jets.* The four m = 1 KH symmetries have nearly equal growth rates and phase speeds, so the upstream guided-mode bands decide the coupling (1SS). The upstream wavenumber matches PIV within 1%; KH matches within 5.6% and its shape comes out too narrow ([Nogueira & Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), p. 15–21).
- *Elliptic jets.* KH predictions hold whether the base flow is a PIV fit or RANS. The guided-mode band is narrow (ΔSt ≤ 0.09) and moves with shear-layer thickness. The mode is neutral at the screech frequency only over a short x band, and the measured upstream-wave peak sits inside it ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 18, 22–24).
- *Rectangular jets.* A shock-free four-tanh fit to minor- and major-axis PIV reproduces centreline decay, shear-layer growth and axis switching ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4216). On it, flapping is the most unstable KH mode, and the flapping guided mode has the same symmetry but decays far more slowly away from the jet (p. 4219). A weakest-link condition, α_GJM = α_KH − k_sh, with α_KH fixed near the lip, α_GJM varying in x and k_sh measured, predicts the flapping tone within a few per cent at NPR 2.5–4.5. The predicted St falls with x as the thickening layer lowers the guided band, and the predicted region usually lies near the POD-mode peak (p. 4219; NPR 2.9 is the exception, note's reading). A constant-thickness base flow fails above NPR 2.6 (p. 4220). Only flapping is modelled, so the varicose and wagging tones remain unexplained (p. 4219).
- *Twin jets without shocks.* Preferred-mode maps show stripes at constant St·s/D, with SA1 sinuous flapping favoured ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 12–13). As spacing changes, the SA guided band moves and the SS band hardly does ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 11, 14–15). When the jets merge, the mode becomes a higher-order mode of an equivalent AR ≈ 2 jet ([Stavropoulos 2024](../notes/stavropoulos-2024-upstream-waves-merging-twin-jets.md), p. 6–13).
- *Streaks in round jets.* Steady |m| = 2 streaks act as a zero-frequency partner that couples m = ±1 ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 7–8) and splits them into flapping and wagging modes (p. 9–11).

All of these are locally parallel. They give wavenumbers, shapes and bands, not growth integrated along x, receptivity or loop selection ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 24–26; [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4220).

## Guided-jet waves and trapped acoustic modes

*Labels.* Mode names collide across groups. This page writes (m, n_r) for (azimuthal, radial order), as [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) and [Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) do; Tam & Ahuja write (n, m) for the same modes ([Tam & Ahuja 1990](../notes/tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md), note's Notation). The A1–A4/H1–H3 of [Bogey & Gojon 2017](../notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md) and the S1–S3/H1–H3 of [Gojon 2018](../notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md) are radial orders, (0, 1)–(0, 4) and (1, 1)–(1, 3), not the A1/A2 screech stages or Towne's S1/S2 saddles. The Pprime group uses (0, 1) for impinging tones ([Jaunet 2019](../notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md), p. 6) and (0, 2) for A1/A2 screech ([Mancinelli 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md)). Each upstream band runs from a *branch point* on the sonic line k = −ω/a∞ (lower St; Mancinelli's B, the "cut-on" of EM 2018) to a *saddle* or double zero (upper St; S(m, n_r)).

**Origin.** [Tam & Hu 1989](../notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md) solved the cylindrical vortex sheet and a half-Gaussian Rayleigh model with Briggs's contour deformation and found three families: KH; supersonic instability waves (p. 456); and subsonic waves, trapped, neutral on a vortex sheet and unstable only with finite thickness (p. 478–479). In supersonic jets the subsonic family includes neutral *upstream* waves (p. 462–463). Each band starts at a sonic-line branch point, J₁(η_iR_j) = 0 in closed form for m = 0 (eqs. 6.1–6.3, p. 476) and an impedance-type condition for m ≠ 0 (eq. 6.5), and ends at a double zero where the upstream and downstream branches coalesce (p. 475–476). The figures plot ω < 0 against k > 0, so "frequencies lower than the minimum" (p. 476) means physically higher ones: the double zero is the *upper* edge, as the note's recomputation confirms. These are the hard-duct (m = 0) and mixed-impedance (m = 1) branch points that [Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) re-derived (p. 30), and the B/S points of Mancinelli. Tam & Hu argued that the upstream wave must live mainly outside the jet or be swept downstream (p. 476), which Nogueira 2024's energy flux confirms (p. 26–27); whether the waves close screech or impingement loops they left open (p. 463, 480). [Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md) had already met non-KH "irregular" roots and upstream waves of uncertain meaning (p. 166, 177), which we read as early sightings.

[Tam & Ahuja 1990](../notes/tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md) made the first application. KH waves hitting a plate excite the upstream neutral modes, which close the loop at the lip (p. 73). The cut-offs on C = −1 are J₁ = 0 for m = 0, i.e. the hard-duct condition J₀′ = 0, and an impedance condition between hard and soft walls for m ≥ 1 (eqs. 19–22, p. 80): again Nogueira 2024's branch points (p. 30–31). The (0, 1) branch passes through the origin (eqs. 23–24, p. 80–81), Mancinelli 2019's B(0, 1). Only (0, 1) lies in the KH band St < 0.7 of a cold subsonic jet, so subsonic impingement resonance must be axisymmetric, while (1, 1) enters at supersonic speeds (p. 81–82). The mean tone St was predicted as that of the least-dispersive (0, 1) wave, d²ω/dk² = 0, matching five data sets for M_j 0.7–0.95 (eq. 25, Fig. 16, p. 83). The criterion foreshadows the saddle picture: in the note's calculation two zero-group-velocity points split off the inflection near M_j 0.81, and at M_j 0.9 they sit at St 0.369 and 0.436, against 0.37 and 0.43 for Towne's isothermal vortex sheet (Table 2), with the inflection between them where the group velocity is positive. For supersonic jets only the part of each branch "right of the maximum" travels upstream (p. 77), the branch-point-to-saddle band later used for screech; the paper's own Fig. 10 puts the (0, 2) band at St 0.43–0.54 at M_j 1.4 (note's calculation), although the text names only (0, 1) and (1, 1) as candidates (p. 82).

Two companion papers on an M_j = 0.9 LES made the subsonic case concrete.
- [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) used linearised Euler, vortex-sheet and soft-duct models. They give duct-like k± modes and an upstream "discrete free-stream" k⁻ mode (p. 14–15). Two neutral saddles, S1 and S2, bound the band where k⁺ and k⁻ coexist (p. 16–18), and that band brackets the near-nozzle tones (p. 19). The three models agree on S1 within about 4% (p. 16–17). In a weakly non-parallel (WKB) view the saddles act as turning points, which gives five resonance loops (p. 25–28). Tones exist only for 0.82 < M < 1 in isothermal jets (p. 31–33). Tam & Hu's subsonic waves are these core modes, but their claim that every subsonic wave of a subsonic jet travels upstream ([Tam & Hu 1989](../notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 473) holds only below M ≈ 0.82, a range they did not study (Towne, p. 33).
- [Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md) found the same waves as lightly damped global modes labelled (m, n_r, n_x). The least damped is (0, 1, 1) at St ≈ 0.37 (p. 8). Rebuilding a dispersion relation from the global modes reproduces the LES frequency–wavenumber bands (p. 16–17), and the modes explain tones that had been blamed on the rig (p. 12–13).

The same family closes other loops:
- *Jet–edge tones.* KH plus k_TH⁻ (M ≤ 0.82) or k_p⁻ (M > 0.82) reproduce the "frequency squeezing" of the tones, and wave trapping sets the high-St cut-off (p. 9–14). The free-stream-sound loop fits neither the spacing nor the Mach trend ([Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md), p. 4–5). Above M ≈ 0.82, loops through k⁻_d and k⁻_p compete ([Stavropoulos 2026](../notes/stavropoulos-2026-jet-edge-interaction.md), not tagged, p. 15–18).
- *Impinging tones.* [Bogey & Gojon 2017](../notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md) carried Tam & Ahuja's bands to a cold, ideally expanded M_j 1.5 LES. The bands order the tones by shape, axisymmetric in (0, 1) and (0, 2) and helical near (1, 1) and (1, 2) (p. 577–579), and a k–ω spectrum on the axis shows waves travelling upstream inside the supersonic core along bands like (0, 1)–(0, 3), slightly above the vortex-sheet curves, peaking on k = −ω/a∞ at the tones (Fig. 24, p. 583–585): the first supersonic counterpart of Towne's and Schmidt's trapped-wave bands. The note's reproduction gives (0, 1) 0–0.199, (0, 2) 0.390–0.491 and (1, 1) 0.343–0.344; the paper's (1, 1) lower-limit dot sits about 8% below its own eq. 3.5. The (0, 2) pressure node at r ≈ 0.54–0.58r0 (note's calculation) matches the r ≈ 0.5r0 minimum of the LES tone fields (p. 583), a cheap eigenfunction test like EM 2018's. At M_j 1.5 the whole upstream part of every branch travels at 0.93–1.00a∞, so the guided-mode loop condition reduces to the free-sound one (eq. 3.6 = eq. 3.1 on k = −ω/a∞; note's checks), which the authors note may be why the classical model works (p. 582). In experiment, impinging tones over M_j 0.7–1.5 die out along the (0, 1) upper limit S(0, 1), an "almost perfect" match ([Jaunet 2019](../notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md), p. 7, 9). That band is isothermal, though: the note reproduces the traced line within 0.003 with T_j = T∞, and the cold jet's band sits 2–12% higher at M_j 0.9–1.5. Detail is on [impinging jets and edge tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md).
- *Round-jet screech.* The upstream wave extracted from PIV matches the (0, 2) vortex-sheet eigenfunction inside the core but decays too slowly outside it, and every A1/A2 tone lies inside its cut-on band ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), p. 9–10). The band test, not the shape match, is decisive, because free-stream sound has similar eigenfunctions (p. 10). [Gojon 2018](../notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md) mapped the bands across all stages: A1, A2 and the secondary u near the (0, 2) lower limit, C and c on the (1, 1) lower limit, B and D outside every band (p. 2919–2920), with LES f–k peaks on k ≈ −ω/a∞ near those limits (p. 2921). Its eigenfunctions match only in the inner core (the (0, 2) node at r/D_j ≈ 0.32 against 0.25 in the model), as in EM 2018, and its thin-sheet (1, 1) band misses the dominant C tone (see open questions; note's reading of Fig. 8). Prediction models built on this ([Mancinelli 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md); [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md)) are on the [screech frequency and global modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md) page.
- *Rectangular-jet screech.* A planar vortex sheet gives closed-form lower band limits, equal to the planar branch points of Nogueira 2024. Heating lowers them, just as cooling raises the round-jet band ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 25). The overexpanded (NPR 3) LES tones sit 16–24% above the first antisymmetric (A1) limit at every temperature, and the cold underexpanded tone 2% above it. The hot underexpanded tones fall 8% and 31% below it, as do, by eye, cold experiments above M_j ≈ 1.45 (p. 26; note's arithmetic). The authors blame the infinitely thin sheet but do not compute a thick one (p. 26). The model gives bands only, with no growth or phase condition, and it ignores the major dimension. It therefore cannot tell minor-axis from diagonal flapping, or explain the switch from one to the other with heating (p. 21–24); the oblique-wave bands of [Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) (p. 28–30) are the natural 3-D extension (our reading). The PIV evidence is stronger: the flapping mode's upstream peak is subsonic and sits near k_KH − k_sh1, and the schlieren tone lies close to a faint negative-k guided arc ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4214, 4218).

[Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) then explained what the wave is. Waves generated inside the jet are totally reflected by the shear layer, and a standing wave between the two shear layers recovers the double-vortex-sheet dispersion relation exactly (p. 19–22). Sound from outside cannot excite the wave (p. 14). The net energy flux sets its upstream direction and roughly locates the saddles (p. 23–28). For m = 1 the branch point does not follow the hard-duct rule (p. 30), as Tam & Hu's and Tam & Ahuja's impedance conditions already implied. The paper argues that screech always needs the guided mode, whereas impingement needs it only when the upstream source sits in the core (p. 31–33). The total reflection is Snell's law: trace-wavenumber matching across any parallel layer, whatever its profile and thickness, leaves no wave outside when |a∞k̄ₓ| > 1 ([Suzuki & Lele 2003](../notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md), eqs. 2.21–2.23, p. 146), the sonic-line edge of Nogueira's total-reflection band (p. 15–17). Suzuki & Lele's "reflected" rays are therefore the ray limit of the in-jet sound that forms the guided wave (the Suzuki note's comparison).

Phase-only resonance models over-predict: with finite thickness they produce a tone for every integer p from 1 to 7 (p. 17). A complex-ω model with a fitted |R₁R₂|(St, M_j) removes most spurious tones, but the fit uses the data it predicts ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), p. 17–21). The complex model of [Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md) is fragile near saddle points (p. 20–24). Bands plus a standing-wave condition also allow crossings that give no tone, e.g. (0, 2) with N = 7 at L = 10r0 ([Bogey & Gojon 2017](../notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md), note's calculation). [Jaunet 2019](../notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md) concede that the end phase φ "technically allows to tune the model" to any observation (p. 10); their extinction evidence is a band-limit argument that does not depend on φ. Their dispersive vortex-sheet KH wave fits better than a 0.5U_j one (p. 7, 10), although its phase speed (≈ 0.88–1.0U_j, note's reading of Fig. 3) is well above measured convection speeds (≈ 0.5–0.6U_j; p. 5; [Bogey & Gojon 2017](../notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 571), and at H/D = 8 the lines for successive n and the two end phases lie only ΔSt ≈ 0.03 apart (note's estimate). Operator models err the other way, amplifying trapped waves more than SPOD shows ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)).

## Wavepacket models

Once matched to data, linear wavepackets also serve as sound sources. For supersonic Mach-wave radiation one coherent wavepacket per frequency dominates and no volume forcing is needed ([Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 16, 26). For BBSAN the wavepacket multiplies the shock cells, and level and directivity then depend on its envelope length, radial shape and coherence, not only on its wavenumber ([Ray 2005](../notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md); [Wong 2021](../notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md)); shock harmonics lengthen the envelope ([Nogueira 2022, PFE](../notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md)). These source models are on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md), and PSE and PFE as marching methods on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md).

**Ray models of the same interaction.** [Suzuki & Lele 2003](../notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md) treat the KH–shock interaction locally. A steep weak shock obeys the same eikonal as high-frequency sound, and a Blokhintzev-type invariant carries its strength along 4-D ray tubes, so geometrical acoustics follows both front and amplitude (eq. 2.14, p. 142–145). A standing shock has zero frequency, so its trace speed is subsonic relative to the ambient and it is evanescent outside a steady layer; that is why the critical angle always lies below the Mach angle and leakage needs unsteady vortices (p. 145–147). The margin is about 29° at M₁ = 1.2 and 7° at 2.5, and heating narrows it: the critical angle minus 90° is 25.1°, 27.0° and 29.7° for T₁/T₂ = 0.75, 1.0 and 1.5, against 56.4° for the Mach angle (note's calculation from eq. 2.23). Unlike the models above, the rays run through the unsteady DNS field, not the mean (p. 153), in the spirit of the POD-reconstructed fields of [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) (p. 26–28); only the inflow forcing uses local stability eigenfunctions (p. 151–152). Fronts match the DNS well, amplitude directivity only qualitatively (p. 159, 163–164). The ray picture is the localised, sharp-front counterpart of the wavenumber-space product waves of [Tam & Tanna 1982](../notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md) and [Ray 2005](../notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md) (note's comparison). The leakage physics is on [screech](screech.md).

## Disagreements and open questions

**What selects the screech frequency?** Four views compete (see also [screech frequency and global modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md)):
- A phase-and-gain loop with reflection coefficients at the nozzle and the shock ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md)).
- An absolute instability that needs no nozzle; the nozzle sets only the amplitude ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 22).
- The "weakest link" of lip receptivity ([Tam 1986](../notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), not tagged, p. 9–10), which [Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) argues is essentially the saddle condition (p. 14–15). It also predicts rectangular flapping tones ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4219).
- The least-stable global mode ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md); [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md) also asks whether the jet is an oscillator at all).

Even linear theory disputes where in an absolute pocket the frequency is set: at the maximum ω₀,ᵢ (Pierrehumbert), at the absolute/convective boundary (Koch), or at the complex-X saddle. A wake simulation favours Koch's boundary, which the review's authors concede "appears to contradict" their saddle ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 493–495, 502). A local saddle frequency is thus a leading-order estimate. *To settle:* vary lip thickness at a fixed mean flow and check whether frequency or only amplitude moves, and measure R₁ and R₂ by projecting LES fields onto k± eigenfunctions (Mancinelli 2021, p. 22).

**Shock-cell questions.** Which shock wavenumber closes each stage, and whether shocks modulate the KH envelope directly or only superpose separate waves, are on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md) (see also [screech staging and mode switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md)).

**Do guided-mode bands survive heating and thick layers?** On thickness the evidence is mixed.
- [Tam & Hu 1989](../notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md) found "little or no" effect on the upstream waves, which stay neutral (p. 479). That concerns neutrality more than band edges, so it does not strictly clash with [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), whose thickening below R/θ ≈ 30 lowers the (0, 2) branch point and saddle (p. 16–17) (Tam & Hu note).
- [Tam & Ahuja 1990](../notes/tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md) give the earliest datum: at b/R_j = 0.5 (R_j/θ ≈ 6) the (0, 1) branch moves by no more than 5%, but the (0, 2) branch loses its hump, i.e. its zero-group-velocity points, and drops by up to about 10% (Fig. 17, p. 84; read from figure). Thickening can thus erase a higher radial mode's band rather than merely shift it, which partly supports Gojon 2019's appeal to Tam & Ahuja (p. 26).
- The temporal mixing layers of [Bogey & Gojon 2017](../notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md) (R/δ_θ ≈ 8) keep the bands, slightly *above* the vortex-sheet curves at large |k| (p. 586–588), consistent with Tam & Ahuja there. They do not contradict Mancinelli either: they judge another part of the branch, by eye, at ΔSt ≈ 0.13–0.32 (note's arithmetic).
- The thin sheet misses helical tones even in cold jets. The dominant C tone of [Gojon 2018](../notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md) (St 0.28) lies 10–14% below the (1, 1) band, the Monash C tone below the cold (1, 1) limit at M_j 1.45 (0.359), and six of Bogey & Gojon's seven helical tones 11–26% below theirs (notes' calculations); the authors' appeal to the vortex sheet (p. 579, 582) is not backed by their own appendix. [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) needed a thicker layer (δ = 0.25) for its m = 1 case (p. 7).
- Heating: the vortex-sheet band misses hot underexpanded rectangular tones ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 26), and a spreading base flow rescues a rectangular prediction that a constant profile misses ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4220). But no finite-thickness model has been run for hot jets, in round jets cooling and thickening cancel so a fit can be right for the wrong reason (Mancinelli 2021, p. 15–16), and even the choice between isothermal and cold moves the (0, 1) edge by 2–12% ([Jaunet 2019](../notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md) note).

*To settle:* finite-thickness or Floquet LSA on cold and hot LES or PIV means, using the velocity θ rather than θ_c (Michalke 1984, p. 162), including the m = 1 branch point, which nobody has computed with finite thickness.

**Can tone data tell guided from free-stream closure?** Near the lower band edge the guided wave travels at about a∞ in phase and group velocity, so loop frequencies, and even f–k peaks on k = −ω/a∞, fit either closure ([Gojon 2018](../notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md), p. 2919–2921; [Bogey & Gojon 2017](../notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 582; [Jaunet 2019](../notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md), p. 7). An impinging LES with upstream waves at −a∞ in the shear layer could not separate the two ([Karami 2020](../notes/karami-2020-acoustic-hydrodynamic-waves-underexpanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 16), and the lip-line helical k–ω evidence of Bogey & Gojon cannot either at ΔkD ≈ 1.26 (note's arithmetic). What discriminates is band edges (tones dying at S(0, 1)), radial structure (a node inside the core, evanescent decay outside) and k–ω energy on the axis ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), p. 10). *To settle:* k–ω spectra on the axis with longer, tapered windows, and a test of the outer decay, which no study has made.

**Flapping axis and helicity.** The LSA makes flapping the more unstable KH mode, but the LES matches the wagging orientation, which the authors attribute to closure. Whether energy flows from screech to the streaks or the reverse is open ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 21, 25). Likewise, KH growth maps favour SA1 near s/D = 3 ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md)), yet 1SS is observed there ([Nogueira & Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md)). *To settle:* forced LES, and cross-plane measurements that can tell the symmetries apart.

## Methods and data

- **Inputs.** Only a mean flow is needed, so non-time-resolved PIV is enough: Gaussian or tanh (Michalke profile 2) fits with Crocco–Busemann temperature ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md)), or for rectangular jets a shock-free product of four tanh profiles fitted to both PIV planes ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4212–4213). Near the nozzle of an underexpanded jet, use the measured or LES profile, not a tanh ([Karami 2020](../notes/karami-2020-acoustic-hydrodynamic-waves-underexpanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 22, 26). Measure shear-layer thickness as the velocity θ rather than θ_c: it controls the guided band and the growth rates more than shock strength does ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md); [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md); [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md)). Global and resolvent inputs are on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md).
- **Quick overlays.** Vortex-sheet branch points are closed form (m = 0: [Tam & Hu 1989](../notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), eq. 6.3; m ≥ 1: [Tam & Ahuja 1990](../notes/tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md), eq. 19); the upper edges need a root solve. State the temperature ratio: cold and isothermal bands differ by 2–12% ([Jaunet 2019](../notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md) note).
- **Validation data.** *Time-resolved* (schlieren, microphones, LES): SPOD modes and k–St maps ([Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md)); k–ω spectra along the axis and lip line, split into n = 0 and 1 ([Bogey & Gojon 2017](../notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md); [Gojon 2018](../notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md)), whose 4.5–5D lines give ΔkD ≈ 1.26–1.4, too coarse to tell C = −0.93 from −1 (notes' arithmetic). *Snapshot PIV*: a POD-pair complex mode, split by the sign of k_x, at a tone fixed by a microphone ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)). Negative k_x is not the same as upstream: duct-like waves have negative phase speed but positive group velocity, and Gojon 2018's spectra carry energy on them ([Gojon 2018](../notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md) note; [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md), p. 8, 15). A radial-node check is cheap: the (0, 2) node sits at r ≈ 0.54–0.58r0 at M_j 1.5 ([Bogey & Gojon 2017](../notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md) note).
- **Metric and cost.** The β projection compares a model wavepacket with measured fields ([Cavalieri 2013](../notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), eq. 4.11). SP-LSA takes about 3 min on a laptop ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 6).

## Relevance to our work

- **Lab screech, impinging and non-axisymmetric data.**
  - Overlay finite-thickness guided-mode bands, built from measured R/θ and temperature, on St–NPR maps. For a first look, the cold thin-sheet (0, 2) branch point puts the Monash A1 and A2 tones (St 0.67, 0.65 at M_j 1.09, 1.14) 6% and 10% above it, inside the band, and the Monash C tone below the (1, 1) band ([Gojon 2018](../notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md) note; [Tam & Hu 1989](../notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md) note). For AR 2 rectangular jets, Gojon's closed-form limit (eq. 16, p. 25) is a quick first overlay and the PIV-fitted model of Nogueira 2025 the fuller one.
  - Split SPOD or POD-pair modes by the sign of k_x, and check the upstream peak against the band, since the mode exists only over a short x range ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)).
  - Record lip thickness: the fitted reflection phases differ between rigs with 15% and 3% lips ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 16).
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Every model here is stationary and single-frequency, so none says anything about intermittency or switching. Testable links to HPOD amplitudes near a staging boundary come from the Floquet growth rates ([shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md)) and the global modes ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)). The models fix the spatial shapes; HPOD tracks their time variation, and instantaneous tone frequencies can be checked against the band edges.
- **PyFR staging LES.** The LES mean flow can supply θ_R(x), shock positions and the k_s spectrum for local and SP-LSA models. The k± projections could estimate the R₁R₂ that [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) had to fit (p. 22). The weakest-link recipe of [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md) (fitted spreading mean, α_KH near the lip, α_GJM(x), measured k_s1 and k_s2; p. 4213, 4219) is cheap enough to run at every continuation step and would test whether A1 closes on k_s1 and A2 on k_s2 (our inference). n = 0 k–ω spectra at r = 0.1–1r0, as in Gojon 2018, should peak near the cold (0, 2) branch point, St 0.634 at M_j 1.09 and 0.592 at 1.14 (note's arithmetic), and would show whether the guided wave or only its shock partner changes across staging. Comparing ω₀,ᵢ or the loop ω_i with the measured 1/t_switch is our inference, not tested in any paper.
- **Rocket noise.** The Mach-wave wavepacket results above are for cold or isothermal jets; the heated BBSAN case is on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md). Rocket plumes are hotter and faster than the KH merging limit (M_j ≈ 2 at T_j/T∞ ≈ 3), so by the vortex sheet the supersonic family, not one KH wavepacket, should dominate ([Tam & Hu 1989](../notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 459, 480; note's inference). See [rocket-noise models](rocket-noise-models.md).
