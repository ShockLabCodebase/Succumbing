# Linear models: local stability and wave models

_Last updated: 2026-10-21 · 32 papers_

## Scope

Linear models that treat a jet's coherent structures as waves on its mean flow: why linearising about the turbulent mean works and how eddy viscosity enters; local spatial stability, vortex sheets and absolute instability; Floquet models periodic in x (shock cells) or in azimuth (non-round and twin jets) and the shock-cell product picture; guided-jet waves, trapped acoustic modes and the resonance models built on them; and wavepacket source models. The page records where they match experiment or LES and where they fail.

Global stability, resolvent analysis, PSE/PFE/OWNS marching and the harmonic resolvent are on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md). Applications are only linked: [screech](screech.md); [screech staging and mode switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md); flapping in [non-axisymmetric jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md); [twin-jet coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md); BBSAN and Mach waves in [jet-noise mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md); [impinging jets and edge tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md); the SPOD and POD used for validation in [modal decomposition](modal-decomposition.md). The theory comes from two classic reviews: local spatial stability ([Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md)) and absolute/convective instability ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md)).

## Current understanding

1. **Linearising about the turbulent mean works, but only statistically.** PSE on measured mean flows reproduce the averaged phase and envelope of wavepackets for St ≈ 0.2–0.9, m = 0–2, up to the end of the potential core, with one free amplitude per (m, ω) ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md), p. 109–113; [Cavalieri 2013](../notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), p. 574–585). Single realisations need not follow the model (Gudmundsson & Colonius, p. 122–123). The envelope's saturation needs no nonlinearity: jet divergence alone caps the linear gain ([Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), p. 190–191).
2. **Shock-free jets are convectively unstable.** Only low-density jets go absolutely unstable (round below ρ_j/ρ∞ = 0.72, planar below 0.90), and only with thin laminar layers at low M; at higher Re and M they revert to amplifiers ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 511–512), so heated turbulent jets stay convective ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md), p. 111). Global operators agree ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)).
3. **The jet is also an acoustic waveguide.** Upstream guided-jet waves and duct-like trapped waves are discrete, near-neutral modes of vortex-sheet, finite-thickness and global operators ([Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md), p. 14–19; [Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 9–11): sound generated inside the jet and totally reflected by the shear layer ([Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md), p. 19–22). Their band brackets the observed screech, edge and near-nozzle tones ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), p. 10; [Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md), p. 10–14; [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md), p. 19); in a rectangular jet the flapping tone's upstream peak sits near k_KH − k_sh1 ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4218). The band moves more with shear-layer thickness and temperature than with shock strength ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), p. 15–17; [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4220).
4. **Shocks turn the jet into a resonator.** KH–shock interaction puts energy at k_kh ± k_s, and global analysis on a mean flow that contains the shocks recovers the screech frequency to about 1% ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 11–17; [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 11). A Floquet model shows that the shocks create a KH–guided-mode saddle (absolute instability) within 2–6% of the screech frequency ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 13–15). Such a local pocket is necessary but not sufficient for a self-excited global mode ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 492–495).
5. **Weak azimuthal non-uniformity selects flapping.** A second jet, an elliptic cross-section or weak |m| = 2 streaks split the degenerate m = ±1 modes into flapping modes ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 10–12; [Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 9–11; [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 17–18).
6. **Wavepacket sound models need the envelope, not only the wavenumber.** BBSAN depends on the wavepacket's radial shape and envelope length ([Ray 2005](../notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md), p. 5–7) and on its coherence ([Wong 2021](../notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), p. 20–25), whereas one coherent wavepacket per frequency dominates supersonic Mach-wave radiation ([Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 16, 26).

## Why mean-flow models work at all

Local theory came first. [Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md) built inviscid spatial LSA of round jets with Crocco–Busemann temperature and the tanh "profile 2" that the group's LSA still uses (p. 161–164). As θ/R grows from 0.05 to 0.4, the most amplified St falls from about 0.75 to 0.21 (m = 0) and 0.11 (m = 1), and St < 0.35 stays unstable over the whole core (p. 169–170). Mach number stabilises m = 0 more than m = 1 in thick layers, and heating destabilises (p. 174–177). A flight stream acts as an axial stretch, and the curves collapse with σ = 1 + 1.4U∞/ΔU (p. 178–181). Slowly diverging theory caps the gain at St 0.5 at about 90, 57 and 26 for m = 0, 1 and 2, a saturation once blamed on nonlinearity (p. 190–191). The local phase speed then depends on radius and on the variable used (p. 189–190), and whether locally parallel phase speeds mean anything was disputed between Crighton & Gaster and Morris & Tam (p. 192); PSE, which march the full eigenfunction, made the dispute moot in practice (our reading). Michalke also anticipated the nozzle's role: a bare edge keeps no memory of a passing wave, a lip wall could close a feedback loop, and feedback puts the natural shear-layer frequency (fθ/U_o ≈ 0.012–0.013) below the most amplified one (0.0164) (p. 194–196), as resolvent analyses with the nozzle later confirmed ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)).

The empirical case followed. [Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md) matched PSE to a POD-filtered near-field array (0.2 < St < 0.65, m = 0–2, cold and hot), and [Cavalieri 2013](../notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md) to time-resolved cross-plane PIV, with |β| ≥ 0.9 between x/D ≈ 2 and the end of the core (p. 583). Both fail below St ≈ 0.2, blamed on non-parallel effects (Gudmundsson & Colonius, p. 110) or left open between non-parallel and nonlinear causes (Cavalieri, p. 575). The later answer, the Orr mechanism, came from resolvent analysis ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)).

[McKeon & Sharma 2010](../notes/mckeon-2010-critical-layer-framework-turbulent-pipe-flow.md) gave the formal basis. The nonlinear terms become forcing of the operator linearised about the measured mean, with no eddy viscosity (p. 8). The response is low rank, σ₁ ≈ 10σ₂ (p. 13), and peaks at the critical layer. [Oberleithner 2011](../notes/oberleithner-2011-swirling-jet-vortex-breakdown-stability.md) showed that mean-flow LSA also reproduces a *self-excited* global mode, the St = 0.49 precessing helix of a swirling jet: wavelength, radial shape and growth and decay all match, except at the wavemaker (p. 26–29). A mean flow is still not an equilibrium; what that does to eigenvalues computed on it is on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md).

**Eddy viscosity.** The turbulence hidden in the mean still damps and forces the waves, and most models stand in for it with a uniform Reynolds number whose value matters. The trapped acoustic modes of a subsonic jet vanish at Re = 10³ and converge only above about 5 × 10⁴ ([Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 25–27), yet the screech global analyses used Re = 10³ as a surrogate and still found guided and duct waves ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md); [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md)). A RANS-based eddy viscosity beats uniform damping in the jet resolvent ([Pickering 2021](../notes/pickering-2021-optimal-eddy-viscosity-resolvent-jets.md), p. 28), because its equivalent forcing roughly reproduces the projections of the true forcing, not because it models the physics ([Morra 2021](../notes/morra-2021-colour-of-forcing-resolvent-channel.md), p. 11–24). Michalke's theory and vortex sheets are inviscid; for them the open choice is the base flow, above all the shear-layer thickness.

## Local stability, vortex sheets and absolute instability

**Vortex sheets** are the cheapest model. They give guided-mode bands ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md); [Mancinelli 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md); [Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md); planar: [Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md)), twin-jet coupling ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md); [Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md)) and the convective-Mach-number collapse of compressible shear-layer growth ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md), p. 14–15). Their known faults:
- The vortex sheet stabilises completely above a critical M_c of about 1.5–2.1, whereas a finite-thickness layer never does, so vortex-sheet KH growth at high M_c is unreliable ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md), p. 14–15, 22–23).
- Finite thickness lowers the KH phase speed and makes the wave more dispersive, so the fitted reflection phase changes ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), p. 9–11).
- Cooling the jet raises the guided-mode band and thickening the shear layer lowers it. The isothermal vortex sheet of Mancinelli 2019 fitted A1/A2 only because these effects cancel (p. 15–16, 25–26). Thickness matters below R/θ ≈ 30 (p. 16–17), and so does which θ: Michalke calls the density-weighted θ_c unsuitable for the velocity layer (p. 162), yet Mancinelli 2021 used it (p. 22–23). For a cold M 1.2 jet the two differ by about 16% ([Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), note's calculation).

**Absolute instability** is the local criterion that the Floquet and guided-wave models test. A parallel profile is absolutely unstable if ω₀,ᵢ > 0 at a saddle that pinches a k⁺ and a k⁻ branch; a saddle of two branches from the same half-plane does not count ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 482–484). A purely spatial calculation sees the transition as k⁺ and k⁻ swapping (p. 486), which is how the review reads the hot-jet "mode mixing" of [Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md) (p. 177; Table 4, p. 530). The WKB turning points of [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) (p. 25–28) and the Floquet saddle of [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) (p. 14) use this machinery. Whether a local pocket yields a global mode is on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md).

## Floquet and shock-cell models

**The product picture.** Tam & Tanna proposed that KH × shock gives waves at k ± λ_n ([Tam & Tanna 1982](../notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md), not tagged, p. 9). It was confirmed experimentally with a linear operator: once the shocks are part of the mean flow, the screech loop is linear except for the KH–shock interaction ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 15–19, 24). Global and harmonic-resolvent versions are on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md).

**Floquet in x** handles the periodicity of the shock cells. In [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) a 2% sinusoidal shock barely moves the eigenvalues (< 0.01%, p. 8) but creates a KH–guided-mode saddle that meets the Briggs–Bers pinch condition (p. 14). The saddle frequency is insensitive to shock amplitude, while its growth rate ω₀,ᵢ depends strongly on shear-layer thickness δ (p. 15–16). The growth rates are not quantitative: the KH growth had to be cut to 20% to compare mode shapes (p. 11–12). [Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) feeds in the two measured shock wavenumbers. Its saddles fall within 10% of A1 and A2 (p. 12), and ω₀,ᵢ ranks A1 above A2 at M_j = 1.12 (0.223 vs 0.057; p. 13). The model over-predicts A2 at M_j = 1.22, where the measured tone is weak (p. 14).

**Floquet in azimuth** covers cross-sections that are not round:
- *Twin jets.* The four m = 1 KH symmetries have nearly equal growth rates and phase speeds, so the upstream guided-mode bands decide the coupling (1SS). The upstream wavenumber matches PIV within 1%; KH matches within 5.6% and its shape comes out too narrow ([Nogueira & Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), p. 15–21).
- *Elliptic jets.* KH predictions hold whether the base flow is a PIV fit or RANS. The guided-mode band is narrow (ΔSt ≤ 0.09) and moves with shear-layer thickness. The mode is neutral at the screech frequency only over a short x band, and the measured upstream-wave peak sits inside it ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 18, 22–24).
- *Rectangular jets.* A shock-free four-tanh fit to minor- and major-axis PIV reproduces centreline decay, shear-layer growth and axis switching ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4216). On it, flapping is the most unstable KH mode, and the flapping guided mode has the same symmetry but decays far more slowly away from the jet (p. 4219). A weakest-link condition, α_GJM = α_KH − k_sh, with α_KH fixed near the lip, α_GJM varying in x and k_sh measured, predicts the flapping tone within a few per cent at NPR 2.5–4.5. The predicted St falls with x as the thickening layer lowers the guided band, and the predicted region usually lies near the POD-mode peak (p. 4219; NPR 2.9 is the exception, note's reading). A constant-thickness base flow fails above NPR 2.6 (p. 4220). Only flapping is modelled, so the varicose and wagging tones remain unexplained (p. 4219).
- *Twin jets without shocks.* Preferred-mode maps show stripes at constant St·s/D, with SA1 sinuous flapping favoured ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 12–13). As spacing changes, the SA guided band moves and the SS band hardly does ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 11, 14–15). When the jets merge, the mode becomes a higher-order mode of an equivalent AR ≈ 2 jet ([Stavropoulos 2024](../notes/stavropoulos-2024-upstream-waves-merging-twin-jets.md), p. 6–13).
- *Streaks in round jets.* Steady |m| = 2 streaks act as a zero-frequency partner that couples m = ±1 ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 7–8) and splits them into flapping and wagging modes (p. 9–11).

All of these are locally parallel. They give wavenumbers, shapes and bands, not growth integrated along x, receptivity or loop selection ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 24–26; [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4220).

## Guided-jet waves and trapped acoustic modes

The idea goes back to Tam & Hu (1989, not in the library). [Michalke 1984](../notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md) had already met non-KH "irregular" roots and upstream-travelling waves of uncertain meaning (p. 166, 177), which we read as early sightings of the guided family. Two companion papers on an M_j = 0.9 LES made it concrete.
- [Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) used linearised Euler, vortex-sheet and soft-duct models. They give duct-like k± modes and an upstream "discrete free-stream" k⁻ mode (p. 14–15). Two neutral saddles, S1 and S2, bound the band where k⁺ and k⁻ coexist (p. 16–18), and that band brackets the near-nozzle tones (p. 19). The three models agree on S1 within about 4% (p. 16–17). In a weakly non-parallel (WKB) view the saddles act as turning points, which gives five resonance loops (p. 25–28). Tones exist only for 0.82 < M < 1 in isothermal jets (p. 31–33).
- [Schmidt 2017](../notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md) found the same waves as lightly damped global modes labelled (m, n_r, n_x). The least damped is (0, 1, 1) at St ≈ 0.37 (p. 8). Rebuilding a dispersion relation from the global modes reproduces the LES frequency–wavenumber bands (p. 16–17), and the modes explain tones that had been blamed on the rig (p. 12–13).

The same family closes other loops:
- *Jet–edge tones.* KH plus k_TH⁻ (M ≤ 0.82) or k_p⁻ (M > 0.82) reproduce the "frequency squeezing" of the tones, and wave trapping sets the high-St cut-off (p. 9–14). The free-stream-sound loop fits neither the spacing nor the Mach trend ([Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md), p. 4–5). Above M ≈ 0.82, loops through k⁻_d and k⁻_p compete ([Stavropoulos 2026](../notes/stavropoulos-2026-jet-edge-interaction.md), not tagged, p. 15–18).
- *Round-jet screech.* The upstream wave extracted from PIV matches the (0, 2) vortex-sheet eigenfunction inside the core but decays too slowly outside it, and every A1/A2 tone lies inside its cut-on band ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), p. 9–10). The band test, not the shape match, is decisive, because free-stream sound has similar eigenfunctions (p. 10). Prediction models built on this ([Mancinelli 2019](../notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md); [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md)) are on the [screech](screech.md) page.
- *Rectangular-jet screech.* A planar vortex sheet gives closed-form lower band limits, equal to the planar branch points of Nogueira 2024. Heating lowers them, just as cooling raises the round-jet band ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 25). The overexpanded (NPR 3) LES tones sit 16–24% above the first antisymmetric (A1) limit at every temperature, and the cold underexpanded tone 2% above it. The hot underexpanded tones fall 8% and 31% below it, as do, by eye, cold experiments above M_j ≈ 1.45 (p. 26; note's arithmetic). The authors blame the infinitely thin sheet but do not compute a thick one (p. 26). The model gives bands only, with no growth or phase condition, and it ignores the major dimension. It therefore cannot tell minor-axis from diagonal flapping, or explain the switch from one to the other with heating (p. 21–24); the oblique-wave bands of [Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) (p. 28–30) are the natural 3-D extension (our reading). The PIV evidence is stronger: the flapping mode's upstream peak is subsonic and sits near k_KH − k_sh1, and the schlieren tone lies close to a faint negative-k guided arc ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4214, 4218).

[Nogueira 2024](../notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) then explained what the wave is. Waves generated inside the jet are totally reflected by the shear layer, and a standing wave between the two shear layers recovers the double-vortex-sheet dispersion relation exactly (p. 19–22). Sound from outside cannot excite the wave (p. 14). The net energy flux sets its upstream direction and roughly locates the saddles (p. 23–28). For m = 1 the branch point does not follow the hard-duct rule (p. 30). The paper argues that screech always needs the guided mode, whereas impingement needs it only when the upstream source sits in the core (p. 31–33).

Phase-only resonance models over-predict: with finite thickness they produce a tone for every integer p from 1 to 7 (p. 17). A complex-ω model with a fitted |R₁R₂|(St, M_j) removes most spurious tones, but the fit uses the data it predicts ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), p. 17–21). The complex model of [Jordan 2018](../notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md) is fragile near saddle points (p. 20–24). Operator models err the other way, amplifying trapped waves more than SPOD shows ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)).

## Wavepacket models

Once matched to data, linear wavepackets also serve as sound sources. [Ray 2005](../notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md) first drove a linearised Euler solver with products of a PSE wave and zero-frequency shock modes on a RANS mean (p. 2–3). At St 0.6 the upstream lobe sits at the local Mach angle of k_t − k_s, and a locally parallel Green's function matches the solver in level and direction (p. 9). At St 0.9 the wave stays in the shear layer, the sources become rings, and interference splits the radiation into narrow lobes whose angles, but not amplitudes, the Green's function reproduces (p. 9–10, 12–13). Level and directivity thus depend on the wavepacket's radial shape and envelope length, not only on its wavenumber (p. 5–7). The forced solver also radiates Mach waves from KH waves it excites itself (p. 7). The study is preliminary and has no comparison with measurements (p. 13).

The envelope is where shock-free models fall short. The parabolised Floquet equations (PFE) of [Nogueira 2022, PFE](../notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md) add shock harmonics α ± jα_s and, in shock-containing jets, give a longer envelope than shock-free PSE, which decay too fast (p. 18–19); given Ray's envelope dependence, this matters for BBSAN. Coherence matters too: fully coherent PSE sources put the n = 1 and n = 2 peaks within ±2 dB/St, and adding LES coherence decay (jitter) recovers up to 15 dB/St between the peaks ([Wong 2021](../notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), p. 20–25). For supersonic Mach-wave radiation, by contrast, one coherent wavepacket per frequency dominates and no volume forcing is needed ([Cavalieri 2019](../notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 16, 26). PSE and PFE as marching methods are on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md).

## Disagreements and open questions

**What selects the screech frequency?** Four views compete (see also [screech](screech.md)):
- A phase-and-gain loop with reflection coefficients at the nozzle and the shock ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md)).
- An absolute instability that needs no nozzle; the nozzle sets only the amplitude ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 22).
- The "weakest link" of lip receptivity ([Tam 1986](../notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), not tagged, p. 9–10), which [Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) argues is essentially the saddle condition (p. 14–15). It also predicts rectangular flapping tones ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4219).
- The least-stable global mode ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md); [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md) also asks whether the jet is an oscillator at all).

Even linear theory disputes where in an absolute pocket the frequency is set: at the maximum ω₀,ᵢ (Pierrehumbert), at the absolute/convective boundary (Koch), or at the complex-X saddle. A wake simulation favours Koch's boundary, which the review's authors concede "appears to contradict" their saddle ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 493–495, 502). A local saddle frequency is thus a leading-order estimate. *To settle:* vary lip thickness at a fixed mean flow and check whether frequency or only amplitude moves, and measure R₁ and R₂ by projecting LES fields onto k± eigenfunctions (Mancinelli 2021, p. 22).

**Which shock wavenumber closes which stage?** Four answers (see also [screech staging and mode switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md)):
- A1 uses k_s1 and A2 uses a secondary k_s2 ([Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 7–14; [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), not tagged). In an AR 2 rectangular jet the dominant flapping tone closes on k_sh1, and a k_sh2 loop is offered for the second flapping tone (p. 4218–4220), though its St also lies near the varicose tone ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), note's reading of Fig. 17).
- The stages differ only by the integer p at one reflection point; App. B of [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) links this to the k_s view (p. 24).
- [Farghadan 2026](../notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) argues the first suboptimal peak is hidden inside the primary peak (p. 16–17).
- A BMD-derived k_S matches k_kh − k_GJM better than either peak ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), not tagged, p. 20–22).

*To settle:* resolve the shock spectrum over a long field of view and run BMD on time-resolved schlieren across a staging sweep. A cheaper check (our inference): map the local Mach angle of k_t − k_s along x, as [Ray 2005](../notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md) did (p. 9), from SPOD k_t(x) and mean k_s(x); 180° is Tam's closure condition.

**Do shocks modulate the KH envelope directly?** PFE says yes: the harmonics are slaved to KH and lengthen the envelope ([Nogueira 2022, PFE](../notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 12, 18–19). [Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) (p. 20–23) and [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) (p. 16) see superposed separate waves instead. The PFE authors concede they cannot yet separate negative-wavenumber modulation from upstream waves (p. 21). It matters for BBSAN, whose level and directivity depend strongly on envelope length (Ray 2005, p. 5). *To settle:* a Floquet OWNS model that carries upstream waves ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)), compared with k_x-filtered SPOD at frequencies away from screech.

**Do guided-mode bands survive heating and thick layers?** The vortex-sheet band misses hot underexpanded rectangular tones ([Gojon 2019](../notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 26). Thickening should lower the band (Mancinelli 2021, p. 15–17), as Gojon's authors argue without computing it (p. 26), and a spreading base flow rescues a rectangular prediction that a constant profile misses ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4220). But no finite-thickness model has been run for hot jets, and in round jets cooling and thickening cancel, so a fit can be right for the wrong reason (Mancinelli 2021, p. 15–16). *To settle:* finite-thickness or Floquet LSA on hot-jet LES or PIV means, using the velocity θ rather than θ_c (Michalke 1984, p. 162).

**Flapping axis and helicity.** The LSA makes flapping the more unstable KH mode, but the LES matches the wagging orientation, which the authors attribute to closure. Whether energy flows from screech to the streaks or the reverse is open ([Nogueira 2026](../notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 21, 25). Likewise, KH growth maps favour SA1 near s/D = 3 ([Rodríguez 2023](../notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md)), yet 1SS is observed there ([Nogueira & Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md)). *To settle:* forced LES, and cross-plane measurements that can tell the symmetries apart.

## Methods and data

- **Inputs.** Only a mean flow is needed, so non-time-resolved PIV is enough: Gaussian or tanh (Michalke profile 2) fits with Crocco–Busemann temperature ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md)), or for rectangular jets a shock-free product of four tanh profiles fitted to both PIV planes ([Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4212–4213). Measure shear-layer thickness as the velocity θ rather than θ_c: it controls the guided band and the growth rates more than shock strength does ([Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md); [Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md); [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md)). Global and resolvent inputs are on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md).
- **Validation data.** *Time-resolved* (schlieren, microphones, LES): SPOD modes and k–St maps ([Towne 2017](../notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md)). *Snapshot PIV*: a POD-pair complex mode, split by the sign of k_x, at a tone fixed by a microphone ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md); [Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)).
- **Metric and cost.** The β projection compares a model wavepacket with measured fields ([Cavalieri 2013](../notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), eq. 4.11). SP-LSA takes about 3 min on a laptop ([Nogueira 2021](../notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 6).

## Relevance to our work

- **Lab screech, impinging and non-axisymmetric data.**
  - Overlay finite-thickness guided-mode bands, built from measured R/θ and temperature, on St–NPR maps. For AR 2 rectangular jets, Gojon's closed-form limit (eq. 16, p. 25) is a quick first overlay and the PIV-fitted model of Nogueira 2025 the fuller one.
  - Split SPOD or POD-pair modes by the sign of k_x, and check the upstream peak against the band, since the mode exists only over a short x range ([Ivelja 2024](../notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)).
  - Record lip thickness: the fitted reflection phases differ between rigs with 15% and 3% lips ([Stavropoulos 2023](../notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 16).
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Every model here is stationary and single-frequency, so none says anything about intermittency or switching. One testable link to HPOD amplitudes near a staging boundary is the ω₀,ᵢ ranking of A1 over A2 ([Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 13); global-mode counterparts are on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md). The models fix the spatial shapes; HPOD tracks their time variation.
- **PyFR staging LES.** The LES mean flow can supply θ_R(x), shock positions and the k_s spectrum for local and SP-LSA models. The k± projections could estimate the R₁R₂ that [Mancinelli 2021](../notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) had to fit (p. 22). The weakest-link recipe of [Nogueira 2025](../notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md) (fitted spreading mean, α_KH near the lip, α_GJM(x), measured k_s1 and k_s2; p. 4213, 4219) is cheap enough to run at every continuation step and would test whether A1 closes on k_s1 and A2 on k_s2 (our inference). Comparing ω₀,ᵢ or the loop ω_i with the measured 1/t_switch is our inference, not tested in any paper.
- **Rocket noise.** The Mach-wave wavepacket results above are for cold or isothermal jets. The one heated supersonic case on this page is a BBSAN source model: at T_j/T∞ = 2 the first shock mode barely changes, so heating acts through the instability waves, whose gains it raises ([Ray 2005](../notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md), p. 10). See [rocket-noise models](rocket-noise-models.md).
