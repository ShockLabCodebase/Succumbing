# Structure of a screeching rectangular jet: a stereoscopic particle image velocimetry study

- **Authors:** Mehmet B. Alkislar, Anjaneyulu Krothapalli, Luiz M. Lourenco, Department of Mechanical Engineering, Florida A&M University and Florida State University, Tallahassee (p. 121). Not Shock Lab group work. Krothapalli is first author of the AR 16.7 screech-and-mixing study ([Krothapalli 1986](krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md)), and this paper continues that line with quantitative PIV.

## Summary
Alkislar, Krothapalli and Lourenco measure a screeching AR 4 convergent–divergent rectangular jet (h × w = 10 × 40 mm, M_d 1.44) at its loudest underexpanded condition, M_j 1.69, with stereoscopic PIV phase-locked to the screech tone (p. 124–130). Sixteen phases (eight measured, eight mirrored) of 35 image pairs each give the mean, periodic and random velocity fields in the minor-axis plane, and cross-planes at several x/h show the streamwise vorticity (p. 128–130, 136–145). A near-field microphone sweep over M_j 1.2–1.8 shows one flapping tone family with a Strouhal jump at M_d. The authors put the jump down to a change in convection speed (0.63U_j overexpanded, 0.50U_j underexpanded) inserted into Tam's (1988) formula, not to a change of mode (p. 131–132). Screech raises the minor-axis spreading rate from 0.065 (ideally expanded jet) to 0.11 over 5–20h and 0.33 beyond 21h, and the jet crosses axes at x = 16h, about 4AR. The ideally expanded jet does not switch axes (p. 135–136). They attribute this to stationary streamwise vortex pairs that form when the screech-driven spanwise rolls deform (p. 136–140, 144). The circulation of the coherent structures, found from R_uu-defined regions at one phase, peaks at the ends of the 3rd and 5th shock cells. It is highest at the 3rd, where Fig. 1 shows the screech wave being born, and lower at M_j 1.74, a quieter condition. From this the authors conclude that coherent vorticity strength, together with shock strength, sets screech amplitude (p. 142–144, 153). Periodic motion carries about half the turbulent energy for x/h < 20, then collapses where the streamwise vortices take over (p. 146–153). There is no simulation or stability analysis. Modelling is limited to Tam's frequency formula with a measured U_c (p. 132).

## Key findings
- **Near-field acoustics** (§3.1, p. 131–133):
  - *Spectrum* (Fig. 6, p. 131): microphone at R = 25h, χ = 90° (nozzle inlet angle) in the central (X, Y)-plane. The screeching jet shows the fundamental and three harmonics; the text says the first harmonic is "comparable" to the fundamental. Read from the rendered figure: fundamental ≈129 dB at St ≈ 0.090, first harmonic ≈132 dB at ≈0.18, second ≈127 dB, third ≈118 dB. The design-condition jet (M_j 1.44) has no tones and is almost 10 dB lower for St < 0.8 (p. 131). The caption gives M_j = 1.68 for the screeching case.
  - *Frequency* (Fig. 7, p. 131–132): St = f h/U_j falls from ≈0.30 at M_j 1.20 to ≈0.077 at 1.80 (read). There is a clear discontinuity at M_d: ≈0.172 at M_j 1.42, then no tone data until ≈0.143 at 1.46 (read).
    - The authors note that such a jump is usually taken as a varicose → flapping switch (Raman 1998). Their PIV, however, shows flapping dominant in both the over- and the underexpanded regimes (p. 131–132). No overexpanded PIV is shown.
    - Instead, they insert measured convection speeds into Tam's (1988) formula: U_c/U_j = 0.63 overexpanded, 0.50 underexpanded. Each branch then follows its own curve, and the discontinuity is attributed to the change in U_c (p. 132).
  - *Amplitude* (Fig. 8, p. 132–133): one second-order fit per regime, following Krothapalli et al. (1986) and Krothapalli & Strykowski (1996). Read from the figure: overexpanded peak ≈118 dB near M_j 1.32–1.35; underexpanded fit peak ≈127 dB at M_j ≈ 1.69, with data up to ≈129 dB at 1.65–1.68; minima of ≈102–104 dB on both sides of M_d. The parabolic trend is "further confirmed" (p. 132).
- **Mean flow** (§3.2, p. 132–140):
  - *Shock cells and cores* (Figs 10–11, p. 133–135): six shock cells in the minor-axis plane. The supersonic region (M = 1 contour) reaches x ≈ 28h, and the velocity potential core ends at 15h.
    - Centreline u/U_j oscillates between ≈0.78 and ≈1.13 over the cells (read). Peaks lie at x/h ≈ 2.1, 4.7, 7.8, 11.5, 14.9, 18.0 (read), a mean spacing of ≈3.2h (my reading; see Data tables).
    - Beyond ≈22h the centreline decays as x⁻¹. The planar x⁻⁰·⁵ region is "almost non-existent" because the jet diffuses so fast in the (X, Y)-plane (p. 135).
  - *Particle lag* (Fig. 11, p. 134): PIV agrees with velocity from total and static probes (isentropic relations) through the shock cells. The authors conclude lag "is not of much concern". Downstream differences are put down to the isentropic assumption failing where viscous effects matter.
  - *Axis switching* (Fig. 9, p. 133): cross-plane mean u at x/h = 6–16 and 25. By 25h the jet is aligned with the minor axis, and the text says its width there is "about three times" that of the ideally expanded jet.
  - *Spreading* (Fig. 12, eq. 11, p. 135–136):
    - The ideally expanded M_j 1.44 jet spreads linearly with k = 0.065 (minor) and 0.058 (major), in line with Krothapalli et al. (1986), and does **not** switch axes.
    - The screeching jet spreads with k = 0.11 over 5 ≤ x/h ≤ 20, put down to self-excited organised vortices, and k = 0.33 beyond x/h ≈ 21, put down to strong streamwise vortices.
    - The crossover is at X_c = 16h. X_c/h ≈ 4AR, as in incompressible rectangular jets (Krothapalli, Baganoff & Karamcheti 1981).
    - The major-axis half-width "does not vary significantly", so the dynamics are set in the minor-axis plane (p. 136). It stays at ≈1.85–2.1h to x/h ≈ 20 (read).
  - *Streamwise vortices* (Fig. 13, p. 136–140):
    - At x/h = 6 the in-plane velocity is 10–50 m s⁻¹ towards the centre, symmetric, with distinct ω_x concentrations. These are attributed to nozzle surface imperfections: the critical disturbance height is about δ*/12 (Novopashin & Perepelkin 1989; Krothapalli, Strykowski & King 1998).
    - The vortices then grow rapidly in the curved shear layer by a Taylor–Görtler-type instability (x/h = 8).
    - At x/h = 16, two counter-rotating vortex pairs dominate, with "a reminder of the corner vortices".
    - The authors identify these with the "ribs" of Husain & Hussain (1993), formed by the deformation of the spanwise rolls (p. 140).
  - *Cause of axis switching (claim)* (p. 140, 153): the screech-driven spanwise rolls deform into fixed streamwise vortices, and these drive the switch. The ideally expanded jet does not switch because it lacks coherent spanwise vortices. Exciting it should make it switch, and suppressing screech should remove the crossover. Only the last two statements' premise (no switch at M_j 1.44) is measured here.
- **Phase-averaged flow** (§3.3, p. 140–145):
  - *Flapping* (Fig. 14, p. 138–140): the shock cells oscillate transversely, with an amplitude that is small at the exit and grows downstream.
    - ⟨v⟩ reaches about ±0.15U_j and is antisymmetric about the centreline for x/h > 7.5.
    - Around the first few cells ⟨v⟩ is symmetric, which the authors read as a dominant symmetric oscillation there (p. 140). Shih, Krothapalli & Gogineni (1992) saw simultaneous symmetric and antisymmetric modes, but here the near-field microphone shows only the flapping frequency (p. 140).
  - *Spanwise vorticity* (Fig. 15, p. 140–141): ⟨ω_z⟩ shows large coherent structures. The contours look fragmented because the structures are strongly three-dimensional.
  - *Structure size* (Figs 16–17, eq. 13, p. 142–143): regions with R_uu > 0.6 around points on the ⟨u⟩ = 0.5U_j line, at phase 135°.
    - R_uu is computed from the random fluctuations u″ at one phase. R_ωω would be "logically a better choice", but did not give smooth contours with the available samples (p. 142–143).
    - Large areas appear at x/h ≈ 10.5 and 17.5 (text, p. 143). Read from Fig. 17: A/h² ≈ 0.93 near x/h 10 and ≈ 1.05–1.08 near 15–16.5, against 0.2–0.4 elsewhere.
  - *Circulation* (Fig. 18, eq. 12, p. 143–144): Γ_A is the integral of ⟨ω_z⟩ over the shaded regions.
    - There are two peaks, at about 10.5h and 17.5h (text), at the ends of the **3rd and 5th** shock cells.
    - The 3rd-cell peak is the highest and "corresponds to the location of the generation of the intense sound" (p. 143).
    - Read from Fig. 18: at M_j 1.69, Γ_A/(hU_j) ≈ 0.65 at x/h 9–10 and ≈ 0.5 at 15–16, with a minimum of ≈ 0.05 at 12.5. At M_j 1.74 ("away from the maximum screeching condition") it is ≈ 0.15–0.35 between 6h and 18h with no comparable peak, but ≈ 0.5 at x/h 4.4, above the M_j 1.69 value there.
  - *Convection velocity* (eq. 14, p. 143–144): the vorticity-weighted velocity of the identified structures is about **0.5U_j**, consistent with the phase-locked schlieren of Krothapalli et al. (1986). It "shows little variation with downstream distance" (data in Alkislar 2001), which supports a constant U_c in screech formulae (p. 144).
  - *Cross-plane at constant phase* (Fig. 19, x/h = 12, phases 0°, 135°, 270°; p. 144–145): counter-rotating streamwise vortex pairs at every phase.
    - Their strength is "an order of magnitude lower" than the spanwise structures: normalised maxima of about 0.01 against 0.5 (p. 144). See the check items: Fig. 19's colour scale runs to ±0.20.
    - Their self-induced outward velocity in the flapping plane pushes the major-axis sides further out. This raises minor-axis spreading (p. 144).
- **Turbulence: triple decomposition** (§3.4, p. 146–152):
  - *Periodic stresses at phase 135°* (Fig. 20, p. 146–148):
    - ũũ is typically ≈0.02U_j² in the shear layer, with a maximum of 0.025 at x = 20h, y = 1h, where the shock-cell structure ends. It sits on the high-speed side of the shear layer and is nearly zero on the centreline.
    - ṽṽ peaks along the centreline because the shock cells flap, with a maximum of 0.027 at 18.5h; the corresponding rms is about 15% of U_j.
    - ũṽ is antisymmetric about the centreline, with a maximum of 0.015 at 18.5h. Unlike low-speed jets and wakes (Husain & Zaman 1981; Cantwell & Coles 1983) it does not alternate in sign between the two shear layers, except beyond x/h ≈ 20.
    - All the periodic stresses fall rapidly after ≈20h. The authors therefore read them as mostly a product of shock-cell flapping, with stress concentrations at the vortex locations and vortex centres on the half-velocity line (p. 147–148).
  - *Random stresses at phase 135°* (Fig. 21, p. 148–149):
    - Their levels are comparable to the periodic ones. Maxima are 0.034 (u″u″), 0.015 (v″v″) and 0.012 (u″v″), at the end of the potential core beyond ≈16h, where the shear layers merge.
    - They spread over both the high- and the low-speed sides of the shear layer.
    - They are nearly zero inside the shock cells, so phase jitter has not seriously affected the random stresses (p. 149).
  - *Vortex and random-stress peak coincide* (Fig. 22, p. 148–149): in a frame moving at 0.5U_j, the vortex at the end of the 3rd cell (x/h ≈ 9.5, y/h ≈ 1.2, read) sits on the ⟨u″u″⟩ peak. Husain & Zaman (1981) saw the same in very-low-speed jets.
  - *Global totals* (Fig. 23, p. 150):
    - u′u′ reaches 0.021 at 19h (intensity 0.145), with local maxima at the shock-cell ends on the high-speed side.
    - v′v′ is periodic-dominated everywhere, with a maximum of 0.019 at 16h (intensity 0.14).
    - u′v′ peaks at 0.0086 at 20.5h, where the shear layers merge.
  - *Centreline* (Figs 24–25, p. 150–151):
    - u′u′ grows exponentially, fitted by 5 × 10⁻⁵ e^{0.25x/h}, from 0.0001 (1% intensity) to ≈0.015 at 23h. It is dominated by random fluctuations, so the shock cells do not move significantly along the axis.
    - v′v′ is dominated by the periodic part up to the end of the potential core, with its maximum at the 5th cell (x/h = 15). Within that cell the intensity swings between 0.035 and 0.14. At x/h ≈ 20 the periodic part collapses and the random part rises.
  - *Shear layer at the half-velocity points* (Figs 26–27, p. 151–152): periodic and random contributions are of the same order up to x/h ≈ 20, after which the periodic part collapses.
  - *Energy share* (p. 151): "a significant part (50%)" of the turbulent energy in the noise-producing region 0 < x/h < 20 comes from the periodic large-scale structures.
  - *Control* (p. 151–152): suppressing these structures may suppress turbulence and so far-field noise. The authors cite promising microjet air/water injection results (Krothapalli, Greska & Arakeri 2002).
- **Conclusions** (p. 153):
  - Strong coherent vortices dominate the shear layer where screech is generated.
  - The birth of the screech wave results from high shear-layer fluctuations at a shock-cell end, caused by the interaction of a concentrated coherent vortex with the shock cell.
  - Screech amplitude is "largely" set by coherent vorticity strength plus shock-cell strength, and reducing either should reduce screech.
  - Periodic turbulence energy drops "precipitously" where the streamwise vortices become prominent.

## Setup / method
- **Facility** (p. 124): blowdown compressed-air facility of the Fluid Mechanics Research Laboratory, Florida State University.
  - A reciprocating compressor fills 10 m³ of storage at up to 14 MPa. A 450 kW resistive heater bank can reach stagnation temperatures of 700 K. The M_j 1.69 jet runs continuously for about 30 min.
  - The jet exhausts into a "quiet surrounding at ambient conditions". No anechoic treatment is described.
- **Nozzle** (p. 124): rectangular C-D, exit h × w = 10 × 40 mm (AR 4).
  - The short-dimension contour was designed by the method of characteristics for M_d = 1.44. The long-dimension walls are parallel downstream of the throat.
  - A 76 mm round supply pipe blends into the rectangular section. Lip thickness is not given.
- **Conditions** (p. 124, 131–135):

  | Quantity | Value |
  |---|---|
  | M_j for the PIV | 1.69 (underexpanded, at the screech-amplitude maximum); 1.74 for the circulation comparison only (Fig. 18) |
  | Ideally expanded reference | M_j 1.44 (spectrum, half-widths) |
  | Acoustic sweep | M_j 1.2–1.8 (Figs 7–8) |
  | T₀ | 336 ± 2 K (relative error 0.6%), chosen to avoid condensation in humid Florida air (also printed as 335 K) |
  | Plenum pressure | held within ±2 kPa |
  | U_j | 495 m s⁻¹ at M_j 1.69, ±3 m s⁻¹ (< 1%) |
  | Re | 4.3 × 10⁵ on h and mean exit velocity |
  | Exit state | laminar boundary layers, top-hat velocity profile |
  | NPR | not given; ≈4.86 at M_j 1.69, ≈3.37 at M_d and 2.42–5.75 over M_j 1.2–1.8 (my isentropic conversions) |
  | Screech frequency | not given in Hz; ≈4.5 kHz at M_j 1.69 from St ≈ 0.090 (my arithmetic: 0.090 × 495/0.010) |

- **Coordinates** (p. 124): origin at the centre of the exit plane, X along the axis, Y across the short dimension and Z across the long one. Measurements are in the central (X, Y)-plane and in (Y, Z) cross-planes at selected x/h.
- **Seeding** (p. 124): submicron (≈0.3 µm) oil droplets from a modified Wright nebulizer, mixed into the supply 1.25 m upstream of the nozzle; 1–5 µm smoke from a Rosco fog generator in the ambient air. The authors argue that the large ambient particles entrained into the jet stay mostly in subsonic regions, so they do not cause lag problems.
- **Stereo PIV hardware** (p. 124–127; Figs 2–4):
  - Two Kodak ES1.0 CCD cameras, 1008 × 1018 px, 9 µm pixels, 30 Hz maximum frame rate. Lenses are 58.37 mm, designed for the laser wavelength. Up to 128 image pairs are acquired per run.
  - Spectra-Physics PIV-400 dual-cavity, frequency-doubled Nd:YAG laser; pulse separation Δt = 1–1.5 µs.
  - Scheimpflug mounts with two remotely controlled motors, the sensor centred on the rotation axis. The angle between the camera axes is about 90°, chosen for the best 3-D reconstruction accuracy (Alkislar 2001).
  - The calibration removes perspective effects and maps between the two views.
  - Two imaging modes: same-side (used for the (Y, Z) cross-planes) and opposite-side (Fig. 3, p. 127).
- **PIV processing** (p. 125–126): the "true resolution", mesh-free algorithm of Lourenco & Krothapalli (2000).
  - Particle masking with a gradient-based threshold removes background, reflections and truncated particles, and the bias of standard FFT correlation.
  - Adaptive windows correlate a smaller window against a larger one, zero-padded, to reduce loss of pairs.
  - Particle images are then paired by group displacement, with direct spatial correlation and Gaussian sub-pixel interpolation.
  - Velocity is fitted by least squares with a second-order polynomial (eq. 1) on an unstructured set of displacements. Windows grow until at least ten displacement values are included, which gives second-order accurate derivatives.
  - Error: 0.03 px conservatively. The maximum displacement is at least 6 px, so the relative error is 0.5%, about ±2.5 m s⁻¹ at 495 m s⁻¹ (p. 126).
  - Vector spacing and final resolution are not stated. The field of view implies about 0.1 mm px⁻¹ (105 mm over ≈1018 px; my arithmetic).
- **Stitching** (p. 126): four zones of 65 × 105 mm with 30 mm overlaps, blended with linearly varying weights to hide edge errors. The (X, Y) field covers 0 ≤ x/h ≤ 32.5 and |y/h| ≤ 3.5 (p. 146).
- **Phase locking** (§2.4, p. 128–130; Fig. 4):
  - A microphone "in the vicinity of the nozzle exit" is band-pass filtered and fed to a custom frequency tracker and pulse generator (400 Hz – 12 kHz).
  - The unit emits a trigger train in phase with the tone, divides it by up to 10,000 to match the 30 Hz laser and camera, and delays it by up to 0.1 s in 0.1 µs steps, with ±1 ns timing accuracy (p. 129).
  - M = 16 phases at 22.5° intervals. Only half the cycle (0°–157.5°) is measured; the other half is the mirror image of the first in the plane of symmetry (p. 130, 140).
  - The mirroring was validated by comparing measured phase 90° with reflected phase 270° (Fig. 5, x/h 4–19): an "excellent match" in u, and in v, whose range is six times smaller (p. 130–131).
  - Each phase average uses about 35 image pairs. Preliminary tests showed that N = 30 converges to a mean error of 5% of the maximum velocity at 90% confidence (p. 128–129).
  - The global mean uses 560 samples = 16 × 35, of which half are mirrored copies (p. 129).
  - This is non-time-resolved PIV. The method needs one steady, trackable tone, and it assumes the oscillation is exactly antisymmetric about y = 0.
- **Pressure probes** (§2.2, p. 127–128):
  - Total and static (Pinckney 1975 design; Wishart 1995) surveys along the centreline and at several stations in the minor-axis plane. Their combination gives the Mach number distribution, which is converted to velocity assuming isentropic flow (p. 134).
  - Three-axis Compumotor traverse, 2 µm resolution. Validyne DP-15 transducers (1 MPa and 70 kPa ranges), sampled at 512 Hz and averaged over 512 samples.
  - The probes were aligned with a He–Ne laser.
- **Acoustics** (§2.3, p. 128): B&K 4136 quarter-inch microphone, 2633 preamplifier, Nexus amplifier with a 20 Hz – 100 kHz band-pass.
  - 320k samples at 250 kHz (1.28 s), FFT with a rectangular window, 60 Hz resolution, ±1 dB (instrument and repeatability) for levels above 120 dB.
  - Block count, overlap and averaging are not stated. 60 Hz bins at 250 kHz imply blocks of about 4,200 samples, so about 75 blocks (my inference).
  - The position is given only for Fig. 6 (R = 25h, χ = 90°).
- **Analyses and their data needs:**
  - *Mean, rms and half-widths:* from the phase-locked ensemble (560 samples) and the probes. No time resolution is needed.
  - *Triple decomposition* (eqs. 2–9): the phase average comes from the triggered ensemble, so it needs the trigger, not time-resolved data. The random part u″ also contains vortex-to-vortex variations in shape and position, which cannot be separated (p. 146).
  - *Coherent-structure area:* threshold R_uu > 0.6 on the random fluctuations at one phase (eq. 13), from N_m ≈ 35 realisations.
  - *Circulation:* eq. 12 over that area.
  - *Convection velocity:* vorticity-weighted mean velocity over the same area (eq. 14).
  - *Spectra:* time-resolved microphone, tone frequencies and levels only.
- **Modelling side:** the paper runs no simulation or stability analysis.
  - Tam's (1988) shock-cell and screech formula for rectangular jets (vortex-sheet shock spacing with an exit-size correction, weakest-link frequency) is used with the measured U_c to draw the Fig. 7 curves (p. 132). The formula, a∞ and the size correction used are not printed.
  - Powell's (1953) stationary sources at the shock-cell ends are invoked for the frequency (p. 133–134).
  - Morris, Giridharan & Lilley (1990, instability-wave model of compressible mixing layers) are cited for the link between instability waves and large structures (p. 123).
  - Manning & Lele (2000, 2-D numerical shock–vortex interaction) are cited for radiated amplitude scaling with instability-wave amplitude and shock strength. The authors argue from this that vortex strength sets screech amplitude (p. 123).
  - Husain & Hussain (1993, simulations and experiments on elliptic jets) are cited for rib formation (p. 140, 144).
  - None of these is compared with the PIV fields.

## Key equations
Eqs. 2–9 and 11–14 checked on rendered pages; eq. 1 from the text layer. No eq. 10 is printed: the numbering jumps from 9 to 11.

- **PIV velocity fit** (eq. 1, p. 125): $u=ax^2+bx+cy^2+dy+exy+f$, fitted by least squares to at least ten displacement values. Derivatives come from differentiating the fit.
- **Convective Mach number as defined** (p. 122): $M_c=U_j/(a_1+a_2)$, for a jet into still air, with a₁ and a₂ the jet and ambient sound speeds.
- **Triple decomposition** (eqs. 2–3, p. 128): $q(\boldsymbol x,t)=\langle q(\boldsymbol x,\tau)\rangle+q''(\boldsymbol x,t)$ and $\langle q(\boldsymbol x,\tau)\rangle=\bar q(\boldsymbol x)+\tilde q(\boldsymbol x,\tau)$. τ is the phase delay within one screech cycle.
- **Phase and global averages** (eqs. 4–5, p. 128): $\langle q(\boldsymbol x,\tau)\rangle_m=\frac{1}{N_m}\sum_{i=1}^{N_m}q(\boldsymbol x,t_i+\tau_m)$ and $\bar q(\boldsymbol x)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\langle q(\boldsymbol x,\tau)\rangle_m$. t_i is the microphone reference time, M = 16 and N_m ≈ 35.
- **Second-order moments** (eqs. 6–9, p. 129):
  - Random part at one phase (eq. 6): $\langle q''r''\rangle_m=\langle qr\rangle_m-\langle q\rangle_m\langle r\rangle_m$.
  - Periodic part (eq. 7): $\overline{\tilde q\tilde r}=\overline{\langle q\rangle\langle r\rangle}-\bar q\bar r$.
  - Total product (eq. 8): $\overline{qr}=\bar q\bar r+\overline{\tilde q\tilde r}+\overline{q''r''}$.
  - Total Reynolds stress (eq. 9): $\overline{q'r'}=\overline{\tilde q\tilde r}+\overline{\langle q''r''\rangle}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}(\tilde q\tilde r+\langle q''r''\rangle)_m$.
- **Vorticity components** (p. 136, 140): $\omega_x=\partial w/\partial y-\partial v/\partial z$ (cross-planes) and $\omega_z=\partial\langle v\rangle/\partial x-\partial\langle u\rangle/\partial y$ (phase-averaged, minor-axis plane).
- **Half-velocity width** (eq. 11, p. 135): $y_{0.5}=k(x-x_0)$, the distance from the centreline to where the mean velocity is U_j/2. k = 0.065 (minor) and 0.058 (major) for M_j 1.44; 0.11 and 0.33 in two ranges for M_j 1.69 (minor axis).
- **Circulation** (eq. 12, p. 142): $\Gamma_A(\boldsymbol x_0)=\iint_A\omega_z\,\mathrm dA$, with A the region where R_uu(x₀, x) > 0.6.
- **Two-point correlation of the random part** (eq. 13, p. 142): $R_{uu}(\boldsymbol x_0,\boldsymbol x)=\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_m}u''(\boldsymbol x_0)\,u''(\boldsymbol x)}{\big(\sum_{i=1}^{N_m}u''(\boldsymbol x_0)^2\big)^{1/2}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{N_m}u''(\boldsymbol x)^2\big)^{1/2}}$, at a single phase m.
- **Vorticity-weighted convection velocity** (eq. 14, p. 143, after Shih, Lourenco & Krothapalli 1995): $\boldsymbol u_c=\dfrac{1}{\Gamma_A}\iint_A\boldsymbol u\,\omega_z\,\mathrm dA$.
- **Centreline growth fit** (Fig. 24, p. 150): $\overline{u'u'}/U_j^2\approx5\times10^{-5}\,e^{0.25x/h}$. Check: at x/h = 23 it gives 0.0157, against the ≈0.015 maximum quoted (my arithmetic).

## Notation
- h, w: nozzle exit height (10 mm, minor dimension) and width (40 mm); AR = w/h = 4.
- M_d: design Mach number (1.44); M_j: fully expanded Mach number; U_j: fully expanded velocity (495 m s⁻¹ at M_j 1.69).
- St = f h/U_j; R: microphone distance; χ: "nozzle inlet angle" of the microphone (90° = sideline).
- X, Y, Z: axial, minor-axis and major-axis directions; the minor-axis plane is (X, Y).
- q̄: global mean; q̃: periodic component; q″: random component; ⟨·⟩: average at constant phase; τ_m: m-th phase delay; M = 16 phases; N_m ≈ 35 samples per phase; q′ = q̃ + q″.
- y₀.₅, z₀.₅: half-velocity half-widths; k: their growth rate; x₀: virtual origin; X_c: axis crossover.
- ω_x, ω_z: streamwise and spanwise vorticity; Γ_A: circulation over region A; R_uu: correlation coefficient of the random u″ at one phase; u_c (U_c): convection velocity.

## Relevance to this project
This is the standard quantitative flow-field study of a screeching rectangular jet. It sits between [Krothapalli 1986](krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md) (schlieren, hot wire, AR 16.7) and modern modal work on non-axisymmetric jets ([Mazharmanesh 2025](mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md); [Ivelja 2024](ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)). Its three lasting claims are:
1. coherent vorticity at a particular shock-cell end sets screech amplitude;
2. screech-driven streamwise vortices cause axis switching;
3. a flapping rectangular jet convects at about 0.5U_j, constant in x.

Each bears on the group's AR 2 rectangular and elliptical data and on how PIV is phase-resolved.

**Reading it critically**
- **The amplitude claim rests on one phase and two conditions.**
  - Γ_A is compared at phase 135° only, for M_j 1.69 and 1.74 (Fig. 18). Each phase is referenced to its own microphone, so at a fixed phase the two jets' structures need not sit at the same place relative to their own shock cells.
  - Fig. 8's fit puts M_j 1.74 only about 1–3 dB below the peak (my reading), yet its 3rd-cell circulation is less than half. At x/h 4.4 the "quieter" jet has the higher circulation.
  - A phase-tracked comparison (Γ of the same structure as it crosses the 3rd-cell end) at several M_j along the Fig. 8 parabola would test the claim properly.
  - [Raman 1998](raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) (p. 49, 64) writes amplitude as growth × shock interaction × feedback efficiency × receptivity. He shows that shock strength keeps rising while amplitude plateaus and then collapses, so one local strength cannot fix the amplitude.
- **Consistency with shock leakage, and its refinement.**
  - Fig. 1's wavefront born at the 3rd-cell end as a vortex arrives, and the circulation peak there, fit the Manning & Lele / Suzuki & Lele leakage picture the paper cites (p. 123).
  - They agree with [Krothapalli 1986](krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md) (source at the end of the 3rd cell, x/D ≈ 7, p. 123). They also agree with [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md), where coherent vorticity in an AR 2 ellipse peaks just before the 3rd shock (p. 2747).
  - [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) later revised the "leak where vorticity peaks" idea (p. 26–30). Leakage happens at the saddle *between* vortices, and vortex strength decides which cell leaks first. A circulation peak at the 3rd cell is therefore evidence for where leakage can start, not proof that the emission sits on the vortex.
  - Raman's two-microphone localisation in a rectangular jet (3rd shock at M_j 1.45, 4th at 1.75, cited in [Mercier 2017](mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) p. 204) and this paper's 3rd cell at M_j 1.69 come from different nozzles (AR 5 convergent against AR 4 C-D), so they are not directly in conflict.
- **The mirroring enforces symmetry.**
  - Building the second half-cycle by reflection (p. 130) assumes the periodic motion is exactly antisymmetric at the fundamental. The cycle-averaged fields in the minor-axis plane (global mean, global stresses) are therefore symmetric about y = 0 **by construction**. The "strong symmetry" of the normal stresses (p. 147) is partly imposed.
  - Any phase-locked varicose content at f_s would be mapped with the wrong sign. The validation is one phase pair, by eye (Fig. 5).
  - The "symmetric oscillation" around the first few cells (p. 140) is read from ⟨v⟩, which includes the steady shock-cell v̄; that v̄ is antisymmetric in sign about the axis, so the pattern looks symmetric. My reading is that this pattern may be mostly the mean shock structure. The periodic ṽ there, which would settle it, is not shown.
- **The St jump at M_d is explained by a fitted U_c.**
  - The 0.63U_j overexpanded value is said to come from phase-resolved PIV (p. 132), but no overexpanded PIV is shown. The claim that flapping persists in the overexpanded regime is also unshown.
  - The tone vanishes between M_j 1.42 and 1.46 (Fig. 8 minima of ≈102–104 dB, read). The jump therefore crosses a no-screech gap, where the sign of the exit pressure mismatch flips; it is not a hysteretic stage jump.
  - This is consistent with [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) (p. 18–19): AR ≥ 4 jets do not stage. Fig. 7 shows one smooth flapping branch on each side of M_d over M_j 1.2–1.8.
  - It qualifies the Raman/Gutmark reading that rectangular-jet St jumps are symmetric ↔ antisymmetric switches ([Raman 1998](raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) p. 54–55), at least for C-D nozzles.
  - [Gojon 2019](gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md) (p. 26) places these data in, or just below, the first antisymmetric band of a planar vortex sheet.
- **Convection velocity.**
  - U_c ≈ 0.5U_j, constant in x, from the vorticity-weighted centroid of identified structures (p. 143–144). This matches Krothapalli 1986's 0.5U₀ from phase-locked schlieren (p. 138–139) and is close to Raman's 0.55U_j for rectangular jets (p. 60–62). It is well below the 0.7U_j of Tam's formula ([tam-1988](tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md)) and the 0.65U_j of the AR 2 ellipse ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md)).
  - It contrasts with round jets, where [Mercier 2017](mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) (p. 214, 224–226) finds U_c rising from about 0.55 to 0.7U_j along the jet. The methods differ: structure centroids over 4–18h here, against phase slopes and wavenumber spectra there.
  - The constancy claim's data are in the thesis, not the paper.
- **"50% of the turbulent energy is produced by" the periodic structures** (p. 151). What is shown is the periodic share of the normal stresses (Figs 24–27), not production. The random part u″ also contains vortex-to-vortex jitter, which the authors acknowledge cannot be separated (p. 146).
- **Axis switching.**
  - Screech-driven switching agrees with [Krothapalli 1986](krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md) (crossover moves upstream at peak screech, p. 133) and with [Samimy 1998](samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md) (AR 3 Mach 2 jets switch only off-design, p. 6–7). It also agrees with [Suzuki 2026](suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md) (stronger forcing moves the switch upstream, p. 6).
  - The mechanism here (ribs from deformed rolls) is inferred from Husain & Hussain (1993), not shown dynamically. At x/h = 6 the streamwise vortices are attributed to nozzle imperfections instead (p. 136–140), so two sources are mixed.
  - [Marshall 2024](marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets.md) (p. 13–17) finds corner vortices are not linear streaks. The stationary pairs at 16h could still be lift-up streaks organised by the flapping ([Nogueira 2019](nogueira-2019-large-scale-streaky-structures-jets.md); my link).
  - The claim that excitation alone makes an ideally expanded rectangular jet switch is untested here.
- **Harmonic at 90°.** Fig. 6's first harmonic (≈132 dB) slightly exceeds the fundamental (≈129 dB) at the sideline (read). This fits Powell's directivity for an antisymmetric source array, with the harmonic lobe at 90° ([Raman 1998](raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) p. 49). It contrasts with [Krothapalli 1986](krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md) (p. 125), who found the fundamental dominant at 90° in an AR 16.7 convergent jet.
- **Spreading and coupling citations.** [Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md) cites this paper for higher spreading under out-of-phase twin-jet coupling, but this paper is about a single jet flapping (see that note, p. 1, 16–17).

**Methods and data for the lab workflow (CLAUDE.md)**
- **Non-time-resolved PIV (the paper's case).**
  - Microphone-triggered phase locking is the older alternative to the group's POD-pair phase recovery. It needs a clean, steady tone and a tracker, and the half-cycle mirroring further needs exact antisymmetry. In exchange it gets every phase without a dominant POD pair.
  - With POD pairs (or symmetry-imposed POD on the odd part, [Nogueira 2021, twin jets](nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md)) the same triple decomposition follows from a rebuild of the pair, as in [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md). Thousands of snapshots binned by POD phase give far more than 35 samples per phase.
  - With 35 samples, the R_uu > 0.6 threshold that sets A and hence Γ is noisy. The standard error of a correlation coefficient of 0.6 is about 0.1 for N = 35 (my estimate).
  - The R_uu-region circulation and the vorticity-weighted U_c (eqs. 12–14) are cheap to repeat on POD-pair-phased fields of the group's AR 2 jets. They would test whether the 3rd-cell circulation peak, and its link to amplitude, holds at AR 2.
- **Cross-plane stereo PIV.** Cross-plane stereo is what planar symmetry-imposed POD cannot do: see the AA/AS blind spot in [Nogueira 2021, twin jets](nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md) (Table 1, p. 7). Cross-planes at several x/h, phased the same way, would show whether the streamwise pairs are fixed or phase-dependent. Fig. 19 suggests both.
- **Time-resolved data and the SPOD → reconstruction → Hilbert POD pipeline.**
  - The amplitude hypothesis is really a dynamic statement, and the pipeline can test it directly. Run SPOD on TR schlieren or TR PIV, rebuild the f_s band, and use HPOD or a Hilbert transform in time to track the instantaneous coherent-structure amplitude at the 3rd-cell end. Compare that with the Hilbert envelope of a near-field microphone, with the acoustic lag.
  - The phase-locked method cannot do this. Any amplitude modulation, intermittency or phase wander goes into u″, so part of the "random" stress at vortex centres (Fig. 22) may be coherent-structure jitter. HPOD would separate it.
  - Nothing in the paper resolves intermittency or switching, but AR ≥ 4 jets are expected to hold one flapping mode ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) p. 18–19).
- **Seeding.** The paper puts 0.3 µm oil in the jet and 1–5 µm smoke in the ambient; [Henderson 2002](henderson-2002-flow-structure-supersonic-impinging-jets.md) does the reverse (p. 2). The Stokes response of 0.3 µm oil is τ_p ≈ 0.25 µs (my estimate), well inside the [Ragni 2011](ragni-2011-particle-tracer-response-shocks-piv.md) criteria. The PIV–probe agreement in Fig. 11 is the paper's own lag check.

**Modelling side and the PyFR staging LES**
- The paper's only model is the Powell/Tam frequency relation with U_c as an input. With the measured U_c ≈ 0.5U_j and L_s ≈ 3.2h it reproduces St to about 1% (my check above). The Tam vortex-sheet spacing (≈3.0h) is about 6% shorter than the measured mean, a minor point.
- The physical argument leans on Manning & Lele's 2-D shock–vortex simulations: radiated pressure scales with instability-wave amplitude and shock strength (p. 123). Modern theory replaces this with:
  - loop gain in resonance models ([Mancinelli 2021](mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md));
  - absolute-instability growth set by the nozzle and shear-layer thickness ([Nogueira 2021, absolute instability](nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md));
  - leakage geometry ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md)).
- None of these predicts amplitude yet, so the paper's correlation remains one of the few measured handles.
- The PyFR LES (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`) is a round convergent jet, but the diagnostics transfer: phase-binned triple decomposition, the circulation of the structure crossing the radiating cell (3rd for A1, 4th for A2 per Edgington-Mitchell 2021), and the vorticity-weighted U_c(x).
  - The paper's hypothesis predicts higher coherent circulation at the radiating cell in whichever of A1 and A2 is louder, at the same NPR inside a bistable band. That is a cheap, discrete check on converged states.
  - LES can also show whether U_c is constant in x, as here, or rising, as in Mercier.

**Rocket noise.** Screech is absent at full scale ([Lubert 2022](lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md)). The paper's closing point (p. 151–152) links the periodic structures to turbulence and noise, and cites microjet air/water injection (Krothapalli et al. 2002, 2003) as a way to suppress them. That is a lab-scale relative of launch-pad water suppression; the link to rocket-noise models is mine.
