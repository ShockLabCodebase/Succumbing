# The role of screech tones in mixing of an underexpanded rectangular jet

## Summary
An experimental study of underexpanded air jets from a convergent rectangular nozzle of aspect ratio 16.7 (3 mm × 50 mm), with a 5 mm × 50 mm (AR 10) nozzle for comparison, over NPR R = 2–5.8 (M_j ≈ 1.05–1.8) (p. 120–121). Spark schlieren in both central planes, a near-field microphone, a pitot tube (x/D < 15) and hot-wire/hot-film traverses (x/D = 15–160) are used to see how screech changes the jet. At the NPR of loudest screech (R = 3.8) a strong, organised cylindrical wave system radiates alternately from the two sides of the jet from a source near the end of the third shock cell (x/D ≈ 7), and the jet spreads much faster in the minor-axis (x, y) plane (total angle 36° vs 20–25° at R = 2.7 and 5.4) (p. 123). The self-excitation triggers large-scale structures in both planes but at different places: minor-axis (flapping) structures just downstream of the source (x/D ≈ 10), and major-axis sideways oscillations only beyond x/D ≈ 60, peaking at x/D ≈ 80 (p. 138–139, 142). Both have St ≈ 0.12 when each is scaled by its own nozzle dimension (D or L), so their frequencies differ by the aspect ratio; the authors relate this to Crighton's elliptic vortex-sheet stability (p. 140, 142). Far downstream (x/D > 80) mean and rms profiles become self-similar in each plane, but with different shapes, so the jet does not become axisymmetric within 160 D (p. 140–142).

## Key findings
- **Screech strongest at R ≈ 3.8–4** (p. 126, Fig. 8): fundamental amplitude at θ = 90° is bell-shaped in R, near zero below R ≈ 3, peak at R ≈ 4, falling to near zero by R ≈ 5.3. Strong effect on the jet only for 3 < R < 4.5 (p. 123, 141–142). Tones are present at all R above critical (1.9) (p. 141).
- **Fundamental dominates the near field** at θ = 90° and 140° (Fig. 5, p. 125–126), with a weaker first harmonic at 2f. This contradicts Powell's claim that the harmonic dominates at 90° (p. 125). The authors conclude one frequency characterises the wave system.
- **Spreading angle** (Fig. 2, p. 123): total spread in the x, y plane ≈ 25° (R = 2.7), 36° (R = 3.8), 20° (R = 5.4).
- **Wave pattern** (p. 123–124): AR 16.7 shows a double cylindrical wave pattern (a second, much shorter wavelength); AR 10 shows a single pattern (Fig. 4), so the double pattern seems linked to large aspect ratio; round jets show none (p. 124). At R = 5.4, Mach-wave radiation at ≈ 45° to the axis from near the exit (p. 123).
- **Source location** at the end of the third shock cell, x/D ≈ 7, found from the centre of the wavefront arcs (p. 123); for AR 10 at the point where the third cell breaks up (p. 124–125). Large lateral (flapping) shock-cell oscillation is visible in Fig. 4(a) (p. 124).
- **Third shock cell is intermittent:** the time-averaged pitot shows two clear cells for x/D < 6; phase-locked schlieren (refs 12, 13) show a complete third cell for only part of each cycle (p. 127). Pitot decay accelerates after x/D ≈ 8; subsonic beyond x/D = 15 (p. 127).
- **Streamwise vortices** in the x, z (major-axis) view for x/D < 10, with spacing growing downstream (Fig. 3, p. 123–124). At R = 3.8 the major-axis width first necks down then grows (p. 124).
- **Strouhal scaling** (Fig. 7, p. 126): St = fD/U₀ falls monotonically with R; St = K R^{-3/2}, K = 0.89, fits both nozzles.
- **Screech wavelength** (Fig. 6, p. 126): λ/D is linear in √(R − R_c) with a slope independent of nozzle width and aspect ratio, consistent with Powell's empirical line.
- **Centreline velocity** (Fig. 11, p. 129): U_c/U₀ ∝ x^{-1} beyond x/D ≈ 80 (the "axisymmetric" region) for all three R; a planar-type decay region precedes it. Higher R (denser jet, T_j/T_∞ ≈ 0.62, ρ_j/ρ_∞ ≈ 1.61 at R = 5.4) decays more slowly; R = 3.8 decays faster than R = 2.7 for x > 80 D because of the screech excitation (p. 129).
- **Half-width growth, minor axis** (Figs 16–17, p. 132–133): y_{1/2} = k(x − x₀) with k = 0.105, x₀ = −5.3 D (R = 2.7) and k = 0.08, x₀ = −21.3 D (R = 5.4): a ≈ 25% drop in spreading with R in the absence of strong tones, attributed to density ratio (as in Brown & Roshko). At R = 3.8, k ≈ 0.16 for 40 < x/D < 80 and ≈ 0.1 beyond, so two virtual origins and two similarity profiles (Fig. 13). Subsonic rectangular jets have k ≈ 0.1 independent of Mach (Hsia et al.; p. 132).
- **Half-width, major axis** (p. 133): z_{1/2} decreases up to x/D ≈ 80, then grows linearly. The axis crossover moves downstream with R (two-dimensionality persists longer), except at R = 3.8 where it moves well upstream and x₀ lies downstream of the exit. Beyond 80 D the y and z half-widths are parallel or diverge, so no true axisymmetry, unlike the incompressible AR 10 result of Trentacosta & Sforza (axisymmetric beyond 150 D) (p. 133–134).
- **Self-similarity** (Figs 12–15, p. 130–131): R = 2.7 minor-axis profiles similar from 40 D and match Gutmark & Wygnanski's AR 38 incompressible profile; similarity profiles narrow with increasing R; major-axis profiles similar beyond 80 D with an axisymmetric-jet-like shape.
- **Centreline rms ε = ⟨e⟩/E₀** (Fig. 18, p. 134–135): rises to a peak at x/D ≈ 10 then falls, like an unexcited subsonic jet (M 0.8 reference). At R = 3.8 there is a second distinct peak at x/D = 80, then faster decay; ε ∝ x^{-1/2} for 15 < x/D < 50. Small bump at x/D ≈ 90 for R = 2.7 (weak excitation). Rms level in the similarity region increases with R.
- **Minor-axis structures at the screech frequency** (Fig. 21, p. 137–138): centreline hot-wire spectra for x/D < 40 peak at 17.5 kHz, equal to the acoustic fundamental; energy moves to low frequencies downstream.
- **Major-axis structures** (Figs 19–21, p. 136–138): beyond x/D ≈ 50 schlieren of the x, z plane suggests sideways oscillation parallel to the long dimension with wavelength ≈ 8 cm, no repeatable phase pattern between images. The oscillogram at x/D = 80 shows a period ≈ 1 ms, and the spectrum has a peak at 1048 Hz. With U_c = 151 m/s at x/D = 80 and u_c ≈ 0.6 U_c (range 0.5–0.7), the spacing gives ≈ 1118 Hz (p. 138). Ratio of the two frequencies ≈ 16.7 = AR (p. 138).
- **Phase-locked schlieren, AR 10, R = 3.5** (Fig. 22, p. 138–139): mic-triggered, 10 µs between frames, half a cycle shown (the two sides are exactly out of phase, i.e. flapping). Vortex spacing ≈ 2.0 cm, convection ≈ 215 m/s (0.5 U₀), giving a passage frequency of 10 750 Hz vs the 10 795 Hz tone.
- **St of both structure families = 0.12** (p. 140): 17 500 Hz × 3 mm / U₀ and 1048 Hz × 50 mm / U₀. The authors infer the most unstable St is the same in both planes but growth rates differ (Crighton 1973): flapping across the minor axis grows fast, sideways oscillation along the major axis slowly (p. 139–140, 142).
- **Literature compilation** (Table 1, p. 140): St at maximum screech is 0.11–0.14 for rectangular jets (AR 1.7–16.7, scaled by the small dimension) and 0.24–0.28 for round jets (scaled by diameter).

## Setup / method
- **Facility** (p. 121): high-pressure blow-down air supply; settling chamber 1.75 m long × 0.6 m diameter; adapter with six screens 5 cm apart; adapter-to-exit area ratio ≈ 40. Each exit is preceded by a 40 mm smooth rectangular channel. The 3 mm nozzle is a single central lobe of a multi-lobed nozzle (Fig. 1). Exposed nozzle surfaces covered with 7 mm acoustic insulation to reduce reflections. Same model and supply as Krothapalli et al. 1981 (JFM 107).
- **Nozzles:** convergent rectangular, 50 mm × 3 mm (AR 16.7; most data) and 50 mm × 5 mm (AR 10) (p. 121). Low exit turbulence, flat exit total-pressure profile (p. 120); at x/D = 0.1 pitot pressure is flat over two-thirds of the width and within 5% of stagnation at the centre (Fig. 10, p. 128).
- **Conditions** (p. 120, 122):

  | Quantity | Value |
  |---|---|
  | Stagnation pressure p₀ | 30–85 psia (±0.02 psia) |
  | NPR R | 2–5.8 (critical 1.9) |
  | M_j (fully expanded) | 1.05–1.8 |
  | T₀ | ambient ±0.5 °C (cold jet) |
  | Re = M a D/ν (ambient) | 7.2 × 10⁴ – 1.0 × 10⁵ |
  | Re at jet exit (isentropic) | 9.35 × 10⁴ – 2.44 × 10⁵ |
  | Detailed hot-wire cases (3 mm) | R = 2.7 (U₀ 393 m/s), 3.8 (433 m/s), 5.4 (476 m/s) (Fig. 11) |

- **Coordinates** (p. 122): x streamwise, y across the small dimension D, z along the long dimension L; traverses in the central x, y and x, z planes, x = 40–160 D (hot wire), full traverses to check symmetry.
- **Schlieren** (p. 121–122): conventional single-pass, folded twice, two 25 cm, 3.05 m focal-length spherical mirrors; stroboscopic source, 1.5 µs flash; knife edge parallel to the axis for Figs 2–4 (tuned to show the outer wave field, not shock cells), perpendicular for Fig. 22. Phase-locked sequence triggered by the near-field microphone (p. 138). Fig. 19 is a mosaic of two images per plane taken at different times (p. 136).
- **Acoustics** (p. 125–126): Brüel & Kjær microphone in the near field on a circular path, θ = 90° and 140° spectra; amplitude vs R at θ = 90°.
- **Pitot** (p. 121, 127–128): 0.46 mm OD, 0.25 mm sensing bore, x/D < 15; no conversion to Mach number because of unknown shock losses.
- **Hot wire / hot film** (p. 122): DISA 55M10 CTA with 55M25 linearizer; 55P11 wire (5 µm, 1 mm) downstream; 55R31 wedge hot film (x/D < 40); ≈ 40 kHz frequency response (square-wave test in an M 0.8 jet); overheat 1.8; linearized 20–280 m/s (wire) and 20–300 m/s (film). Because density and total-temperature fluctuations contaminate the signal (Horstmann & Rose), rms is reported as ε = ⟨e⟩/E (or ⟨e⟩/E₀ with E₀ = K U₀ on the centreline), equal to ⟨u⟩/U only at low Mach. Mean and rms from DISA voltmeters with 10 s integration; spectra from a Nicolet 660B analyser (p. 122).
- **Data dependence:** all statistics are single-point, long-time averages; the only temporal analyses are single-probe FFT spectra, oscillograms and one microphone-triggered phase-locked schlieren sequence. No modal decomposition, no multi-point correlation, no time-resolved imaging. Large-structure wavelengths in the major-axis plane are inferred from single spark images.
- **Modelling:** none of their own. Interpretation leans on Powell's feedback loop (p. 120), Crighton's (1973) vortex-sheet stability of an elliptic jet (spatial growth: minor-axis flapping strongly amplified, major-axis sideways mode weakly), Morris & Miller's finite-thickness elliptic calculations (private communication) that confirm Crighton's trends (p. 139), and Brown & Roshko's density-ratio argument for spreading (p. 133, 138).

## Key equations
- Strouhal number (p. 126, 140): $St = f D / U_0$, with $U_0$ the ideally expanded isentropic exit velocity and D the small dimension (or L for the major-axis structures).
- Empirical frequency fit (p. 126): $St = K R^{-3/2}$, $K = 0.89$ (3 mm nozzle data; 5 mm agrees). Check: $R = 3.8$ gives $0.89 \times 3.8^{-1.5} = 0.12$, matching Table 1.
  > Fig. 7's legend prints the curve as "St = R^{-3/2}" without K; the text gives K = 0.89. Verify against the PDF.
- Wavelength (Fig. 6, p. 126): $\lambda/D$ linear in $\sqrt{R - R_c}$, $R_c$ the critical pressure ratio.
  > My estimate, not in the paper: with $f = 17.5$ kHz and $a_\infty \approx 343$ m/s at R = 3.8, $\lambda/D \approx 6.5$ and $\sqrt{R-R_c} \approx 1.38$ ($R_c = 1.893$), so the slope is ≈ 4.7, consistent with the figure (λ/D ≈ 5 at √(R−R_c) = 1).
- Half-width (p. 132): $y_{1/2} = k(x - x_0)$; centreline decay $U_c/U_0 \propto x^{-1}$ for x/D > 80 (p. 129).
- Passage frequency check (p. 138): $f \approx u_c/\lambda_s$, e.g. $0.6 \times 151/0.08 \approx 1.1$ kHz.

## Notation
- D: nozzle small dimension (3 or 5 mm); L: long dimension (50 mm); AR = L/D.
- R: nozzle pressure ratio p₀/p_a; R_c: critical pressure ratio (≈ 1.9).
- U₀: ideally expanded isentropic exit velocity; U_c: measured centreline velocity.
- x, y, z: streamwise, across D, across L. x₀: virtual origin.
- y_{1/2}, z_{1/2}: half-velocity half-widths in the x, y and x, z planes.
- ε = ⟨e⟩/E (or ⟨e⟩/E₀): hot-wire rms voltage ratio used as turbulence intensity.
- θ: microphone polar angle from the downstream jet axis (90° sideline, 140° downstream as stated on p. 126).
  > The θ convention is not defined explicitly; the paper calls 140° "the downstream direction" (p. 126). Check against Fig. 5(b).

## Relevance to this project
This project studies resonance and feedback in supersonic jets, including non-axisymmetric nozzles, from Shock Lab group experiments (CLAUDE.md). This paper is the classic demonstration that screech changes the jet, not just its sound: at peak screech the rectangular jet spreads faster near the nozzle, with a second turbulence peak far downstream, and the excitation organises structures in both planes at frequencies tied by the aspect ratio.

- **Agreement with later work:**
  - The antisymmetric, side-to-side alternating wave system and the out-of-phase shear layers in Fig. 22 are the minor-axis flapping mode found in rectangular and elliptical jets by SPOD and POD: [mazharmanesh-2025](mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), [ivelja-2024](ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) (Crighton-type se₁ flapping, with Floquet LSA), [yeung-2022](yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md) and [jeun-2024](jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md). Raman's review lists rectangular jets as having one antisymmetric mode ([raman-1998](raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md)).
  - The screech data of both nozzles are among the measurements that [tam-1988](tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) uses to validate its rectangular shock-cell and screech formula (its Fig. 7), so the St–R curve here can be compared directly with that prediction.
  - The source near the end of the third shock cell, and the intermittent third cell in phase-locked images, fit the later view that the loop closes at a particular shock-cell interaction region. The modern closure is the upstream guided jet mode rather than free-stream sound ([edgington-mitchell-2018](edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), [edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), [nogueira-2024](nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md)). This paper predates that and uses Powell's external acoustic loop.
  - Mean-flow change caused by the tone (wider similarity profile and two virtual origins at R = 3.8) is an early, long-time-average version of the mean-flow deformation that [mazharmanesh-2026](mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) resolves tone by tone with short-time means. [samimy-1998](samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md) warns that a flapping jet's mean image looks artificially thick, a caveat for the spreading angles here, which come from single spark schlieren.
- **Points of tension or caution:**
  - The paper treats screech as one fundamental at a given R and does not discuss staging or mode switching. The St = 0.89 R^{-3/2} fit is smooth, so any jumps would be hidden; compare the staged rectangular/elliptical jets in [mazharmanesh-2025](mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) (AR 2, staging at NPR ≈ 2.2).
  - The claim that the fundamental dominates at 90° contradicts Powell (p. 125). Later round-jet work generally finds the harmonic strong at sideline angles, so this may depend on aspect ratio or on the near-field position.
  - The "same St = 0.12 in both planes" result rests on one major-axis peak (1048 Hz) at one station and frequencies that decrease downstream as structures grow; the match to the AR could be partly coincidental. It is an inference, not a stability calculation. A modern test would be SPOD of major-axis-plane data, or a Floquet/biglobal LSA of the rectangular mean flow as in [ivelja-2024](ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md).
  - Hot-wire rms in a compressible, cold, high-density jet is only a relative measure (p. 122, 134), so compare trends, not values, with PIV.
  - Streamwise vortices in the major-axis view relate to streaks in rectangular jets ([marshall-2024](marshall-2024-prediction-streaks-rectangular-jets.md)) and to the |m| = 2 streak-flapping link in round jets ([nogueira-2026](nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md)).
- **Methods and data type:** the paper is pre-modal. Today the same questions suit:
  - time-resolved schlieren in both views with SPOD (frequency-by-frequency mode shapes, flapping vs wagging, as in [mazharmanesh-2025](mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md)) and BMD to test whether the low-frequency major-axis structures are tied to the tone by triads ([schmidt-2020-bmd](schmidt-2020-bispectral-mode-decomposition.md));
  - non-time-resolved PIV with POD pairs to phase the flapping mode, as a replacement for the mic-triggered phase-locked schlieren of Fig. 22;
  - the SPOD → reconstruction → Hilbert POD pipeline ([nekkanti-2021](nekkanti-2021-spod-reconstruction-denoising-frequency-time.md), [raiola-2025](raiola-2025-hilbert-pod.md)) to test whether spreading and the far-field rms peak follow the instantaneous tone amplitude, i.e. whether "screech-enhanced mixing" holds moment by moment. The bell-shaped amplitude curve (Fig. 8) gives the expected NPR window (3–4.5) where this would show.
- **Rocket-noise link:** the AR-dependent screech and enhanced mixing are lab-scale cold-jet effects; [lubert-2022](lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) notes that screech is not seen at full rocket scale.
- **Related works cited, candidates for `/add-paper`:** Powell 1953 (three papers: Aero. Q. 4, JASA 25, Proc. Phys. Soc. B66); Hammitt 1961; Glass 1968 (acoustic feedback and jet spread); Krothapalli, Baganoff & Karamcheti 1981 (JFM 107, incompressible rectangular jet); Krothapalli, Baganoff & Hsia 1983 (AIAA 83-0727, screech mechanism); Crighton 1973 (JFM 59, elliptic-jet instability); Gutmark & Wygnanski 1976; Brown & Roshko 1974; Yu & Seiner 1983.
