## Summary
The paper supplies azimuthally decomposed sound-field data for shock-containing jets, so that BBSAN models built on instability waves (wavepackets) can be checked mode by mode rather than against the total spectrum only (p. 2015–2016). Unheated, screeching jets from an 8 mm convergent nozzle at NPR 2.25, 3.10 and 3.67 (M_j 1.14, 1.38, 1.50) were measured in a new anechoic chamber, the SJAF, which the paper describes and qualifies (p. 2016–2018, 2023–2025). Single-microphone far-field spectra at 50D recover the known BBSAN directivity, the Harper-Bourne & Fisher / Tam & Tanna peak-frequency trend and the β⁴ scaling of OASPL (p. 2018–2019). A ring at r = 11D, with one fixed and one traversed microphone, gives the azimuthal cross-spectrum, which is fitted, Fourier transformed in azimuth and summed over ±m (p. 2018, 2024–2026). The axisymmetric mode is not dominant: the ±1 pair leads below St ≈ 0.6–0.7, the ±1 to ±3 pairs are about equal above it, and the number of modes needed to rebuild the total within 1 dB/St rises with frequency, from 2–3 for the primary lobe at the higher NPRs to 4–5 at NPR 2.25 and for the secondary lobe (p. 2019–2021). The authors conclude that Tam's axisymmetric (1987) and Ray & Lele's m ≤ 2 (2007) treatments miss high-frequency BBSAN, and that wavepacket models must predict higher azimuthal modes (p. 2021–2023).

## Key findings
- **Far field, single microphone (R = 50D)** (Figs 2–3, p. 2018–2020):
  - The BBSAN peak frequency falls as NPR and θ rise, the usual trend (p. 2018).
  - OASPL follows the β⁴ law for β⁴ > 0.5 (Fig. 2c, p. 2019). Read from Fig. 2c: OASPL rises from about 113.5 dB at β⁴ ≈ 0.1 to about 120–122 dB at β⁴ ≈ 1–1.6, against a β⁴ line from 120 to 122 dB.
  - Strong screech peaks are present. The nozzle has no screech suppression, so no comparison with screech-free BBSAN data (André et al. 2013; Norum & Seiner 1982; Savarese et al. 2013; Viswanathan et al. 2010) was attempted (p. 2019).
  - Eq. 1 with u_c = 0.7u_j and the Pack–Prandtl vortex-sheet spacing L₁ agrees with the measured directivity at NPR 3.10 and 3.67; a second lobe from the n = 2 shock-cell mode is visible. At NPR 2.25 the measured peak is higher than eq. 1, which the authors attribute to a mismatch between Pack–Prandtl and the real spacing (p. 2019–2020).
  - Mixing noise is not prominent at these sideline and upstream angles (Sinha et al. 2014) (p. 2019).
  - Data are shown only up to St = 1.5, "due to the upper frequency limit of the microphones" (p. 2019).
- **The axisymmetric mode is not dominant** (Fig. 4, p. 2019):
  - The BBSAN lobe appears in every azimuthal mode and follows the known trends with M_j and angle.
  - Below St ≈ 0.6–0.7 the single helical mode (±1) dominates, and successive modes m > 1 fall off in turn. The separation shrinks as frequency rises.
  - m = 0 is "on average 3–4 dB/St" below the dominant mode(s) (p. 2019). The authors link this to subsonic jets, where higher modes gain at high frequency and upstream angles while m = 0 dominates downstream (Jordan & Colonius 2013).
  - At St ≈ 0.6–0.7 and above, m = 1, 2 and 3 contribute about equally at all three NPRs, and the trend continues to higher m (not shown) (p. 2019). This agrees with Kuo et al. (2013) and Vold et al. (2012) for ideally expanded and overexpanded jets (p. 2020).
- **The modal mix depends on where the lobe sits** (Figs 4–5, p. 2020):
  - At NPR 2.25 and θ = 80°, m = 1, 2, 3 are similar on the BBSAN lobe; at NPR 3.67 and θ = 115° they are distinct, with m = 1 strongest. Secondary (n = 2) lobes are more multimodal than primary (n = 1) ones.
  - Eq. 1 with the same inputs fits the n = 1 and n = 2 lobes of every azimuthal mode at NPR 3.10 and 3.67, so the authors infer that the instability waves of all azimuthal modes convect at similar speeds (p. 2020).
  - Because the lobe moves up in frequency as M_j and θ decrease, BBSAN becomes more multimodal in azimuth at low NPR and low θ. At NPR 2.25 the lobe lies above St ≈ 0.7 and the first four nonzero modes contribute about equally. At NPR 3.67, sideline and upstream BBSAN (θ > 90°) is dominated by m = 0, 1, 2, while for θ < 90° modes 3 and 4 are "non-negligible"; NPR 3.10 is similar (p. 2020).
  - This agrees with the non-screeching M_j 1.15 LES of Arroyo & Moreau (2019), so screech "does not seem to affect" the frequency dependence of the azimuthal modes (p. 2020).
- **Low-rank reconstruction** (Fig. 6, p. 2020–2021):
  - "Low rank" here means a rank-N truncation of the azimuthal Fourier basis that is within 1 dB/St of the full-rank data (p. 2020). Modes are added in the order m = 0, ±1, ±2, …, and each ±m pair counts as one mode.
  - m = 0 alone is never enough. Below St ≈ 0.6–0.7, 2–3 modes recover the total, apart from some inconsistencies at NPR 3.10 (p. 2020).
  - The primary lobe at NPR 3.10 and 3.67 also needs only 2–3 modes, but more at lower emission angles: at NPR 3.10, five modes at θ = 70° against three at θ = 100° (p. 2020).
  - At NPR 2.25 the high-frequency lobe needs 4–5 modes throughout (p. 2021).
  - At θ = 95° convergence is fast at low St and slows at high St; the secondary lobe is more complex than the primary (Fig. 6, bottom row, p. 2021).
  - The number of modes needed therefore depends on both the operating point and the observer angle, rising with frequency (p. 2021). The summary puts the range at "approximately 3–5" modes (p. 2022); the abstract says four (p. 2015).
- **Consequences for models** (p. 2021–2023):
  - Tam's (1987) removal of the azimuthal dependence cannot represent the multimodal high-frequency field. Ray & Lele's (2007) truncation at m ≤ 2 "likely contributed" to their "missing sound" at high frequency (p. 2021).
  - Such models had either poor high-frequency amplitudes or empirical matching to the total sound; the authors propose mode-by-mode comparisons, as Cavalieri et al. (2012) did for subsonic jets (p. 2022).
  - The findings fit wavepacket BBSAN models, in which the shocks are axisymmetric and stationary and the wavepacket alone sets frequency and azimuthal content (p. 2022).
  - BBSAN sources move towards the nozzle as frequency rises (Suzuki 2016). PSE wavepackets shorten and peak closer to the nozzle as m rises (Ray & Lele 2007; Rodríguez et al. 2015), so higher-m wavepackets can be acoustically efficient only at high frequency. Ray & Lele also found instability-wave energy spread more evenly over m as frequency rises (p. 2022–2023).
  - Wavepacket models are valid to St = 4.0 and m = 4 in an M 0.9 jet (Sasaki et al. 2017), so they should cover the BBSAN range (p. 2023).
- **Literature position** (p. 2016): earlier azimuthal studies of shock-containing jets mostly identified screech modes. Arroyo & Moreau (2019, LES) and Suzuki (2016, LES) found higher modes peaking at higher frequency and angle; Vold et al. (2012) measured this in an overexpanded jet but did not study observer position. Only Suzuki compared modes with a BBSAN model, with poor agreement, and all these studies had β < 0.8. Tam (1987) compared only with total sound (Norum & Seiner 1982); Ray & Lele combined and scaled the first three modes to the total; the wavepacket models of Lele (2005) and Wong et al. (2019a, 2019b) matched amplitudes empirically.
- **Chamber qualification** (App. A, Fig. 7, p. 2023–2025): the inverse-square law holds within ISO 3745 tolerance at the frequencies of interest; slight deviations appear near the nozzle at θ = 140°, attributed to upstream piping (p. 2024). Read from Fig. 7 (my reading): tolerances are ±1.5 dB at 500, 750 and 12 000 Hz and ±1.0 dB at 5000 Hz. The points outside them are 500 Hz at 140° (off the scale below −2.5 dB at about 25D, +1.8 dB at 50D), 5000 Hz at 90° and 12.5D (about −1.65 dB), and 12 000 Hz at 90° and 12.5D and at 140° and 25D (about −1.75 dB each).

## Setup / method

### Facility: SJAF (p. 2016–2018; Fig. 1)
A **Shock Lab group facility**: the Monash LTRAC Supersonic Jet Anechoic Facility (SJAF), for acoustics and flow fields of cold, small-scale supersonic jets. The paper does not name the lab space.
- **Chamber:** 1.5 m × 1.2 m × 1.4 m wedge tip to wedge tip; foam wedges 400 mm deep, nominal cut-on 500 Hz; floor lined with wedge panels; entrained and exhaust air replaced through two openings in the upstream wall, shielded by an overhanging wall (p. 2016). All solid surfaces, including the ring, are covered with acoustic foam (p. 2018).
- **Air supply:** heatless-desiccant dryer and a 5 m³ reservoir; continuous running up to NPR 5. The supply is shared with the supersonic wind tunnel (Knast et al. 2017), so a butterfly valve directs it to a mixing chamber, with an eccentric rotary plug control valve and a Fairchild 100 High-Flow regulator to set NPR. A braided PTFE hose feeds the plenum (p. 2016–2017). Fig. 1(a) also shows co-flow piping, not used here.
- **NPR:** NPR = P_t/P_∞; plenum pressure by an RS-3100 transducer (0–10 bar, ±0.25% stated accuracy); the measured range in the plenum was about ±1% (p. 2017). No flow conditioning upstream of the nozzle exit.
- **Nozzle:** converging, round, D = 8 mm, lip thickness 0.5 mm. The plenum is connected through an 800 mm bent and straight pipe of 25.4 mm diameter; the contour is a fifth-order polynomial (Morel 1975); contraction ratio 10. Modular mild-steel plenum and nozzles on a stand that slides on rails for axial positioning (p. 2017). The small nozzle allows long continuous runs at high NPR, and the frequencies of interest are far above the chamber cut-on (p. 2018).

### Acquisition and spectra (p. 2018, App. B p. 2024)
- G.R.A.S. Type 46BE 1/4-in. pre-amplified microphones (4 Hz–100 kHz, 3.6 mV/Pa), amplifier, National Instruments DAQ; pistonphone calibration at 114 dB, 1 kHz; P_ref = 20 µPa (p. 2018).
- 400 000 samples at 200 kHz (2 s) per recording. The signal is checked for spurious trends and spikes, low-pass filtered at 80 kHz, and the mean and a linear trend are removed (eq. B1) (p. 2024).
- Welch PSD: blocks of N_fft = 2048 with a Hanning window and √(8/3) energy correction, averaged over N_B = 974 blocks (eqs. B2–B3), converted to SPL in dB/St (eq. B4) by scaling with U_j/D_j (p. 2024).

> My derivations, not stated: Δf = 200 000/2048 ≈ 98 Hz, i.e. ΔSt ≈ 0.002. 974 blocks of 2048 from 400 000 samples need a hop of about 409 samples, i.e. about 80% overlap; the overlap is not given. Eq. B2 scales by 1/N_fft and eq. B3 averages |p_k|², which gives mean-square per bin; the division by the bin width that a density per Hz or per St needs is not written (p. 2024).

### Microphone layout (p. 2018; Fig. 1b,c)
- Cylindrical jet coordinates (x, r, φ) overlaid with spherical acoustic coordinates (R, θ, Φ), θ from the downstream axis (Cavalieri et al. 2012 convention) (p. 2018).
- **Far field:** one microphone at R = 50D, θ = 60°–120°, to validate the new facility against the literature (p. 2018).
- **Midfield ring:** r = 11D from the axis, assumed to be outside the hydrodynamic field of a shock-containing jet (Seiner & Yu 1984; Suzuki 2016; Tam 1987) (p. 2018). One microphone is fixed and the other moves in 10° azimuthal steps; a 180° sweep was found sufficient for the frequencies of interest. The ring is traversed axially to change θ, so R changes with θ (p. 2018). The method follows Sinha et al. (2018): slower than a fixed array (Suzuki & Colonius 2006), but with far fewer microphones (p. 2018).
- **Polar-angle origin** for the ring data: x₀ = 5D downstream of the exit, as in Gojon & Bogey (2017), to approximate the source position. The authors call this an indirect use of the result that BBSAN comes from the downstream shock cells (Tam et al. 1985) (p. 2019). Midfield maps cover θ = 50°–125° (p. 2020).

### Azimuthal decomposition (App. C, p. 2024–2026)
1. Cross-spectrum of the fixed and moving microphones from the Fourier blocks of eq. B2 (eq. C1). Azimuthal homogeneity makes R_pp depend only on ψ = φ₂ − φ₁ (p. 2024).
2. The 10° grid is too coarse, so R_pp is interpolated at each St. Its real part a(ψ) is symmetric and fitted with A sinc(Bψ); its imaginary part b(ψ) is antisymmetric and fitted with the derivative of a sinc (eqs. C2–C4). Where the sinc fails, the grid points are joined linearly. The fit is meant to follow the data, not to carry physical meaning (p. 2024–2025). Fig. 8 shows fits at NPR 3.67, θ = 95°, St = 0.2 and 0.7 (p. 2025).
3. Fourier transform of the fitted R̃_pp in ψ over [−π, π] gives the mth azimuthal mode (eq. C5); m = 0 is kept alone and ±m are summed for m ≥ 1 (eqs. C6–C8) (p. 2025–2026).
4. At ψ = 0 the coefficients are the PSD of each mode (eq. C9; circumferential isotropy, Michalke & Fuchs 1975), which goes into eq. B4 (p. 2026).
5. Reconstruction: modes are summed in order m = 0, ±1, ±2, … until the total is matched within 1 dB/St (p. 2020).

**Data requirements of the method.** Only two simultaneous channels are needed, but the field must be statistically stationary and azimuthally homogeneous over the whole traverse, since each separation ψ is a separate recording. The output is a block-averaged (Welch) azimuthal spectrum; nothing is resolved in time, so mode switching, helicity reversals or intermittent screech within or between recordings are averaged together. No SPOD, BMD or Hilbert analysis is used.

> My reading of Fig. 8 and App. C (verify before reuse):
> - Fig. 8 shows the data at Δφ from about −1.57 to +1.57 rad, so the 180° sweep is ±90°. The fit is then integrated over ±π (eq. C5), so for |ψ| > π/2 the modal spectrum rests on the fitted form, not on data. This matters most at low St, where R_pp has not decayed by π/2 (St = 0.2 in Fig. 8a).
> - Over an infinite domain the Fourier transform of A sinc(Bψ) is a top-hat in m: equal power up to a cut-off set by B, nothing above. The sinc form therefore biases the decomposition towards "approximately equal" modes up to a cut-off. The paper's finding that m = 1–3 are about equal above St ≈ 0.6–0.7 may partly reflect this choice of fit.
> - Eqs. C6–C8 as printed omit the factor e^{imψ}. They are exact only at ψ = 0, which is the only place they are used (eq. C9).
> - An antisymmetric imaginary part of R_pp (Fig. 8, about ±1.5 against a real peak of about 7 at St = 0.2, and ±2 against 5 at St = 0.7) means unequal power in +m and −m. Its transform is real and odd in m, so it cancels in the ±m sums (eq. C8). It could come from a preferred direction of rotation or from a ring tilt that makes the axial offset vary with ψ. A constant phase mismatch between the two channels would not give an antisymmetric part. The paper does not discuss it.

## Key equations
θ from the downstream axis.

- **BBSAN peak frequency** (Harper-Bourne & Fisher 1973; Tam & Tanna 1982; eq. 1, p. 2015):
  $$f_p=\frac{u_c}{L_n\,(1-M_c\cos\theta)},\qquad n=1,$$
  with u_c the convection velocity of the large-scale structures, L₁ the spacing of the first shock-cell mode (L₁ ≈ 2π/k₁ for waveguide modes k_n), and M_c the convective Mach number. The same form with n ≥ 2 predicts the higher-order peaks (p. 2015). The inputs used are u_c = 0.7u_j and the Pack–Prandtl vortex-sheet L₁ (p. 2019).
- **Off-design parameter** (p. 2015, 2018): β = (M_j² − M_d²)^{1/2} = (M_j² − 1)^{1/2} for a convergent nozzle; BBSAN amplitude ∝ β⁴.
- **Strouhal number** (p. 2018): St = fD_j/U_j.
- **Inverse-square qualification** (App. A, eqs. A1–A4, p. 2024; method of Cunefare et al. 2003):
  $$\mathrm{SPL}_p(r_i)=20\log_{10}\Big[\frac{a}{r_i-r_0}\Big],\qquad a=\frac{\big(\sum r_i\big)^2-N\sum r_i^2}{\sum r_i\sum q_i-N\sum r_iq_i},\qquad r_0=\frac{\sum r_i\sum r_iq_i-\sum r_i^2\sum q_i}{\sum r_i\sum q_i-N\sum r_iq_i},$$
  with q_i = 10^{−0.05 SPL_pi}, and ΔSPL_p(r_i) = SPL_pi − SPL_p(r_i). Here a is the apparent source strength and r₀ the offset of the acoustic centre.
- **Signal processing** (App. B, eqs. B1–B4, p. 2024):
  $$p(t)=\Big(P(t)-\frac1{N_t}\sum_{n=1}^{N_t}P(t)\Big)-\tilde P(t),\qquad p_k(f)=\frac{\sqrt{8/3}}{N_{\mathrm{fft}}}\,\mathrm{FFT}\big(H_0(t)\,p_k(t)\big),$$
  $$\mathrm{PSD}(f)=\frac1{N_B}\sum_{k=1}^{K}p_k(f)\,p_k^*(f),\qquad \mathrm{SPL}(f)=10\log_{10}\Big(\frac{\mathrm{PSD}(f)}{P_{\mathrm{ref}}^2}\Big),$$
  with P̃(t) the linear trend, H₀ the Hanning window, K = N_B = 974 and N_fft = 2048.
- **Two-point cross-spectrum** (App. C, eqs. C1–C4, p. 2024–2025):
  $$R_{pp}(\phi_1,\phi_2;f)=\frac1{N_B}\sum_{k=1}^{K}p_k(\phi_1;f)\,p_k^*(\phi_2;f),\qquad R_{pp}(\psi;f)=a(\psi;f)+\mathrm i\,b(\psi;f),\quad \psi=\phi_2-\phi_1,$$
  $$a(\psi;f)\approx A\,\mathrm{sinc}(B\psi),\qquad b(\psi;f)\approx\frac{C\,\big(D\psi\cos(D\psi)-\sin(D\psi)\big)}{D\psi^2},$$
  with A, B, C, D fitting parameters. The sinc convention (sin x/x or sin πx/πx) is not stated; the denominator of C4 is printed "Dψ²" (D times ψ²); the exact derivative of sin(Dψ)/(Dψ) has (Dψ)²/D, which only rescales C.
- **Azimuthal modes and reconstruction** (eqs. C5–C9, p. 2025–2026):
  $$\tilde R_{pp}(f,m)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\tilde R_{pp}(\phi_1,\phi_2;f)\,e^{-\mathrm im\psi}\,\mathrm d\psi,\qquad \tilde R_{pp}(\psi;f)=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\tilde R_{pp}(f,m),$$
  $$\tilde R_{pp,0}(\psi;f)=\tilde R_{pp}(f,m=0),\qquad \tilde R_{pp,m}(\psi;f)=\tilde R_{pp}(f,m)+\tilde R_{pp}(f,-m)\ \ (m\ge1),\qquad \mathrm{PSD}(f)=\tilde R_{pp}(\psi=0;f),$$
  and |R̃_pp,m(ψ = 0; f)| is substituted into eq. B4 for the spectrum of each mode (p. 2026).

It does not affect the other results.
> - **Eq. 1 curves in Figs 3 and 5.** I measured the dashed curves in Fig. 3 at pixel level (600 dpi render). At θ = 90°, where M_c drops out, the n = 1 curves sit at St ≈ 0.97, 0.555 and 0.48 for NPR 2.25, 3.10 and 3.67. 0.7D_j/L₁ with Pack's L₁ = πβD_j/σ₁ = 1.306βD_j gives 0.98, 0.56 and 0.48, which confirms u_c = 0.7U_j and Pack's spacing. At NPR 3.10 the n = 1 curve reaches St ≈ 0.384 at θ ≈ 119° and ≈ 1.06 at θ ≈ 61°. That matches M_c = 0.7M_j = 0.97, i.e. u_c over the jet sound speed, not u_c/c_∞ ≈ 0.82 for this cold jet, which would give 0.40 and 0.94.
> - **Higher shock-cell modes.** At θ = 90° the second and third curves sit at 2.0× and 2.9× the n = 1 Strouhal number (1.11 at NPR 3.10; 0.96 and 1.39 at NPR 3.67). So the curves use L_n = L₁/n, the harmonics of a periodic train. Pack's vortex-sheet modes would give σ_n/σ₁ = 2.30 and 3.60.

## Notation
- x, r, φ: jet cylindrical coordinates; R, θ, Φ: acoustic spherical coordinates, θ from the downstream axis (origin at the exit for the far field, at x₀ = 5D for the ring).
- D, D_j: nozzle and ideally expanded diameters; U_j, u_j: ideally expanded velocity; M_j, M_d: ideally expanded and design Mach numbers; β = (M_j² − M_d²)^{1/2}; NPR = P_t/P_∞; St = fD_j/U_j.
- f_p: BBSAN peak frequency; u_c: convection velocity; M_c: convective Mach number (taken as 0.7M_j in the plotted curves, my check); L_n, k_n: shock-cell spacing and wavenumber of mode n; n: shock-cell mode number.
- m: azimuthal mode number; "m = 1" in the figures means the ±1 pair summed (eq. C8). "Rank N" or "N modes": the first N terms of m = 0, ±1, ±2, …
- R_pp, R̃_pp: measured and fitted two-point cross-spectrum; ψ = φ₂ − φ₁; a, b: real and imaginary parts; A, B, C, D: fit parameters.
- p_k, N_fft, N_B (= K), H₀: Fourier block, block length, number of blocks, Hanning window; P_ref = 20 µPa.
- SPL_p, a, r₀, q_i: inverse-square model level, apparent source strength, acoustic-centre offset, linearised level (App. A).

## Relevance to this project
A Shock Lab group paper from Wong's PhD (with Jordan), and the reference for the **SJAF** anechoic chamber and its 8 mm nozzle. Later group acoustics use this facility: [wong-2021](wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md) (the NPR 3.67 data and this decomposition are its target), [edgington-mitchell-2021](edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) (far-field tones at 50D), [stavropoulos-2023](stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) (which cites this paper for the facility) and [nogueira-2025](nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md) (PIV and acoustics).

**How it fits the BBSAN modelling thread.**
- [wong-2019](wong-2019-coherence-decay-wavepacket-bbsan.md) used a line source (m = 0 only) and matched levels to Norum & Seiner's total spectra; it blamed the upstream high-frequency "missing sound" on missing higher shock-cell modes and coherence decay. This paper adds a third cause, the azimuthal truncation of the source (p. 2021). The three are compatible: the secondary lobe needs n = 2 shock modes, and here it is also the most multimodal in azimuth (p. 2021).
- [wong-2021](wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md) compared m = 0, 1, 2 predictions with these data mode by mode, and cites this paper for the 4–5 modes the n = 2 lobe needs in the total (its p. 22). By this paper's Fig. 5, m = 3–4 are non-negligible below θ = 90° at NPR 3.67, so a sum of m = 0–2 under-states the measured total there; the per-mode comparisons in 2021 avoid this.
- [tam-1982](tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md) and [tam-1986](tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md): eq. 1 is their peak-frequency law. Tam et al. (1986) place higher peaks at the shock Fourier wavenumbers, not exact harmonics. This paper's plotted n = 2 and 3 curves are exact harmonics of n = 1 (my check above), and the measured secondary lobes agree "favourably" with them (p. 2020). The data were not used to tell 2.0 from Pack's 2.30.
- [ray-2005](ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md): the conference precursor of Ray & Lele (2007), criticised here for m ≤ 2 (p. 2021). Its ring sources at St 0.9 (p. 12–13 there) fit the rise of higher-m content at high St found here.
- [andre-2013](andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md): screech lowers BBSAN and its peak frequency. These jets screech strongly, so levels and peak St are those of screeching jets (p. 2019), and the authors only infer from the agreement with Arroyo & Moreau's non-screeching LES that the azimuthal frequency dependence survives screech (p. 2020). The screech St here (≈ 0.66 at M_j 1.14, ≈ 0.31 at 1.38 and 1.50) matches André's Sr ≈ 0.65 at M_j 1.10–1.15 and 0.32 at 1.35.
- [cavalieri-2013](cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md) and [jordan-2013](jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise.md): in subsonic jets m = 0 carries the low-angle sound and higher m gain upstream and at high frequency, consistent with the BBSAN picture here (p. 2019). BBSAN differs in that m = 0 is never dominant at the sideline and upstream angles where it radiates.

**A caveat on "m = 0 is 3–4 dB weaker"** (my reading). The comparison is between the single m = 0 mode and the ±1 pair summed (eq. C8, p. 2019). An azimuthally narrow correlation gives about equal power per signed mode, and then every ±m pair is 3 dB above m = 0 automatically. Per signed mode, m = 0 and m = +1 (or −1) are within about 0–1 dB. A model that predicts one signed mode, or a helical wavepacket of one handedness, should be compared at half the plotted pair power. The "four modes" of the abstract are seven signed modes (m = 0, ±1, ±2, ±3).

**Methods and data type (CLAUDE.md workflow).**
- *Time-resolved data needed:* the decomposition needs simultaneous two-channel time series to form cross-spectra, which microphones always give. It does not need a simultaneous array, only stationarity over the traverse (p. 2018). The Welch parameters are N_fft = 2048, Hann, 974 blocks, 2 s at 200 kHz (p. 2024).
- *Non-time-resolved data:* not used. Mean PIV or mean schlieren would supply the measured shock spacing that eq. 1 needs; the NPR 2.25 mismatch is blamed on the Pack–Prandtl spacing (p. 2019). Our schlieren mean fields could replace it directly.
- *Line-of-sight imaging:* planar schlieren integrates over azimuth and cannot give these modes. TR schlieren SPOD gives the wavepacket side (convection speed, envelope) for the same jets, and this paper's inference that all m convect at similar u_c (p. 2020) could be tested with SPOD of azimuthally resolved data or LES.
- *Intermittency and mode switching:* the method averages over each recording and over the traverse sequence, so it cannot see helicity reversals ([edgington-mitchell-2023](edgington-mitchell-2023-screeching-jet-seen-from-all-sides.md)), staging jumps or intermittent screech. A simultaneous ring of 6–8 microphones would give azimuthal coefficients in time, and a Hilbert transform or HPOD of the tone-band coefficients would give instantaneous amplitude and phase of each rotation sense. That would extend the SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD pipeline to acoustics (suggestion, not in the paper). The antisymmetric Im R_pp in Fig. 8 is a hint that ±m are not balanced, worth checking first.

**Modelling side.**
- Wavepacket models (PSE, resolvent) for BBSAN need azimuthal modes up to about m = 4 at St ≳ 0.7, as accurately as the low modes (p. 2023); Sasaki et al. (2017) suggest linear models can manage this up to St 4 and m = 4 at M 0.9. See [cavalieri-2019](cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md) for why higher m radiate efficiently only at high frequency.
- The shock side is axisymmetric and stationary in these models, so all azimuthal content comes from the wavepacket (p. 2022). Shock oscillation at the screech frequency, which these screeching jets have, is outside that picture.

**PyFR staging LES** (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`, NPR 2.10–2.25).
- The NPR 2.25 case (M_j 1.14, D = 8 mm) is the upper end of the PyFR A1/A2 range. Its screech is at St ≈ 0.66 (Fig. 3a), close to the 15 mm rig's A2 at 0.63–0.65 ([edgington-mitchell-2018](edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md)) and to 0.64 in the same SJAF rig ([edgington-mitchell-2021](edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md)). The paper does not name the stage.
- BBSAN at this NPR is weak (β = 0.55), lies above St ≈ 0.7 and needs 4–5 azimuthal modes (p. 2020–2021). An FW-H or ring decomposition of the LES at this point would need azimuthal resolution to at least m = 5, and could be compared mode by mode with Figs 4–6 if U_j/D_j scaling is used.
- Fig. 2 shows (my reading) a drop in screech St from about 0.6 at NPR 2.3 to about 0.41 at NPR 2.4 in this nozzle: a stage change just above the PyFR range, in a nozzle with a contraction ratio of 10 and Re ≈ 3 × 10⁵, against over 100 and Re ≈ 4.5 × 10⁵ in the 15 mm rig. It is a further rig against which LES staging limits could be checked.

**Facility side** (for [diagnostics-and-facilities](../topics/diagnostics-and-facilities.md)).
- SJAF is anechoic, unlike the group's optical jet rigs. Its cut-on is 500 Hz, against 150–160 Hz for the larger chambers in [mayer-2019](mayer-2019-bristol-aeroacoustic-wind-tunnel.md) and the Syracuse and OSU rooms. The LMFA room of [mercier-2017](mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) and [andre-2013](andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md) is 10 × 8 × 8 m.
- The qualification stops at 12 kHz, whereas BBSAN here lies at roughly 15–74 kHz (derived above, with an assumed T₀). Several points closest to the source (12.5D at 90°, 25D at 140°) fall outside the ISO 3745 tolerance (Fig. 7), and the ring sits at R ≈ 11–14D. So the midfield levels are not formally qualified, although the inverse-square deviations near the nozzle concern a point source, not the extended jet.
- The ring at 11D is acoustically far for these frequencies (kR ≳ 20 at St 0.3, my estimate) but not geometrically far from a source spread over several diameters, which is why the angle origin is moved to x₀ = 5D (p. 2019). The captions of Figs 4 and 5 say "R = 11D", but R varies from 11D at 90° to about 14D at 50° (my geometry); the paper does not say whether the levels are corrected to a common distance.

**Rocket noise.** Little direct transfer: the jets are small and cold. BBSAN is absent from full-scale rocket spectra ([lubert-2022](lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md)), and Mach waves, which dominate rockets, are carried by low m. The general point does carry over: any azimuthally resolved source model (wavepacket-based rocket-noise models, or LES of clustered nozzles) should be validated mode by mode, as proposed here (p. 2022).
