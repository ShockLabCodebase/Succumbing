# Experimental characterisation of the screech feedback loop in underexpanded round jets

- **Authors:** Bertrand Mercier, Thomas Castelain, Christophe Bailly. Mercier and Bailly are at Université de Lyon, École Centrale de Lyon and LMFA (UMR CNRS 5509), Écully; Castelain at Université de Lyon, Université Lyon 1 and LMFA, Villeurbanne (p. 202). Not Shock Lab group work.

## Summary
Mercier, Castelain and Bailly take apart the screech loop of 10 unheated underexpanded round jets (M_j 1.07–1.50, D = 38 mm convergent nozzle) at the LMFA anechoic room, covering A1, A2 and the flapping B mode (p. 202, 205). Two-microphone near-field surveys give the phase of the screech wave along a line parallel to the jet. A phase reversal marks where upstream-travelling sound gives way to downstream-convected hydrodynamic pressure, and this places a single monopole source at the **4th shock tip for A1 and A2** and at the **3rd or 4th for B** (p. 219–222). High-speed schlieren on a thin strip along the lip line (220–430 kHz) gives the screech-frequency phase from the nozzle to the source, and from it the mean convective velocity (Ū_c/U_j = 0.52–0.59). Wavenumber spectra of the same data give about 0.6U_j upstream of the source and about 0.7U_j downstream, so U_c grows along the jet and no single U_c belongs to a mode (p. 212–215, 224–225). Adding a straight acoustic return at c₀ = 337 m s⁻¹, the total loop phase is within +0.5π of 2πm. The loop holds **m = 4 periods for A1, 5 for A2, and 3 or 4 for B** (equal to the radiating shock number), so the A1→A2 jump adds one period to the loop while the source stays at the 4th shock (p. 222–224). The paper uses Powell / Gao & Li phase-criterion kinematics only: it has no stability model and cannot tell free-stream sound from a guided jet mode in the return path (p. 213).

## Key findings
- **Source location from near-field phase** (§4, p. 215–222; Table 5):
  - The model is a single monopole on the lip line (y = 0.5D) at a shock tip, with expected phase lag Δφ = k_s(r − r₀) between the moving and the fixed microphone (eq. 4.1, p. 215).
  - *Radial traverse* in the nozzle exit plane (y/D 0.5–8; M_j 1.13, 1.15, 1.35; Fig. 7, p. 216–217).
    - The phase matches the monopole prediction only for y/D 0.5–4, and there it cannot tell the 3rd, 4th and 5th shock tips apart. In this geometry moving the source barely changes the phase difference between the two microphones (p. 216).
    - Farther out the phase "draws unexpected patterns". The screech level at the moving microphone falls 20 dB by y = 5.5D (M_j 1.35; Fig. 8, p. 218), so reflections take over. The authors compare this with Norum's B-mode directivity: a maximum near 170°, about 10 dB down at 133° and 10 dB more at 123° (p. 216).
    - Verdict: the source lies between the 3rd and 5th shock tips (p. 216).
  - *Axial traverse* along y = 1.5D (2D for the highest M_j), z/D −0.1 to 6 or 8, for all 10 jets (Figs 10–11, p. 219–221).
    - The phase slope changes sign (Fig. 11). Upstream of the reversal the slope matches acoustics with c₀ = 337 m s⁻¹. Downstream it matches a hydrodynamic wave with dφ_s/dz = ω_s/(0.7U_j) (eq. 4.2), so the downstream near field is dominated by hydrodynamic pressure (p. 221).
    - Fig. 10 (M_j 1.32, y = 1.5D) shows the zero-crossing wavefronts reversing direction near z = 5D, with four fronts each way over two cycles (p. 219).
  - **Selection rule** (p. 221):
    - Acoustic phase slopes are negative only upstream of the source, while hydrodynamic slopes are positive everywhere. A negative slope therefore means the source lies downstream.
    - The hydrodynamic pressure is modulated at the shock spacing, so acoustics would reappear (negative slope) within one cell of the reversal if the source lay farther downstream. The source must therefore lie between the last negative-slope point and one shock cell downstream of it.
    - Constrained to a shock tip, the source is the first shock downstream of the last negative slope. The neighbouring shocks fit visibly worse (Fig. 11).
  - **Result:** the 4th shock tip for M_j 1.07, 1.10, 1.13 (A1), 1.15 (A2), 1.32 and 1.35 (B); the 3rd for 1.23, 1.37, 1.45 and 1.50, with z_N/D = 2.0–4.8 (p. 221; Table 5).
- **Loop structure** (§5, p. 222–225; Fig. 12, Table 5):
  - The loop time is T_loop = z_N/Ū_c + z_N/c₀ (eq. 5.1). If a new structure is triggered exactly when the acoustic wave reaches the lip, the hydrodynamic and acoustic waves are in phase at the exit. Then T_loop = mT_s, i.e. φ_loop = 2πT_loop/T_s is a multiple of 2π (p. 222).
  - φ_loop is close to a multiple of 2π in every case, with excesses of 0 to +0.5π, all positive (p. 224). The printed values are in the Data tables below.
  - **m = 4 for A1** (N = 4; M_j 1.07, 1.10, 1.13). **m = 5 for A2** (N = 4; M_j 1.15). **m = 3** for the flapping cases with N = 3 (1.23, 1.37, 1.45) and **m = 4** for those with N = 4 (1.32, 1.35) (p. 224; Table 5). No m is given for M_j 1.50, which shows no phase-slope inversion (Fig. 12j).
  - So the **A1 → A2 jump adds one period to the loop, with the source fixed at the 4th shock** (z_N 2.7D → 2.8D) and St rising from 0.59 to 0.64 (Tables 2, 5).
  - **b versus B:** the N = m = 3 flapping set "could show the characteristics" of Powell's secondary b mode and the N = m = 4 set of B. But Powell's b coexists with A2 or C, while every case here has a single tone and no C mode is seen (p. 224).
  - A nine-microphone azimuthal array (Massey & Ahuja method) shows that all the antisymmetric cases are flapping (p. 227–228).
  - **The positive excess** may come from the shock modulation of the phase, or from the instability being triggered slightly downstream of the lip. Mitchell, Honnery & Soria (2012) saw an incited vortex ring 0.25D downstream in an impinging-jet loop (p. 224).
  - A return wave slowed inside the shear layer would add at most 0.4π (worst case M_j 1.15), which does not change the nearest 2π multiple (p. 224).
- **Convective velocity** (p. 212–215, 224–226; Tables 4–5, Fig. 13):
  - Upstream of the source (wavenumber analysis): U_c/U_j = 0.54–0.61, i.e. about 0.6 (Table 4, p. 212).
  - Downstream of the source: 0.61–0.75, i.e. about 0.7 (Table 4, p. 214). A rise with x was also seen in LES (Gojon, Bogey & Marsden 2015) and in the authors' AIAA 2016 paper (p. 214).
  - Nozzle-to-source mean from the phase at the source (eq. 5.2): 0.52–0.59 (Table 5). The two estimates differ by at most about 8% (M_j 1.07 and 1.45; p. 225).
  - The wavenumber estimates are mostly the higher ones: the spatial FT is weighted by the growing instability-wave amplitude, so it favours the fast region near the source (p. 225).
  - Within A1, Ū_c rises as the source moves downstream (Fig. 13). "A fixed convective velocity cannot be attributed to a mode" (p. 225).
  - M_j 1.23 (0.52) and 1.15 (0.56) have nearly the same z_N; the authors put the difference down to the mode switch or to compressibility. M_j 1.35 and 1.50, with similar z_N, have similar Ū_c, so compressibility seems not to matter there (p. 225).
  - Conclusion: c₀ can be taken as constant along the return path, but U_c must be averaged from the nozzle to the source. Frequency prediction therefore needs the source position (p. 226).
  - The introduction notes that the literature's U_c/U_j is 0.55–0.7 (p. 203). Veltin & McLaughlin (2008) measured 0.7 at M_j 1 falling to 0.57 at M_j 1.56, 4D downstream (p. 203–204).
- **Screech wavenumbers** (§3.2, p. 212–215; Table 4, Fig. 6):
  - Spatial FT of the schlieren Fourier coefficient G_s(z) at f_s along y/D = 0.55, over 0 ≤ z ≤ z_N. There are two dominant peaks: k_h > 0 (instability wave) and a negative peak near −ω_s/c₀ (upstream sound) (p. 212).
  - The upstream wave speed c₀ = ω_s/k_s is within 5% of 337 m s⁻¹ in 7 of 10 cases. The errors are larger at M_j 1.35, 1.45 (493 m s⁻¹, from peak overlap and shock-oscillation harmonics) and 1.50 (p. 212–213). The conclusion quotes ±10% (p. 226).
  - The authors say this scatter "prevents us from providing evidence for the existence or absence" of other feedback types (Shen & Tam 2002; Chatterjee, Ghodake & Singh 2009; Edgington-Mitchell, Honnery & Soria 2015), i.e. the guided-jet-mode closure (p. 213).
  - **Tam's condition** k_h − k_sc = −k_s (eq. 3.3) is tested as (k_h + k_s)/k_sc, with k_sc averaged over the shocks in 0 ≤ z ≤ z_N. It equals 1.01–1.05 except at M_j 1.13 (1.10), 1.15 (1.28) and 1.45 (0.97, unreliable) (Table 4, p. 214).
    - The authors attribute the 28% at A2 to "the jump in frequency", with no change in the jet's aerodynamics (citing Clem, Zaman & Fagan 2016) (p. 214).
    - They call condition 3.3 "overly rigid"; the loop instead follows the wave-superposition relation 3.1a (p. 211).
  - **Downstream of the source** (z_N ≤ z ≤ z_max): the negative peak k_d⁻ satisfies (k_h + k_d⁻)/k_sc = 0.96–1.16. The authors identify it as the shock-cell × instability-wave interaction, with subsonic phase speed in all cases (p. 215). There is no upstream acoustic peak there, consistent with z_N, and no downstream acoustic peak, which they ascribe to screech's upstream directivity (p. 215).
  - M_j 1.50 is the only case with a stronger negative than positive peak, and its schlieren phase falls everywhere. This is "currently not understood" (p. 223).
- **Lobed near-field pattern** (§3.1, p. 208–211; Figs 4–5):
  - |G_s| along the lip line shows lobes, the standing waves of Westley & Woolley (1969), on a bell-shaped envelope: the instability wave grows, saturates and decays (p. 208).
  - Panda's (1999) superposition gives L⁻_sw upstream of the source and L⁺_sw downstream (eqs. 3.1–3.2). With U_c = 0.6U_j: 0.63D and 2.68D at M_j 1.13, and 1.20D and 7.26D at M_j 1.37 (p. 210).
  - **L⁺_sw is never seen.** Only M_j 1.13 and 1.15 show L⁻_sw < L_sc, upstream of the 4th shock or of a point between the 3rd and 4th, with L⁻_sw = L_sc downstream. All other jets show a single wavelength equal to L_sc everywhere (p. 210–211).
  - Downstream of the source the lobes (period L_sc) are put down to shock-cell modulation of the instability wave. They decay downstream of the last visible shock at M_j 1.07, 1.37 and 1.45, as in André's (2012) PIV (p. 211).
  - L⁺_sw is missing for two reasons given by the authors: k⁺_sw = k_h − k_s is small at high M_j, and the screech sound is weak downstream (p. 211).
- **Receptivity region** (p. 222–223; Fig. 12): close to the nozzle the schlieren phase slope is negative, i.e. dominated by the upstream acoustic wave while the instability wave is still weak. The slope reverses upstream of the 2nd shock, earliest at z/D ≈ 0.5 (M_j 1.07) and latest at z/D ≈ 2 (M_j 1.35). The phase is wavy downstream from the KH–acoustic–shock interaction (p. 223).

## Setup / method
- **Facility** (p. 205): 10 m × 8 m × 8 m anechoic room of LMFA, École Centrale de Lyon. Dry air from a centrifugal compressor, continuous, up to 0.85 kg s⁻¹. Maximum NPR 3.9 (M_j 1.54).
  - Static pressure is measured in the inlet duct 15D upstream and combined with ambient pressure through isentropic relations to give M_j, held within ±0.2%.
  - The total temperature (K-type thermocouple) settles at 30 ± 2 °C after half an hour. The ambient temperature is also monitored, and gives c₀ = 337 m s⁻¹ (p. 213).
- **Nozzle** (p. 205): convergent, D = 38 mm, lip 0.5 mm thick (t/D ≈ 0.013), contraction ratio 4.4:1 (André 2011). The exit boundary-layer state is not given.
- **Conditions** (Table 2, p. 205): 10 setting points, M_j 1.07–1.50, NPR 2.06–3.67, Re_j = ρ_jU_jD/μ 1.16–2.02 × 10⁶, St_s = f_sD/U_j (nozzle D, ideally expanded U_j).
  - Three A1 points, and only one A2 point, because A2 "tends to coexist with another mode as soon as it moves apart from M_j = 1.15". Six flapping points from M_j 1.23 (p. 205).
  - NPR and f_s are measured; U_j is calculated.
- **Two campaigns** (p. 206): M_j 1.10 and 1.35 come from 2011 (André, Castelain & Bailly 2011a), the rest from 2016, with "identical" set-ups.
  - M_j 1.15 was measured in both. Far-field spectra at 55D and 130° agree in screech frequency and amplitude and in BBSAN peak (Fig. 1).
  - The distance from the 2nd to the 7th shock leading edge is 1.3% longer in 2016 (Figs 2–3; p. 206).
- **Microphones** (p. 206, 215–219): two 1/8-inch B&K 4138 microphones with a Nexus 2692 conditioner. The reference is fixed to the nozzle rig; the other sits on a motorised axial/radial traverse that also carries the schlieren optics.
  - Sampling: 204,800 Hz for 2 s, synchronous. Steps of 3–5 mm give about 20–30 points per acoustic wavelength (p. 215).
  - *Radial traverse:* reference at z = −1.8D, y = 1.8D (±0.03D); moving microphone at y/D 0.5–8 in the nozzle exit plane. Phase from the cross-spectrum at f_s, unwrapped (p. 216).
  - *Axial traverse:* reference at y = 2.4D, z = 0.4D; moving microphone along y = 1.5D (2D for M_j 1.45 and 1.50 in the text; the Fig. 11 caption gives 2D for M_j 1.35–1.50), from z/D = −0.1 to 6 (M_j ≤ 1.23) or 8 (p. 215, 217, 220).
  - *Phase averaging instead of cross-spectra* (p. 218–219). Near the jet the hydrodynamic and acoustic fields interfere, so 2π jumps cannot be told from sharp phase changes. The procedure:
    1. Band-pass the reference with an FIR filter (cut-offs ±10% of f_s, −30 dB at ±30%), applied zero-lag so that a drifting f_s causes no phase bias.
    2. Mark periods by the maxima of the filtered reference.
    3. Split each period into 20 phase bins and average the moving-microphone samples in each bin, over two consecutive periods (0–4π, 40 phase steps) so that one wavefront can be followed from the source to both ends of the domain.
    4. Track the zero crossings (rising and falling edges) of the rebuilt field p_s(z, φ_s), and compare one continuous wavefront with eq. 4.1 (Figs 9–10).

    The method needs time-resolved microphone signals and assumes one steady tone.
- **Schlieren** (p. 206–208; Table 3):
  - Z-type system with two 200 mm f/8 parabolic mirrors 2.5 m apart; focused high-power Cree XP-L LED; knife edge perpendicular to the jet axis (axial density gradients), about 50% cut-off; Phantom V12 camera; Sigma 120–400 mm f/4.5–5.6 lens. Grey level g(y, z, t).
  - Two record types per campaign. "Shear-layer" records are thin strips spanning y/D 0.44–0.55: 640 × 16 px at 430,769 fps (2011) and 1024 × 24 px at 220,472 fps (2016). "Surroundings" records are full-field at 7,200 or 18,000 fps (Table 3).
  - The rate follows Veltin, Day & McLaughlin (2011): at least 15f_c with f_c = U_j/D_j ≈ 10 kHz at M_j 1.50, hence more than 150 kHz (p. 207).
  - The frame must hold at least the first five shock cells. For M_j > 1.23 the mirror diameter forces several films, with the camera shifted in 100 mm (2.6D) steps (p. 207–208).
  - The number of frames, record length and FFT parameters are not stated. Line-of-sight integration is acknowledged, but argued to be weak for azimuthally coherent screech (p. 208).
- **Schlieren analyses** (all need time-resolved data and assume one steady tone):
  - G_s(y, z): the modulus of the Fourier transform of g at f_s at each pixel (Fig. 4c), read along y/D = 0.55 (M_j < 1.32) or y/D = 0.75 (higher M_j). These lines are as close to the lip line as possible without shocks crossing them (p. 208).
  - *Wavenumber spectrum* (p. 212):
    - Spatial DFT of the complex G_s(z) over [0, z_N] (two to four wavelengths, so coarse) and over [z_N, z_max].
    - Gaussian window of width five standard deviations, and zero padding to twice the length. Tests show the padding does not affect the two main peaks.
    - Peak positions from a three-point Gaussian fit (Gasior & Gonzalez 2004), which has a maximum error below 1% of the bin; the worst bin is 80 rad m⁻¹ (M_j 1.07, unpadded).
    - Results depend weakly on radial position, except at M_j 1.45 (peaks close, and shocks crossing the line make harmonics).
  - *Shock positions* come from centreline profiles of the mean images: ±0.007D from shock width, plus "±0.04z" from the pixel scaling (p. 213).
  - *Phase along the shear layer* (p. 222): cross-spectrum between the pixel at the nozzle exit, g(0.55D, 0, t), and pixels along z at y/D = 0.55. The phase at f_s is unwrapped and shifted by 2π multiples so that the positive-slope part, extrapolated, crosses z = 0 at φ_s = 0, which places the trigger at the lip.
    - The phase at the source, φ_N, is read from a straight-line fit over z_N ± L_sc/2, because the modulation makes the local phase unreliable (p. 224).
    - Error bars convert the shock-oscillation amplitude into phase (FWHM of the shock-passage peak in g(0.50D, z, t) fluctuations), with a ±0.05π floor for the "unknown third digit" of φ_s (p. 224–225).
  - *Mode shapes* (appendix, p. 226–227): real part of the Fourier coefficient at f_s on full-field films (Fig. 14).
- **Modelling side.** There is no simulation or stability analysis. The models used are kinematic:
  - Powell (1953) / Harper-Bourne & Fisher (1973) / Tam, Seiner & Yu (1986): f_s = U_c/[L_sc(1 + U_c/c₀)] (eq. 1.1). Powell, Umeda & Ishii (1990) added a source/nozzle phase lag that "does not seem to be widely used" (p. 203).
  - Gao & Li (2010, from URANS) generalised it with a single source at shock N and m periods per loop (eq. 1.2), handling staging by fitting m, N and U_c/U_j per mode (p. 203).
  - Panda's (1999) standing-wave relation (eqs. 3.1–3.2) and Tam's necessary condition (eq. 3.3) are the other ingredients.
  - Assumptions of the loop model: a single monopole at a shock tip on the lip line; return straight to the nozzle at c₀ outside the shear layer; triggering exactly at the lip; and U_c averaged over 0–z_N (p. 215, 222, 224).
  - The authors list modelling evidence for leakage as the generation mechanism (Suzuki & Lele 2003 DNS; Shariff & Manning 2013 ray tracing; Berland, Bogey & Bailly 2007 planar-jet LES) and for the convective-velocity rise (Gojon et al. 2015 LES), but do not test any of them (p. 203–204).

## Key equations
All checked against rendered pages, except 4.1–4.2 (text layer).

- **Powell / Tam frequency** (eq. 1.1, p. 203): $f_s = \dfrac{U_c}{L_{sc}(1+U_c/c_0)}$.
- **Gao & Li single-source frequency** (eq. 1.2, p. 203): $f_s = \dfrac{m\,U_c}{N L_{sc}(1+U_c/c_0)}$, where N is the number of shock cells between nozzle and source and m the number of wavelengths in the loop. This form is dimensionally consistent, unlike the Gao & Li formula quoted in the [edgington-mitchell-2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) note (its eq. 1.4 check item).
- **Standing-wave wavenumbers** (eqs. 3.1a,b, p. 209), all wavenumbers taken positive: $k^-_{sw}=k_h+k_s$ (upstream of the source, sound retrograde) and $k^+_{sw}=k_h-k_s$ (downstream, sound co-propagating), with $k_h=\omega_s/U_c$ and $k_s=\omega_s/c_0$ (p. 212).
- **Standing-wave lengths** (eqs. 3.2a,b, p. 209): $L^-_{sw}=\dfrac{U_c}{f_s(1+U_c/c_0)}$, $L^+_{sw}=\dfrac{U_c}{f_s(1-U_c/c_0)}$. Eq. 3.2a has the form of Powell's eq. 1.1 with L_sc replaced by L⁻_sw.
- **Tam's condition** (eq. 3.3, p. 211): $k_h-k_{sc}=-k_s$, which with 3.1a forces $L^-_{sw}=L_{sc}$. Combined (eq. 3.4, p. 211): $k_h-k_{sc}=k_h-k^-_{sw}=-k_s=-\omega_s/c_0$.
- **Downstream interaction peak** (p. 215): $k_d^-\simeq k_{sc}-k_h$, phase speed ω_s/k_d⁻ subsonic.
- **Monopole phase lag** (eq. 4.1, p. 215): $\Delta\phi=k_s(r-r_0)$, with r, r₀ the distances from the source to the moving and the reference microphones.
- **Hydrodynamic phase slope** (eq. 4.2, p. 221): $\mathrm d\phi_s/\mathrm dz=\omega_s/U_c$, used with U_c = 0.7U_j in Fig. 11.
- **Loop time and phase** (eq. 5.1, p. 222): $T_{loop}=\dfrac{z_N}{\overline{U_c}}+\dfrac{z_N}{c_0}=m\,T_s$, i.e. $\phi_{loop}=2\pi T_{loop}/T_s=2\pi m$.
- **Mean convective velocity** (eq. 5.2, p. 224): $\overline{U_c}=f_s\dfrac{2\pi}{\phi_N}z_N$, with φ_N the schlieren phase at the source.
- **Mean shock-cell length** (eq. 5.3, p. 224): $\overline{L_{sc}}=z_N/N$.
- **Link between the two tests** (my derivation, not in the paper): from eq. 5.1, $(k_h+k_s)z_N=2\pi m$, so $\dfrac{k_h+k_s}{\overline{k_{sc}}}=\dfrac{m}{N}$ with $\overline{k_{sc}}=2\pi N/z_N$. Tam's condition (eq. 3.3) is the special case m = N. The Table 4 ratio is therefore the wavenumber-space image of the loop count: 1.01–1.05 for the m = N cases, and **1.28 for A2, against m/N = 5/4 = 1.25**. The two sides use different U_c estimates and slightly different k_sc averages, so the match is approximate.

> Checked numerically (my arithmetic): Table 2 St_s = f_sD/U_j with D = 38 mm (e.g. 6508 × 0.038/335 = 0.738). Table 4 wavenumbers are in rad m⁻¹ (2π·6508/128 = 319 m s⁻¹, as tabulated). Eq. 5.2 with the Fig. 12 phases reproduces the Table 5 Ū_c/U_j to ±0.02 (e.g. M_j 1.15: 2·6024·0.1064/6.4 = 200 m s⁻¹ = 0.56U_j). Eq. 5.3 reproduces the Table 5 L_sc/D to ±0.02. φ_loop − φ_N equals 2πf_sz_N/c₀ within about 0.1π (e.g. M_j 1.15: 3.80π against 10.2π − 6.4π).

## Notation
- M_j, U_j, ρ_j, D_j: ideally expanded Mach number, velocity, density, diameter; D = 38 mm nozzle diameter; NPR = p₀/p∞; St_s = f_sD/U_j; Re_j = ρ_jU_jD/μ.
- z: axial distance from the nozzle exit; y: transverse; lip line y = 0.5D.
- f_s, ω_s, T_s: screech frequency, angular frequency, period; c₀: ambient sound speed (337 m s⁻¹).
- U_c: convective velocity at f_s; Ū_c: its mean from the nozzle to the source.
- k_h = ω_s/U_c (instability wave); k_s = ω_s/c₀ (sound); k_sc = 2π/L_sc (shock cells); k±_sw, L±_sw: standing-wave wavenumbers and lengths; k_d⁻: negative-wavenumber peak downstream of the source. All taken positive except as signed in Table 4.
- N: number of the radiating shock tip (written n in the Table 5 caption); m: screech periods per loop; z_N: source position.
- g(y, z, t): schlieren grey level; G_s: its Fourier coefficient at f_s; Φ_s: wavenumber spectrum of G_s; φ_s: phase at f_s; φ_N: phase at the source; φ_loop: total loop phase; Δφ: inter-microphone phase lag.
- Modes: A1, A2 (axisymmetric), B (flapping), b (Powell's secondary flapping mode), C (helical), D.

## Relevance to this project
This is the standard experimental source for "screech radiates from one shock and the loop holds an integer number of periods". [Mancinelli 2019](mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) (p. 4–5) and [Mancinelli 2021](mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) (p. 7) fixed their reflection point at the 4th shock on its authority. [Nogueira 2022](nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) (p. 10) and [Knast 2018](knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) (p. 10–11) cite it for A1/A2 regions and for U_c not explaining staging. It speaks directly to the A1/A2 staging that the PyFR LES targets.

**Reading it critically**
- **The source is imposed, then located.** A single monopole at a shock tip on the lip line is an assumption (p. 215), and the selection rule has one-cell resolution.
  - [edgington-mitchell-2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) (p. 20–21) calls single-source localisations, this one included, inconclusive.
  - Direct leakage imaging shows several emitting cells, with the first one wandering over the 3rd–5th cells ([edgington-mitchell-2021-generation](edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) p. 19, 28–30). Read N as the dominant or "effective" source.
  - The leakage results nonetheless agree well with this paper:
    - A2: 4th cell in both (NPR 2.25 there, M_j 1.15 here).
    - A1: the 3rd cell there (NPR 2.10, M_j 1.09) against the 4th here. That is one cell, within both methods' scatter.
    - M_j 1.45: the 3rd shock here (flapping) matches the 3rd-cell C-mode leakage there and Raman's 3rd shock in a rectangular jet ([raman-1998](raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) p. 71–73).
    - Both contradict a source at the instability-wave peak ([tam-1986](tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md) p. 4) and the 5D–6D of Powell et al. (1992) in Table 1.
- **The loop count and Tam's condition are the same measurement** (my derivation in Key equations). Whenever the integer-loop phase criterion holds, (k_h + k_s)/k̄_sc = m/N, so the A2 "anomaly" of 1.28 in Table 4 is the wavenumber image of m = 5 with N = 4 (5/4 = 1.25). The authors present the two results separately and explain the 1.28 by a frequency jump without a change in shock spacing (p. 214).
  - This links directly to the k_s2 explanation of staging. [edgington-mitchell-2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) (p. 11) finds that the lip-line standing wavenumber follows k_s1 for A1 and B and k_s2 for A2 and C, and its Fig. 6 gives k_s2/k_s1 ≈ 1.1–1.27 (values read in that note). This paper's k⁻_sw = 1.28k̄_sc for A2 is the same observation five years earlier, with k̄_sc the mean over the first four cells rather than a long-field spectral peak.
  - The A1 case just below the jump (M_j 1.13) already shows 1.10. That may be the growing k_kh − k_s2 energy seen before the A1 → A2 switch (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 13). This is my speculation; one case and the coarse spectra cannot show it.
  - On the [staging page](../topics/screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md)'s open question "one reflection point or two shock wavenumbers", these data cannot discriminate. A fixed 4th-shock source with one more period (Mancinelli's s = 4, p = 5) and Tam & Tanna closure on an effective k_s ≈ 1.25k̄_sc are the same statement here. This is the equivalence [Mancinelli 2021](mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) (p. 24) shows algebraically, (p − φ/2π)/N_s = 1 for equal spacing.
  - What the paper does show is that **the source does not move at the A1→A2 jump** (z_N 2.7D → 2.8D, N = 4 both). So the extra period comes from the phase budget, not from a longer loop.
  - [Nogueira 2022](nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) (p. 10) says its "A1 near-nozzle, A2 downstream" picture agrees with this paper. The agreement can only be with the standing-wave difference of the M_j 1.13–1.15 cases, not with the source position.
- **Measured m against the resonance-model integers** (my comparison).
  - [Mancinelli 2019](mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) needed p = 3 (A1) and 4 (A2) with φ = π in a vortex-sheet model. That model's KH phase speed is about 0.9U_j (that note's reading), against the 0.53–0.59U_j measured here. A KH wave that fast removes roughly one period from the hydrodynamic leg: at M_j 1.10, 1.7 instead of 2.7 cycles.
  - The finite-thickness model of Mancinelli 2021 uses s = 4, φ = 0 and p = 4, 5 for A1, A2 (p. 17 of that note), i.e. exactly this paper's N and m. So p is a fitting label in the vortex-sheet model but coincides with the measured cycle count in the finite-thickness one.
- **The return path is assumed, not identified.** The loop uses free-stream sound at c₀ straight to the lip, and the upstream peak gives c₀ only to ±10% (0.95–1.01c₀ for the A1/A2 cases). That cannot separate sound from the guided jet mode, whose phase speed is subsonic but close to c∞ ([Mancinelli 2019](mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) p. 3–4; [Edgington-Mitchell 2018](edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) p. 9–11). The authors concede this (p. 213).
  - A guided-mode return at about 0.9c₀ would add roughly 0.25–0.4π to φ_loop (my estimate). The +0.5π cases would then sit near the halfway point between integers, so the integer count is less robust than the clean Fig. 12 numbers suggest, unless a reflection or receptivity phase is added.
  - The shear-layer crossing correction (+0.4π at most) works in the same direction. All excesses are already positive.
- **Downstream negative peak.** The k_d⁻ ≈ k_sc − k_h wave downstream of the source has phase speed 0.66–0.90c₀ (my arithmetic from Table 4). That is the Tam & Tanna difference wave, which in today's terms is the G-JM or duct-like product of the KH–shock triad ([edgington-mitchell-2020](edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) p. 14–17).
  - It exists wherever the KH wave and the shocks coexist, so the absence of a sonic upstream peak downstream of z_N says little about where the loop's upstream wave is born.
  - The lobes downstream of the source, which the authors put down to shock modulation of the instability wave (p. 211), may equally be interference between k_h and this wave. The waves paper argues for that reading of the near-field standing wave, rather than shock modulation of KH growth (p. 20–23 of that note). When k_h + k_up = k_sc the two pictures give the same wavelength; only M_j 1.13 and 1.15 separate them here.
- **The b/B split rests on N alone.** Table 2 labels all six flapping cases B, and St falls smoothly (0.38, 0.32, 0.31, 0.30, 0.25, 0.24).
  - Between M_j 1.35 (N = m = 4) and 1.37 (N = m = 3), St changes by only 3%, because z_N and m change together (4.8D → 3.8D, 4 → 3).
  - The sequence N = 3, 4, 4, 3, 3, 3 is not monotonic. It uses a different microphone line for the higher M_j, and the authors themselves note that Powell's b coexists with other modes, which is not the case here (p. 224).
  - Treat b as tentative. If real, a source jump without a tone jump would be a kind of staging invisible in spectra. This is worth checking with modern data.
- **Convective velocity.** U_c rising from about 0.55 to 0.7U_j along the jet agrees with Gojon et al. (2015) and Veltin & McLaughlin (2008) (p. 203–204, 214). It agrees with [Knast 2018](knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) (p. 10–11), where U_c/U_j = 0.57–0.60 across twin-jet modes, so U_c does not explain staging. [Ivelja 2024](ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) (p. 19–20) cites it as consistent with LSA phase speeds in an elliptical jet.
  - It undercuts Gao & Li's constant U_c/U_j per mode, and Powell/Tam formulae with a fixed 0.6–0.7U_j ([tam-1995](tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) p. 37; the frequency table on [screech frequency and global modes](../topics/screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md)).
- **Nozzle differences.** This nozzle has a thin lip (t/D 0.013) and a low contraction ratio (4.4:1). The Monash leakage/PIV nozzle has t/D 0.33 and more than 100:1, and the PyFR model a 1 mm lip on 16 mm ([staging page](../topics/screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md)). The A1 → A2 jump here falls between M_j 1.13 and 1.15 (NPR 2.22–2.27). That matches the Monash/Tsinghua range (NPR 2.17–2.25) more closely than Pprime (M_j ≈ 1.12).

**Methods and data for the lab workflow (CLAUDE.md)**
- **Time-resolved, block-averaged.** Every analysis (G_s, cross-spectral phase, wavenumber spectra, phase-averaged pressure) uses one Fourier coefficient at f_s over the whole record. Each assumes a single steady tone, and A2 points away from M_j 1.15 were dropped because of mode coexistence (p. 205). Nothing here resolves intermittency or switching.
- **Thin-strip TR schlieren.** 16–24 px across the lip line at 220–430 kHz is enough for lip-line phase and wavenumber work. That is a useful design if the group's SA-Z must run much faster than 150 kHz for the shear layer.
  - Peak interpolation helps with short fields of view: a Gaussian window, ×2 zero padding and a three-point Gaussian fit (p. 212).
  - The full-field 7.2 kfps record (2011) is below Nyquist for the 2011 M_j 1.10 tone (5882 Hz). A pure tone can still be read at its alias, but the paper does not discuss this (my observation).
- **Near-field phase averaging** (p. 218–219) avoids 2π ambiguity where hydrodynamic and acoustic pressure mix. It needs a time-resolved reference.
  - Phase-based localisation fails where reflections compete: here only 20 dB down at y = 5.5D, even in an anechoic room (p. 216). In the group's non-anechoic rigs the schlieren-phase route is safer.
- **Non-time-resolved PIV.** The loop count can be done from the group's POD-pair data (suggestion). Take the phase of the complex POD mode (a₁ − ia₂) along the lip line instead of a cross-spectrum, z_N from the mean shock positions, and c₀ for the return. On the Monash NPR 2.10 (A1) and 2.25 (A2) datasets ([edgington-mitchell-2018](edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), [edgington-mitchell-2020](edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md)) this would test m = 4 → 5 at fixed N on a thick-lip nozzle. Wavenumber-filter first (k_x > 0) so the G-JM does not corrupt the KH phase near the nozzle.
- **SPOD → reconstruction → Hilbert POD.** In switching records, reconstruct the A1 and A2 bands separately along the lip line from SPOD ([nekkanti-2021](nekkanti-2021-spod-reconstruction-denoising-frequency-time.md)).
  - A time-domain Hilbert transform then gives an instantaneous phase per band, so φ_N(t), φ_loop(t) and m(t), with HPOD amplitudes ([raiola-2025](raiola-2025-hilbert-pod.md)) to condition on.
  - That turns this paper's static (N, m) into a time series across a switch, and tests whether the source cell moves or only m changes.
  - Do not take the Hilbert transform along +z: it would discard the upstream (k < 0) part that carries the return wave.

**Modelling side and the PyFR staging LES**
- The LES (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`; A1 at NPR 2.10, A2 at 2.25) can repeat this whole analysis on numerical schlieren or density along y/D = 0.55, using cross-spectra with a lip point. It should give N and m for converged A1 and A2 states.
  - The prediction from this paper is N = 4 in both and m = 4 → 5. If both states can be held at the same NPR inside a bistable band, the (N, m) pair is a discrete label that separates them more robustly than frequency, which differs by only about 8–10%. The continuation driver could use it to classify states.
- The LES also has what the experiment lacked:
  - the core field, to test whether the upstream wave at about −ω_s/c₀ is the G-JM or free-stream sound;
  - U_c(z) without schlieren integration effects;
  - the receptivity region (negative phase slope to z/D ≈ 0.5–2 here), to compare with the guided-mode/KH projection near the lip suggested on the [staging page](../topics/screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md) (PyFR item 5).
- (k_h + k_up)/k̄_sc = m/N is a cheap check on the LES mean shock spacing against the loop count. Comparing it with long-field k_s1/k_s2 spectra would test whether A2's extra period corresponds to k_s2.

**Rocket noise.** Screech itself is absent at full scale ([lubert-2022](lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md)). The single-monopole, two-microphone phase localisation is a near-field analogue of apparent-source-location ideas in SP-8072-type models. It would not transfer to the distributed sources of rocket plumes without beamforming or holography.
