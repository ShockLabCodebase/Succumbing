## Summary
The paper revisits Powell's 1953 survey of screech from a convergent round nozzle, here a D = 10 mm nozzle with a very thick (8 mm) lip, from NPR 2.0 to 5.84 (M_j 1.05–1.81), using FFT spectra, spark schlieren and two near-field microphones whose separation angle θ is stepped round the jet (p. 2824–2825). The dominant tone falls with NPR in five segments, A1, A2, B, C and D, and the paper identifies each mode from the phase difference ψ(θ) between the microphones. A1 and A2 are varicose (ψ ≈ 0), C is helical (ψ ∝ θ, either sense) and B and D are sinuous (ψ = 0 or π) (p. 2825, 2829–2830). Secondary tones about 25 dB weaker continue the frequency curves of the dominant tones on both sides of each jump. One such tone links B to D, so the authors treat B and D as one sinuous mode interrupted by C (p. 2825, 2833). B and D differ in behaviour, though. B oscillates mostly in a preferred plane, with spasmodic flips to other planes and helical-looking episodes. D oscillates in a single plane that rotates unsteadily about the axis at the equivalent of about 13–90 Hz, so the tone at a fixed microphone is almost 100 % modulated (p. 2829–2832). C is very stable and 14–23 % higher in frequency than the sinuous mode at the same NPR (p. 2823, 2833). The C ↔ D jump is hysteretic (around NPR 4.48; p. 2826). The convection Mach number of the instability waves rises from about 0.6 (A) to 0.8 (B, C), and U_c/U_j averages 0.61 (p. 2827–2828). The discussion explains each stage by an integer change in the loop cycle count N + p, estimates source centroids at 5–6D, and rates each mode by an array-directivity angle β (p. 2833–2835). There is no simulation or stability calculation. The theory is Powell's 1953 feedback picture plus a linear-superposition argument relating sinuous and helical modes (p. 2833–2834).

## Key findings
- **Frequency staging** (Fig. 2, p. 2825; values read from Figs 2 and 19 unless quoted):
  - Dominant tones (at least 10 dB above the local broadband, usually more than 30 dB) form five continuous segments, A1, A2, B, C and D (p. 2825).
  - A1 ≈ 21.5 → 19.4 kHz over NPR ≈ 2.14–2.24. A2 ≈ 21.3 → 20.0 kHz over 2.36–2.44. B starts near 14.6 kHz at NPR ≈ 2.45 and falls to ≈ 11.0 kHz at 3.27. It jumps up to C (≈ 13.3 kHz at 3.3), which falls to ≈ 10.9 kHz at 4.6. D follows from ≈ 9.1 kHz at 4.48 to ≈ 8.2 kHz at 5.84.
  - Exact values (Fig. 3 caption, p. 2826): at NPR 4.48 the tone is C at 11.00 kHz with pressure increasing and D at 9.125 kHz with pressure decreasing.
  - Every jump goes up in frequency except A → B and C → D, which go down. D resumes the B frequency trend (p. 2834).
- **Hysteresis only at C ↔ D** (p. 2823–2826):
  - Powell (1953c) had found the C → D jump at a different NPR from D → C, the only hysteretic jump he saw. It is confirmed here at NPR 4.48 (Fig. 3).
  - The band, read from figures: Fig. 2 arrows at NPR ≈ 4.34 and 4.64; Fig. 17 boundaries at ≈ 4.71 rising and ≈ 4.50 falling, i.e. M_j ≈ 1.62–1.67.
  - In the up and down OASPL sweeps of Fig. 17 the A1/A2 (≈ 2.3), A2/B (≈ 2.5) and B/C (≈ 3.2) boundaries sit at the same NPR both ways, at the ≈ 0.1 NPR point spacing (read from Fig. 17; the paper comments only on C/D).
- **A2 and B both dominant, switching rapidly** (p. 2825, 2835): at NPR 2.45 two strong dominant tones (A2 and B) occur together and "the oscillation switches rapidly between them". Powell (1953c) had called this region "no discrete frequency", which the authors now read as "no *single* discrete frequency". Merle and Davies & Oldfield reported A2 there, implying B was absent or rare in their rigs (p. 2825).
- **Secondary tones** (Figs 2 and 18, p. 2825, 2832–2833, 2835):
  - Secondary tones are 5–10 dB above the broadband and are labelled a1, a2, b, c, d. Each forms a smooth continuation of a dominant tone's frequency curve, and they appear next to every jump except perhaps A2/B, where either mode can be dominant (p. 2825).
  - They average about 25 dB below the dominant tones, falling somewhat with NPR. Points between the two levels all lie in transitions, especially the hysteretic one (p. 2833).
  - NPR 2.35 has two secondaries at once, a1 and b, beside the dominant A2 (p. 2835). A tone "u" (≈ 16 → 13 kHz over NPR 3.2–4.4, read from Fig. 2) may extend a2, as Merle reported, but "must be considered as unidentified" (p. 2825).
  - The authors find it surprising that a secondary survives at all next to a large-amplitude dominant mode that "generally appears to suppress" other inharmonic dominant modes, and that its frequency continues so smoothly (p. 2835).
  - Exact harmonics and scattered tones less than 5 dB above the broadband are not plotted (p. 2825).
- **Mode identification by azimuthal phase** (Figs 9–12, p. 2828–2830):
  - The method was checked on a two-dimensional (AR 8) screeching jet (ψ = 0 or ±π with a jump at the symmetry plane) and on a supersonic hole tone (ψ ≈ 0). It fails when the moveable microphone is within about 15° of the symmetry plane, presumably from low signal-to-noise without filtering, plus broadband noise and reflections (p. 2828–2829).
  - Criteria: ψ = 0 for all θ means varicose; ψ = 0 and π separated by a gap at the symmetry plane means sinuous; ψ varying linearly from 0 to ±π with θ means helical, the sign giving the sense (p. 2829).
  - A1 and A2: ψ ≈ 0 at all θ, axisymmetric, with more scatter than the hole tone (p. 2829).
  - C: two linear branches of opposite sign, helical of either sense with close to equal probability. The sense stayed fixed while the jet ran but could change when it was turned off and on again (p. 2829).
  - B: most points cluster at ψ = 0 and π, with the symmetry plane "often being fixed at about 60°" from the fixed microphone. A smaller group follows the helical pattern. The scatter is put down to oscillations about other planes (Fig. 12a, p. 2829–2830).
  - D: clusters at 0 and π but with a marked overlap. The share of ψ = 0 points falls roughly linearly with θ (one marked deviation), so the symmetry plane lies at "equally likely angles" (Fig. 12b, p. 2829).
- **Envelopes and the rotating D plane** (Figs 10, 13–16, p. 2829–2832):
  - Fixed-microphone traces: A2 steady with a finely ragged envelope; B random on a longer time scale, with an envelope that looks like noise; C steady with fine raggedness; D almost 100 % modulated at about 100 Hz for the trace shown (p. 2829).
  - B's amplitude varies very irregularly but its frequency stays constant, with no spectral broadening (p. 2830).
  - With microphones 90° apart, D shows three behaviours: alternating maxima (a sinuous plane changing orientation); one steady signal while the other briefly grows (the plane "vibrating" about a given plane); and phase reversals across each minimum, which the authors take as strong evidence that the plane rotates (Figs 13–14, p. 2830–2831).
  - The plane may dwell, rotate at a variable rate for few or many revolutions, or change suddenly. One case rotated "rather slowly, say the equivalent of 13 Hz" and accelerated smoothly to about 90 Hz (p. 2831–2832). The cross-correlation coefficient at zero delay and θ = π/2 swings between ±0.5 during rotation and stays near 0 during a dwell (Fig. 16, p. 2831).
  - Cause unknown. The candidates are irregular motion in the downstream part of the source region and unsteady initial swirl (Powell 1953), which would apply to B too. Why B and D behave differently "is not apparent" (p. 2832, 2834–2835).
- **Schlieren mode shapes** (Fig. 4, p. 2826–2827):
  - Cell length grows with NPR. The first one or two cells are sharp, then the cells blur into large disturbances (p. 2827).
  - The alternating near-field density gradients next to the jet, visible up to about 1D outside it, are called "evanescent waves" (p. 2827).
  - Sound waves are clearest for B and C, in opposite phase on the two sides of the jet. For A they are weaker and symmetric (p. 2827).
  - Instability-wave amplitude peaks at 3–5D for A, 4–6D for B, 6–8D for C and 8–10D for D (p. 2827, 2833–2834).
  - In the A2 photo (NPR 2.35) the vortex "rings" tilt and the pattern turns asymmetric with a longer wavelength at the right edge, where the spectrum also has the sinuous b secondary (p. 2827).
  - B spreads most: the visible boundaries reach about 4D wide at 7D. D spreads possibly less than C and much less than B. D's amplitude varies a lot from photo to photo, consistent with Merle's "not always visible" (p. 2827).
- **Convection speed** (Figs 5–6, p. 2827–2828): U_con = Λf, with Λ the evanescent-wave wavelength in the source region (averaged over repeated measurements of each photo) and f the tone frequency.
  - M_con = U_con/c₀: A modes mean 0.64; B rises steadily from 0.60 to 0.75 (mean 0.68); C mean 0.80, with the most scatter; D 0.75 from two points. Overall M_con rises from about 0.6 to 0.8 up to NPR 3.5–4, then levels off or falls (p. 2827–2828).
  - U_con/U_j (U_j fully expanded): B still rises with NPR, but the overall trend is slightly downward, with a mean of 0.61 over 14 points. Read from Fig. 6, values run from ≈ 0.54 (D at NPR 4.85) to ≈ 0.67 (C at 3.3) (p. 2828).
- **Near-field levels** (Figs 17–18, p. 2832–2833):
  - OASPL at the fixed microphone (re 20 µPa, close to the fundamental level) rises very fast through A1 and A2 into B, from ≈ 100 dB at NPR 1.95 to ≈ 128 dB. Then the slope changes suddenly, and B, C and D all cluster at 125–130 dB (p. 2832).
  - With NPR falling, the only significant change is that D is "noticeably lower" than C near the D → C transition (≈ 119–124 dB, read) (p. 2832).
  - Read from Fig. 17: within stage A, A1 reaches ≈ 114–116 dB and A2 only ≈ 106–110 dB.
  - The authors read this as "some sort of limit cycle" reached in mid-B and kept through C and D (p. 2834).
- **Loop-count explanation of the jumps** (p. 2833):
  - A1 → A2 is attributed to the number of cycles in the loop, N + p, changing to the next integer between the stationary-source centroid and the nozzle. As the wavelength grows with cell length, the mode becomes less unstable, and a jump up in frequency restores a more unstable wavelength (Powell 1961, edge-tone analogy).
  - A2: source centroid about 5D, so N + p is between 7 and 8. B: about 5D, N + p about 5. C: about 6D, N + p about 5.
  - p is an undetermined phase that "cannot be considered *a priori* to be zero" (p. 2833).
  - If B and C differ only in N, the frequency ratio should be 6/5 = 1.2 or 7/6 = 1.17. The measured C/B ratio peaks at 1.23 near NPR 3.5 and falls to 1.14 near 5 (Fig. 20). The authors call the peaked, non-monotonic shape harder to explain (p. 2833).
- **Sinuous vs helical** (p. 2833–2834):
  - In linear theory a helix is two sinuous modes at right angles in quadrature, and a sinuous mode is two equal counter-rotating helices. B's occasional helical look is then explained if its unsteady sinuous motion sometimes has the right components (p. 2833).
  - Fully developed, the helical mode looks like a continuous helical vortex. The sinuous mode looks like a Kármán street curved round the jet, a series of "horseshoes", whose two sides are less connected. Superposing two such sinuous modes cannot give a continuous helical vortex.
  - Since B, C and D are all large and highly nonlinear, the authors suggest treating fully developed sinuous and helical modes as "essentially different" modes. This may explain the varying C/B ratio, even though their M_con are "apparently not significantly different" (p. 2834).
- **Directionality angle β** (Fig. 21, p. 2834–2835):
  - β (eq. below) measures how far the broad maximum of Powell's source array points from the upstream jet direction, i.e. how well the radiation from cells spaced s apart reinforces at the nozzle.
  - Mean β: B 16.7° (closest to upstream), C 55.5° (furthest), D 36.8°, "significantly different" from B.
  - Read from Fig. 21: A1 ≈ 32° and 47°, A2 ≈ 32°.
  - s is taken from the second cell, because the source region is too disturbed to measure (Umeda et al. 1987b). β ignores the helical nature of C (p. 2834–2835).
- **Lip thickness and initial conditions** (p. 2835):
  - The larger the lip, the greater the loop "gain" and the more vigorous the oscillations (Powell 1953d; Norum 1983).
  - Merle's 2.5D lip gave extensive secondary oscillations that were not found here.
  - Boundary-layer state, exit velocity profile, swirl, turbulence level, upstream acoustic waves and the acoustic environment, including reflections, are all candidate influences. So "different experimental setups will display different detailed behavior".

## Setup / method
- **Nozzle** (Fig. 1, p. 2824): convergent, round, exit diameter d = 10 mm; a short parallel section after a 16° half-angle cone; area contraction 100:1; maximum NPR 5.84.
  - Outer diameter 26 mm at the exit plane, so the lip is 8 mm thick (t/D = 0.8, "almost equal to the exit diameter"). No absorbing material or angled reflector on the lip.
  - For comparison: Powell's 1953 lip was half the exit diameter (D = 25.4 mm); Davies & Oldfield's was unstated and apparently much thinner; Merle's was about 2.5D.
- **Conditions** (p. 2825): NPR (written R) 2.0–5.84, i.e. M_j 1.05–1.81. Unheated air is implied; stagnation and ambient temperature are not stated. For each data sample (microphone angle, NPR, photo) the jet was turned off and restarted, except in the hysteresis tests (p. 2825). Each mode identification is therefore an ensemble over restarts.
- **Microphones** (Fig. 1, p. 2824):

  | Item | Value |
  |---|---|
  | Fixed mic M_f | B&K 4135, 6.4 mm |
  | Moveable mic M_m | B&K 4138, 3.2 mm, rotated about the axis in 15° steps of θ |
  | Radial distance | r = 77 mm (7.7D) |
  | Axial position | both in the plane s = 40 mm (4D) upstream of the nozzle face |
  | Recording | TEAC R-410 data recorder, direct mode, 100 Hz–100 kHz |
  | Analysis | Fujitsu M780-30 mainframe (Kyoto Univ.), TEAC C-120 digital correlator, Ono Sokki CF 300 FFT analyser |

  Sampling rate, FFT block length and number of averages for the spectra are not stated.
- **Phase measurement** (Fig. 7, p. 2828): ψ = 2π ΔT/T from the cross-correlogram of the two microphone signals (ΔT = delay of the first correlation maximum, T = period). 1024 averages, delay resolution Δτ = 2 µs, and 18 independent measurements per θ (each a separate jet start), shown as means with scatter bars (p. 2828–2830). Fig. 16 gives the zero-delay correlation coefficient as a function of time, but its averaging window is not stated.
- **Schlieren** (p. 2825): conventional single-pass system, 20 cm mirrors with 200 cm focal length, Sugawara MS-230 spark of about 1 µs. Knife edge normal to the jet axis, so only axial density gradients show (positive bright). These are single-shot, not time-resolved, images (Umeda et al. 1987a).
- **Validation cases** (Fig. 8, p. 2824, 2828): a convergent rectangular nozzle 40 × 5 mm (AR 8) at NPR 4.48 (sinuous screech), and a supersonic hole tone (Umeda et al. 1988) with nozzle-to-hole diameter ratio 2.20 (as printed), spacing 3.00 nozzle diameters, NPR 3.90 (varicose).
- **Modelling** (p. 2823–2824, 2833–2835): no computation. The framework is Powell's (1953) loop: sinuous (or other) disturbances are excited at the lip, grow, interact with the cells to form an array of stationary sources, and radiate back to the nozzle. It has a phase criterion (the wavelengths against the loop size) and an amplitude criterion (loop gain, i.e. instability amplification times the effectiveness of radiation back to the nozzle). Stage jumps are explained by N → N + 1, by analogy with edge tones (Powell 1961). β comes from Powell's (1953a) array directivity. The sinuous/helical relation is argued from linear superposition. A cylindrical-source effect of the helical mode was discussed in Powell et al. (1990) (p. 2834).
- **Method dependence on data:** the phase method needs simultaneous time-resolved signals at two points but gives only a time-averaged phase per run. Mode type comes from its distribution over θ and over restarts. Convection speed combines a single-shot wavelength with the spectral frequency and assumes one dominant mode in the photo. Envelopes (Figs 10, 13–16) are read from oscilloscope traces, with no Hilbert transform or envelope statistics.

## Key equations
Checked against the rendered pages 2828, 2833 and 2834.

- **Phase from the correlogram** (p. 2828): $\psi = 2\pi\,\Delta T/T$.
- **Convection speed** (p. 2827): $U_{con}=\Lambda f$, $M_{con}=U_{con}/c_0$ (c₀ ambient sound speed); also $U_{con}/U_j$ with $U_j$ the fully expanded velocity (p. 2828).
- **Loop count** (p. 2833): the loop holds $N+p$ cycles, $N$ an integer and $p<1$. The explicit form is not printed. In Powell's picture with a source centroid at distance $h$ (my form), $N+p = hf\left(\dfrac{1}{U_{con}}+\dfrac{1}{c_0}\right)=\dfrac{h}{\Lambda}\,(1+M_{con})$.
  > Check (mine, c₀ = 343 m/s assumed, f read from Fig. 2): A2 at NPR 2.35, h = 5D, f ≈ 21.3 kHz, M_con 0.64 gives N + p ≈ 7.9 ("between 7 and 8"). C at 3.90, h = 6D, f ≈ 11.9 kHz, M_con 0.73 gives ≈ 4.9 ("about 5"). B at 2.93, h = 5D, f ≈ 11.9 kHz, M_con 0.70 gives ≈ 4.2, lower than the stated "about 5".
- **Sinuous–helical superposition** (p. 2833):
  $$\cos\theta\,\sin(\omega t-kx)+\sin\theta\,\cos(\omega t-kx)=\sin(\theta+\omega t-kx),$$
  $$\sin(\theta+\omega t-kx)+\sin(-\theta+\omega t-kx)=2\cos\theta\,\sin(\omega t-kx),$$
  with k the downstream wavenumber and θ the azimuth.
- **Array directionality angle** (p. 2834):
  $$\beta=\cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{s}-\frac{1}{M_{con}}\right),$$
  where λ is the acoustic wavelength, s the cell (source) spacing (second cell used), M_con the convection Mach number "averaged over the source region", and β the angle of the broad array maximum from the upstream jet direction.
  > Derivation and check (mine): sources s apart with phase lag 2πfs/U_con reinforce towards polar angle φ (from downstream) when $fs(1/U_{con}-\cos\varphi/c_0)=1$. With β = π − φ and λ = c₀/f this gives the formula, and β = 0 is exactly Powell's frequency formula $f=U_{con}/[s(1+M_{con})]$. So $f/f_{Powell}=(1+M_{con})/(1+M_{con}\cos\beta)$: about 1.02 for B (β 16.7°, M_con 0.68), 1.09 for D (36.8°, 0.75) and 1.24 for C (55.5°, 0.80). C thus sits about 20 % above Powell's formula with the second-cell spacing, close to the measured C/B ratio of 1.14–1.23. Also for B at NPR 2.93, β = 16.7° implies s ≈ 1.2D, close to a Pack-type spacing estimate (≈ 1.2D), which supports reading λ as the acoustic wavelength.

## Notation
- R: pressure ratio (NPR, p₀/p∞); M_j: fully expanded Mach number; U_j: fully expanded velocity; d = D = 10 mm exit diameter.
- A1, A2, B, C, D: dominant modes ("stages" in the edge-tone sense; the paper prefers "modes" because the azimuthal form changes, keeping "stages" A1 and A2 of mode A; p. 2824). a1, a2, b, c, d: secondary tones continuing them; u: unidentified secondary.
- Varicose = toroidal = axisymmetric; sinuous = lateral = flapping; helical = spinning.
- M_f, M_m: fixed and moveable microphones; θ: their angular separation; ψ: phase difference; ΔT, T: correlogram delay and period; τ: delay; N: number of measurements (18) per θ; R_xy: zero-delay cross-correlation coefficient.
- Λ: instability (evanescent-wave) wavelength; U_con, M_con = U_con/c₀: convection velocity and Mach number; c₀: ambient sound speed.
- N + p: cycles in the feedback loop; λ: acoustic wavelength; s: cell spacing (also used for the axial microphone offset in Fig. 1); β: array-maximum angle from the upstream direction.

## Relevance to this project
This is the primary source for the round-jet staging map that every later staging paper cites: the A1/A2/B/C/D labels, B and D as sinuous and C as helical, and the C/D hysteresis. It is central to the [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](../topics/screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md) thread, and it settles points the reviews quote second-hand ([raman-1998](raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) p. 49, 52 reads its C/B ratio, and D "resisted classification" before this paper).

**Staging and switching (the lab's and the PyFR LES's question)**
- **Hysteresis exists, at C/D.** The topic page says hysteresis "has not been tested" for staging. Here it was, by sweeping NPR up and down without restarting the jet. The only hysteretic jump is C ↔ D (band ≈ NPR 4.34–4.71 depending on the figure, M_j ≈ 1.62–1.67). A1/A2, A2/B and B/C boundaries coincide in the two sweeps at the ≈ 0.1 NPR resolution of Fig. 17 (my reading). So any A1/A2 bistable band is narrower than ΔNPR ≈ 0.1 (ΔM_j ≈ 0.03), consistent with the ΔM_j ≈ 0.01–0.015 switching window of [mancinelli-2019](mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) (p. 6–7), but it is not resolved.
- **A test case for the continuation driver** (my suggestion). `Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/` assumes a saddle-node structure for A1/A2. C ↔ D is the one round-jet transition with documented hysteresis, so stepping NPR across 4.3–4.8 (up from C, down from D) would be a positive control: two distinct limits should appear there, whereas a coincident pair at A1/A2 would then mean a sharp exchange rather than a failure of the method. It is costlier (Mach disk, longer source region at 6–10D) and needs a domain long enough for the D instability peak at 8–10D.
- **The A1 → A2 boundary depends on the rig.** Here it is at NPR ≈ 2.3 (M_j ≈ 1.16, between the A1 photo at 2.26 and the A2 photo at 2.35), above the 2.17–2.27 of the topic page's five facilities. The lip is very thick (t/D = 0.8), against the PyFR nozzle's 1 mm flat lip, Mazharmanesh's l/D_e = 0.31 and the leakage nozzle's t/D = 0.33. Powell's St are also lower (A2 ≈ 0.55–0.60 against 0.65–0.71 elsewhere, with my T₀ assumption). This adds to the "staging NPR belongs to the rig" claim, as does Sec. H of the paper itself (p. 2835).
- **Switching and coexistence.** At NPR 2.45 A2 and B are both dominant and the jet "switches rapidly" between them (p. 2825). That is the earliest report in the library of mutually exclusive switching at a staging point, later quantified with 1–4 s dwell times at A1/A2 (Mancinelli 2019) and as anti-correlated amplitudes ([mazharmanesh-2026](mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) p. 8–13).
- **Secondary tones are not proof of coexistence** (my critique). Elsewhere the paper calls the secondaries "simultaneous", 25 dB down on both sides of every jump. Its spectra are averages, so a weak continuous loop (like the coherence "processes" of [bell-2021](bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md) p. 7–9) cannot be told from rare short episodes of the other loop. Both readings would give the smooth frequency continuation in Fig. 19. Bell's coexisting but uncorrelated twin-jet processes, and the multimodal SPOD peaks of [edgington-mitchell-2022](edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) (p. 15–17), are the modern versions of the same observation. The authors' point that a dominant mode "generally appears to suppress" others but the secondary survives fits a mean-flow-mediated competition picture (Mazharmanesh 2026).
- **Loop counts against modern counts.** The paper's N + p ≈ 7–8 (A2) and ≈ 5 (B, C) use source centroids at 5–6D. [mercier-2017](mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) puts the source at the 4th shock (≈ 2.8D) with loops of 4 (A1), 5 (A2) and 3–4 (B) periods, and its Table 1 cites this paper's 5–6D as a contrasting value (Mercier note, p. 204). With the same h f(1/U_c + 1/c₀) scaling, moving the A2 source from 5D to 2.8D turns 7.9 into ≈ 4.4, close to Mercier's 5 (my estimate). The 5–6D centroid is where the sound *looks* centred in a single spark photo, not a measured phase origin, and the paper admits p is unknown.
- **β as an early k_s2 signature** (my derivation). β = 0 is Powell's formula with the second-cell spacing, and the measured β gives f/f_Powell ≈ 1.02 (B), 1.09 (D) and 1.24 (C). So B obeys Powell's formula with the primary spacing, and C runs about 20 % above it, close to its 14–23 % excess over B. This matches the assignment B ↔ k_s1, C ↔ k_s2 with k_s2/k_s1 ≈ 1.1–1.27 (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 11–13; [nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech](nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) p. 7–13). The paper's "if B and C differ only in N, the ratio is 6/5 or 7/6" is the loop-count form of the same statement, as in Mercier's (k_h + k_s)/k̄_sc = m/N. The A modes give β ≈ 32–47° from three points, too few to show an A1/A2 difference.
- **A thick lip and "re-emerging B".** [ponton-1992](ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) (p. 537, 547) finds that with t/D 0.4 and 0.625 the B mode re-emerges and dominates above M_j ≈ 1.7. Here, with t/D 0.8, the sinuous D takes over from M_j ≈ 1.62–1.67, and a secondary tone connects it to B (Fig. 19). Ponton's re-emerging B and Powell's D are probably the same sinuous family (my inference; Raman 1998, p. 75, already mentions a C→D switch in Ponton's thick-lip data). Ponton's thin-lip B → C at M_j 1.43–1.44 also matches the B → C jump here (NPR ≈ 3.28, M_j 1.42).

**Flapping, helical and precession**
- The D plane rotating at 13–90 Hz, B's preferred plane with spasmodic flips, and C's sense being chosen at each start are the experimental facts behind [nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets](nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md). There, weak |m| = 2 streaks split the degenerate m = ±1 pair into flapping and wagging modes and set and slowly precess the flapping axis. Powell's superposition argument (p. 2833) is the degenerate linear case; his suggestion that nonlinear sinuous and helical modes are "essentially different" foreshadows the streak-induced splitting. His candidate causes, unsteady initial swirl and irregular downstream motion, are alternatives to streaks that a swirl-controlled or streak-forced test could separate.
- Time scales: 100 Hz modulation on a 9 kHz D tone is St ≈ 0.002 (my estimate), i.e. hundreds of screech cycles per revolution, like the slow streak rotation (≈ π/2 over a whole LES record) in Nogueira 2026 (p. 14). For a sinuous plane, a fixed-mic envelope ∝ |cos(φ − Ωt)| modulates at twice the rotation rate, so the "≈ 100 Hz modulation" and "about 100 revolutions per second" (p. 2829) may differ by a factor of two (my observation).
- B's larger spreading (p. 2827) is the basis for the δ = 0.25 used for m = 1 in [nogueira-2021-absolute-instability](nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) (p. 7).

**Convection speed and frequency models**
- U_con/U_j ≈ 0.54–0.67 (mean 0.61) in the source region agrees with Mercier's 0.54–0.61 upstream of the source (p. 212), with the 0.57–0.60 twin-jet values of [knast-2018](knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) (p. 10–11), and with the 0.59–0.68 range of [edgington-mitchell-2014](edgington-mitchell-2014-coherent-structure-helical-screech-mode.md) for C. It is lower than Tam's 0.7 ([tam-1986](tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md)). M_con for B and C differs (0.68 against 0.80), although the authors call it "apparently not significantly different" (p. 2834). As Knast found, convection speed alone does not separate the stages.
- The paper predates guided-jet-mode closure ([mancinelli-2019](mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md); [mancinelli-2021](mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md)). Its β deviations from free-stream-sound reinforcement are what a different return path or a shifted shock wavenumber would produce. The weak "u" tone above C is the "U" that Mancinelli 2021 sees in its m = 0 spectrum. Powell et al. only say it *may* extend a2 and call it unidentified (p. 2825), which is weaker than the Mancinelli 2021 note's wording.

**Methods and data for the lab workflow (CLAUDE.md)**
- **Two-microphone phase over restarts** works with any time-resolved pair of signals but gives only a time-averaged ψ per run. Here it identifies a rotating plane only through the spread of ψ over 18 restarts. An azimuthal ring (Mancinelli 2019) with complex demodulation at the tone, or the Hilbert envelopes and phases of two microphones 90° apart, would give the instantaneous plane angle φ(t) = atan2 of the two envelopes, and hence rotation rate, dwell times and reversals, the quantities Powell read by eye from oscilloscope traces.
- **Time-resolved schlieren (SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD):** a flapping plane that precesses splits the tone across m = ±1, or across SPOD modes 1 and 2 (Nogueira 2026, p. 22), and block-averaged SPOD hides the rotation. A reconstruction of the tone band followed by HPOD should recover the slow rotation as a phase drift between the two leading HPOD modes, and B's flips as amplitude exchanges. That needs records of several seconds, given 13–90 Hz rotation. For C ↔ D hysteresis, short-time SPOD or HPOD during slow NPR ramps would map the bistable band directly.
- **Non-time-resolved PIV:** POD pairs assume one steady tone and a fixed plane. For B and especially D the plane wanders between snapshots, so a planar POD pair would mix flapping and helical appearances (as Powell's photos did). Conditional sampling on a simultaneous two-microphone phase would be needed.
- **Single-shot schlieren with the tone frequency** (U_con = Λf) is a cheap convection-speed estimate that works on non-time-resolved images, but it assumes a single dominant mode in each photo, which fails near switching (A2 photo with b present, p. 2827).

**Rocket noise:** not directly relevant. Screech is absent at full scale. The thick-lip and reflection sensitivity (p. 2835) matter for subscale tests with flat nozzle bases.
