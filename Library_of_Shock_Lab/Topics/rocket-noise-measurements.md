# Rocket-noise measurements

_Last updated: 2026-10-25 · 24 papers_

## Scope

Full-scale measurements of rocket and high-power jet noise: horizontal static firings (Shuttle RSRM, GEM-60, SLS QM-1/QM-2, Antares stage 1), launches (Scout, Delta, Peacekeeper and Titan IV around 1990; Antares, Falcon 9, SLS Artemis I, Atlas V, Vulcan, Firefly Alpha and Starship since), on-vehicle Shuttle ignition overpressure, and the F-22A and F404 aircraft data that first showed nonlinear propagation and full-scale BBSAN. Subscale and lab data are included where they test the full-scale picture: ONERA's small static rockets, a 12.7 cm solid motor, the 1/42 Epsilon and 1/40 MARTEL jets, and a cold Mach 3 lab jet used to ask when steepening is cumulative. It covers levels, spectra, directivity, source location, sound power, ignition overpressure, pad and terrain effects, nonlinear propagation, crackle and sonic booms. The models themselves (SP-8072, the 1996 maximum-OASPL formula, DSM-2, SATURN, the ignition-overpressure model, CAA/LES) are on [rocket-noise-models](rocket-noise-models.md); source physics in lab jets on [jet-noise-mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md); impingement feedback on [impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md). The numbers are collected in [rocket_noise.csv](../Data/rocket_noise.csv). Most modern work is from BYU (Gee's group) with NASA, Blue Ridge Research and JAXA; the older data come from Rockwell, the University of Alabama (McInerny) and ONERA, and the lab and simulation work on nonlinearity from UT Austin and ONERA. None is Shock Lab group work.

## Current understanding

1. **Rocket noise is broadband mixing noise dominated by Mach-wave radiation.** No screech, BBSAN, impingement tones or spatiospectral lobes have been found in full-scale rocket data ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 9, 12–16). RSRM spectra are a single low-frequency hump ([Kenny 2009](../Notes/kenny-2009-space-shuttle-srm-plume-acoustics.md), p. 13–14), and a subscale Epsilon free jet shows no BBSAN at 90–120° ([Tsutsumi 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 13). The absence is not a matter of scale alone: a full-scale afterburning F404 shows BBSAN at discrete shock cells ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 32), and so does a 1/40-scale overexpanded Mach 3.1 jet at T_i/T_∞ = 6.5 ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 19).
2. **Acoustic efficiency is 0.3–0.4%**, below the classic 0.5% and SP-8072's 1%: RSRM 0.43%, Falcon 9 0.34% ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 8), revised to 0.30% from near-pad data ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 14); Atlas V 0.33%, but Vulcan Centaur needs 0.17% ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 375). Small static rockets gave about 0.7% once ground reflection was modelled ([Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md), p. 1854).
3. **The dominant source sits 17–22 D_e downstream and moves upstream with frequency** ([Gee 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 4; [Gee 2016](../Notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 1, 5; [Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 12–14). SP-8072's source-location curve inherits a plotting error from Potter (1968) (Lubert, p. 12–13). Small static rockets, measured at several times the full-scale Strouhal number, put the peak-octave sources at 5–15D ([Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md), p. 1855).
4. **Peak directivity is 60–72° from the plume axis once angles are referred to that source**, not SP-8072's ~50° ([James 2012](../Notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md), p. 8; [Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 625; [Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 11–12; [Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 82), as vertical launches already showed in 1957 ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md), p. 512). An Oertel convective Mach number predicts the angle ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 14).
5. **Spectra peak at Sr = fD/U_e ≈ 0.01–0.025**, about ten times lower than lab and military jets, for reasons not yet understood ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 10; [Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 628–629; [Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 15–16). Hot subscale jets sit in between, near 0.07–0.1 ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 27; [Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md), p. 1855, the note's arithmetic).
6. **Waveforms carry acoustic shocks at every range measured**, from 15 km in 1990s Titan IV records ([McInerny & Ölçmen 2005](../Notes/mcinerny-2005-high-intensity-rocket-noise-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 584–586) to ~65 km for Falcon 9 ([Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 624). Nonlinear steepening replaces high-frequency energy that linear absorption would remove ([Gee 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 9–10; [Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 84–85) but barely changes OASPL near the source (Gee 2008, p. 12; [Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 25). Full-scale jets sit deep in the cumulative regime (effective Gol'dberg number Λ ≈ 760 for an F-35A) and a cold Mach 3 lab jet far outside it (Λ ≈ 0.15), so lab and subscale waveform statistics are not full-scale ones ([Baars et al. 2014](../Notes/baars-2014-cumulative-nonlinear-waveform-distortions-high-speed-jets.md), p. 347, 353).
7. **The pad controls the first seconds.** On the Shuttle, ignition overpressure reached the vehicle as an N-wave of about 13 kPa from the duct openings, followed by a later, lower-frequency duct overpressure from the duct exits ([Ikawa & Laspesa 1985](../Notes/ikawa-1985-ignition-duct-overpressure-space-shuttle-srm.md), p. 481, 483). In the far field it is aimed along the flame-trench exit ([Gee 2023](../Notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 4; [Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 51; [Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 5), and duct and deck sources dominate until the free plume clears the pad ([Panda 2013](../Notes/panda-2013-noise-sources-rocket-test-firings-launch-phased-array.md), p. 19–20).
8. **Data-built models now beat the legacy ones.** The 1996 maximum-OASPL formula fitted its own five launches to about 4 dB rms ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md), Table 1, p. 514; [Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 53), and environmental-assessment models miss by 10–20 dB for SLS and Starship ([Gee 2023](../Notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 3–4; [Gee 2024](../Notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 5), while SATURN is within ~1 dB inside 20 km ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 91, 95).

## Static firings: from subscale motors to the SLS booster

NASA's first full-scale solid-motor database came from three horizontal RSRM firings (TEM-13, FVM-2, FSM-15; D_e 3.88 m) at Promontory, Utah, gathered so SP-8072 would no longer be extrapolated from 5–50 kN subscale motors to 13 MN ([Kenny 2009](../Notes/kenny-2009-space-shuttle-srm-plume-acoustics.md), p. 7). The first look found positively skewed waveforms, a near-field OASPL peak of ≈156.5 dB at ≈23 D_n on a line 67 m off-axis, a far-field peak near 50° on a 304 m arc, and OASPL that tracked nozzle vectoring more than the 27% thrust decay (Kenny, p. 11–16). Haynes & Kenny turned the same arc into Ares I directivity indices ([Haynes 2009](../Notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md), p. 12–13). Two reanalyses changed how those arc data are read. Gee et al. mapped OASPL and skewness between the 18D line and the 80D arc: peak radiation at ~56° traces back to a source at 17–18D; the source moves upstream and the directivity sideline as frequency rises; Sk{∂p/∂t} exceeds 30 and grows with distance; and beyond 40D, where the plume hits a hillside "bowl", high-frequency content and skewness collapse ([Gee 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 4–11). They warned that the 80D arc is not in the geometric far field at low frequency (p. 6). James et al. made this quantitative: the 10 Hz source sits ≈31 D_e downstream, and referring each band to its apparent source rotates the DSM peak from 51° to 65°, matching the Oertel estimate; DSM-1 and DSM-2 agree only beyond 200–300 D_e, about 1 km ([James 2012](../Notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md), p. 5–11). Lubert et al. restate the result as 53° → 65° ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 14–15).

The five-segment SLS booster (QM-2, 2016) was measured only in the far field: five stations at 80–120°, 2.40–2.64 km away, with OASPL falling from 118 to 106 dB, peaks at 10–20 Hz that move lower towards the aft angles (opposite to lab jets), energy out to ~10 kHz, and Sk{∂p/∂t} of 21–32 at 80–100° but under 3 at 110–120° ([Reichman 2018](../Notes/reichman-2018-qm2-srm-far-field-acoustics.md), p. 6–10). QM-1, at similar levels a year earlier, peaked near Sk ≈ 3; the authors favour the atmosphere over motor temperature as the cause (p. 9). Horizontal firings into terrain are not clean free jets, which is why SATURN rejects the James source curve ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 79).

Subscale static firings show how easily the ground and the instruments distort these quantities. ONERA fired four similar rockets (D 3.6–27 cm, M_e 3.3, U_e ≈ 2500 m/s) horizontally 1.05 m above concrete, with 20D and 40D arcs and a line array at 5–40D ([Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md), p. 1853–1854). Integrating over the 20D arc gave η rising with size from 0.4 to 0.6%, an artefact of the nozzle height falling from about 30D to 4D; the arc missed sources beyond 30D; and protection grids raised levels above 10 kHz, inflating the smallest rocket's η to about 1% (p. 1854–1855). An image-jet fit gave 0.7% for the other three (Table 2, p. 1854). The octave peak fell from 8 to 1 kHz with size, Strouhal scaling at fD/U_e ≈ 0.1 (p. 1855; the note's arithmetic). The very overexpanded 84 cm Rita II booster radiated "unstable" emission, possibly from a Mach disk (p. 1856).

## Near-field intensity and source location

Vector intensity became the main source-location tool because far-field beamforming assumes straight rays. On a 12.7 cm solid motor, tetrahedral probes within ~6D of the shear layer showed the source contracting and moving upstream with frequency, radiation at 44–51°, and refraction near the plume; the OASPL maximum lay no further downstream than 10–12D, while the strongest single band (500 Hz) came from ~20D ([Gee 2010](../Notes/gee-2010-near-field-vector-intensity-solid-rocket-motor.md), p. 3–6). On the 1.09 m GEM-60, twelve probes were processed with the phase-and-amplitude-gradient estimator (PAGE), which stays accurate far past the spatial Nyquist limit where finite-difference intensity fails ([Gee 2016](../Notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 4; [Reichman 2018](../Notes/reichman-2018-qm2-srm-far-field-acoustics.md), p. 7). Ray tracing put the 3 dB region at 17–32D at 50 Hz and 10–20D above 600 Hz, with radiation angles rising from 65–70° to 80–85°; the true source is at least 30D long and contracts less, and lies further downstream, than an afterburning F-22A source ([Gee 2016](../Notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 1, 4–5). These intensity locations underlie SATURN's s(Sr) = 9.85 Sr^−0.21 D_eff ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 80) and Lubert's ~22 D_e at Sr 0.025 ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 13–14).

Aircraft sources sit much closer to the nozzle: holography places afterburning-fighter sources at ~6–15 D_e (Lubert, p. 12), and on the installed F404 at afterburner the dominant lipline maximum lies between the potential-core end (~7 D_j) and the supersonic-core end (~12–13 D_j), with BBSAN sources at shock cells II–VIII, cell I silent and VI–VII loudest ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 18, 32).

Beamforming has mostly mapped pads. On the ONERA rockets a focused line array put the peak-octave sources at 5–15D, but the extended, directive sources defeated a precise peak location ([Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md), p. 1855). A 70-microphone array 400 ft from Wallops pad 0A found the water-quenched flame-duct exit to be the dominant Antares static-fire source at all frequencies, with the engine mount 16 dB weaker, contrary to SP-8072's distributed plume. At launch the source moved from mount openings to the duct, then over the deck when hot plume spilled during the TEL-avoidance tilt before the rainbirds reached full flow, and only after ~7 s to the free plume ([Panda 2013](../Notes/panda-2013-noise-sources-rocket-test-firings-launch-phased-array.md), p. 17–23). Its SEM deconvolution broke distributed plume sources into spurious spots (p. 16).

## Directivity, convective Mach number and flight

The gap between static and launch angles is old: Cole et al. (1957) found 50–60° for horizontal firings and 70–80° for vertical launches, which McInerny puts down to the ground. On the first Titan IV launch, vertical-launch factors (8 dB at 70–75°) reproduced the timing and trend of OASPL from 0.5 to 15.7 km much better than static-firing factors, which put the 0.5 km peak 3–4 s late ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md), p. 512, 514–515). Measured peak angles agree once the origin is right: RSRM 65° ([James 2012](../Notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md), p. 8), GEM-63 ~65° ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 15), and Falcon 9 64° both at 7–14 km (1 dB-down 58–70°; [Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 625) and from stations at 240–750 m (1 dB band 58–72°, 3 dB band 48–79°; [Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 11–12), with SLS at 66° and Antares 230 at 60–70° as cited by Kellison (p. 12). The static Oertel number gives 69° for Falcon 9; subtracting the vehicle Mach number at peak radiation (M_v ≈ 0.55) gives 64°, and the convective-velocity form needs κ ≈ 0.31, half the lab range 0.6–0.85 ([Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 625–627). Kellison et al. compared five convective Mach numbers: the supersonic instability-wave value (66°) fits best and the KH form (72°) is too far from the axis. Their frequency-resolved Q(θ, Sr) peaks at 10–30° below Sr 0.004 and settles at 60–75° above 0.0125 ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 12–13). The Atlas V map behind SATURN settles at ≈72° above Sr 0.03, 15–20° further from the axis than SP-8072's indices ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 82), and near-field vectors at high frequency (80–85°) point upstream of every far-field index ([Gee 2016](../Notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 5). The 3 dB width is ≈30–35°, against 56–57° in the Sutherland and Eldred models ([Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 627).

Subscale arcs carry the near-field bias that James describes. Rita II peaks at 40–50° on nozzle-centred 11–20D arcs ([Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md), Fig. 9, p. 1856), and the MARTEL jet at θ′ ≈ 48° on a 41.6D arc, 15° short of its Oertel angle, which the authors blame on distributed sources and an arc that is too close ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 16, 19).

## Sound power, spectra and clustering

Sound power has been measured by integrating a far-field directivity (Falcon 9, 196 dB re 1 pW, η 0.34%; [Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 627–628) and by sweeping one microphone through angle along the trajectory (195.4 ± 0.7 dB, η 0.30%; [Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 14); Kellison et al. say the earlier value lacked a ground-reflection correction worth 2–3 dB (p. 15). Scaled to 100 D_eff, OASPL collapses at 141–144 dB for Falcon 9, GEM-63 and RSRM, well below the Greska et al. convective-Mach fit at Falcon 9's M_O = 2.81, which they read as η saturating at rocket conditions (p. 16–17). The maximum-OASPL formula (0.5% of ½TV_e, spread spherically from R = d/sin 70°, plus 8 dB) was first tested on five launches of Scout, Delta, Peacekeeper and Titan IV at 0.3–16 km: about 4 dB rms over 19 stations, too high for the small motors and too low near the second Titan IV, which McInerny read as η rising with motor thrust ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md), Table 1, p. 513–514; [Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 53). With modern inputs it fits Falcon 9 within 1 dB at 100 D_e ([Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 628), all eleven Atlas V stations within ±2.5 dB ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 53), and two Atlas V launches to 20 km with RMSE 1.8 dB once a ground factor δ = 1.9 is included ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 375–376).

Peak Strouhal numbers range from 0.010 to 0.025 across RSRM, Falcon 9, Atlas V and SATURN (values and bases in the CSV; see open questions). The 1990s constant-bandwidth PSDs peak at fD_e/V_e ≈ 0.015–0.02, near SP-8072's 0.025, but are "deficient in energy" relative to it at high frequency ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md), p. 513, 516). Exit-sound-speed and critical-diameter scalings collapse rockets with other jets better than fD/U_e does ([Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 628–629).

Clustering matters. The Atlas V's two RD-180 nozzles (S/D ≈ 1.14) give a lower peak frequency broadside (32 Hz) than end-on (43 Hz); both collapse to Sr ≈ 0.019 if the broadside view uses the merged diameter D√2, and OASPL shows no asymmetry beyond ±1.9 dB ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 53–55). Starship (74.4 MN) is 5–10.5 dB louder than SLS and 9–17 dB louder than Falcon 9, more than thrust alone suggests ([Gee 2024](../Notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 7), while Vulcan's separated plumes radiate at half the Atlas V efficiency ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 375), and Delta's six 380 kN strap-ons behaved like small motors although the vehicle's lift-off thrust is 3.1 MN ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md), p. 513–514).

## Nonlinear propagation and crackle

The first rocket evidence is in records made around 1990. McInerny's spectra above 120 dB overlay at high frequency whatever the distance: Titan IV levels near fD_e/V_e ≈ 1 fell only 25–30 dB between 0.5 and 12.6–15.7 km after spreading correction, against about 50–65 dB from linear absorption (Fig. 4 read, with the note's ISO 9613-1 estimate) ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md), p. 515–516). In the Titan IV and Scout waveforms, rises above a transition size follow the steady weak-shock law dp/dt ∝ Δp², which shaped Gaussian noise with the same spectrum and ground reflection never does; the fitted diffusivities are of the order expected for relaxing air, though the note finds one fit inconsistent with steady-shock theory, and "vestiges" of shocks remain at 15 km ([McInerny & Ölçmen 2005](../Notes/mcinerny-2005-high-intensity-rocket-noise-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 584–586). The 20 kHz bandwidth hid the absorption at shocks rising in about 50 µs, the instrument limit (p. 582, 586–587), and the Morfey–Howell indicator Q/S shows energy leaving the 16 Hz Titan peak for frequencies above 1–2 kHz (p. 588).

The effect was demonstrated with a propagation model on F-22A run-ups: a generalized-Burgers propagator fed with 23 m waveforms matched 305 m one-third-octave spectra within 2–7 dB to 20 kHz, while a linear model was 7–12 dB low at 4 kHz and 120–140 dB low at 20 kHz, at every angle from 90° to 145° from the inlet; OASPL changed by about 1 dB or less ([Gee 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 9–12). This contradicts an earlier F/A-18E bispectral result of no nonlinearity at 90° (p. 11). The later rocket data agree. Sk{∂p/∂t} exceeds 30 near the RSRM peak direction and grows with distance ([Gee 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 8); QM-2 still shows 21–32 at 2.5 km ([Reichman 2018](../Notes/reichman-2018-qm2-srm-far-field-acoustics.md), p. 8); SLS keeps 80–95 dB at 10 kHz at 1.5–5 km ([Gee 2023](../Notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 6); Falcon 9 launch and boom spectra hold the weak-shock ≈10 dB/decade roll-off to 10 kHz ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 6). Atlas V spectra lose ≈15 dB at 1 kHz over 7 km against 26 dB from linear absorption, and 30–40 dB at 10 kHz against more than 1200 dB ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 84–85).

Baars et al. turned this into a criterion. From the level and peak frequency on the peak-noise ray they estimate a shock-formation distance r̄ and an effective Gol'dberg number Λ for spherically spreading waves; Λ ≫ 1 guarantees shocks and Λ ≲ 1 makes nonlinearity negligible ([Baars et al. 2014](../Notes/baars-2014-cumulative-nonlinear-waveform-distortions-high-speed-jets.md), p. 344–347). Published F-35A levels give Λ ≈ 760 and r̄ ≈ 6 m (Table 4, p. 353); a cold Mach 3 lab jet gives Λ ≈ 0.15 and r̄ ≈ 18 m, five times the chamber's reach, and its spectra and Q/S indeed stay unchanged along rays out to 140 D_j (p. 347, 355, 360–361); a heated Mach 1.55 jet reaches Λ ≈ 1.1 (Table 5, p. 353–354). Matching M_j, T_j and St preserves r̄/D_j but not Λ, so ΛD_j, not the jet condition, has to be matched (p. 350–351, 363). Rockets, with lower peak frequencies and larger diameters, should have larger Λ still (the note's inference). Q/S is already non-zero for the input of a generalized-Burgers run, so a single-station Q/S like McInerny & Ölçmen's cannot by itself show cumulative distortion (p. 357–361). In the hot 1/40 MARTEL jet (M 3.1, T_i/T_∞ 6.5), waveform metrics keep changing out to 40D, and the 2.5 m arc measured S(ṗ) = 1.79 and WSF = 0.68 at 40° ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 25–27). An LES coupled to a nonlinear Euler solver follows these trends (1.21 and 0.73), while linear FW-H extrapolation from 11.2D gives Gaussian waveforms (S(ṗ) ≈ 0, WSF 1.00) at the same OASPL: the F-22A split, reproduced by simulation (Table III, p. 25–27). Lab and subscale S(ṗ) (up to 1.7 on the cold jet's 100 D_j arc; Baars, Table 8, p. 363) stay an order of magnitude below the full-scale values above, which come from longer ranges, counted in shock-formation distances, and different sampling.

The atmosphere matters. A foggy Atlas V launch decayed at the weak-shock rate r^−1.1 and a clear one at r^−1, which Mathews & Gee suggest is lower absorption in humid air keeping waveforms shock-like ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 375); the effective diffusivity falls 5–9× from 30% to 70% RH at 21 °C ([McInerny & Ölçmen 2005](../Notes/mcinerny-2005-high-intensity-rocket-noise-nonlinear-propagation.md), Table III, p. 586), which supports that reading (the note's link). QM-1's skewness was about a tenth of QM-2's (Reichman, p. 9). The 1990s Titan IV maxima fell faster than spherical spreading to 13–16 km (see open questions). Crackle is reported but not quantified at SLS, Starship and Falcon 9 launches ([Gee 2023](../Notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 5; [Gee 2024](../Notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 4–5; Kellison, p. 5). In the MARTEL LES, high S(ṗ) and K(ṗ) and WSF above 1 near the shear layer at x ≈ 25D mark sharp pressure jumps at the source, besides the steepening further out ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 25).

## Ignition overpressure, pads and terrain

Ignition overpressure (IOP) is a directional transient set by the pad. The earliest data are on the vehicle: on STS-1, before suppression, the Orbiter base heat shield saw an N-wave of about +12.8/−13.4 kPa lasting about 0.1 s ([Ikawa & Laspesa 1985](../Notes/ikawa-1985-ignition-duct-overpressure-space-shuttle-srm.md), Fig. 3 read, p. 483), in line with the ~10 kPa typical of IOP ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 33); the duct-free horizontal RSRM firing gave about 4.5 kPa peak-to-peak at 67 m ([Kenny 2009](../Notes/kenny-2009-space-shuttle-srm-plume-acoustics.md), p. 11–12). The 6.4% model tests revealed a second wave, a delayed, low-frequency duct overpressure (DOP) leaving the downstream duct exits about 100 ms after the chamber-pressure rise, which re-examined flight data also contained, hidden behind the IOP (Ikawa & Laspesa, p. 483). Water-trough covers and water injection were adopted from STS-2 (p. 481). The model motor's ignition transient did not scale, so IOP and DOP overlapped at subscale but arrive separately at full scale (p. 486–487). The authors' model (see [rocket-noise-models](rocket-noise-models.md)) also predicts that a 15 m/s crosswind offsets the DOP from two opposed duct exits by 10–15 ms, a yaw load no campaign has measured (p. 487).

In the far field, the Artemis I SRB rarefaction reached ≈400 Pa (146 dB) at 1.78 km on the trench side, while post-lift-off radiation was north–south symmetric ([Gee 2023](../Notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 4–5). Distance-scaled Atlas V levels spread by ~30 dB with azimuth, peaking at the trench exit, with a "whooshing" waveform shared with Antares that the thesis puts down to flame-trench resonance or the engine ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 50–51); Falcon 9 shows ~6 dB between trench and opposite side, with energy at 2–3 Hz ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 5). A DOP radiated from the trench exit is a candidate mechanism for this directionality, and Ikawa & Laspesa's multi-cycle internal DOP, whose waveform changes with duct length (Fig. 16, p. 487), for the whooshing signature; that is the library's link, not a claim of these papers. SATURN omits the deflected plume, so its comparisons start 5–8 s after ignition (Kellison, p. 9; Mathews 2025, p. 96–97).

Terrain and ground also shape the data. Terrain shielded one Falcon 9 site by ≥10 dB until line of sight, which a thin-barrier model reproduces ([Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 630–631). Pole microphones show ground-interference nulls that wind can move ([Kenny 2009](../Notes/kenny-2009-space-shuttle-srm-plume-acoustics.md), p. 14; [Gee 2010](../Notes/gee-2010-near-field-vector-intensity-solid-rocket-motor.md), p. 4), and ground-plate microphones need a pressure factor δ ≈ 1.9 ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 373). In subscale Epsilon tests at an intermediate vehicle height (H/D_e = 14), the fairing load came from free-jet Mach waves reflected off the ground in the open flame path, not from deflector impingement; a front cover cut it by 3 dB ([Tsutsumi 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 15–16).

## Launch campaigns against community-noise models and SATURN

SLS Artemis I gave 127–136 dB maximum OASPL at 1.5–5.2 km, nearly 20 dB above the Ares-V-based environmental assessment (<110 dB at 4.5 km), and 105.6 dBA at 5.15 km ([Gee 2023](../Notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 3–7). For Starship Flight 5 the RNOISE-based assessment underpredicted L_Z,max by 2–9 dB from 10 to 35.5 km but overpredicted SEL_A by 10–18 dBA, so it mishandles how spectrum and level change during ascent; infrasound kept L_Z above ambient for 16–17 min, and the landing burn was as loud as lift-off ([Gee 2024](../Notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 5). SATURN, built from three Atlas V stations within 1 km plus an empirical propagation-loss filter, predicted Firefly Alpha and Starship Flight 6 maximum levels within ~1 dB inside 20 km, against 2.2–2.7 dB for SP-8072 ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 72, 91, 95). On Falcon 9 it matched running OASPL within 1 dB around the peak at 1.1 and 2.6 km but put the 1.1 km spectral peak at 12 Hz against 20–30 Hz measured ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 8–10). Beyond 20 km its Starship error grows to ≈12 dB at 35.5 km (SP-8072 ≈18 dB), put down to launch-day atmospheric conditions (Mathews 2025, p. 95).

## Sonic booms

Booster flyback produces the largest-amplitude event: for Starship a triple-shock boom of 7.1 psf at 10.5 km and near or above 10 psf at the nearest stations, above the updated assessment ([Gee 2024](../Notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 5–6); for Falcon 9 ~5 psf at 750 m and above 8 psf at 1–2 km from the landing zone, not decaying monotonically ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 6, 8). Ascent booms over Ventura County from six Falcon 9 launches were Friedlander-type single shocks near the acceleration focus (maximum 1.90 psf, 133 dB peak) and multi-arrival rumbles downrange (69 of 132 recordings below 0.5 psf). Low-level wind moved the carpet off land for one launch and a 155° azimuth for another, and the PCBoom-based assessment placed the focus near Santa Barbara, where no booms were measured ([Gee 2026](../Notes/gee-2026-falcon-9-ascent-sonic-boom.md), p. 4–7).

## Disagreements and open questions

**Falcon 9 peak angle: 64° or 71°?** Mathews & Gee revised the Falcon 9 value from 64° to 71° after a 3-D angle correction and used 71° for Atlas V ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 375, 378), yet Kellison et al. measured 64° again from near-pad stations, while SATURN's Atlas V map peaks at 72° ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 11–12; [Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 82). Kellison et al. note that straight rays, a fixed 18 D_eff source and no moving-source correction could bias their angle by 2–3° (p. 12–13). Ray-traced, moving-source-corrected directivity from the same near-pad array on several vehicles would settle it.

**Where is the source, and are static firings biased?** Intensity puts the peak at 17–32 D_e ([Gee 2016](../Notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 1), small static rockets at 5–15D, near Varnier's model value of ≈11D ([Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md), p. 1855), and the Varnier-type lengths used for vehicles give 9–10 D_e ([Haynes 2009](../Notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md), p. 9; [Tsutsumi 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 9). The small motors peak at several times the full-scale Strouhal number and sources move upstream with frequency ([Gee 2010](../Notes/gee-2010-near-field-vector-intensity-solid-rocket-motor.md), p. 3–6), so part of the gap may be frequency (a reading of the notes, not a published test). Horizontal firings also impinge on hillsides beyond ~40D ([Gee 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 10–11) or sit a few diameters above the ground (Varnier, p. 1854). Near-field intensity on an unobstructed plume, compared band by band with the small-motor data on one Strouhal axis, is the missing measurement.

**Why Sr ≈ 0.01–0.02, and which diameter for clusters?** The order-of-magnitude gap to lab jets is "still unclear" ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 10). Published values mix single-nozzle and equivalent diameters, and peak-angle SPL with sound-power spectra, so Falcon 9's 0.010 ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 15–16), 0.019 ([Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 629) and SLS's 0.015 (Kellison, p. 15) are not directly comparable. The 1990s records show how large the band-versus-narrowband offset is: constant-bandwidth peaks at 0.015–0.02 against one-third-octave peaks of 0.022–0.043 in the same launches ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md), p. 513, 516; the note's arithmetic). Hot subscale jets (0.07–0.1) sit between lab and full-scale values. Values on one basis, plus hot high-Mach lab jets with known D and U, would test whether temperature belongs in the scaling, as Mathews suggests ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 105).

**Is η constant?** McInerny read the 1990s launches as η rising with motor thrust ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md), p. 513–514, 516), while Varnier found η size-independent at 0.7% after tracing an apparent size trend to ground reflection ([Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md), p. 1854). Kellison et al. read the plateau of scaled OASPL against M_O as saturation and SLS as low-efficiency ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 17); Vulcan needs half the Atlas V value ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 375), while clustered Starship and Saturn V seem louder than thrust predicts ([Gee 2024](../Notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 7). Every value depends on the ground treatment, and two Titan IV launches differed by about 4 dB with no instrumental cause found (McInerny, p. 514). Near-pad sound power with one ground treatment across many vehicles is needed.

**Shocks: radiated or formed?** Whether far-field shocks leave the source as shocks or form by steepening is unresolved ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 10), but it is now a question of proportion. Baars et al.'s criterion says cumulative steepening must dominate at full scale and cannot occur in a lab chamber, and leaves open how far pre-steepened source waveforms shorten the formation distance ([Baars et al. 2014](../Notes/baars-2014-cumulative-nonlinear-waveform-distortions-high-speed-jets.md), p. 348, 363). The MARTEL LES shows both, with sharp jumps by the shear layer and continued steepening out to 40D ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 25). McInerny & Ölçmen's distant stations, which saw different emission times, could not decide it; they named the deciding measurement: near-source shock thickness with bandwidth well above 20 kHz and no ground reflection ([McInerny & Ölçmen 2005](../Notes/mcinerny-2005-high-intensity-rocket-noise-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 589–590). Nonlinear propagation is also offered for the near- versus far-field directivity mismatch ([Gee 2016](../Notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 5) and for the high-frequency narrowing of the RSRM indices ([James 2012](../Notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md), p. 6), which Haynes & Kenny put down to terrain ([Haynes 2009](../Notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md), p. 13); the F-22A and F/A-18E disagree on sideline nonlinearity ([Gee 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 11). Simultaneous near- and far-field arrays with meteorology, one sampling rate (Sk{∂p/∂t} depends on it, and Gee et al. 2012 mixed 48 and 96 kHz, as that note points out) and indicators tracked along rays would separate the effects.

**Why no tones?** Lubert et al. suggest masking by mixing noise and pad geometry for the missing impingement tones (p. 16–17); this is untested. Merlin 1D plumes are overexpanded, so shock cells exist ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 4); the F404 shows full-scale BBSAN; and BBSAN is clear in the 1/40 MARTEL jet but absent from the hotter 1/42 Epsilon jet ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 19; [Tsutsumi 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 13). Whether temperature, nozzle state or masking decides it, and whether the guided-jet-mode loop fails or is merely masked in rocket plumes, is open (see [screech](screech.md)).

**What does the far field see of ignition?** Ikawa & Laspesa found no simple way to separate overlapping IOP and DOP in measured data ([Ikawa & Laspesa 1985](../Notes/ikawa-1985-ignition-duct-overpressure-space-shuttle-srm.md), p. 486–487), and the kilometre-range campaigns report trench-directed IOP without identifying the wave ([Gee 2023](../Notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 4; [Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 50–51; [Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 5). Whether it is the direct IOP, a duct or trench-exit DOP, or trench resonance is untested. Arrival direction from a small near-pad array, or time–frequency analysis of single transients (the IOP N-wave is shorter than the DOP), could separate them (the Ikawa note's suggestions).

**What sets long-range levels?** The 1990s Titan IV maxima decayed about as r^−1.2 to 13–16 km, 5–7 dB beyond spherical spreading where low-frequency absorption is negligible, and McInerny suggested nonlinear transfer out of the spectral peak ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md), p. 514–516; the note's arithmetic), whereas near the source nonlinearity changes OASPL by about 1 dB or less ([Gee 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 12). Refraction, grazing-incidence ground impedance and the different emission times seen by each station are the alternatives the note raises. Long-range Starship levels, SEL_A errors and the ascent-boom focus likewise point to weather and terrain the models leave out ([Gee 2024](../Notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 5; [Gee 2026](../Notes/gee-2026-falcon-9-ascent-sonic-boom.md), p. 6–7). McInerny's 120 dB level, above which high frequencies stop decaying like absorption (p. 516), is a candidate threshold for the nonlinear regime, resting on two vehicles. Nonlinear ray tracing with the as-flown trajectory and launch-day atmosphere, checked against a line of stations, is the test.

## Methods and data

Nearly every result here comes from time-resolved pressure sampled at 48–204.8 kHz; the far stations of the first 1990s Titan IV launch kept only 1 s one-third-octave levels ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md), p. 511–513). None of the methods applies to non-time-resolved data such as PIV. Recurring practice:
- **Mounting:** ground-plate microphones with δ ≈ 1.9 ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 373) against pole mounts with wind-shifted interference nulls ([Kenny 2009](../Notes/kenny-2009-space-shuttle-srm-plume-acoustics.md), p. 14). Microphones 37 cm above the ground add a 1.5 ms reflection that double-counts shocks and corrupts Q/S at 100–600 Hz ([McInerny & Ölçmen 2005](../Notes/mcinerny-2005-high-intensity-rocket-noise-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 580–582, 588). Protection grids inflate levels above 10 kHz ([Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md), p. 1854–1855). Pole-shift filtering recovers sub-Hz booms ([Gee 2026](../Notes/gee-2026-falcon-9-ascent-sonic-boom.md), p. 3).
- **Block statistics:** 1 s running OASPL, spectra averaged over the 3 dB-down window (6 dB-down in the 1990s data, so levels from the two should not be mixed; [McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md), p. 513), and 4 s blocks for Q(θ, Sr), with the moving vehicle turning time into angle ([Gee 2023](../Notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md), p. 3; [Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 11–13). This is a crude treatment of non-stationarity, and 1 s band levels misrepresent the ignition transient (McInerny 1996, p. 514–515).
- **Waveform statistics:** Sk{p} and Sk{∂p/∂t}, with Sk{∂p/∂t} ≥ 5 as the threshold for significant shocks ([Reichman 2018](../Notes/reichman-2018-qm2-srm-far-field-acoustics.md), p. 8), although Langenais et al. quote > 1.5 from the same source ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 27); the wave steepening factor; and the rise rate against pressure rise of each rising segment, set against a same-spectrum Gaussian surrogate, where 48 kHz sampling caps rise rates at dp/dt = 25 000 Δp ([McInerny & Ölçmen 2005](../Notes/mcinerny-2005-high-intensity-rocket-noise-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 580, 584–586). Derivative metrics compare only at matched sampling rate and bandwidth. The effective Gol'dberg number says whether steepening can be cumulative, and Q/S is read only as an evolution along a ray ([Baars et al. 2014](../Notes/baars-2014-cumulative-nonlinear-waveform-distortions-high-speed-jets.md), p. 344–347, 364).
- **Arrays:** PAGE intensity probes ([Gee 2016](../Notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 4); beamforming of pads, where SEM fragments distributed sources ([Panda 2013](../Notes/panda-2013-noise-sources-rocket-test-firings-launch-phased-array.md), p. 16), and focused line arrays on small rockets, which locate the source only roughly ([Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md), p. 1855); holography with OLVR partial fields for aircraft ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 13–16).
- **Geometry:** polar arcs must be centred on the apparent source, not the nozzle ([James 2012](../Notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md), p. 5; [Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 14), and propagation is judged along rays from that source: spectra collapse along such a ray but not along one from the nozzle exit, so a wrong path can fake nonlinear effects ([Baars et al. 2014](../Notes/baars-2014-cumulative-nonlinear-waveform-distortions-high-speed-jets.md), p. 354–355).

No paper uses SPOD, wavelets or Hilbert envelopes, so intermittent features (crackle; the L_Z,max scatter the Starship letter attributes to short, high-amplitude events, [Gee 2024](../Notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md), p. 5; overlapping ignition and duct waves) are averaged away or left unseparated.

## Relevance to our work

- **Feedback is absent at full scale.** The lab screech and impinging-jet loops do not appear in rocket data ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 16–17), yet installation still dominates the near field (Panda's duct exit, Tsutsumi's reflected Mach waves). A hot or high-NPR lab jet on an inclined plate, with time-resolved schlieren and microphones, would ask when the loop survives; Mathews calls for exactly such synchronised schlieren and acoustics ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 104–105).
- **Cheap metrics for our microphones.** Add Sk{∂p/∂t}, the rise-rate-against-pressure-rise scatter with a same-spectrum surrogate as the null case, and the one-third-octave slope test (−3 dB/octave holding with distance signals shocks; [Gee 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 6) to the basic-statistics step; place microphones on rays from the apparent source; small PAGE probes could locate where screech energy leaves the jet. A lab chamber is far too short for cumulative steepening ([Baars et al. 2014](../Notes/baars-2014-cumulative-nonlinear-waveform-distortions-high-speed-jets.md), p. 361), so shock-like features in the group's far field are born at or near the source (the note's inference for our smaller, cooler jets).
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Launch records are non-stationary and intermittent. Hilbert envelopes of high-pass microphone signals, or SPOD-filtered reconstructions on arrays, would track shock activity and source changes that block averages hide. Shock metrics must be computed on the raw signals, since a band-limited reconstruction removes the harmonics that make a shock (McInerny & Ölçmen and Baars notes).
- **Ignition transients.** A starting underexpanded jet impinging into a model duct, with time-resolved schlieren and synchronised microphones, would measure the wall-jet front that Ikawa & Laspesa make the generator of IOP and DOP, and test the trench-exit hypothesis above (the Ikawa note's suggestion).
- **Rocket-noise modelling and simulation.** The measured η, source curves, directivity and propagation losses are the inputs for SATURN-type models (see [rocket_noise.csv](../Data/rocket_noise.csv)). LES/CAA comparisons need nonlinear propagation for waveforms but not for OASPL: Tsutsumi's hybrid RANS/LES with linear FW-H was up to 4 dB high ([Tsutsumi 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 12–13), while the tripped MARTEL LES is within about 1 dB, its earlier 4–6 dB error having come from the nozzle-exit state rather than the propagation method ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 6, 16, 28). The PyFR staging LES is not directly affected.

## Papers

- [Ikawa & Laspesa 1985](../Notes/ikawa-1985-ignition-duct-overpressure-space-shuttle-srm.md): earliest IOP data (STS-1 N-wave of ~13 kPa on the Orbiter base); discovery of duct overpressure from the duct exits; IOP and DOP overlap in the 6.4% tests.
- [McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md): Scout, Delta, Peacekeeper and Titan IV launches at 0.3–16 km; first test of the maximum-OASPL formula (~4 dB rms); 70° launch directivity; high frequencies decay slower than absorption.
- [Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md): ONERA small static rockets and Rita II; ground and grid artefacts in η, 0.7% once corrected; peak-octave sources at 5–15D.
- [McInerny & Ölçmen 2005](../Notes/mcinerny-2005-high-intensity-rocket-noise-nonlinear-propagation.md): Titan IV and Scout waveforms follow the weak-shock rise-rate law to 15 km; bandwidth and ground-reflection limits on shock metrics.
- [Gee 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md): F-22A run-ups; first full-scale demonstration of nonlinear propagation with a propagation model (Burgers vs linear).
- [Kenny 2009](../Notes/kenny-2009-space-shuttle-srm-plume-acoustics.md): first RSRM static-firing database; skewness, vectoring, 23 D_n near-field peak, ~50° directivity.
- [Haynes 2009](../Notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md) (not tagged): RSRM 305 m arc as SP-8072 directivity; Saturn V lift-off spectra.
