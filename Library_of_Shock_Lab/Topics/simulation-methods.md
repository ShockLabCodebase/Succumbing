# Simulation methods

_Last updated: 2026-10-24 · 17 papers_

## Scope
Solvers and numerics for compressible flow (PyFR flux reconstruction, OpenFOAM `rhoCentralFoam`, Charles and the other jet LES solvers, cut-cell Euler, a URANS precursor), how the jet LES used across the library were set up, far-field propagation from simulations (FW-H, Kirchhoff, Euler and RANS-based CAA), shock-dynamics computations (Euler, geometrical shock dynamics, the transient-oblique-shock model), machine learning for fluids, and the public simulation databases. The last section sets this against the user's PyFR A1/A2 staging LES ([README](../../Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/README.md)). Linear operators (global stability, resolvent, PSE, OWNS) are in [linear-operator-methods](linear-operator-methods.md), local stability and wave models in [linear-models](linear-models.md); SPOD/BMD/DMD algorithms in [modal-decomposition](modal-decomposition.md); screech physics in [screech](screech.md), LES and models against experiment in [screech frequency and global modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md); what the impinging-jet LES found in [impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md); SP-8072-type prediction in [rocket-noise-models](rocket-noise-models.md); schlieren and BOS practice in [schlieren-and-density-imaging](schlieren-and-density-imaging.md); PIV practice and facilities in [diagnostics-and-facilities](diagnostics-and-facilities.md).

## Current understanding
1. **Wall-modelled unstructured LES with the nozzle inside the domain predicts screech blind.** The Charles LES of the OSU twin rectangular jets matched near-field screech frequency and amplitude up to St ≈ 1 and OASPL to about 1 dB without seeing the data ([Brès et al. 2021](../Notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 9–13). The recipe (nozzle meshed; synthetic turbulence, near-wall refinement and a wall-stress model for a turbulent exit layer; sponges; small co-flow; FW-H with end-caps) gives sub-dB spectra to St ≈ 2 on 16M cells ([Brès & Lele 2019](../Notes/bres-2019-modelling-jet-noise-les-perspective.md), p. 5) and underlies the Charles databases ([Schmidt et al. 2017](../Notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 3; [Towne et al. 2022](../Notes/towne-2022-database-reduced-complexity-modeling.md), p. 4; [Nogueira et al. 2026](../Notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 13). Its 2017 predecessor, laminar at reduced Re, had OASPL within 1.5–2.5 dB but a tilted directivity ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1176). Other groups use other recipes (table below).
2. **Nozzle details are part of the physics, not decoration.** A lip groove makes the exit boundary layer turbulent and supports its own cavity waves ([Brès et al. 2021](../Notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 9, 13); the nozzle-internal boundary layer seeds high-m streaks ([Nogueira et al. 2026](../Notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 15); a laminar exit layer changes near-nozzle KH growth and radiates 3–4 dB more at high frequency ([Brès & Lele 2019](../Notes/bres-2019-modelling-jet-noise-les-perspective.md), p. 5–6). Adding a geometric trip and tripling the lip resolution brought a hot-jet LES from about 4–6 dB high (note's reading) to about 1 dB ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 5–9, 28). Lip thickness and nearby reflectors move staging boundaries (see Relevance).
3. **Reduced-Re LES gets resonance frequencies right, not necessarily amplitudes.** ILES at Re 6 × 10⁴ with a laminar exit layer reproduces measured impinging tones and their staging paths ([Bogey & Gojon 2017](../Notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 565, 576), and a laminar-inflow LES without a nozzle at Re 5 × 10⁴ gets an impinging tone within about 1% ([Karami et al. 2020](../Notes/karami-2020-acoustic-hydrodynamic-waves-underexpanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 5, 18). An untripped ILES of rectangular screech gets St within 0.01 at six conditions, yet its amplitude rises with heating while the measured one falls ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 18–19, 24). A URANS put tone levels near experiment, but with the dominant mode wrong at three of five M_j (note's reading; [Shen & Tam 2002](../Notes/shen-2002-three-dimensional-numerical-simulation-jet-screech.md), p. 36–37).
4. **Shock capturing is local, and its dissipation still shows.** Second-order capturing gets mean shock structure right: `rhoCentralFoam` puts the Ladenburg Mach-disc triple point within about 3% ([Greenshields et al. 2010](../Notes/greenshields-2010-central-schemes-polyhedral-fv.md), p. 18), cut-cell Euler converges from 23 cells per duct width ([Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2022](../Notes/rezay-haghdoost-2022-shock-dividers.md), p. 6–7), and an overexpanded C-D jet LES gives the first-cell-to-Pack ratio (0.82) measured in other overexpanded jets ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1174). Dissipation-sensitive detail is not: the limiter sets separation length ([Greenshields et al. 2010](../Notes/greenshields-2010-central-schemes-polyhedral-fv.md), p. 19–21), inviscid shear layers roll up too strongly ([Thethy et al. 2022](../Notes/thethy-2022-shock-diffraction-non-quiescent-medium.md), p. 19), and in the C-D LES the shock flux left near-field levels at St ≲ 0.4 about 5–6 dB low ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1175).
5. **Front-tracking shock models are cheap and good near the source only.** GSD matches leading-front shapes but under-predicts branch separation and misses the wall shock and post-shock flow ([Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2022](../Notes/rezay-haghdoost-2022-shock-dividers.md), p. 7, 9; [Thethy et al. 2022](../Notes/thethy-2022-shock-diffraction-non-quiescent-medium.md), p. 20–21, 38).
6. **PyFR's flux reconstruction is accurate and GPU-efficient on paper, but nothing in the library validates it for jets, shocks or ILES.** The only PyFR paper is v0.1.0, which had no shock capturing, SGS model or absorbing boundaries ([Witherden et al. 2014](../Notes/witherden-2014-pyfr-flux-reconstruction-framework.md), p. 10, 24–25). The nearest evidence is Euler acoustics from the same family: fourth-order nodal DG at 5 points per wavelength, with about 10⁻³ dB of dissipation per wavelength ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 4–5).
7. **Linear FW-H gets OASPL; waveform shape needs nonlinear propagation.** Open or single-cap FW-H surfaces add up to 10 dB of spurious low-St noise, which averaged end-caps remove ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1179). From 11.2D outwards, linear FW-H and nonlinear DG Euler give the same OASPL for a hot M 3.1 jet (θ ≤ 100°), but only Euler follows the measured derivative skewness and wave steepening ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 25–27).
8. **ML helps most when physics is built in, and interpolates rather than extrapolates** ([Brunton et al. 2020](../Notes/brunton-2020-machine-learning-fluid-mechanics.md), p. 491, 493); a physics-informed BOS reconstruction roughly halves density error in a shocked flow ([Molnar et al. 2023](../Notes/molnar-2023-physics-informed-bos-supersonic.md), p. 13).
9. **One LES family (Brès, Charles) underpins most of the library's SPOD, resolvent and HPOD method papers;** only its M_j 0.9 jet is documented as public ([Towne et al. 2022](../Notes/towne-2022-database-reduced-complexity-modeling.md), p. 3–5).

## Solvers and numerics
PyFR recasts flux reconstruction (which unifies nodal DG and spectral difference) as dense matrix multiplies plus generated point-wise kernels, so explicit FR runs near the hardware's bandwidth limit on GPUs ([Witherden et al. 2014](../Notes/witherden-2014-pyfr-flux-reconstruction-framework.md), p. 3–5, 9–15). It reaches design order ℘ + 1 on mixed and unstructured meshes (Couette, Tables 2, 4, p. 22–24) and scales weakly from 1 to 104 GPUs (p. 27–28). Its viscous validation cases all use Rusanov fluxes with LDG β = ½, τ = 0.1 (p. 21, 24). Its only unsteady 3D case, a Re 3900 cylinder at ℘ = 4 with no SGS model, has no quantitative check (p. 24–25).

`rhoCentralFoam` takes the opposite route: Kurganov–Tadmor and Kurganov–Noelle–Petrova central fluxes need no Riemann solver, so they suit colocated polyhedral finite volumes ([Greenshields et al. 2010](../Notes/greenshields-2010-central-schemes-polyhedral-fv.md), p. 4, 6–7). The limiter matters more than the flux (KNP + van Leer is recommended, p. 13, 21; double-cone separation 47.5 mm against a converged 56.1 mm, p. 19–21). Its validation is laminar and mostly steady (p. 6), so it says nothing about resonance or acoustics.

**Shock capturing switched on by sensors.** Every jet solver here keeps a low-dissipation scheme in smooth flow and captures shocks only where a sensor fires. Charles blends central and HLLC fluxes per face by a mesh-set weight and switches to ENO + HLLC at shocks flagged by a Hill–Pullin switch ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1165–1167). LMFA adds a sensor-scaled second-order shock filter to 11-point low-dispersion differences ([Bogey & Gojon 2017](../Notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 566). An EDGE-type FV solver weights Jameson–Schmidt–Turkel dissipation by a Ducros sensor ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 5–6). LTRAC's ECNSS switches sixth-order central differences to fifth-order WENO ([Karami et al. 2020](../Notes/karami-2020-acoustic-hydrodynamic-waves-underexpanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 6). JAXA computes WCNS nonlinear weights only where a density sensor fires, cutting a roughly tenfold cost ([Nonomura et al. 2011](../Notes/nonomura-2011-supersonic-jet-impinging-inclined-plate-acoustic-waves.md), p. 406–407), or switches compact differencing to SLAU + MUSCL ([Tsutsumi et al. 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 11–12). PyFR has its entropy filter. The sensor matters: Charles' fired on under 1% of faces, and the authors blame its shock flux for damping near-nozzle wavepackets, a deficit that disappeared once later runs flagged only compressions ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1170, 1175). The library compares none of these schemes on one jet.

**SGS model or implicit LES.** Charles (Vreman), ECNSS (dynamic Germano–Lilly) and ONERA's CEDRE (Smagorinsky) add an explicit SGS model ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1166; [Karami et al. 2020](../Notes/karami-2020-acoustic-hydrodynamic-waves-underexpanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 6; [Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 4). LMFA's relaxation filtering ([Gojon et al. 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md), p. 2920), the EDGE-type artificial dissipation ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 6), JAXA's WCNS MILES ([Nonomura et al. 2011](../Notes/nonomura-2011-supersonic-jet-impinging-inclined-plate-acoustic-waves.md), p. 407) and the PyFR run rely on the scheme instead. Minimum-dissipation schemes need an SGS model to stop energy piling up at the grid scale, while schemes with built-in dissipation tend to over-dissipate when one is added; no cross-solver comparison exists ([Brès & Lele 2019](../Notes/bres-2019-modelling-jet-noise-les-perspective.md), p. 14–15). The pre-LES alternative is URANS: an axisymmetric k–ε with five azimuthal Fourier modes on a DRP scheme, with selective damping capturing the shocks ([Shen & Tam 2002](../Notes/shen-2002-three-dimensional-numerical-simulation-jet-screech.md), p. 33–35).

**What recurs and what varies.** Most of these mesh the nozzle interior. [Karami et al. 2020](../Notes/karami-2020-acoustic-hydrodynamic-waves-underexpanded-impinging-jets.md) start from a tanh profile in a hole in a flat wall (p. 5), [Nonomura et al. 2011](../Notes/nonomura-2011-supersonic-jet-impinging-inclined-plate-acoustic-waves.md) prescribe an inflow profile without describing the nozzle wall (p. 404), and a DNS drops the nozzle, citing Brès et al. (2018) on why it matters ([Suzuki et al. 2026](../Notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 3). The exit layer is set in four ways: synthetic turbulence and a wall model at lab Re (Charles since 2018; [Brès & Lele 2019](../Notes/bres-2019-modelling-jet-noise-les-perspective.md), p. 5); a laminar layer at reduced Re, its thickness matched to a RANS estimate ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1169) or seeded with weak forcing ([Bogey & Gojon 2017](../Notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 565); an untripped nozzle disturbed by its own internal shocks ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 7–11); or a geometric step, whose turbulence relaminarises through the throat to about 2% at the exit ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 7–9). In a resonant jet the exit state is partly an output: with fixed forcing, exit turbulence rose from 2.6% to 5.2% as the plate moved closer (Bogey & Gojon, p. 565). Sponges are universal; a weak co-flow (M 0.009–0.1) against spurious recirculation is Charles practice ([Brès et al. 2021](../Notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 7).

Acoustic resolution is set by points per wavelength, St_lim = D/(8 Δl M_a) ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1168) or St_c = c∞D_j/(PPW·K·Δ·u_j) with K = 1.2 ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 4). Gojon et al. 2019 sized theirs on the cold jet (11 cells per wavelength at St 3, p. 7–8), which resolves only St ≈ 1.8–2.2 in the hot jets (note's arithmetic). Grid checks are rare. The most systematic is Gojon et al.'s 40M/80M/160M study: refinement brought the shock-cell length closer to PIV and raised exit turbulence from 1.05% to 1.75% (p. 8–10). Doubling the plume resolution of the M 0.9 Charles jet took the St limit from 2 to 4, at 69M against 16M cells and 10× the CPU ([Brès & Lele 2019](../Notes/bres-2019-modelling-jet-noise-les-perspective.md), p. 15; cf. [Towne et al. 2017](../Notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md), p. 4–5). The rest are single checks (Brès et al. 2017, p. 1180; Brès et al. 2021, p. 13–14) or none (Nonomura et al., Karami et al.).

**Records are short:** tens to a few hundred tone cycles (40–95 in Bogey & Gojon 2017, about 140 in [Gojon et al. 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md), 400 in [Jeun et al. 2024](../Notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md); table). That is enough to see intermittent coupling (Jeun et al., p. 9) and converged screech SPOD ([Nogueira et al. 2026](../Notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 25), but not slow dynamics: the streaks there rotate only π/2 over the record, so a record at least 4× longer would be needed to average them out (p. 14). Spectra are often one transform: a single Hanning window with bin averaging, giving 11–26 periods at St 0.05 ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1170, 1181), or one FFT with 5-point smoothing ([Nonomura et al. 2011](../Notes/nonomura-2011-supersonic-jet-impinging-inclined-plate-acoustic-waves.md), p. 408). Shen & Tam's analysis block, about 100 D/a∞ by the note's inference, is about one real A1/A2 switch long. [Brès et al. 2021](../Notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md) judge a short LES against a long experiment by the block-to-block spread of short-time experimental spectra (±0.5–1.5 dB, p. 11), a transferable test.

## Far-field sound from simulations
**Permeable FW-H** is the default. [Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md) set the practice later Charles papers cite: a pressure-based surface flared with the jet in isotropic cells, where its position hardly matters; 16 averaged end-caps, or an open surface run through the sponge, which matches them; one window over the record, at least 10 periods of the lowest frequency, and sampling at least twice the grid cut-off (p. 1168–1170, 1177–1181). A single end-cap with a quadrupole correction is the cheaper alternative ([Brès & Lele 2019](../Notes/bres-2019-modelling-jet-noise-les-perspective.md), p. 7–8). Others differ: exact microphone positions ([Brès et al. 2021](../Notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 7, 9), a 100D sphere ([Nogueira et al. 2026](../Notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 13), an open ellipsoid with no end-caps ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 19), or a Kirchhoff surface on top of the computational slab, which leaves out plate reflection beyond it ([Nonomura et al. 2011](../Notes/nonomura-2011-supersonic-jet-impinging-inclined-plate-acoustic-waves.md), p. 409).

**Nonlinear Euler CAA.** [Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md) couple the LES two-way, every step, to nodal DG Euler, p-adapted from second order at the interface to fourth outside (p. 4–5). It matches linear FW-H in OASPL but alone follows the measured waveform statistics (claim 7), and it still misses the measured shocks, which lie above its St 0.22 cut-off (p. 27).

**CAA on a RANS mean flow** is the cheaper route through installations. Casalino's GFD solver of Pierce's equation (3–4 points per wavelength) captured pad-cavity confinement but still over-predicted by 2–3 dB, and a shock-containing mean flow made it too "stiff", so an equivalent fully expanded plume was used ([Casalino et al. 2009](../Notes/casalino-2009-hybrid-empirical-caa-rocket-noise.md), p. 6, 12, 15). [Tsutsumi et al. 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md) blame insufficient resolution and linear FW-H for their 4 dB excess (p. 13); Langenais et al.'s comparison points to resolution and inflow, not linear propagation, for most of an OASPL error (note's reading).

## Shock-dynamics computations: cut-cell Euler, GSD and TOS
The group's TU Berlin collaboration uses one solver family across three papers: second-order MUSCL with a van Leer limiter on Cartesian cut cells, with AMR and an exact Riemann solver in 3D ([Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2020](../Notes/rezay-haghdoost-2020-transient-supersonic-trailing-jet-shocklet.md), p. 6–7), and dimensionally split in 2D and axisymmetric ([Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2022](../Notes/rezay-haghdoost-2022-shock-dividers.md), p. 5–6; [Thethy et al. 2022](../Notes/thethy-2022-shock-diffraction-non-quiescent-medium.md), p. 6–8). The work moved from simulation as a matched companion to schlieren, to simulation as a controlled experiment, to reduced models:
- **2020:** inflow calibrated by a 1D reactive model against a measured total-pressure trace; numerical schlieren matches the experiment qualitatively; a constant-inflow re-run shows the second triple point is intrinsic, not a supply transient (p. 7–12). The transient-oblique-shock model (Rankine–Hugoniot in a frame moving with each shock element) then explains the shocklet from reflected-shock rotation (p. 12–19).
- **2022 (dividers):** a grid study (4–6 cells per shock at any N_D; converged from N_D = 23) and GSD alongside. Shock separation at the exit is 0.55D measured, 0.53 CFD, 0.39 GSD; both models run fast enough (about 5–10 min) for design iteration (p. 6–9, 15).
- **2022 (diffraction):** Euler with geometry and background flow varied separately rules out nozzle focusing as the cause of the front distortion (p. 14–19). A GSD with an added background velocity reproduces the measured fronts from M₀ and M_b alone, and closed-form results follow (generalised Skews angle, eq. 5.8, p. 23; weak-shock angle, eq. 5.13, p. 26).

The shared limitation is inviscid shear layers: roll-up comes from numerical dissipation ([Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2020](../Notes/rezay-haghdoost-2020-transient-supersonic-trailing-jet-shocklet.md), p. 6–7) and KH vortices are "significantly overestimated" ([Thethy et al. 2022](../Notes/thethy-2022-shock-diffraction-non-quiescent-medium.md), p. 19). Grid spacing is not reported in the 2020 paper.

## Machine learning for fluids
[Brunton et al. 2020](../Notes/brunton-2020-machine-learning-fluid-mechanics.md) is the map. POD is a linear autoencoder, but autoencoder modes are neither orthogonal nor ordered (p. 485, 490), which matters for POD-pair phase recovery. Cluster-based ROMs give a Markov view of switching between states (p. 491). Superresolution and CNN PIV exist but are bounded by their training data (p. 491–493), and the authors call for curated benchmark data (p. 499–500). [Molnar et al. 2023](../Notes/molnar-2023-physics-informed-bos-supersonic.md) is the one worked case: a PINN fitted to raw BOS image differences with a steady Euler + irrotationality loss gives density at 3.75% NRMSE (against 6.12% for Tikhonov unified BOS and 8.34% for Abel inversion), plus velocity and pressure (≤ 3.09%) (p. 13–14). Its practical lessons match the review's cautions: non-dimensional residuals, fixed loss weights, excluding the corner singularity, and 2–3 h of GPU training against 10 min for the CFD it replaces (p. 10, 14–16). The Euler loss excludes shear layers, so it fits shock cores, not jets as a whole (note's Relevance). [Taira et al. 2020](../Notes/taira-2020-modal-analysis-applications-outlook.md) (not tagged) covers ML and SINDy in its outlook.

## Public databases
[Towne et al. 2022](../Notes/towne-2022-database-reduced-complexity-modeling.md) release about 8 TB in six time-resolved sets with MATLAB readers (p. 3). The jet is the Brès M_j 0.9 LES: 10,000 snapshots every 0.2 c∞/D on a cylindrical grid to 30D, plus azimuthal modes and the mean flow (p. 4–6). There is also a wall-resolved NACA 0012 LES (35M cells, p. 35). The same jet, or its M 0.4 and M 1.5 siblings, is the test data in [Schmidt et al. 2017](../Notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), [Towne et al. 2017](../Notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md), [Schmidt et al. 2018](../Notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), [Pickering et al. 2020](../Notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md), [Nekkanti & Schmidt 2021](../Notes/nekkanti-2021-spod-reconstruction-denoising-frequency-time.md), [Yeung & Schmidt 2024](../Notes/yeung-2024-adaptive-spod-broadband-tonal.md) and [Raiola & Kriegseis 2025](../Notes/raiola-2025-hilbert-pod.md). It is subsonic with broadband wavepackets plus trapped-wave tones, so it is a clean test for the SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD chain, but it has no shocks or screech. The screeching heated M_j 1.35 and twin-rectangular databases are not described as public in the notes.

## Disagreements and open questions
**Is ILES at reduced Re enough for staging?** The PyFR run has adopted Re_D = 10⁵ with a laminar exit layer and no SGS model ([M3 setup](../../Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/M3_SETUP.md), §5, §9). For frequencies the support is now direct: such LES reproduce impinging tones and staging paths at Re 5–6 × 10⁴ (claim 3), trapped modes in a mean-flow operator are Re-independent above about 5 × 10⁴ ([Schmidt et al. 2017](../Notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 25–27), and the Shock Lab nozzle's exit layer is laminar and thin ([Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2021](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 8). The case against concerns amplitude and thresholds. A frequency-validated ILES got the amplitude trend backwards ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 18–19), reduced Re carries documented broadband biases ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1176), and the A1 cut-off near M_j 1.12 moves with shear-layer thickness ([Nogueira et al. 2022](../Notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 12). Staging limits depend on relative loop gain, which is nearer amplitude than frequency, so frequency validation does not carry over. Higher Re also changes intermittency and high-order statistics, which matters for dwell times ([Brès & Lele 2019](../Notes/bres-2019-modelling-jet-noise-les-perspective.md), p. 2–3). Built-in dissipation argues against adding an SGS model (p. 15), which favours ILES for PyFR's upwind-flux FR in principle (note's inference; no FR evidence). In 2019 the review found no LES that reproduced screech staging (p. 11). Repeating one bracket at two Re and two exit-layer thicknesses would settle it.

**Does shock-capturing dissipation bias resonance?** The nearest evidence is broadband. Brès et al. attribute their overexpanded jet's 5–6 dB deficit at St ≲ 0.4 to shock-flux dissipation; a compression-only sensor removed it ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1175), and the wall-modelled rerun of that condition screeches at St 0.265 ([Nogueira et al. 2026](../Notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 16). But the exit layer and Re changed too, and no paper tests a scheme on a tone. In the PyFR pilot, large time steps let the entropy filter (the likely cause) keep the shear layer laminar to x ≈ 3 and nearly remove the shock cells ([M3 setup](../../Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/M3_SETUP.md), §8). At NPR 2.10 the shock-induced transverse velocity is below 3% of U_e ([Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2021](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 9), so at the production step the filter may barely act. Logging where it acts and checking that the tone is unchanged between ℘ = 3 and 4 would settle it.

**Bistable edges or one noisy state?** The driver assumes two saddle-node edges with hysteresis. The evidence is consistent but indirect: in the lab the tones never coexist ([Mancinelli et al. 2019](../Notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md), p. 7; [Mazharmanesh et al. 2026](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 11–12), and both stages are absolutely unstable at M_j 1.12 ([Nogueira et al. 2022](../Notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 13). A deterministic URANS instead settled into two-tone states that its authors read as coexistence ([Shen & Tam 2002](../Notes/shen-2002-three-dimensional-numerical-simulation-jet-screech.md), p. 35–39), but its cold starts at ΔM_j 0.1, none at the staging point, could not test hysteresis. Tam's rule that the largest total growth wins ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 39) is single-valued instead. No note reports up-and-down NPR ramps. Slow ramps in the 16 mm rig with TR schlieren would settle it, and so would LES branches that fail to overlap.

**Lab switching may be noise-driven.** At NPR 2.19 a slow drift of tone strength was put down to supply-pressure fluctuation ([Mazharmanesh et al. 2026](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 26–28). An LES with a steady inlet has none of this, so measured dwell times need not follow from the LES edges. A deterministic URANS cannot switch by noise at all, so switching in the PyFR ILES (which has its own turbulence) but not in deterministic models would itself be informative (Shen & Tam note).

**Euler versus viscous for transient shock flows.** [Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2022](../Notes/rezay-haghdoost-2022-shock-dividers.md) argue viscosity is marginal for front propagation (p. 7, 11); [Thethy et al. 2022](../Notes/thethy-2022-shock-diffraction-non-quiescent-medium.md) show it is not marginal for shock–vortex interaction (p. 19). A viscous run of one case would bound the error.

## Methods and data
All simulation output here is time-resolved, so it suits SPOD, BMD, k–ω maps ([Towne et al. 2022](../Notes/towne-2022-database-reduced-complexity-modeling.md), p. 7) and time-direction HPOD. Decimating snapshots gives a PIV-like ensemble on which to test POD-pair and space-only HPOD routes on a known flow. The in-jet guided waves were found in LES by k–ω spectra along axial lines inside the jet, split by azimuthal order, plus single-frequency DFT maps at each tone ([Bogey & Gojon 2017](../Notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 573–585; [Gojon et al. 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md), p. 2921–2923). Their 10r₀ lines give ΔkD ≈ 1.3–1.4, too coarse to tell a guided wave from sound near a branch point; use lines of at least 10D_j with a taper (Gojon et al. 2018 note). For comparison with schlieren, path-integrate ∂ρ/∂x into a numerical schlieren image ([Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2020](../Notes/rezay-haghdoost-2020-transient-supersonic-trailing-jet-shocklet.md), p. 8), or for BOS, ray-trace synthetic images through the flow ([Molnar et al. 2023](../Notes/molnar-2023-physics-informed-bos-supersonic.md), p. 11–12). For 3D fields, a D2 split before SPOD cuts memory 4× ([Yeung et al. 2022](../Notes/yeung-2022-3d-spod-twin-rectangular-jet.md)), and streaming SPOD avoids storing snapshots ([Schmidt & Towne 2019](../Notes/schmidt-2019-streaming-spod.md)). Single transient shock events don't suit block-averaged methods; front tracking and x–t diagrams are what these papers use. RANS mean flows are a cheaper base for LSA but give narrower shear layers and drift downstream ([Ivelja et al. 2024](../Notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 18, 23).

## Relevance to our work
**Practices the PyFR staging LES should follow** (setup in the [README](../../Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/README.md) and [base.example.ini](../../Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/base.example.ini)):
- **Match the rig's nozzle exterior and lip.** Lip thickness shifts staging boundaries and A2 amplitude through reflection off the exit face ([Ponton & Seiner 1992](../Notes/ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md), p. 538, 548), and flanges and plates change staging ([Knast et al. 2018](../Notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md), p. 3–4; [Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2022](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 16). The mesh keeps the outer body to 4.7D upstream, which is right. But the CAD has a 1 mm flat lip (about 0.06D), while the 16 mm Shock Lab nozzle in [Mazharmanesh et al. 2026](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) is listed with mean lip thickness l/D_e = 0.31 (p. 5). Confirm that the two are the same nozzle and what "mean lip thickness" measures.
- **Set, report and monitor the exit boundary layer.** It, more than Re, moves the A1 cut-off ([Nogueira et al. 2022](../Notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 12). Feedback changes it ([Bogey & Gojon 2017](../Notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 565), so log it at each anchor; resolve the sharp lip edge, which receptivity needs ([Karami et al. 2020](../Notes/karami-2020-acoustic-hydrodynamic-waves-underexpanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 6); and test the staging limits' sensitivity to it (claim 3).
- **Boundaries:** keep the absorbing-layer test (A1 tone unchanged when `xsp`/`rsp` move), and consider a weak co-flow (M ≈ 0.01) as Charles uses ([Brès et al. 2021](../Notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md), p. 7). Make sponges at least one wavelength of the lowest frequency of interest, and discard two to three acoustic transits after any restart ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1168–1169), which bears on each NPR visit's `transient`.
- **Acoustics:** the lip ring suffices for the driver; a ring at r = 0.642D would match Ponton & Seiner's lip-face transducers (Shen & Tam note). Comparison with far-field or exit-plane array data ([Mancinelli et al. 2019](../Notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md)) needs FW-H (see Far-field) or probes at the experimental positions.
- **Guided-wave diagnostic:** n = 0 k–ω spectra at r = 0.1r₀, 0.5r₀ and r₀, with the (0, 2) branch point overlaid, would show whether the guided wave's band moves across staging (Gojon et al. 2018 note).
- **A "coexisting" outcome for the classifier.** r(t) = ln(A_in/A_out) assumes one tone dies, so Shen & Tam's dominant tone with a steady partner 13 dB down would register as "not switched". A third outcome (both envelopes steady, partner 9–13 dB down, A2 − A1 band phase-locked to a₁a₂*) would cover it. *(Shen & Tam note's suggestion.)*
- **A control visit:** re-run the anchor with no NPR step to measure the step transient in r(t), as [Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2020](../Notes/rezay-haghdoost-2020-transient-supersonic-trailing-jet-shocklet.md) separated supply transients from intrinsic dynamics (p. 12). *(My suggestion.)*
- **Treat each converged state's mean separately.** A1 and A2 each carry their own short-time mean, and the long-time mean is a state the jet never occupies ([Mazharmanesh et al. 2026](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 11–13). Compute LSA base flows and shock spectra per anchor.
- **Sampling and length:** published screech LES sample at fD/c∞ ≈ 10–13 and run 60–400 cycles. For a cold M_j 1.12 jet U_j/c∞ ≈ 1.0 (my estimate), so fD/c∞ = 10 gives about 15 samples per A1 cycle.

**Validation data in the library:**
- *Same diameter, Shock Lab:* 16 mm nozzle, 150 kHz schlieren, 400,000 frames per case. A1 alone at NPR 2.19 (St 0.607), A2 alone at 2.25 (St 0.665), mutually exclusive switching at 2.20 (St 0.606 / 0.694, ρ = −0.82), with per-state mean shock cells ([Mazharmanesh et al. 2026](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 4–12).
- *Anchor NPRs 2.10 and 2.25:* PIV of the 15 mm, 5 mm-lip nozzle. Gives guided-mode structure and wave decomposition, St 0.67 and 0.65, and global LSA on the mean at St 0.667 ([Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md); [Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2021](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 8–12).
- *Staging point across facilities:* Pprime switching between M_j 1.115 and 1.13 (NPR ≈ 2.18–2.22, my isentropic conversion), dwell times 1–4 s, St_d ≈ 0.069 modulation ([Mancinelli et al. 2019](../Notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md), p. 6–7). Tsinghua switches at NPR 2.25 with a k_kh − k_s2 precursor ([Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2022](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 11–13). This spread of about 0.05 in NPR is ten times the driver's bracket width, so the LES bracket is only meaningful against the matched nozzle.
- *Models to compare against:* linear growth rates of A1 vs A2 (ω0i 0.223 vs 0.057 at M_j 1.12; [Nogueira et al. 2022](../Notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 13) and the ω_i ≥ 0 regions of [Mancinelli et al. 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md). Global modes at St 0.66/0.72/0.77 on an M_j 1.12 PIV mean ([Farghadan et al. 2026](../Notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 11).
- *Mean shock cells:* NASA static-pressure surveys for a Mach 1.0 nozzle at M_j 1.02–1.28 ([Norum & Seiner 1982](../Notes/norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md), App. A). The Ladenburg triple point covers higher NPR ([Greenshields et al. 2010](../Notes/greenshields-2010-central-schemes-polyhedral-fv.md), p. 18).

**Gaps:** no paper on PyFR's entropy filter, FR or DG ILES of jets, prisms or single-precision accuracy (the pilot found single precision enough; [M3 setup](../../Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/M3_SETUP.md), §8). The later PyFR papers that [Witherden et al. 2014](../Notes/witherden-2014-pyfr-flux-reconstruction-framework.md) lists as follow-ups would fill them.

**Elsewhere:** cut-cell Euler + GSD is the group's toolkit for transient shock flows. The background-flow GSD may bear on ignition overpressure leaving into moving gas (an inference in the [Thethy et al. 2022](../Notes/thethy-2022-shock-diffraction-non-quiescent-medium.md) note). For rocket noise, LES + FW-H and RANS-based CAA of lift-off still over-predict by 2–4 dB, while a tripped hot free-jet LES with DG Euler propagation reaches about 1 dB below St 0.22 ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 28); see [rocket-noise-models](rocket-noise-models.md).
