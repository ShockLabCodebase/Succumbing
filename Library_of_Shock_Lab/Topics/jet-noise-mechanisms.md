# Jet noise mechanisms

_Last updated: 2026-10-24 · 32 papers_

## Scope
How turbulent and shock-containing jets make broadband noise: mixing noise from large-scale structures (Mach wave radiation) and fine-scale turbulence, the similarity spectra that describe it, the instability-wave families behind Mach waves, broadband shock-associated noise (BBSAN) from Tam & Tanna's interaction model to wavepacket and statistical (RANS-informed) source models, how screech changes BBSAN, and the nonlinear steepening of high-amplitude jet noise into acoustic shocks (crackle). The last section says how these mechanisms carry over to rockets.

Left to other pages: the screech loop, shock leakage and the guided jet mode ([screech](screech.md)) and screech staging ([screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md)); PSE and resolvent operators in their own right ([linear-operator-methods](linear-operator-methods.md)); Floquet and wavepacket models ([linear-models](linear-models.md)); shock-cell models and the shock side of BBSAN sources ([shock-cell-models](shock-cell-models.md)); SP-8072, SATURN and other empirical rocket models ([rocket-noise-models](rocket-noise-models.md)); full-scale launch data and long-range propagation ([rocket-noise-measurements](rocket-noise-measurements.md)); impinging-jet tones ([impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md)); rectangular and elliptical jets ([non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md)); twin jets ([twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md)); SPOD practice ([modal-decomposition](modal-decomposition.md)); LES numerics ([simulation-methods](simulation-methods.md)).

**Angle conventions.** θ is measured from the downstream jet axis. Angles from papers that measure from the inlet axis (Tam & Chen 1994, Tam et al. 1996 and 2008, Norum & Seiner 1982, Brès et al. 2017, Gojon et al. 2019) are converted, θ = 180° − χ, or marked "inlet". Langenais et al. 2019 centre θ′ on the end of the potential core, and Nonomura et al. 2011 measure from the plate. The sign in the BBSAN peak-frequency formula flips with the convention ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) note, eqs. 10–11).

**Convective Mach number.** Papamoschou & Roshko's M_c is the flow Mach number in the frame of the large structures, U_j/(a_j + a_∞) for a jet into still air. Most BBSAN and wavepacket papers, and Michalke's c_ph/a_∞ ([Michalke 1984](../Notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), p. 174), use instead the eddy Mach number U_c/a_∞ with a measured or assumed U_c. The two coincide only for U_c = u_j/(1 + a_j/a_∞) ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 472). Wong et al. 2020 plot a third form, 0.7M_j (that note's pixel check).

## Current understanding
1. **Three components, four in hot jets.** An imperfectly expanded jet radiates mixing noise (dominant downstream), BBSAN (sideline and upstream) and screech tones ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 18); a heated rectangular LES adds Mach waves at TR ≥ 2 ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 19–22). A tab removes the tones and leaves the BBSAN hump ([Norum & Seiner 1982](../Notes/norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md), App. E p. 149, 162), but it also shortens the shock spacing; a notched lip that keeps the first cells is the cleaner screech-free reference ([André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 665, 668).
2. **Mixing noise has a coherent aft part and an incoherent sideline part**: Mach waves from large-scale structures that behave statistically as instability waves (wavepackets), and flatter sideline noise that Tam attributes to fine-scale turbulence. The F and G similarity spectra fit them ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 19–20; [Tam et al. 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), p. 3–5), and far-field and flow–acoustic correlations confirm the coherence contrast ([Tam et al. 2008](../Notes/tam-2008-sources-of-jet-noise-experimental-evidence.md), p. 274–280). Whether the sideline part is a separate source is disputed (below).
3. **The radiating part of the turbulence is low-energy, low-order and linear.** Below St 1 the sound is almost all in m = 0–2 ([Jordan & Colonius 2013](../Notes/jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise.md), p. 176), which carry under 10% of the bulk two-point coherence ([Jaunet et al. 2017](../Notes/jaunet-2017-two-point-coherence-wave-packets-turbulent-jets.md), p. 9–10). Linear models on the mean flow reproduce the m = 0 and 1 wavepackets to the end of the potential core ([Cavalieri et al. 2013](../Notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), p. 566–585), and resolvent modes reproduce the Mach waves seen in SPOD of an M_j 1.5 LES ([Schmidt et al. 2018](../Notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 21–23).
4. **Mach wave radiation is acoustic matching**: only supersonic-phase-speed wavenumbers radiate, as Tam & Burton first derived for a computed non-parallel wave ([Tam & Burton 1984](../Notes/tam-1984-sound-instability-waves-supersonic-axisymmetric-jets.md), eq. 5.8, p. 283). In line-source form the peak is at θ = cos⁻¹(1/M_c), and jitter matters far less for supersonic than for subsonic jets ([Cavalieri et al. 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 11–16). The most amplified supersonic instability wave travels at u_j/(1 + a_j/a_∞), Papamoschou & Roshko's convective speed ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 472), and M_c also governs shear-layer growth ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../Notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md), p. 19–20). In LES, Mach waves appear once U_c/a_∞ exceeds 1 ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 16, 20–21).
5. **BBSAN is a difference-wavenumber Mach wave** from instability waves interacting with the shock cells, with one spectral peak per shock-cell mode ([Tam & Tanna 1982](../Notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md), p. 8–11; [Tam et al. 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), p. 3–6). Linearised-Euler and kinematic wavepacket models confirm that only k − k_s radiates ([Ray et al. 2005](../Notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md), p. 4–5, 9; [Wong et al. 2019](../Notes/wong-2019-coherence-decay-wavepacket-bbsan.md), p. 983). It is multimodal in azimuth, with m = ±1 leading and 2–5 modes needed ([Wong et al. 2020](../Notes/wong-2020-azimuthal-decomposition-radiated-noise-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 2019–2021), and an inside-out wavepacket model predicts the peaks within ±2 dB/St ([Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), p. 20–23). The peak-frequency relation holds at full scale and in overexpanded LES with measured, not analytic, shock spacing ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 36, 40; [Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1176; [Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 19). Screech lies at its upstream limit in strongly underexpanded jets ([Tam et al. 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), p. 7–8), but apparently not at M_j 1.10–1.35 (the [André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md) note, from p. 669–670).
6. **Screech weakens BBSAN.** A screech-free twin keeps a longer mean shock train, and its BBSAN is up to 4 dB louder and higher in frequency; in the screeching jet the peak follows 2St_s through each stage jump ([André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 667–671). This is the rapid shock decay that shock-cell solvers missed ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 32).
7. **Two routes to BBSAN sources disagree on where they are.** Wavepacket × shock-cell models ([Ray et al. 2005](../Notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md); [Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md)) and statistical acoustic analogies calibrated with screech-filtered PIV ([Tan et al. 2019a](../Notes/tan-2019-correlation-analysis-piv-screeching-jets.md); [Tan et al. 2019b](../Notes/tan-2019-equivalent-bbsan-source-reconstruction-screeching-jets.md)) both run on mean flows, but on the same jet they put the source inside the plume and on the sonic line respectively.
8. **High-amplitude jet noise propagates nonlinearly**, steepening into acoustic shocks that keep high-frequency energy linear absorption would remove, while OASPL barely changes ([Gee et al. 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 9–12; [Gee et al. 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 8). LES of a hot jet also puts steep events at the source ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 25).
9. **At rocket scale** Mach waves dominate, BBSAN and screech are absent, and the lab calibrations (similarity spectra, κ, peak St) do not transfer ([Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 9–15).

## Mixing noise: two sources and similarity spectra
**Stochastic instability-wave model (1994).** [Tam & Chen 1994](../Notes/tam-1994-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md) make Tam & Burton's single-wave solution (next section) broadband. Instability waves of every (n, ω) are excited by white noise at the lip, so they are uncorrelated and one constant sets the level, and each direction picks out each wavepacket's axial wavenumber spectrum at (ω/a_∞) cos θ (eqs. 9, 19, p. 1776–1777). Mach 2 directivities at T_0 500 K and 1114 K agree within about 2.5 dB, except St 0.067 at 500 K (about 9 dB low, the note's reading) and St 0.1 at small angles at 1114 K (p. 1778–1779). The constant differs by a factor of 2.1 between the jets, so it is calibrated per jet (the note's arithmetic); it implies a large-structure u_rms of only 1.2–1.5% of u_j (p. 1779–1780). Only n = ±1 radiates at 500 K, St 0.12, but n = 0 to ±5 at 1114 K, St 0.4, which broadens the directivity (Figs 7–8, p. 1779).

**Similarity spectra (1996).** F (sharp, +18.4/−27.7 dB per decade) and G (broad) fit 1,900 Langley spectra by shape ([Tam et al. 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), p. 5). Large-structure noise dominates for χ > 125° and fine-scale noise for χ < 110° at Mach 2 (p. 3; Tam & Chen print 130°, p. 1774). Peak levels collapse on V_j/a_∞ and T_r/T_∞, not M_j, with velocity exponents of about 9.5 (large scale) and 7.6 (fine scale) cold; heating to T_r/T_∞ = 2 at fixed V_j lowers the levels by 8.6 and 6.7 dB (p. 6). Shock cells barely change F and G once BBSAN is set aside (p. 7–8), the assumption Tam & Tanna needed to subtract mixing noise ([Tam & Tanna 1982](../Notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md), p. 17), and the spectra also fit an M_j 0.98 jet and pilot AR 3 and AR 7.6 nozzles (p. 9–11). F and G cover almost the same frequencies, so "large scales make low frequencies" is wrong (p. 5); from shape alone the authors infer two kinds of turbulence with no cascade between them (p. 10–11). The lab baseline in [Norum & Seiner 1982](../Notes/norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md) is a maximum OASPL 22.5–30° from the jet axis, up to 139.1 dB, with near-design C-D jets broadband only (p. 50, 175, 190).

**Evidence beyond spectral shape (2008).** [Tam et al. 2008](../Notes/tam-2008-sources-of-jet-noise-experimental-evidence.md) add four tests, each with its change-over at 120–140° inlet. The total-OASPL velocity exponent rises from about 8 sideline to 9.9 at 150° inlet (cold) and from 6.4 to 9.1 (T_r/T_a 2.7), changing abruptly near 120° (p. 263–266). Microphones 10° apart correlate up to 0.59 above 130° inlet but at most about 0.1 sideline (p. 274–278). Rayleigh-scattering flow signals correlate with 150° sound by up to about 22% and fall to the noise floor below 120° (p. 279–280). Mirror maps put all sideline sources at one x just past the end of the potential core, while the aft peak moves upstream as the inlet angle grows (p. 283–289). The exponents match the 1996 ones within about 0.4, but the 2.4D_j "radial correlation length" (p. 272) is fixed by the F spectrum itself (the note's checks). The note also flags a scaling tension: the large-structure peak collapses here on fD_j/u_j (≈ 0.17 at 150°, p. 262–263), but in 1996 on fD_j/a_∞ (≈ 0.19 at 160°).

**Wavepackets as the large-scale source.** [Jordan & Colonius 2013](../Notes/jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise.md) list the cold-jet facts a wavepacket model must explain: a peak 20–40° from the jet axis at St ≈ 0.2, broadening to St ≈ 0.4 at 90°; OASPL ∝ U_j^n with n = 8 at 90° and n > 8 at shallow angles; almost all St < 1 sound in m = 0–2, from Michalke & Fuchs's radial-compactness Bessel factor; and bursts at St ≈ 0.2 (p. 176). The coherent motion holds about 15% of the low-frequency energy but radiates efficiently through coherence and intermittency, and loud far-field events coincide with wavepacket breakdown near the end of the potential core (p. 180–181, 186). The same compactness explains why lipline velocity energy at m ≈ 4–11 is silent; the m = 0 velocity–pressure correlation is about 10% ([Cavalieri et al. 2013](../Notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), p. 566–571). The line-source review of [Cavalieri et al. 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md) gives superdirectivity for subsonic M_c and Mach waves for supersonic M_c (p. 10–13). Jitter adds about 30 dB at M 0.6 but barely changes the M 2 peak, so subsonic jets need three SPOD modes for their sound and supersonic jets one (p. 15–18), and at supersonic Mach numbers the first resolvent gain separates as a Mach-wave mode (p. 26, 28).

**Measured coherence (2017).** Dual-plane time-resolved PIV of an M 0.4 jet measures what jitter models assume ([Jaunet et al. 2017](../Notes/jaunet-2017-two-point-coherence-wave-packets-turbulent-jets.md)). m = 0, ±1, ±2 carry under 10% of the bulk two-point coherence, which the energetic m ≈ 2–8 eddies set (p. 9–10). The m = 0 coherence length varies tenfold with x and St (≈ 0.2–1.9D), is up to three times the eddies' where linear theory works, falls to eddy values past the core (p. 14–17), and is exponential and asymmetric, unlike the constant Gaussian of the models (p. 17–19). For St ≲ 0.3 the m = 0 phase speed reaches about U_j near the nozzle against about 0.6U_j for the eddies (p. 20–21). In the Cavalieri–Agarwal source, 0.5 → 0.6U_j at St 0.4 changes the low-angle PSD by about 24 dB (the note's calculation), so a fixed M_c ≈ 0.6M is questionable for subsonic jets at low St.

PSE wavepackets match the leading SPOD mode of an M_j 1.5 LES, Mach waves included ([Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), p. 17), and LES shows low-rank KH packets giving way to non-low-rank Orr packets away from the peak conditions ([Brès & Lele 2019](../Notes/bres-2019-modelling-jet-noise-les-perspective.md), p. 12–13). Tam's "instability waves" and the modern "wavepackets" are the same object.

**Statistical source inputs.** Acoustic-analogy models of fine-scale noise need fourth-order turbulence scales. Screech-filtered PIV of the group's C-mode jets gives c_L1111 ≈ 0.37–0.39, matching Karabasov et al.'s LES fit of 0.37, and amplitude ratios R₂₂₂₂/R₁₁₁₁ ≈ 0.15–0.17 and R₁₂₁₂/R₁₁₁₁ ≈ 0.22–0.24 that agree with hot-wire data but not with that LES ([Tan et al. 2019a](../Notes/tan-2019-correlation-analysis-piv-screeching-jets.md), Table 5, p. 27, 30).

**Control evidence.** Forcing m = ±1 at St_DF ≈ 0.3 adds about 4–5 dB, while high St_DF or high m cuts about 0.6–1 dB ([Samimy et al. 2006](../Notes/samimy-2006-plasma-actuators-high-speed-jet-flow-noise-control.md), p. 19), and forcing at LSA-stable frequencies thickens the mean flow and lowers noise while unstable ones add it (Koenig 2011, in [Jordan & Colonius 2013](../Notes/jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise.md), p. 188–189). Trailing-edge cutouts cut 30° noise from an underexpanded AR 3 jet by up to 12 dB ([Kerechanin et al. 2001](../Notes/kerechanin-2001-trailing-edge-rectangular-jet-acoustics.md), p. 3). In a heated military-nozzle LES at equal thrust, an afterburner annulus round a cooler core lowers peak OASPL by about 2 dB ([Brès & Lele 2019](../Notes/bres-2019-modelling-jet-noise-les-perspective.md), p. 8–9; the note's reading).

## Mach wave radiation and the convective Mach number
Papamoschou & Roshko defined M_c in the frame of the large structures ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../Notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md)). Normalised growth starts falling at M_c ≈ 0.25 and levels at about 0.2 by M_c ≈ 0.6–0.8 (p. 19–20); large structures persist up to M_c ≈ 1.8 (p. 8); and a vortex sheet stabilises above a critical M_c but finite-thickness layers do not (p. 14–15). They read the growth reduction as energy radiated away (p. 21–22).

**Three instability families.** A high-speed jet carries KH waves, supersonic instability waves (Mach waves trapped between its boundaries) and subsonic waves with no near field ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 447, 452–457). Supersonic waves exist only for u_j > a_j + a_∞ (eq. 2.13, p. 456), M_j > 2.5 in cold jets (the note's calculation), and their most amplified member travels at u_j/(1 + a_j/a_∞) for any mode, M_j or thickness up to b/R_j ≈ 0.3 (eq. 5.1, p. 472, 480): the theory behind the Papamoschou–Roshko speed. Above a critical M_j that falls with temperature (≈ 2.8 at T_j/T_∞ = 1, ≈ 2.0 at 3), the KH wave merges into the supersonic family (Fig. 9, p. 459). The upstream subsonic waves are the guided jet mode ([screech](screech.md)).

**Near-field fronts are not yet sound.** Outside a growing wave the fronts are oblique, with speed c_w = a_∞M_c cos μ, which reaches a_∞ only for neutral waves with M_c > 1. Growing waves therefore give visible schlieren fronts, tilted by up to 30–45° at M = 1.2, that Michalke declines to call sound; their radiated part needs a uniformly valid analysis ([Michalke 1984](../Notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), eqs. 28–31, p. 175–176, 192).

**First prediction (1984).** [Tam & Burton 1984](../Notes/tam-1984-sound-instability-waves-supersonic-axisymmetric-jets.md) supplied it: a non-parallel instability wave matched to a Fourier–Hankel outer field, whose far field is the wave's axial wavenumber spectrum at (u_j/a_∞)ω cos χ, so only wavenumbers below the sonic branch point radiate (eq. 5.8, p. 283, 289). For a forced cold M 2.1 jet it reproduces the measured near-field contours, "nearly perfect" at St 0.4 with one level fitted, while St 0.2 needs the field shifted downstream (p. 291–293); single modes radiate near 40° (n = 0) or within 35° (n = 1) of the axis (p. 290–292). The radiating waves travel at 0.80–0.91u_j (U/a_∞ ≈ 1.2–1.4), although the Papamoschou–Roshko M_c is only 0.89 and the supersonic family needs M_j > 2.37 at this temperature (the note's readings): these are KH waves faster than a_∞, which supports the eddy-Mach-number convention. There are no far-field data (p. 274).

**Direction.** The peak follows the Mach angle of the most amplified wave; its growth and decay broaden the wavenumber spectrum, so even subsonic-phase-speed waves radiate a little ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 27–28; [Tam et al. 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), p. 8–9). Computed KH wavefront angles match shadowgraphs, ≈ 47–52° in nitrogen jets at M_j 1.5–3.0 and ≈ 31–37° in helium ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), Figs 19–20, p. 467–469). Cavalieri et al.'s closed form is PSD ∝ exp[−½k_h²L²(1 − M_c cos θ)²], with M_c ≈ 0.6M for cold lab jets ([Cavalieri et al. 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 9, 11). The peak moves with frequency: in Tam & Chen's Mach 2 data from θ ≈ 25–30° at St ≈ 0.06 to 40–45° (500 K) and 50–60° (1114 K) at St 0.2–0.4 (the [Tam & Chen 1994](../Notes/tam-1994-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md) note's readings of Figs 5–6). The heated rectangular LES shows the threshold directly: no Mach waves at U_c/a_∞ = 0.94 (cold), Mach waves at 1.21–1.29 peaking about 40° from the axis against 37° from Oertel's formula, and much stronger in the flapping plane ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), Table 2, p. 16, 20–21).

**Heating** broadens the dominant sector and adds low-St noise ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 20). It destabilises the KH wave in spatial theory (peak −α_iθ 0.14 against 0.113 at T_o/T_∞ = 2, for M = 0 and laminar profiles only; [Michalke 1984](../Notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), Fig. 19, p. 177–178), and the supersonic waves too ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 480). In LES of a C-D round jet the aft Mach waves (30–45° from the axis) are strongest in the hottest jet, and heating shortens the potential core from 9.7D to 8.4D, as Lau's (T₀/T∞)^−0.2 predicts ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1170–1173). U_c/u_j falls with heating: from 0.80 to 0.64 over TR 1–3 in the rectangular LES, as Liu et al. found in round jets ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 16), and to a most likely 0.43 in a hot (TR 6.5) M 3.1 overexpanded LES, where u_c oscillates about Oertel's fastest speed M_c3 (θ′ 66°) before the end of the core and falls to M_c1 (θ′ 40°) beyond it ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 17–20). For rockets, M_c = κU_e/c_a needs κ ≈ 0.31–0.32, against 0.6–0.85 for lab jets ([Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 14–15; [Mathews et al. 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 627).

**Wall-jet Mach waves.** An M_j 2 jet on a 45° plate adds Mach waves from the supersonic wall jet and broadband impingement-region waves, for which SP-8072 has no source ([Nonomura et al. 2011](../Notes/nonomura-2011-supersonic-jet-impinging-inclined-plate-acoustic-waves.md), p. 414, 422). Raising TR_c from 1 to 5.4 turns the wall-jet waves from about 30° to 40–50° from the plate at r = 20D and the impingement-region waves from 75° to 95° (p. 416, 421), which the authors put down to the faster convection of hot jets (p. 421). By the note's parallax estimate the wall-jet U_c is ≈ 1.3a_∞ cold and 2.1a_∞ hot, so U_c/u_J falls from ≈ 0.9 to 0.6 with heating here too. The note adds two cautions: the "significantly" lower hot peak St is mostly the u_J normalisation (in fD/a_∞ it is no lower), and the ≈ 7 dB rise at the Mach-wave peak exceeds the 3.7 dB of ρ_Ju_J³ at constant efficiency.

## BBSAN: mechanism, models and the effect of screech
**Mechanism (1982).** Tam & Tanna replaced Harper-Bourne & Fisher's point-source array with the product of an instability wave and Pack shock cells ([Tam & Tanna 1982](../Notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md)):
- Only k − λ₁ radiates effectively. The k + λ_n waves are subsonic, and k − λ₂ is 7.2 dB weaker for a uniform train (p. 9).
- On an M_d 1.672 C-D nozzle, f_p = u_c/[L_s(1 − M_c cos θ)] with u_c = 0.7u_j lands within one third-octave band at 75–135° (p. 16–18).
- Under- and overexpanded jets fall on one (M_j² − M_d²)² line; cold jets are up to 3 dB quieter (p. 17–20).
- At design the nozzle is about 9 dB quieter than a convergent one at 120° (p. 12–13), matching the dip at design in [Norum & Seiner 1982](../Notes/norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md) (p. 37, 40).

The authors called the model "still quite tentative"; it gives no absolute level or spectral shape (p. 21).

**Multiple peaks (1986) and stochastic theory (1995).** Narrowband spectra show second and third peaks, set by the shock-cell Fourier wavenumbers rather than harmonics; one-third-octave bands hide them ([Tam et al. 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), p. 3–5). The peak narrows upstream as (1 − M_c cos θ)⁻², the basis of the "weakest link" screech argument (p. 6–10; see [screech](screech.md)). Tam's four-parameter stochastic theory reaches engineering accuracy for M_j and M_d of 1–2 but misses dips at small inlet angles, and shock-cell solvers missed the rapid shock decay of strongly screeching jets ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 32–36).

**Shock leakage, a local view (2003).** A steady shear layer never lets a weak shock out, since the Mach angle always exceeds the critical angle for total internal reflection; unsteady vortices let it leak near the saddles between them ([Suzuki & Lele 2003](../Notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md), p. 145–149). In a 2-D DNS of an M 1.2 layer the leaked jump is up to about 0.1 of the incident one and radiates broadly sideline, tilting from about 84° to 74° with heating (Figs 24–27, p. 162–163). Downstream, turbulence scatters the thin fronts into broadband shock noise (p. 164): the sharp-front form of the difference-wave picture (the note's reading).

**First-principles linear computation (2005).** [Ray et al. 2005](../Notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md) compute the product rather than postulate it: PSE m = 0 waves times multiple-scales shock modes on a RANS mean drive the linearised Euler equations of an overexpanded M_j 1.28 jet (M_d 1.5, β ≈ 0.78) at T_j/T_∞ = 1 and 2 (p. 2–5). Only k_t − k_s radiates upstream, at θ_M = cos⁻¹(1/c_s1), and a longer envelope makes it louder and narrower (p. 4–5). At St 0.6 the upstream lobe (≈ 100–140°) is the fan of local Mach angles over a centreline source at x/D 2–6 (Fig. 7, p. 9); at St 0.9 the sources become interfering rings at r/D ≈ 0.5 (p. 12–13), and only there does the second shock mode radiate (p. 9), the n = 2 peak that [Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md) found missing from Ray & Lele 2007 (p. 23, 29). Heating leaves the shock modes unchanged, moves the lobe about 15° upstream (p. 10; sign doubtful) and raises the entropy source from under 5% to about 20% at T_j/T_∞ = 2 (p. 12). The runs are partly unconverged, with no comparison with measured sound (p. 9–10, 13).

**Coherence decay (2019).** [Wong et al. 2019](../Notes/wong-2019-coherence-decay-wavepacket-bbsan.md), the group's first wavepacket BBSAN model, extend Cavalieri & Agarwal's jittering line source to a Gaussian wavepacket times Pack shock cells at NPR 3.4 (M_j 1.45), with a constant Gaussian coherence length of 1.0D (p. 973–977). Unlike in subsonic jets, coherence decay lowers the peak by about 2–5 dB and broadens the directivity (Fig. 2, p. 978): the difference wave k_h − k_s, the only radiating lobe, already lies inside the radiation square, so jitter smears energy out of it (p. 981–983). With ten Pack modes the coherent model shows narrow secondary peaks and dips at high St and upstream angles; coherence decay pulls the n = 2 lobe into the radiation square and fills the "missing sound" of Ray & Lele (2007) and Suzuki (2016), which the authors blame on missing shock modes, not on linear theory (p. 980, 984–987). Levels are matched to the data, so only shape is tested (p. 979). The note derives the mechanism (ours, not the paper's): Cavalieri et al.'s closed form applied to the k_h − k_s wave gives peak drops of −3.9, −2.9 and −1.8 dB at St 0.3, 0.4 and 0.6, as in Fig. 2, so jitter attenuates matched difference waves and amplifies subsonic ones (n = 1 below St ≈ 0.22, n = 2 below St ≈ 0.51). The paper calls the coherent model's secondary peaks "artificial" (p. 984, 987), whereas Wong et al. 2021 and Tam et al. 1986 find a real n = 2 peak; the note reads "artificial" as too narrow and too deep.

**Screech lowers BBSAN (2013).** [André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md) compare a smooth and a 24-notch convergent nozzle with the same first shock cells, shock strength and M_j 1.0 mixing noise (p. 667–668):
- Without screech the mean shock train lasts much longer (to x/D ≈ 11 against collapse near 5.3 at M_j 1.35; Fig. 5, p. 667–668), so screech itself damps the cells.
- The screech-free BBSAN is louder by ≈ 2.5–4 dB at M_j 1.10, 0–2 dB at 1.15 and 0–2.5 dB at 1.35, equal at 1.50 where screech is weak (Fig. 13, p. 671), and peaks higher (Sr 0.76 against 0.65 at M_j 1.35; p. 669).
- With screech the 90° peak follows 2St_s through every stage jump, and U_c from the λ_p–cos θ slope is 0.42–0.65U_j depending on the mode, against 0.65–0.66U_j at every M_j without it (Fig. 8, Table 1, p. 669–670). Tam & Tanna's u_c ≈ 0.7u_j fits non-screeching jets.
- Near-field microphones put the strongest, highest-frequency sources at the downstream cells (peak near x/D 4.5–5), which screech removes (Figs 11–12, p. 670–671).
- By the note's arithmetic (f_p(90°) ≈ 2f_s; λ_p reaching ≈ 2.3D at cos θ = −1 against λ_s ≈ 2.8D) screech is not the upstream limit at these M_j; Tam et al. showed that limit only at M_j 1.8 and 2.24 ([Tam et al. 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), p. 8), so this restricts rather than refutes it.

**Statistical source models (2017–2019).** The group's other route is the RANS-informed acoustic analogy of Morris & Miller (2010) and Kalyan & Karabasov (2017), driven by turbulence scales rather than wavepackets ([Tan et al. 2019a](../Notes/tan-2019-correlation-analysis-piv-screeching-jets.md), p. 4–13).
- *Screech-free turbulence.* Subtracting the leading POD modes of 8500 snapshot PIV fields removes the tone: two modes for the helical C mode, 18 for the precessing B mode ([Tan et al. 2017](../Notes/tan-2017-broadband-velocity-fluctuations-screeching-jets.md), p. 2325–2328). The broadband scales then settle to L⁽¹⁾_uu/δθ ≈ 2.1 and L⁽¹⁾_vv/δθ ≈ L⁽²⁾_uu/δθ ≈ 1.0 at M_j 1.25–1.59, the non-screeching values, while total fluctuations overestimate them by 15–140% (Table 3, p. 2331–2332).
- *Coefficients.* On the lip line of the C-mode jets (M_j 1.45, 1.59) the scales follow k^{3/2}/ε or k^{1/2}D_j/V̄₁ with coefficients that differ by 0–9.5% between M_j, against up to 30% for the shock-modulated total field (2019a, Tables 3–4, p. 26–29). Axial-velocity anisotropy is ≈ 0.5, not the 0.3 of earlier BBSAN predictions (p. 29), and the shock time scale stays secondary, so the mixed-scale model reduces to Morris & Miller there (2019a, p. 33; [Tan et al. 2019b](../Notes/tan-2019-equivalent-bbsan-source-reconstruction-screeching-jets.md), p. 20–21).
- *Source maps.* With k–ω SST RANS, the equivalent sources at Sr = 1 sit on the sonic line just upstream of each shock reflection, mainly in cells 1–5 and strongest at the second, with PIV- and RANS-based maps within about 2% on the lip line (2019b, p. 7, 36–40). PIV gives no integral time scale, so no noise is predicted and all agreement is internal (p. 7).
- *Against far-field fits.* Morris & Miller's tuned streamwise coefficients (3.0–3.25) are about ten times the PIV-fitted ones (0.28, 0.43) (2019b, Tables 5–6, p. 24–25), which they had put down to large structures coherent over long axial distances (2019a, p. 12).
- *Caveats (the notes).* The "below 3%" RMSE sits oddly with ±13–23% coefficient scatter (2019a); Sr = 1 is about twice the sideline fundamental, so the maps may weight the second shock-cell harmonic (2019b); all are cold jets.

**Azimuthal content (2020).** [Wong et al. 2020](../Notes/wong-2020-azimuthal-decomposition-radiated-noise-shock-containing-jets.md) give the library's first azimuthally decomposed BBSAN data: unheated, screeching convergent-nozzle jets at M_j 1.14–1.50 (β 0.55–1.12) in the group's SJAF, with a two-microphone ring at r = 11D (p. 2016–2018). m = 0 is never dominant: the ±1 pair leads below St ≈ 0.6–0.7, with m = 0 on average 3–4 dB/St lower, and above that the ±1 to ±3 pairs are about equal (p. 2019); about 3 dB of the gap is built in, since one mode is compared with a summed pair (the note's reading). Two to three modes rebuild the primary lobe within 1 dB/St at NPR 3.10–3.67, but NPR 2.25, low θ and the n = 2 lobe need 4–5 (p. 2020–2021), so the authors add azimuthal truncation (Tam 1987; Ray & Lele 2007, m ≤ 2) to the causes of the high-frequency "missing sound" (p. 2021). That is compatible with Wong et al. 2019, since the secondary lobe needs n = 2 shocks and is also the most multimodal (the note's reading). OASPL follows β⁴ for β⁴ > 0.5, and the peak formula with u_c = 0.7u_j and Pack spacing fits NPR 3.10–3.67 but not 2.25 (p. 2019–2020). Screech was not suppressed; only agreement with a non-screeching LES suggests it leaves the azimuthal frequency dependence unchanged (p. 2020), which is no controlled test.

**Inside-out wavepacket model (2021).** [Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md) built the source from flow data only. Wavepackets come from an ideally expanded M_j 1.5 LES or PSE on its mean flow; shock cells are Pack–Prandtl modes corrected with mean PIV of a screeching M_j 1.45 jet from a Shock Lab group facility, the jet Tan et al. also used (k_s,mod = 0.79 k_s + 1.02) (p. 6, 8–14). The results:
- m = 0–2 peaks match the 2020 data (NPR 3.67) mode by mode within ±2 dB/St, and the n = 2 peak comes from the second shock-cell mode (p. 20–23, 29), though the m = 0–2 total under-predicts that lobe, which needs 4–5 modes (p. 22).
- Perfectly coherent PSE leaves dips up to 20 dB/St deep between peaks; imposing the LES coherence (jitter) recovers up to 15 dB/St (p. 24–25), the subsonic-difference-wave mechanism of Wong et al. 2019.
- The source sits inside the plume, two lobes per shock cell, strongest at cells 3–5 (2D–8D), and moves upstream as frequency rises (p. 27).
- Peak frequency is under-predicted at 115–125°, a screech harmonic sits on the measured peak near 80°, and nothing is predicted below St ≈ 0.4, where mixing noise was excluded (p. 20, 23).

The shocks are steady and the wavepackets come from a shock-free jet, as in Ray et al. Shock modulation lengthens the envelope that shock-free PSE predicts to decay too fast ([Nogueira et al. 2022](../Notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 18–19), consistent with Wong's PSE falling below the LES beyond x ≈ 6D (p. 17).

**Measured spacing sets the peak (2017–2025).** In overexpanded C-D jets the peak relation works with measured, not analytic, spacing.
- *Heated LES.* For an overexpanded M_d 1.5 jet the LES gives the 90° peak at St 0.78 against 0.75 measured; the formula with U_c = 0.7U_j gives 0.75 with the measured first cell (0.93D) but 0.61 with Pack's 1.14D ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1173–1176). The stationary shocks also radiate circular waves to θ ≈ 90–130° (p. 1170–1171), and the LES BBSAN falls off too slowly with angle, over-predicting St 1–2 at θ ≈ 60–70° (p. 1176).
- *Hot M 3.1 LES.* Tam & Tanna's formula matches the peaks at θ′ = 101° and 129° only with the mean lipline spacing (2.2D_j, shrinking downstream), not the 2.6D_j first centreline cell ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 19). Upstream BBSAN is mostly m = 1, and the lipline shocks coincide with m = 1, 2 peaks (Figs 22, 26, p. 21, 24), as in Wong et al.'s cold jets. A frequency-locked upstream lobe at St 0.066 near θ′ ≈ 155°, without screech, is named "MW-BBSAN" (p. 22–24), but its criterion is exactly the θ′ → 180° BBSAN limit, Tam et al.'s screech estimate (the note's check), so it fits Tam 1986 rather than being a new source.
- *Full scale.* Holography of an afterburning F404 (M_j 1.46, M_d 1.55, TTR 7, overexpanded) puts BSN sources at shock cells II–VIII, loudest at VI–VII ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 32). Spacing shrinks from about 0.97 to 0.72 D_j along the train; the spacing formulas all fail, yet the Harper-Bourne–Fisher peak frequency agrees "excellently" with the measured spacing (p. 34, 36). Each shock source is coherent with both forward BSN and aft Mach waves, and coherence islands between shocks show one wavepacket meeting several shocks (p. 38–40), as in Wong's distributed, partially coherent source (Wong 2021, p. 28–29).

**Non-round jets.** On an overexpanded AR 3 jet, symmetric cutouts raise BBSAN along with stronger screech ([Kerechanin et al. 2001](../Notes/kerechanin-2001-trailing-edge-rectangular-jet-acoustics.md), p. 4–5), but they change the shocks strongly, so this is no clean test of "screech lowers BBSAN" (André note). In the heated AR 2 LES the hump (20–120° from the axis) follows Harper-Bourne & Fisher only with an ad hoc shock length of 0.8L_model, and the first screech harmonic sits on it near 90°, dominating for TR ≥ 2 ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 20–21).

## Crackle and nonlinear steepening
No laboratory study of crackle generation is indexed (Ffowcs Williams et al. 1975 and the primary Nichols et al. papers are not in the library), but two LES accounts reach the source. In Nichols et al.'s (2013) LES, validated at NTR 3.65, crackle leaves as weak shocklets traced back to shear-layer eddies; near-nozzle shocklets fit Mach-wave emission at Oertel's M_co, while the intense events are faster and rare ([Brès & Lele 2019](../Notes/bres-2019-modelling-jet-noise-les-perspective.md), p. 10, Fig. 6; secondary). In the hot M 3.1 LES coupled to a nonlinear Euler solver, a wave steepening factor above 1 and high S(ṗ) and K(ṗ) mark steep events by the shear layer at x ≈ 25D, and waves keep steepening out to 40D; a linear FW-H gets the OASPL but not these statistics, and the cut-off St_c = 0.22 truncates the shocks (S(ṗ) below 1.5 at 40D against over 1.75 measured) ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 25–27). The propagation side is better covered:
- **F-22A run-ups.** At 90% rpm and afterburner, a generalised Burgers model from 23 m matches 305 m spectra within 2–7 dB to 20 kHz; the linear model is 120–140 dB low at 20 kHz ([Gee et al. 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 9–10). Nonlinearity spans 90–145°, contradicting an earlier F/A-18E bispectral result at 90° (p. 11). OASPL and loudness metrics barely change (p. 12).
- **Rocket waveforms.** Rocket noise is positively skewed near the peak direction (Sk{p} ≈ 0.6–0.75), and Sk{∂p/∂t} exceeds 30 and grows with distance ([Gee et al. 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 7–8). At 2.5 km from an SLS booster firing it is still 21–32 at 80–100° ([Reichman et al. 2018](../Notes/reichman-2018-qm2-srm-far-field-acoustics.md), p. 8–9).
- **Spectra and losses.** Launch waveforms are shock-dominated, with a weak-shock 10 dB/decade roll-off to 10 kHz ([Kellison et al. 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 5–6). Over 7 km, Atlas V loses about 15 dB at 1 kHz against 26 dB predicted by linear absorption ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 84–85).

Other links to the source are indirect. Gee et al. propose that plume impingement breaks up the Mach-wave-radiating structures, which would explain a downstream zone of unskewed, low-frequency noise; this is untested ([Gee et al. 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 10). In the lab, upstream waves from impinging jets are shocks at birth (M_s ≈ 1.07) or coalesce within 10D ([Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2025](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md), p. 3, 10–11), and leaked shock fronts are nonlinear at the source, the compression branch keeping its strength while the expansion branch spreads ([Suzuki & Lele 2003](../Notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md), p. 159).

## Carry-over to rocket noise
Details are on [rocket-noise-models](rocket-noise-models.md) and [rocket-noise-measurements](rocket-noise-measurements.md); the mechanism-level points are these.
- **Mach waves dominate**, with sound power ∝ U_e³ and η ≈ 0.3–0.5% ([Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 7–9; [Kellison et al. 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 14). Near-field intensity vectors of a solid motor fit the two-source picture, but only by inspection ([Gee et al. 2010](../Notes/gee-2010-near-field-vector-intensity-solid-rocket-motor.md), p. 6–7). Rocket plumes are past the critical M_j at which KH merges into the supersonic family, so the supersonic waves should dominate (the [Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md) note's inference from Fig. 9, p. 459).
- **The similarity spectra fit poorly.** F fits only the RSRM 45° peak ([Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 10), and a modified F with f^2.5/f^−2.2 slopes is used instead ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 377). The 1996 fits stop at T_r/T_∞ ≈ 4.9 and the 2008 tests at 3.2, without nonlinear propagation.
- **Peak Strouhal number falls about tenfold**, to Sr ≈ 0.01–0.025 against about 0.2, for reasons "still unclear" ([Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 10; [Kellison et al. 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 15–16). The F404 at TTR 7 already puts much energy below Sr 0.1 ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 16), and the hot M 3.1 jet peaks at St ≈ 0.02 at its shallowest angles ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 21). Flight lowers it further: Michalke's stretching σ = 1 + 1.4U_∞/ΔU collapses growth rates and spectra of jets in flight ([Michalke 1984](../Notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), Fig. 26, p. 178–182) and gives a ~23% downward shift for an ascending Falcon 9 ([Mathews et al. 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 629).
- **No BBSAN or screech** at sideline and forward angles, perhaps because BBSAN saturates with temperature or is buried under Mach waves ([Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 9). Yet the afterburning F404 shows BSN clearly ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 6, 32), and so do a hot (TR 6.5) M 3.1 subscale jet and its LES ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 16–19).
- **Non-axisymmetric exhausts** can have azimuth-dependent Mach-wave directivity, which axisymmetric SP-8072-type models ignore (the [Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md) note's inference from p. 16).
- **Installation:** reflected free-jet Mach waves in the flame path, not deflector impingement, set Epsilon fairing levels at H/D_e = 14 ([Tsutsumi et al. 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 15–16); on an inclined plate the wall jet and impingement region add their own waves (Nonomura et al. 2011, above).

## Disagreements and open questions
**Two sources or one jittering wavepacket?** [Tam et al. 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md) infer two kinds of turbulence from spectral shape (p. 10–11), and [Tam et al. 2008](../Notes/tam-2008-sources-of-jet-noise-experimental-evidence.md) show that the aft field is coherent and the sideline field is not (p. 274–280). [Jordan & Colonius 2013](../Notes/jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise.md) reject the split, since spectral shape evolves smoothly with angle and aft and sideline peaks span similar bands, but concede that the wavepacket share of sideline noise "remains to be quantified" (p. 181, 189); jitter spreads wavepacket radiation to wider angles ([Cavalieri et al. 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 14–16). A single-point correlation cannot tell fine-scale sources from short-coherence wavepackets (the Tam 2008 note's reading), and [Jaunet et al. 2017](../Notes/jaunet-2017-two-point-coherence-wave-packets-turbulent-jets.md) measure the coherence a jitter model needs without computing sideline noise. Hardest for a one-source view are the fixed sideline source position and the abrupt change of velocity exponent near 120° inlet (Tam et al. 2008, p. 265, 285–289). Projecting sideline spectra onto a few SPOD or coupled-resolvent modes of the same jet, or setting a wavepacket model with measured coherence against G in shape and level, would test how much of G they recover.

**Which convective Mach number sets the rocket peak angle?** The candidates disagree:
- The Oertel M_CO is favoured by [Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) (p. 13–15) and [Mathews et al. 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md) (p. 625–627), but Falcon 9's 64° matches it only after subtracting the vehicle Mach number.
- [Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md) revise that angle to 71° after a 3-D geometry fix (p. 375, 378).
- [Kellison et al. 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md) measure 64° again and prefer the "supersonic" Tam & Hu M_c (66°) to Oertel (69°) (p. 11–13).

A single M_c may not exist even in the lab: Tam & Chen's Mach 2 peak moves with St ("Direction" above), and in the hot M 3.1 LES u_c falls along the jet from M_c3 to M_c1, with the far-field peak at θ′ ≈ 48° against 63° for M_co, which the authors blame on the 41.6D arc ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 18–19). Theory favours the supersonic speed at rocket conditions, where no separate KH wave exists ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 459, 472). Kellison's KH and "subsonic" candidates are Oertel's empirical speeds, not Tam & Hu's, whose subsonic waves have no near field to radiate Mach waves (p. 456, 465). Ray-traced near-pad arrays, plus wavenumber-filtered SPOD of heated-jet LES to see which family radiates, would decide it.

**What fills the BBSAN dips?** Tam (1987, as cited by Wong) blamed shock unsteadiness; [Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md) attribute most of it to jitter (p. 25), which fills them in a kinematic model without shock motion ([Wong et al. 2019](../Notes/wong-2019-coherence-decay-wavepacket-bbsan.md), p. 984–987), and azimuthal truncation adds to the high-frequency deficit ([Wong et al. 2020](../Notes/wong-2020-azimuthal-decomposition-radiated-noise-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 2021). In screeching jets the shocks themselves oscillate at the tone ([André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 672). Time-resolved schlieren of screeching and screech-free jets at the same β, or a model with oscillating shocks, would separate jitter from shock motion.

**Where are the BBSAN sources?** Inside the plume or towards the end of the core: cells 3–5 ([Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), p. 27), the centreline at x/D 2–6 at St 0.6 ([Ray et al. 2005](../Notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md), p. 9), the downstream cells near x/D 4.5–5, longer without screech ([André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 670–671), 2 < x/D_j < 16 in the hot M 3.1 LES ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 19), and cells VI–VII at full scale ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 32). Or on the sonic line in the first cells, strongest at the second reflection ([Tan et al. 2019b](../Notes/tan-2019-equivalent-bbsan-source-reconstruction-screeching-jets.md), p. 36–38), whose authors blame refraction and tabs for the end-of-core results (p. 38). Three factors separate them:
- *Model structure:* Tan's source is local shock pressure times local turbulence, so it can only sit where both overlap; Wong's wavepacket reaches into the core (Tan 2019b note).
- *Frequency:* sources move upstream or off axis as frequency rises in Wong (p. 27) and Ray (p. 12), yet André's near-field peak frequency rises downstream. Cells that shrink downstream (Mathews p. 34; 5.5% per cell in [Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 15; 6–7% in [Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1174) would raise the local peak there, an untested reconciliation; and Tan's Sr = 1 may be the second harmonic.
- *Screech:* Wong's shock cells and Tan's turbulence come from the same screeching jet, and screech removes the downstream cells (André, p. 668–670). Screech itself is generated between cells 3 and 4 ([Tam et al. 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), p. 8), and leakage puts tonal sources at cells 2–4 and broadband ones further on ([Suzuki & Lele 2003](../Notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md), p. 164).

One rig with a notched screech-free twin, a β and TTR sweep, mean PIV and a near-field array, giving frequency-resolved maps for both models, would settle it.

**Statistical or wavepacket sources?** Far-field fits need streamwise length coefficients ten times the PIV-measured ones and anisotropy 0.3 against 0.5 ([Tan et al. 2019a](../Notes/tan-2019-correlation-analysis-piv-screeching-jets.md), p. 12, 29; [Tan et al. 2019b](../Notes/tan-2019-equivalent-bbsan-source-reconstruction-screeching-jets.md), p. 24–25). If that long axial coherence is the coherent wavepacket, the gap is the statistical model's version of the wavepacket view (our reading). Bulk two-point statistics are indeed set by acoustically inefficient m ≈ 2–8 eddies ([Jaunet et al. 2017](../Notes/jaunet-2017-two-point-coherence-wave-packets-turbulent-jets.md), p. 9), and only the low-order wavepacket coherence matters for BBSAN ([Wong et al. 2019](../Notes/wong-2019-coherence-decay-wavepacket-bbsan.md), p. 972, 987). Brès & Lele rank Morris & Miller's model above Suzuki's (2016) wavepacket form of Harper-Bourne & Fisher for spectral shape ([Brès & Lele 2019](../Notes/bres-2019-modelling-jet-noise-les-perspective.md), p. 11), but that verdict predates Wong et al. 2021 and concerns Suzuki's model only. Far-field spectra from the PIV-calibrated model, with time scales from time-resolved data, set against Wong's prediction for the same jet would decide.

**Does screech tune BBSAN through the mean flow or its harmonic?** The screeching-jet peak follows 2St_s ([André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 669), a harmonic sits on Wong's measured peak (p. 23), and the first harmonic dominates the hot rectangular LES's hump near 90° ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 20). André et al. invoke staging-driven changes in spacing or U_c (p. 669); the André note reads it as the harmonic's KH wavenumber matching k_s, radiating sideways. Bicoherence or BMD at (St_s, St_s) would separate the two.

**Why is BBSAN absent from rockets** when the F404 at TTR 7 shows it? The models give no reason for it to vanish with heat: heating leaves the shock modes unchanged and raises the entropy source ([Ray et al. 2005](../Notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md), p. 10, 12), hot plumes leak shocks more easily ([Suzuki & Lele 2003](../Notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md), p. 147, 164), and the absence of screech raises lab BBSAN ([André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 671). A hot (TR 6.5) M 3.1 subscale jet still shows upstream BBSAN that follows Tam & Tanna ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 16–19). Saturation with heat is thus unsupported at subscale, which leaves masking by Mach waves or an effect of full scale. Forward-arc measurements of strongly overexpanded motors and high-TTR lab jets with β sweeps would test these.

**Shock spacing in overexpanded C-D jets** has no working formula ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 34–35). Pack's first cell is about 20% too long in an M_d 1.5 jet (L₁/L_s ≈ 0.82, as in Norum & Seiner's data; [Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1174), lipline and centreline spacings differ in a hot M 3.1 jet ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 11, 19), Wong's correction is fitted to one jet, and Tam & Tanna's uniform train ([Tam & Tanna 1982](../Notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md), p. 9) is contradicted by trains that shrink downstream. Multiple-scales modes on a RANS mean reproduce a mildly overexpanded (β ≈ 0.78) train for about five cells ([Ray et al. 2005](../Notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md), p. 6–7), the candidate, untested at strong overexpansion, heat or a Mach disk. For an AR 2 C-D nozzle Tam's first-cell length is within 1–5% of LES ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 15).

**How does screech raise broadband noise?** Strong screech adds about 3 dB ([Tam et al. 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), p. 8), and screeching jets are 2–4 dB louder below Sr 0.6 than their notched twins (André Fig. 2, p. 667, the note's reading), which would make André's BBSAN reduction a lower bound. Yet the filtered broadband length scales match non-screeching jets ([Tan et al. 2017](../Notes/tan-2017-broadband-velocity-fluctuations-screeching-jets.md), p. 2331–2332), and the model coefficients do not change with screech strength ([Tan et al. 2019a](../Notes/tan-2019-correlation-analysis-piv-screeching-jets.md), p. 28–29). So screech may act through faster shear-layer growth (the Tan 2017 note's inference) or through noise from the coherent motion, which these models neglect (2019a, p. 17). Broadband statistics from time-resolved data, conditioned on instantaneous screech amplitude, would separate these.

**Is crackle radiated as shocks or formed in propagation?** [Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) leave it unresolved (p. 10). The LES evidence says both: shocklets leave the shear layer, and Tam et al. (2018) stress that propagation still steepens them ([Brès & Lele 2019](../Notes/bres-2019-modelling-jet-noise-les-perspective.md), p. 10), while the hot M 3.1 LES shows steep events at the source plus steepening to 40D ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 25). But that LES truncates shocks at St_c = 0.22 (p. 27), the Nichols account is second-hand, and neither is a mechanism study. Skewness tracked from near to far field, with Burgers propagation from near-field inputs as in [Gee et al. 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), on a heated lab jet with simultaneous schlieren would locate where shocks form.

## Methods and data
- **Far-field spectra** are the core data, and time-averaged PSDs suffice for F/G fits and BBSAN checks. Narrowband resolution is needed for multiple BBSAN peaks; plotting a_∞/(f_p D) against cos θ gives U_c/a_∞ and k₁ from an arc ([Tam et al. 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), p. 3, 8) and shows that U_c depends on the screech mode ([André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 669–670). For a screech-free reference, notch the lip and check the first cells with mean schlieren (André, p. 666–668).
- **Azimuthal decomposition** needs only two microphones, one traversed, with stationarity and azimuthal homogeneity over the traverse; it gives block-averaged spectra only ([Wong et al. 2020](../Notes/wong-2020-azimuthal-decomposition-radiated-noise-shock-containing-jets.md), App. C, p. 2024–2026), and its sinc fit may bias the modes towards equality (the note's reading).
- **Shock cells come from mean fields.** Non-time-resolved PIV is enough for a BBSAN shock model ([Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), p. 10–11), and mean static pressure follows from mean PIV ([Tan et al. 2019b](../Notes/tan-2019-equivalent-bbsan-source-reconstruction-screeching-jets.md), p. 17–18); in overexpanded jets measure the spacing rather than using Pack. Spacing inferred from a screech tone with a fixed M_c is just 1/f ([Kerechanin et al. 2001](../Notes/kerechanin-2001-trailing-edge-rectangular-jet-acoustics.md), p. 5).
- **Screech filtering of snapshot PIV** by POD subtraction needs ≥ 3000 snapshots, SNR > 500 and Δx/L < 0.1 for converged length scales, and is clean only when one in-plane pair carries the tone ([Tan et al. 2017](../Notes/tan-2017-broadband-velocity-fluctuations-screeching-jets.md), p. 2328, 2332). It gives no time scales or convection speeds.
- **Wavepackets, jitter and source–sound links need time-resolved data**: SPOD, two-point coherence (dual-plane TR PIV, [Jaunet et al. 2017](../Notes/jaunet-2017-two-point-coherence-wave-packets-turbulent-jets.md), p. 2–9) and flow–acoustic correlation (point Rayleigh scattering against a far-field microphone, [Tam et al. 2008](../Notes/tam-2008-sources-of-jet-noise-experimental-evidence.md), p. 278–280). PSE or resolvent need only a mean flow plus one amplitude per (m, ω). Higher SPOD modes, which carry the jitter, converge slowly ([Cavalieri et al. 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 29).
- **Schlieren of near-field waves.** Fronts outside a growing wave are not sound unless their speed reaches a_∞, so check the phase speed first ([Michalke 1984](../Notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), p. 176); a single-shot wavefront angle gives it ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 464–469).
- **LES far fields.** An FW-H surface recovers all four components and the tone frequency, but the heated rectangular LES got the screech amplitude trend with heating wrong ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 18–22). Blind LES of a C-D round jet gets OASPL within 1.5–2.5 dB, with a directivity tilt the authors attribute to the laminar exit layer, and needs averaged FW-H end-caps ([Brès et al. 2017](../Notes/bres-2017-unstructured-les-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1176–1179). A linear FW-H misses the waveform steepening that nonlinear Euler propagation captures ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), p. 25–27).
- **Full-scale sources** come from holography, partial fields and vector intensity, all on synchronous arrays ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 13–16).
- **Nonlinearity** is measured by skewness of p and ∂p/∂t (sampling-rate dependent), the wave steepening factor, spectral slopes and Burgers propagation, all from single-point pressure.

## Relevance to our work
- **Lab acoustics.** F + G fits split mixing noise by angle before modal analysis. Narrowband BBSAN peaks against angle, with shock spacing measured from mean schlieren rather than Prandtl–Pack, test Tam & Tanna on our nozzles. Check screech strength before attributing broadband changes to geometry: screech adds about 3 dB of broadband noise but lowers BBSAN and its peak frequency (André et al. 2013), so our screeching jets under-state screech-free BBSAN; a notched twin nozzle gives the reference.
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Streamwise transforms of SPOD modes isolate the radiating band |k| < ω/c_∞ (Mach waves) and the k_kh − k_s products (BBSAN; the same products close screech). Hilbert POD on a multi-mode reconstruction would show jitter, which Wong et al. could only treat statistically, as amplitude and phase modulation, and could track the shock oscillation they left unmodelled. Two-point coherence along the shear layer in TR schlieren, which line-of-sight integration weights towards low m, would give Jaunet-type wavepacket coherence lengths (Jaunet note). Subtracting an HPOD-tracked tonal field rather than a fixed POD pair would give broadband statistics conditioned on screech state (Tan 2017 note). These are suggestions, not results of these papers.
- **Group data.** Wong's shock model and Tan's statistical models use PIV of the same M_j 1.45 jet, so the two source locations can be compared on one jet, and the SJAF ring gives the azimuthal far field at nearby conditions (Wong et al. 2020).
- **PyFR staging LES.** At M_j ≈ 1.1, β and hence BBSAN are small. André's M_j 1.10 and 1.15 cases bracket A1/A2, so an FW-H surface could test whether the BBSAN peak near 90° sits at 2f_s and jumps between A1 and A2 (André note). Wong et al. 2020's NPR 2.25 case sits at the top of the PyFR range and needs 4–5 azimuthal modes, so a mode-by-mode comparison needs m resolved to at least 5 (Wong 2020 note). Gojon's untripped LES got the tone amplitude trend wrong and Brès et al.'s laminar exit layer tilted the directivity, so staging limits need a boundary-layer sensitivity check.
- **Rocket noise.** Lab jets are cold (κ 0.6–0.85) and show BBSAN and screech; rockets are hot (κ ≈ 0.31) and show neither. Lab mechanisms transfer through Mach-wave directivity, wavepacket source models and nonlinear propagation; a heated BBSAN model needs the entropy source (Ray et al. 2005) and a flight correction Michalke's stretching. Derivative skewness is a cheap step-1 statistic for heated or impinging cases.
