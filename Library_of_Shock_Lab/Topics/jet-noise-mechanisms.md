# Jet noise mechanisms

_Last updated: 2026-10-22 · 21 papers_

## Scope
How turbulent and shock-containing jets make broadband noise: mixing noise from large-scale structures (Mach wave radiation) and fine-scale turbulence, the similarity spectra that describe it, the instability-wave families behind Mach waves, broadband shock-associated noise (BBSAN) from Tam & Tanna's interaction model to wavepacket and statistical (RANS-informed) source models, how screech changes BBSAN, and the nonlinear steepening of high-amplitude jet noise into acoustic shocks (crackle). The last section says how these mechanisms carry over to rockets.

Left to other pages: the screech loop, shock leakage and the guided jet mode ([screech](screech.md)) and screech staging ([screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md)); PSE and resolvent operators in their own right ([linear-operator-methods](linear-operator-methods.md)); Floquet and wavepacket models ([linear-models](linear-models.md)); shock-cell models and the shock side of BBSAN sources ([shock-cell-models](shock-cell-models.md)); SP-8072, SATURN and other empirical rocket models ([rocket-noise-models](rocket-noise-models.md)); full-scale launch data and long-range propagation ([rocket-noise-measurements](rocket-noise-measurements.md)); impinging-jet tones ([impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md)); rectangular and elliptical jets ([non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md)); twin jets ([twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md)); SPOD practice ([modal-decomposition](modal-decomposition.md)).

**Angle conventions.** Here θ is measured from the downstream jet axis. Tam et al. 1996 (χ), Norum & Seiner 1982 (ψ) and Gojon et al. 2019 measure from the inlet axis, so θ = 180° − χ. The sign in the BBSAN peak-frequency formula flips with the convention ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) note, eqs. 10–11).

**Convective Mach number.** Papamoschou & Roshko's M_c is the flow Mach number in the frame of the large structures, U_j/(a_j + a_∞) for a jet into still air. Most BBSAN and wavepacket papers, and Michalke's c_ph/a_∞ ([Michalke 1984](../Notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), p. 174), use instead the eddy Mach number U_c/a_∞ with a measured or assumed U_c. The two coincide only for U_c = u_j/(1 + a_j/a_∞) ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 472).

## Current understanding
1. **Three components, four in hot jets.** An imperfectly expanded jet radiates mixing noise (dominant downstream), BBSAN (sideline and upstream) and screech tones ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 18); a heated rectangular LES adds Mach waves at TR ≥ 2 ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 19–22). A tab removes the tones and leaves the BBSAN hump ([Norum & Seiner 1982](../Notes/norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md), App. E p. 149, 162), but it also shortens the shock spacing; a notched lip that keeps the first cells is the cleaner screech-free reference ([André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 665, 668).
2. **Mixing noise has two parts:** Mach waves from large-scale structures, which behave statistically as instability waves (wavepackets), and flatter sideline noise from fine-scale turbulence. Two empirical similarity spectra, F and G, fit both ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 19–20; [Tam et al. 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), p. 3–5).
3. **The radiating part of the turbulence is low-energy, low-order and linear.** Linear models on the mean flow reproduce the m = 0 and 1 wavepackets that carry the sound ([Cavalieri et al. 2013](../Notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), p. 566–585), and resolvent modes reproduce the Mach waves seen in SPOD of an M_j 1.5 LES ([Schmidt et al. 2018](../Notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md), p. 21–23).
4. **Mach wave radiation is acoustic matching**: only supersonic-phase-speed wavenumbers radiate, the peak is at θ = cos⁻¹(1/M_c), and jitter matters far less for supersonic than for subsonic jets ([Cavalieri et al. 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 11–16). The most amplified supersonic instability wave travels at u_j/(1 + a_j/a_∞), which is Papamoschou & Roshko's convective speed ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 472); M_c also governs shear-layer growth ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../Notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md), p. 19–20). In LES, Mach waves appear once U_c/a_∞ exceeds 1 ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 16, 20–21).
5. **BBSAN is a difference-wavenumber Mach wave** from instability waves interacting with the shock cells, with one spectral peak per shock-cell mode ([Tam & Tanna 1982](../Notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md), p. 8–11; [Tam et al. 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), p. 3–6). A linearised-Euler computation confirms that only k − k_s radiates upstream, at its local Mach angle ([Ray et al. 2005](../Notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md), p. 4–5, 9). An inside-out wavepacket model predicts the peaks within ±2 dB/St ([Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), p. 20–23), and the peak-frequency relation holds at full scale with measured shock spacing ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 36, 40). Screech lies at its upstream limit in strongly underexpanded jets ([Tam et al. 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), p. 7–8), but apparently not at M_j 1.10–1.35 (the [André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md) note, from p. 669–670).
6. **Screech weakens BBSAN.** A screech-free twin keeps a longer mean shock train, and its BBSAN is up to 4 dB louder and higher in frequency; in the screeching jet the peak follows 2St_s through each stage jump ([André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 667–671). This is the rapid shock decay that shock-cell solvers missed ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 32).
7. **Two routes to BBSAN sources disagree on where they are.** Wavepacket × shock-cell models ([Ray et al. 2005](../Notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md); [Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md)) and statistical acoustic analogies calibrated with screech-filtered PIV ([Tan et al. 2019a](../Notes/tan-2019-correlation-analysis-piv-screeching-jets.md); [Tan et al. 2019b](../Notes/tan-2019-equivalent-bbsan-source-reconstruction-screeching-jets.md)) both run on mean flows, but on the same jet they put the source inside the plume and on the sonic line respectively.
8. **High-amplitude jet noise propagates nonlinearly**, steepening into acoustic shocks that keep high-frequency energy linear absorption would remove, while OASPL barely changes ([Gee et al. 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 9–12; [Gee et al. 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 8).
9. **At rocket scale** Mach waves dominate, BBSAN and screech are absent, and the lab calibrations (similarity spectra, κ, peak St) do not transfer ([Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 9–15).

## Mixing noise: two sources and similarity spectra
Tam's stochastic model treats the large structures as instability waves seeded at the lip, with one free constant. Fitted, it matches Mach 2 hot-jet directivities within about 2.5 dB, and the constant implies a large-structure u_rms of only 1.2–1.5% of U_j at the exit ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 25–30). The 1996 paper is purely empirical. F (sharp, +18.4/−27.7 dB per decade) and G (broad) fit 1,900 Langley spectra by shape ([Tam et al. 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), p. 5):
- Large-structure noise dominates for χ > 125° and fine-scale noise for χ < 110° in a Mach 2 jet (p. 3).
- Peak levels collapse on V_j/a_∞ and T_r/T_∞, not M_j. The velocity exponents are about 9.5 (large scale) and 7.6 (fine scale) for cold jets; heating to T_r/T_∞ = 2 at fixed V_j lowers them by 8.6 and 6.7 dB (p. 6).
- Shock cells barely change F and G once BBSAN is set aside (p. 7–8). That is the assumption Tam & Tanna needed to subtract mixing noise ([Tam & Tanna 1982](../Notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md), p. 17).
- The same spectra fit an M_j 0.98 jet and pilot AR 3 and AR 7.6 non-axisymmetric nozzles (p. 9–11).

F and G cover almost the same frequencies, so "large scales make low frequencies" is wrong (p. 5). The authors also infer, from spectral shape alone, two distinct kinds of turbulence with no cascade between them, and reject a Lighthill single-quadrupole source (p. 10–11). The lab baseline in [Norum & Seiner 1982](../Notes/norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md) is a maximum OASPL 22.5–30° from the jet axis, up to 139.1 dB, with near-design C-D jets broadband only (p. 50, 175, 190).

**Wavepackets as the large-scale source.** Cavalieri et al. explain why low-energy structures dominate. Lipline velocity energy peaks at m ≈ 4–11, but high m radiate weakly at low St because the source is radially compact; the m = 0 velocity–pressure correlation is about 10% ([Cavalieri et al. 2013](../Notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), p. 566–571). Their review turns this into a line-source model ([Cavalieri et al. 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md)):
- A Gaussian wavepacket gives superdirectivity for subsonic M_c (matching measured m = 0 data) and Mach waves for supersonic M_c (p. 10–13).
- Coherence decay (jitter) adds about 30 dB at M 0.6 but barely changes the M 2 peak (p. 15). Subsonic jets need three SPOD modes for their sound; supersonic jets need one (p. 16–18).
- At supersonic Mach numbers the first resolvent gain separates as a Mach-wave mode (p. 26, 28).

PSE wavepackets match the leading SPOD mode of an M_j 1.5 LES, Mach waves included ([Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), p. 17). Tam's "instability waves" and the modern "wavepackets" are the same object; SPOD and resolvent analysis now show it directly.

**Statistical source inputs.** Acoustic-analogy models of fine-scale noise need fourth-order turbulence scales. Screech-filtered PIV of the group's C-mode jets gives c_L1111 ≈ 0.37–0.39, matching Karabasov et al.'s LES fit of 0.37, and amplitude ratios R₂₂₂₂/R₁₁₁₁ ≈ 0.15–0.17 and R₁₂₁₂/R₁₁₁₁ ≈ 0.22–0.24 that agree with hot-wire data but not with that LES ([Tan et al. 2019a](../Notes/tan-2019-correlation-analysis-piv-screeching-jets.md), Table 5, p. 27, 30).

**Control evidence.** Forcing m = ±1 at St_DF ≈ 0.3 adds about 4–5 dB, while forcing at high St_DF or high m cuts about 0.6–1 dB ([Samimy et al. 2006](../Notes/samimy-2006-plasma-actuators-high-speed-jet-flow-noise-control.md), p. 19). That is the radial-compactness argument used as a design rule. Trailing-edge cutouts cut 30° noise from an underexpanded AR 3 jet by up to 12 dB through faster mixing ([Kerechanin et al. 2001](../Notes/kerechanin-2001-trailing-edge-rectangular-jet-acoustics.md), p. 3).

## Mach wave radiation and the convective Mach number
Papamoschou & Roshko defined M_c in the frame of the large structures ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../Notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md)):
- Normalised growth starts falling at M_c ≈ 0.25 and levels at about 0.2 by M_c ≈ 0.6–0.8 (p. 19–20).
- Large structures persist up to M_c ≈ 1.8 (p. 8).
- A vortex sheet stabilises above a critical M_c, but finite-thickness layers do not (p. 14–15). They read the growth reduction as energy radiated away (p. 21–22).

**Three instability families.** A high-speed jet carries KH waves, supersonic instability waves (Mach waves trapped between its boundaries) and subsonic waves with no near field ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 447, 452–457). Supersonic waves exist only for u_j > a_j + a_∞ (eq. 2.13, p. 456), M_j > 2.5 in cold jets (the note's calculation), and their most amplified member travels at u_j/(1 + a_j/a_∞) for any mode, M_j or thickness up to b/R_j ≈ 0.3 (eq. 5.1, p. 472, 480). That is the theory behind the Papamoschou–Roshko speed, as Tam & Hu note (p. 472). Above a critical M_j that falls with temperature (≈ 2.8 at T_j/T_∞ = 1, ≈ 2.0 at 3), the KH wave merges into the supersonic family (Fig. 9, p. 459). The upstream subsonic waves are the guided jet mode ([screech](screech.md)).

**Direction.** The peak noise direction follows the Mach angle of the most amplified instability wave. Its growth and decay broaden the wavenumber spectrum, so even subsonic-phase-speed waves radiate a little ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 27–28; [Tam et al. 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), p. 8–9). Computed KH wavefront angles match shadowgraphs, ≈ 47–52° in nitrogen jets at M_j 1.5–3.0 and ≈ 31–37° in helium ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), Figs 19–20, p. 467–469); their claim that supersonic-wave fronts lie near normal to the jet (p. 464) holds only just above threshold (the note's check). Cavalieri et al. give the closed form, PSD ∝ exp[−½k_h²L²(1 − M_c cos θ)²], with M_c ≈ 0.6M for cold lab jets ([Cavalieri et al. 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 9, 11). The heated rectangular LES shows the threshold directly: no Mach waves at U_c/a_∞ = 0.94 (cold), Mach waves at 1.21–1.29, peaking about 40° from the downstream axis against 37° from Oertel's formula, and much stronger in the flapping plane ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), Table 2, p. 16, 20–21).

**Near-field fronts are not yet sound.** Outside a growing wave the pressure fronts are oblique, with speed c_w = a_∞M_c cos μ. It reaches a_∞ only for neutral waves with M_c > 1, so growing waves give visible schlieren fronts, tilted by up to 30–45° at M = 1.2, that Michalke declines to call sound ([Michalke 1984](../Notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), eqs. 28–31, p. 175–176). Their radiated part needs the uniformly valid analysis of Tam & Morris (1980) (p. 192), i.e. the envelope-driven radiation above.

**Heating** broadens the dominant sector and adds low-St noise ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 20). It destabilises the KH wave in spatial theory (peak −α_iθ 0.14 against 0.113 at T_o/T_∞ = 2, for M = 0 and laminar profiles only; [Michalke 1984](../Notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), Fig. 19, p. 177–178), and the supersonic waves too ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 480). Measured U_c/u_j falls with heating, from 0.80 to 0.64 over TR 1–3 in the rectangular LES, as Liu et al. found in round jets ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 16). For rockets, M_c = κU_e/c_a needs κ ≈ 0.31–0.32, against 0.6–0.85 for lab jets ([Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 14–15; [Mathews et al. 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 627).

## BBSAN: mechanism, models and the effect of screech
**Mechanism (1982).** Tam & Tanna replaced Harper-Bourne & Fisher's point-source array with the product of an instability wave and Pack shock cells ([Tam & Tanna 1982](../Notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md)):
- Only k − λ₁ radiates effectively. The k + λ_n waves are subsonic, and k − λ₂ is 7.2 dB weaker for a uniform train (p. 9).
- On an M_d 1.672 C-D nozzle, f_p = u_c/[L_s(1 − M_c cos θ)] with u_c = 0.7u_j lands within one third-octave band at 75–135° (p. 16–18).
- Under- and overexpanded jets fall on one (M_j² − M_d²)² line; cold jets are up to 3 dB quieter (p. 17–20).
- At design the nozzle is about 9 dB quieter than a convergent one at 120° (p. 12–13), matching the dip at design in [Norum & Seiner 1982](../Notes/norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md) (p. 37, 40).

The authors called the model "still quite tentative"; it gives no absolute level or spectral shape (p. 21).

**Multiple peaks (1986) and stochastic theory (1995).** Narrowband spectra show second and third peaks, set by the shock-cell Fourier wavenumbers rather than harmonics; one-third-octave bands hide them ([Tam et al. 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), p. 3–5). The peak narrows upstream as (1 − M_c cos θ)⁻², the basis of the "weakest link" screech argument (p. 6–10; see [screech](screech.md)). Tam's four-parameter stochastic theory reaches engineering accuracy for M_j and M_d of 1–2, but misses dips at small inlet angles ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md), p. 34–36). Linear shock cells with an empirical initial amplitude suffice there, but shock-cell solvers missed the rapid shock decay of strongly screeching jets (p. 32).

**Shock leakage, a local view (2003).** A steady shear layer never lets a weak shock out, since the Mach angle always exceeds the critical angle for total internal reflection; unsteady vortices let it leak near the saddles between them ([Suzuki & Lele 2003](../Notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md), p. 145–149). In a 2-D DNS of an M 1.2 layer the leaked jump is up to about 0.1 of the incident one and radiates broadly sideline, tilting from about 84° to 74° with heating while its total amplitude falls (Figs 24–27, p. 162–163). Downstream, smaller eddies destroy the saddles and turbulence scatters the thin fronts into broadband shock noise (p. 164). It is the localised, sharp-front form of the difference-wave picture (the note's reading).

**First-principles linear computation (2005).** [Ray et al. 2005](../Notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md) compute the product rather than postulate it: PSE m = 0 waves times multiple-scales shock modes on a RANS mean drive the linearised Euler equations of an overexpanded M_j 1.28 jet (M_d 1.5, β ≈ 0.78) at T_j/T_∞ = 1 and 2 (p. 2–5).
- In a Gaussian model only k_t − k_s radiates upstream, at θ_M = cos⁻¹(1/c_s1), reproducing Harper-Bourne & Fisher; a longer envelope makes it louder and narrower (p. 4–5). That is Cavalieri's superdirectivity form with the difference wavenumber, and it confirms Tam & Tanna's k − λ₁.
- At St 0.6 the upstream lobe (≈ 100–140°) is the fan of local Mach angles over the centreline source region, x/D 2–6 (Fig. 7, p. 9). At St 0.9 the sources become rings at r/D ≈ 0.5 whose interference splits the sound into narrow lobes (p. 12–13).
- The second shock mode radiates only at St 0.9 (p. 9); across frequency it sets the n = 2 peak that [Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md) found missing from Ray & Lele 2007 (p. 23, 29).
- Heating leaves the shock modes unchanged and moves the lobe about 15° upstream (p. 10; sign of the printed shift doubtful). The entropy source is under 5% isothermal but about 20% at T_j/T_∞ = 2 (p. 12), so Wong's neglect of it holds for cold jets only.
- The runs are partly unconverged and there is no comparison with measured sound (p. 9–10, 13). The shock side is on [shock-cell-models](shock-cell-models.md).

**Screech lowers BBSAN (2013).** [André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md) compare a smooth and a 24-notch convergent nozzle with the same first shock cells, shock strength and M_j 1.0 mixing noise (p. 667–668):
- Without screech the mean shock train lasts much longer (to x/D ≈ 11 against collapse near 5.3 at M_j 1.35; Fig. 5, p. 667–668), so screech itself damps the cells.
- The screech-free BBSAN is louder by ≈ 2.5–4 dB at M_j 1.10, 0–2 dB at 1.15 and 0–2.5 dB at 1.35, equal at 1.50 where screech is weak (Fig. 13, p. 671), and peaks higher (Sr 0.76 against 0.65 at M_j 1.35; p. 669).
- With screech the 90° peak follows 2St_s through every stage jump, and U_c from the λ_p–cos θ slope is 0.42–0.65U_j depending on the mode, against 0.65–0.66U_j at every M_j without it (Fig. 8, Table 1, p. 669–670). Tam & Tanna's u_c ≈ 0.7u_j fits non-screeching jets.
- Near-field microphones put the strongest, highest-frequency sources at the downstream cells (peak near x/D 4.5–5; local Sr_p rising from ≈ 1 to ≈ 2.4–2.6), which screech removes (Figs 11–12, p. 670–671).
- The note's arithmetic from figure readings (f_p(90°) ≈ 2f_s; the λ_p line reaching ≈ 2.3D at cos θ = −1 against λ_s ≈ 2.8D) says screech is not the BBSAN upstream limit at these M_j. Tam et al. showed that limit only at M_j 1.8 and 2.24 ([Tam et al. 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), p. 8), so this restricts rather than refutes it.

**Statistical source models (2017–2019).** The group's other route is the RANS-informed acoustic analogy of Morris & Miller (2010) and Kalyan & Karabasov (2017), driven by turbulence scales rather than wavepackets ([Tan et al. 2019a](../Notes/tan-2019-correlation-analysis-piv-screeching-jets.md), p. 4–13).
- *Screech-free turbulence.* Subtracting the leading POD modes of 8500 snapshot PIV fields removes the tone: two modes for the helical C mode, 18 for the precessing B mode ([Tan et al. 2017](../Notes/tan-2017-broadband-velocity-fluctuations-screeching-jets.md), p. 2325–2328). The broadband scales then settle to L⁽¹⁾_uu/δθ ≈ 2.1 and L⁽¹⁾_vv/δθ ≈ L⁽²⁾_uu/δθ ≈ 1.0 at M_j 1.25–1.59, the non-screeching values of Fleury et al. and mostly of André et al.'s notched jets, while total fluctuations overestimate them by 15–140% (Table 3, p. 2331–2332).
- *Coefficients.* On the lip line of the C-mode jets (M_j 1.45, 1.59) the random-field scales follow k^{3/2}/ε or k^{1/2}D_j/V̄₁ with coefficients that differ by 0–9.5% between M_j, against up to 30% for the shock-modulated total field (2019a, Tables 3–4, p. 26–29). The anisotropy of the axial-velocity scales is ≈ 0.5, not the 0.3 of earlier BBSAN predictions (p. 29), and the transverse-velocity scales are nearly isotropic ([Tan et al. 2019b](../Notes/tan-2019-equivalent-bbsan-source-reconstruction-screeching-jets.md), p. 24). The shock time scale stays secondary on the lip line, so the mixed-scale model reduces to Morris & Miller there (2019a, p. 33; 2019b, p. 20–21). The D_j/V̄₁ form avoids ε, which planar PIV resolves poorly, and is closed (2019a, p. 13, 34–35).
- *Source maps.* With k–ω SST RANS, the equivalent sources at Sr = 1 sit on the sonic line just upstream of each shock reflection, mainly in cells 1–5 and strongest at the second, with PIV- and RANS-based maps within about 2% on the lip line; this matches Savarese et al. 2013 and Kalyan & Karabasov 2017 (2019b, p. 7, 36–40). PIV gives no integral time scale, so no noise is predicted and all agreement is internal (p. 7).
- *Against far-field fits.* Morris & Miller's tuned streamwise coefficients (3.0–3.25) are about ten times the PIV-fitted ones (0.28, 0.43) (2019b, Tables 5–6, p. 24–25), which they had put down to large structures coherent over long axial distances (2019a, p. 12).
- *Caveats (the notes).* The "below 3%" RMSE sits oddly with ±13–23% coefficient scatter (2019a); Sr = 1 is about twice the sideline fundamental (St ≈ 0.5–0.55), so the maps may weight the second shock-cell harmonic (2019b); all are cold jets.

**Inside-out wavepacket model (2021).** [Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md) built the source from flow data only. Wavepackets come from an ideally expanded M_j 1.5 LES or PSE on its mean flow. Shock cells are Pack–Prandtl modes corrected with mean PIV of a screeching M_j 1.45 jet from a Shock Lab group facility, the jet Tan et al. also used (k_s,mod = 0.79 k_s + 1.02) (p. 6, 8–14). The results:
- m = 0–2 peaks match azimuthally decomposed measurements within ±2 dB/St; the n = 2 peak comes from the second shock-cell mode (p. 20–23, 29).
- Perfectly coherent PSE leaves dips up to 20 dB/St deep between peaks; imposing the LES coherence (jitter) recovers up to 15 dB/St (p. 24–25). This is the shock-noise counterpart of the subsonic jitter result: k − k_s components can be subsonic even in a supersonic jet.
- The source sits inside the plume, two lobes per shock cell, strongest at cells 3–5 (2D–8D), and moves upstream as frequency rises (p. 27).
- Peak frequency is under-predicted at 115–125°, St_p is 0.6 against 0.55 measured near 80° with a screech harmonic on the peak, and nothing is predicted below St ≈ 0.4, where mixing noise was excluded (p. 20, 23).

The shocks are steady and the wavepackets come from a shock-free jet, as in Ray et al. Shock modulation lengthens the envelope that shock-free PSE predicts to decay too fast ([Nogueira et al. 2022](../Notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 18–19), consistent with Wong's PSE falling below the LES beyond x ≈ 6D (p. 17).

**Full scale (2025).** Holography of an afterburning F404 (M_j 1.46, M_d 1.55, TTR 7, overexpanded) gives the following ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md)):
- BSN sources sit at shock cells II–VIII; cell I is silent and VI–VII are loudest (p. 32).
- Spacing shrinks from about 0.97 to 0.72 D_j along the train. The spacing formulas all fail for overexpanded C-D jets, yet the Harper-Bourne–Fisher peak frequency agrees "excellently" with the measured spacing (p. 34, 36).
- Each shock source is coherent with both forward BSN and aft Mach waves, and coherence islands between shocks show one wavepacket meeting several shocks (p. 38–40). This matches Wong's distributed, partially coherent source (Wong p. 28–29).

**Non-round jets.** On an overexpanded AR 3 jet, symmetric cutouts raise BBSAN along with stronger screech ([Kerechanin et al. 2001](../Notes/kerechanin-2001-trailing-edge-rectangular-jet-acoustics.md), p. 4–5), against "screech lowers BBSAN", but the cutouts change the shocks strongly, so it is no clean test (André note). In the heated AR 2 LES the hump (20–120° from the downstream axis) follows Harper-Bourne & Fisher only with an ad hoc shock length of 0.8L_model, and the first screech harmonic sits on it near 90°, dominating for TR ≥ 2 ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 20–21). The match is qualitative; the printed upstream limits do not reproduce (Gojon note).

## Crackle and nonlinear steepening
The library holds no laboratory study of how crackle is generated in heated supersonic jets (Ffowcs Williams et al. 1975 and later shocklet work are not indexed; not in the library). It holds the propagation side:
- **F-22A run-ups.** At 90% rpm and afterburner, a generalised Burgers model from 23 m matches 305 m spectra within 2–7 dB to 20 kHz; the linear model is 120–140 dB low at 20 kHz ([Gee et al. 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), p. 9–10). Nonlinearity spans 90–145°, contradicting an earlier F/A-18E bispectral result at 90° (p. 11). OASPL and loudness metrics barely change (p. 12).
- **Rocket waveforms.** Rocket noise is positively skewed near the peak direction (Sk{p} ≈ 0.6–0.75), and Sk{∂p/∂t} exceeds 30 and grows with distance ([Gee et al. 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 7–8). At 2.5 km from an SLS booster firing it is still 21–32 at 80–100° ([Reichman et al. 2018](../Notes/reichman-2018-qm2-srm-far-field-acoustics.md), p. 8–9).
- **Spectra and losses.** Launch waveforms are shock-dominated, with a weak-shock 10 dB/decade roll-off to 10 kHz ([Kellison et al. 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 5–6). Over 7 km, Atlas V loses about 15 dB at 1 kHz against 26 dB predicted by linear absorption ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 84–85).

The source link is indirect. Gee et al. propose that plume impingement breaks up the Mach-wave-radiating structures, which would explain a downstream zone of unskewed, low-frequency noise; this is untested ([Gee et al. 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 10). In the lab, upstream waves from impinging jets are shocks at birth (M_s ≈ 1.07) or coalesce within 10D, too soon for propagation steepening ([Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2025](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md), p. 3, 10–11). Leaked shock fronts are already nonlinear at the source: the compression branch keeps its strength while the expansion branch spreads, and finite-amplitude fronts run ahead of the linear eikonal ones ([Suzuki & Lele 2003](../Notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md), p. 159).

## Carry-over to rocket noise
Details are on [rocket-noise-models](rocket-noise-models.md) and [rocket-noise-measurements](rocket-noise-measurements.md); the mechanism-level points are these.
- **Mach waves dominate**, with sound power ∝ U_e³ and η ≈ 0.3–0.5% ([Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 7–9; [Kellison et al. 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 14). Near-field intensity vectors of a solid motor fit the two-source picture, but only by inspection ([Gee et al. 2010](../Notes/gee-2010-near-field-vector-intensity-solid-rocket-motor.md), p. 6–7). Rocket plumes are past the critical M_j at which KH merges into the supersonic family, so the supersonic waves should be the dominant instability (the [Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md) note's inference from Fig. 9, p. 459; vortex sheet, equal γ).
- **The similarity spectra fit poorly.** F fits only the RSRM 45° peak ([Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 10), and a modified F with f^2.5/f^−2.2 slopes is used instead ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md), p. 377). The 1996 fits stop at T_r/T_∞ ≈ 4.9 and include no nonlinear propagation.
- **Peak Strouhal number falls about tenfold**, to Sr ≈ 0.01–0.025 against about 0.2, for reasons "still unclear" ([Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 10; [Kellison et al. 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md), p. 15–16). The F404 at TTR 7 already puts much energy below Sr 0.1 ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 16). Flight lowers it further: Michalke's stretching σ = 1 + 1.4U_∞/ΔU collapses growth rates and measured pressure spectra of jets in flight ([Michalke 1984](../Notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), Fig. 26, p. 178–182), and the flight model built on it gives a ~23% downward shift for an ascending Falcon 9 ([Mathews et al. 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md), p. 629).
- **No BBSAN or screech** at sideline and forward angles, perhaps because BBSAN saturates with temperature or is buried under Mach waves ([Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 9). Yet the afterburning F404 shows BSN clearly ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 6, 32).
- **Non-axisymmetric exhausts** can have azimuth-dependent Mach-wave directivity, which axisymmetric SP-8072-type models ignore (the [Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md) note's inference from p. 16).
- **Installation:** reflected free-jet Mach waves in the flame path, not deflector impingement, set Epsilon fairing levels at H/D_e = 14 ([Tsutsumi et al. 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md), p. 15–16).

## Disagreements and open questions
**Two sources or one jittering wavepacket?** [Tam et al. 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md) infer two kinds of turbulence from spectral shape alone (p. 10–11). [Cavalieri et al. 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md) show that jitter spreads wavepacket radiation to wider angles (p. 14–16), so part of the fitted "fine-scale" noise might be jittering wavepackets. Projecting measured sideline spectra onto a few SPOD or coupled-resolvent modes of the same jet would test how much of G they recover.

**Which convective Mach number sets the rocket peak angle?** The candidates disagree:
- The Oertel M_CO is favoured by [Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) (p. 13–15) and [Mathews et al. 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md) (p. 625–627), but Falcon 9's 64° matches it only after subtracting the vehicle Mach number.
- [Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md) revise that angle to 71° after a 3-D geometry fix (p. 375, 378).
- [Kellison et al. 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md) measure 64° again and prefer the "supersonic" Tam & Hu M_c (66°) to Oertel (69°) (p. 11–13).

Theory favours the supersonic speed at rocket conditions, where no separate KH wave exists ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 459, 472). Kellison's KH and "subsonic" candidates are Oertel's empirical speeds, not Tam & Hu's, and Tam & Hu's subsonic waves have no near field to radiate Mach waves (Tam & Hu note; p. 456, 465). What the new families do to spreading and the spectrum is open (p. 481). Ray-traced near-pad arrays, plus wavenumber-filtered SPOD of heated-jet LES to see which family radiates, would decide it.

**What fills the BBSAN dips?** Tam (1987, as cited by Wong) blamed shock unsteadiness; [Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md) attribute most of it to jitter (p. 25). In screeching jets the shocks themselves oscillate at the tone ([André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 672). Time-resolved schlieren of screeching and screech-free jets at the same β, or a model with oscillating shocks, would separate the two.

**Where are the BBSAN sources?** The answers split. Inside the plume or towards the end of the core: cells 3–5 ([Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), p. 27), the centreline at x/D 2–6 at St 0.6 ([Ray et al. 2005](../Notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md), p. 9), the downstream cells near x/D 4.5–5, longer without screech ([André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 670–671), and cells VI–VII at full scale ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 32). On the sonic line in the first cells, strongest at the second reflection ([Tan et al. 2019b](../Notes/tan-2019-equivalent-bbsan-source-reconstruction-screeching-jets.md), p. 36–38), whose authors blame refraction and tabs for the end-of-core results (p. 38). Three factors separate them:
- *Model structure:* Tan's source is local shock pressure times local turbulence, so it can only sit where both overlap; Wong's wavepacket reaches into the core (Tan 2019b note).
- *Frequency:* sources move upstream or off axis as frequency rises in Wong (p. 27) and Ray (p. 12), yet André's near-field peak frequency rises downstream. Cells that shrink downstream (Mathews p. 34; 5.5% per cell in [Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 15) would raise the local peak there, an untested reconciliation; and Tan's Sr = 1 may be the second harmonic.
- *Screech:* Wong's shock cells and Tan's turbulence come from the same screeching jet, and screech removes the downstream cells (André, p. 668–670). Screech itself is generated between cells 3 and 4 ([Tam et al. 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), p. 8), and leakage puts tonal sources at cells 2–4 and broadband ones further on, qualitatively ([Suzuki & Lele 2003](../Notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md), p. 164).

One rig with a notched screech-free twin, a β and TTR sweep, mean PIV and a near-field array, giving frequency-resolved maps for both models, would settle it.

**Statistical or wavepacket sources?** Far-field fits need streamwise length coefficients ten times the PIV-measured ones and anisotropy 0.3 against 0.5 ([Tan et al. 2019a](../Notes/tan-2019-correlation-analysis-piv-screeching-jets.md), p. 12, 29; [Tan et al. 2019b](../Notes/tan-2019-equivalent-bbsan-source-reconstruction-screeching-jets.md), p. 24–25). If that long axial coherence is the coherent wavepacket, the gap is the statistical model's version of the wavepacket view (our reading). Far-field spectra from the PIV-calibrated model, with time scales from time-resolved data, set against Wong's prediction for the same jet would decide.

**Does screech tune BBSAN through the mean flow or its harmonic?** The screeching-jet peak follows 2St_s ([André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 669), a harmonic sits on Wong's measured peak (p. 23), and the first harmonic dominates the hot rectangular LES's hump near 90° ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 20). André et al. invoke staging-driven changes in spacing or U_c (p. 669); the André note reads it as the harmonic's KH wavenumber matching k_s, radiating sideways. Bicoherence or BMD at (St_s, St_s) would separate the two.

**Why is BBSAN absent from rockets** when the F404 at TTR 7 shows it? The models give no reason for it to vanish with heat: heating leaves the shock modes unchanged and raises the entropy source to about 20% at T_j/T_∞ = 2 ([Ray et al. 2005](../Notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md), p. 10, 12); hot plumes leak shocks more easily, and turbulence still scatters them into broadband noise ([Suzuki & Lele 2003](../Notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md), p. 147, 164); and the absence of screech raises lab BBSAN ([André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 671). Saturation is thus unsupported up to T_j/T_∞ = 2, which leaves masking by Mach waves. Forward-arc measurements of strongly overexpanded motors and high-TTR lab jets with β sweeps would test both.

**Shock spacing in overexpanded C-D jets** has no working formula ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 34–35). Wong's correction is fitted to one jet, and Tam & Tanna's uniform-train assumption ([Tam & Tanna 1982](../Notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md), p. 9) is contradicted by trains that shrink downstream. Multiple-scales modes on a RANS mean reproduce Norum & Seiner's mildly overexpanded (β ≈ 0.78) train for about five cells ([Ray et al. 2005](../Notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md), p. 6–7), so they are the candidate, untested at strong overexpansion, heat or a Mach disk. For an AR 2 C-D nozzle Tam's first-cell length is within 1–5% of LES ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 15).

**How does screech raise broadband noise?** Strong screech adds about 3 dB ([Tam et al. 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), p. 8), and screeching jets are 2–4 dB louder below Sr 0.6 than their notched twins (André Fig. 2, p. 667, the note's reading), which would make André's BBSAN reduction a lower bound (André note). Yet the filtered broadband length scales, relative to the screeching jet's own δθ, match non-screeching jets ([Tan et al. 2017](../Notes/tan-2017-broadband-velocity-fluctuations-screeching-jets.md), p. 2331–2332), and the model coefficients do not change with screech strength ([Tan et al. 2019a](../Notes/tan-2019-correlation-analysis-piv-screeching-jets.md), p. 28–29). So screech may act through faster shear-layer growth (the Tan 2017 note's inference) or through noise from the coherent motion, which these models neglect (2019a, p. 17); Tan 2019b only assumes the amplification stays in the filtered field (p. 20). Broadband statistics from time-resolved data, conditioned on instantaneous screech amplitude, would separate these.

**Is crackle radiated as shocks or formed in propagation?** [Lubert et al. 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) leave it unresolved (p. 10). Skewness tracked from near to far field, with Burgers propagation from near-field inputs as in [Gee et al. 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md), on a heated lab jet with simultaneous schlieren would locate where shocks form.

## Methods and data
- **Far-field spectra** are the core data, and time-averaged PSDs suffice for F/G fits and BBSAN checks. Narrowband resolution is needed for multiple BBSAN peaks; plotting a_∞/(f_p D) against cos θ gives U_c/a_∞ and k₁ from an arc ([Tam et al. 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), p. 3, 8), and shows that U_c depends on the screech mode ([André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), p. 669–670). Azimuthal decomposition needs only two microphones, one traversed ([Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), p. 8). For a screech-free reference, notch the lip and check the first cells with mean schlieren and the M_j 1.0 spectra (André, p. 666–668).
- **Shock cells come from mean fields.** Non-time-resolved PIV is enough for a BBSAN shock model (Wong p. 10–11), and mean static pressure follows from mean PIV ([Tan et al. 2019b](../Notes/tan-2019-equivalent-bbsan-source-reconstruction-screeching-jets.md), p. 17–18). Spacing inferred from a screech tone with a fixed M_c is just 1/f ([Kerechanin et al. 2001](../Notes/kerechanin-2001-trailing-edge-rectangular-jet-acoustics.md), p. 5).
- **Screech filtering of snapshot PIV** by POD subtraction needs ≥ 3000 snapshots, SNR > 500 and Δx/L < 0.1 for converged length scales, and is clean only when one in-plane pair carries the tone ([Tan et al. 2017](../Notes/tan-2017-broadband-velocity-fluctuations-screeching-jets.md), p. 2328, 2332). It gives no time scales or convection speeds.
- **Wavepackets and jitter need time-resolved data**: SPOD, two-point coherence and flow–acoustic correlation. PSE or resolvent need only a mean flow plus one amplitude per (m, ω). Higher SPOD modes, which carry the jitter, converge slowly ([Cavalieri et al. 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 29).
- **Schlieren of near-field waves.** Fronts outside a growing wave are not sound unless their speed reaches a_∞, so check the phase speed first ([Michalke 1984](../Notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), p. 176); a single-shot wavefront angle gives it ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 464–469).
- **LES far fields.** An FW-H surface recovers all four components and the tone frequency, but the heated rectangular LES got the screech amplitude trend with heating wrong ([Gojon et al. 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 18–22).
- **Full-scale sources** come from holography, partial fields and vector intensity, all on synchronous arrays ([Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 13–16).
- **Nonlinearity** is measured by skewness of p and ∂p/∂t (sampling-rate dependent), spectral slopes and Burgers propagation, all from single-point pressure.

## Relevance to our work
- **Lab acoustics.** F + G fits split mixing noise by angle before modal analysis. Narrowband BBSAN peaks against angle, with shock spacing measured from mean schlieren rather than Prandtl–Pack, test Tam & Tanna on our nozzles. Check screech strength before attributing broadband changes to geometry: screech adds about 3 dB of broadband noise but lowers BBSAN and its peak frequency and makes its U_c mode-dependent (André et al. 2013). Our screeching jets therefore under-state screech-free BBSAN; a notched twin nozzle gives the reference.
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Streamwise transforms of SPOD modes isolate the radiating band |k| < ω/c_∞ (Mach waves) and the k_kh − k_s products (BBSAN; the same products close screech). Hilbert POD on a multi-mode reconstruction would show jitter, which Wong et al. could only treat statistically, as amplitude and phase modulation, and could track the shock oscillation they left unmodelled. Subtracting an HPOD-tracked tonal field rather than a fixed POD pair would give broadband statistics conditioned on screech state (Tan 2017 note), and conditioning the short-time mean shock train on HPOD amplitude would test whether André's shock damping follows instantaneous screech level (André note). These are suggestions, not results of these papers.
- **Group data.** Wong's shock model and Tan's statistical models use PIV of the same M_j 1.45 jet, so the two source locations can be compared on one jet; the lab's mean PIV and BOS fields feed both.
- **PyFR staging LES.** At M_j ≈ 1.1, β and hence BBSAN are small and Tam's screech formula fails. André's M_j 1.10 and 1.15 cases bracket A1/A2, so an FW-H surface could test whether the BBSAN peak near 90° sits at 2f_s and jumps between A1 and A2, and the mean centreline pressure whether screech damps the train (André note). Gojon's untripped LES got the tone right and its amplitude trend wrong (Gojon note), so staging limits need a boundary-layer sensitivity check.
- **Rocket noise.** Lab jets are cold (κ 0.6–0.85) and show BBSAN and screech; rockets are hot (κ ≈ 0.31) and show neither. Lab mechanisms transfer through Mach-wave directivity, wavepacket source models and nonlinear propagation; a heated BBSAN model needs the entropy source (Ray et al. 2005) and a flight correction Michalke's stretching. Derivative skewness is a cheap step-1 statistic for heated or impinging cases.
