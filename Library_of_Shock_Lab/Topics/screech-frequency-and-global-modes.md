# Screech frequency prediction and global modes

_Last updated: 2026-10-22 · 34 papers_

## Scope

How well models predict the screech tone, and what the successful ones say about how the loop picks its frequency. The models are Powell's and Tam's weakest-link formulas, integer-loop phase criteria, guided-jet-mode (G-JM) band and resonance models, local and Floquet stability, and global stability and resolvent analysis of measured mean flows. The page also covers convection speed and quick frequency checks; absolute instability and whether screech is a self-excited global mode or a forced response; what the models can say about amplitude; and how LES and linear models compare with experiment. The loop itself (G-JM closure, shock leakage, source location), receptivity experiments and triads are on [screech](screech.md), and stage jumps on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md). The machinery is described once elsewhere: vortex sheets, guided-mode bands and absolute instability in [linear-models](linear-models.md); shock spacing, the multi-peak shock spectrum and Floquet models in x in [shock-cell-models](shock-cell-models.md); global, resolvent and harmonic-resolvent practice in [linear-operator-methods](linear-operator-methods.md); LES numerics in [simulation-methods](simulation-methods.md).

## Current understanding

1. **Frequency is predictable; amplitude is not.** Linear models reach 1–10 % and LES about 0.01 in St, but no model in the library predicts amplitude ([Raman 1998](../Notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) p. 48, 101; [Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) p. 40), and an LES with every frequency right can get the heating trend of amplitude wrong ([Gojon 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md) p. 18–19, 24).
2. **Every model that gets A1 and A2 right returns through the guided jet mode**, the neutral upstream wave of [Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md) (p. 462–463, 475–477), who left its role in screech open (p. 463, 480). Tones sit near the lower edge of its narrow bands ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) p. 10; [Gojon 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md) p. 2919–2920), and closing the loop with free-stream sound predicts tones where none exist ([Mancinelli 2019](../Notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) p. 6; [Mancinelli 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) p. 15). The same KH–G-JM condition predicts the flapping tones of an AR 2 rectangular jet ([Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md) p. 4219).
3. **The frameworks largely coincide.** The phase criterion and the Tam & Tanna relation are one condition (Mancinelli 2021, p. 24), and the weakest link is essentially the saddle-point condition ([Nogueira 2022](../Notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) p. 14–15). They differ on whether nozzle reflection matters.
4. **Shear-layer thickness matters more than shock amplitude**, through the G-JM band edges and the absolute growth rate ([Nogueira 2021, absolute instability](../Notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) p. 16; Mancinelli 2021, p. 16–17; [Ivelja 2024](../Notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) p. 23). A spreading PIV mean predicts rectangular tones where a constant-profile one fails (Nogueira 2025, p. 4220). Thickness moves the band edges but leaves the upstream wave neutral (Tam & Hu 1989, p. 479).
5. **Screech is locally absolutely unstable but globally slightly stable on the measured mean.** A weak shock train creates a KH–G-JM saddle with positive absolute growth (Nogueira 2021, absolute instability, p. 13–16), which is necessary, not sufficient, for a global mode ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../Notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md) p. 494), and the least-damped global mode of a measured mean is slightly stable ([Farghadan 2026](../Notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) p. 11, 14). Whether screech is self-excited is therefore open.
6. **LES matches the tones; amplitude trends are less secure** ([Edgington-Mitchell 2023](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2023-screeching-jet-seen-from-all-sides.md) p. 2; Gojon 2019, p. 13–24).
7. **Quick formulas work because their constants are tuned.** Measured convection speeds run from 0.5 to 0.75U_j with rig, mode, position and method ([Alkislar 2003](../Notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md) p. 143–144; [Mercier 2017](../Notes/mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) p. 214, 224–226; Gojon 2018, p. 2923), and Tam's 0.8 spacing factor is the standing-wave length Panda measured, L_sw ≈ 0.8L ([Panda 1999](../Notes/panda-1999-experimental-investigation-screech-noise-generation.md) p. 81–83).

The history runs from free-stream sound to the G-JM. Powell and Tam close the loop with sound outside the jet and give one smooth curve. Tam & Hu 1989 had already found the upstream neutral waves, with a closed-form lower band edge at the sonic-line branch point (eq. 6.3, p. 476) and an upper edge at a double zero (p. 475–476): the branch and saddle points the resonance models later use (Tam & Hu 1989 note). Band membership came first. It puts A1/A2 near the (0, 2) cut-on (Edgington-Mitchell 2018, p. 10), C and c on the (1, 1) edge, and B and D outside every band (Gojon 2018, p. 2919–2920), but it says where a loop can close, not which frequency wins, and both A stages share one band (Gojon 2018 note). The resonance models of Mancinelli replace the return path with the G-JM and recover the A1/A2 trends; the stability models drop the reflection coefficients and find the tone as a saddle or a global eigenvalue.

The frameworks are closer than they look. Powell's phase criterion and the Tam & Tanna relation coincide, with k_s = (2πp − φ)/L_s for irregular spacing, so a resonance model's integer p stands in for the choice of shock peak (Mancinelli 2021, p. 24), though Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 4) objects that changing p is not a mechanism; which shock peak a loop uses is discussed on [shock-cell-models](shock-cell-models.md). Integer loop counts go back to [Powell 1992](../Notes/powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md), which explains each stage jump as the cycle count N + p moving to the next integer, p being "not a priori zero" (p. 2833). By the note's derivation its measured array angles (p. 2834) put B on Powell's formula with the second-cell spacing and C about 24 % above it, as a loop on the suboptimal shock peak would be. The finite-thickness fit's s = 4 and p = 4, 5 are exactly Mercier's measured N and m (Mancinelli 2021, p. 17; Mercier 2017, p. 224): a measured period count, but still a label rather than a cause. Tam's weakest-link condition is essentially the saddle-point condition (Nogueira 2022, p. 14–15); Nogueira 2025 uses it as a stand-in for the absolute instability, with α_KH fixed near the lip and α_GJM(x) free (p. 4213, 4219). Fits are rig-specific: the Monash twin-jet rig reproduces the finite-thickness fit (s = 4, φ = 0, p = 4, 5) but needs a different vortex-sheet φ, put down to lip thickness of 15 % against 3 % ([Stavropoulos 2023](../Notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) p. 16–17).

## Convection speed and quick checks

Weakest-link and convected-wave checks hinge on U_c. In round jets it rises from about 0.57U_j upstream of the source to about 0.7U_j downstream, and the nozzle-to-source mean (0.52–0.59U_j) grows with source distance within A1, so prediction needs the source position (Mercier 2017, p. 214, 224–226). Schlieren wavelength times tone frequency gives 0.54–0.67U_j (mean 0.61) across stages A to D (Powell 1992, Figs 5–6, p. 2827–2828), twin jets give 0.57–0.60 ([Knast 2018](../Notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) p. 10), and Panda's shear-layer phase fits give about 0.67 and 0.57U_j at M_j 1.19 and 1.42 (p. 85; T₀ = T_a assumed in the note), so Tam's 0.7U_j fits only the first. None of these separates the stages (Knast 2018, p. 10–11). Heating lowers U_c (broadband u_c/u_j 0.80 → 0.64 for TR 1 → 3; Tam's formula is then within 10 %; Gojon 2019, p. 16, 19). The choice moves the answer: Tam's formula gives St 0.285 with u_c = 0.65u_j against LES tones of 0.28 and 0.305 (Gojon 2018, p. 2920), and 0.303 with the LES's own broadband 0.75u_j (p. 2923; the note's arithmetic). In an AR 4 rectangular jet the structures convect at a near-constant 0.5U_j from 4h to 18h (Alkislar 2003, p. 143–144; the constancy data are in the thesis), and the St jump at the design Mach number is fitted by switching U_c to 0.63 (p. 132), on PIV not shown (Alkislar 2003 note).

How U_c is measured matters: local phase-gradient speeds swing between subsonic and supersonic with the standing-wave period L_sw (Panda 1999, Figs 7–8, p. 84–87), which is why Mercier read the source phase from a straight-line fit over one cell (p. 224). Panda's formula with L_sw in place of the shock spacing (eq. 3, p. 82) is an identity, since L_sw is defined from c/f_s and u_c/f_s (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 3). Its content is the measurement L_sw ≈ 0.8L (Table 1, p. 81), which is why Tam's empirical 0.8 factor works: with u_c = 0.7u_j and Prandtl–Pack spacing it reproduces Tam's 0.67 (eqs. 5–7, p. 82–83). Edgington-Mitchell 2022 reads k_sw as the active loop's shock wavenumber, k_s1 in A1 and B and k_s2 in A2 and C (p. 11), and Panda's jets are A2 and C, so L_sw < L fits (Panda 1999 note).

Convected-wave checks, St = (U_c/U_j)(D/λ), match tones to a few per cent but contain no loop (0.54 against 0.52 in an AR 2 ellipse, [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) p. 2744, 2747; within 0.01 for twin-jet POD pairs, [Bell 2021](../Notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md) p. 27). Powell's formula with that ellipse's 0.62D shock spacing is 20 % high (Edgington-Mitchell 2015 note), so which spacing a non-round loop uses is unresolved, and Tam's closed-form formula is a poor check in the A1/A2 range (0.92 against 0.67 at M_j 1.09, by the note author's calculation; Tam 1995 note). Spacing formulas themselves, and why they fail downstream, are on [shock-cell-models](shock-cell-models.md).

## Absolute instability and global modes

The framework is Huerre & Monkewitz 1990, set out on [linear-operator-methods](linear-operator-methods.md): a local pocket of absolute instability is necessary but not sufficient for a self-excited global mode, whose frequency to leading order is ω₀ at the saddle X_s of ω₀(X) in the complex x-plane, with ω_G,i ≤ ω₀,ᵢ(X_s) ≤ ω₀,ᵢ|max (eqs. 38–39, p. 493–494); only global properties can be measured (p. 516). Global instability by pressure feedback off surfaces, as in edge, collar and cavity tones, is a separate class (p. 498). The saddle view of screech (Nogueira 2021, 2022) belongs to the first class and the resonance-loop models (Mancinelli 2021) to the second; the classes are not exclusive (Huerre 1990 note).

Nogueira 2021, absolute instability (p. 13–16, 21–22), showed that a weak sinusoidal shock train barely moves the eigenvalues but creates a KH–G-JM saddle with positive absolute growth, even in a cold jet. Downstream shear-layer thickening makes it a pocket of absolute instability, the nozzle is not needed to select the frequency, and the model gives no reason for B–C–D switching. Ivelja's streamwise-limited G-JM band (p. 24–25) fits this pocket. The saddle needs shocks: Tam & Hu's double zero (p. 475–476) is a neutral coalescence that sets the band's upper edge, not the KH–G-JM saddle (Tam & Hu 1989 note). Their upstream waves stay neutral with a finite-thickness shear layer (p. 479), so the G-JM's damping must come from viscosity or turbulence (see the eddy-viscosity discussion on [linear-models](linear-models.md)). The 2–6 % match between local saddle and tone implies that ω₀,r varies little along x, as Huerre & Monkewitz conjecture for wakes (p. 502; the Huerre 1990 note's inference). Global analyses of measured mean flows agree on the frequency, but Farghadan 2026 finds the least-stable mode slightly stable, so screech must be forced (p. 11, 14). That is not a direct test, since the theory linearises about base flows near onset rather than the mean of a saturated state (Huerre 1990 note). The same operator holds an St 0.31 duct resonance of the [Towne 2017](../Notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) type (Farghadan 2026, p. 13–14).

## What the models say about amplitude

The models give partial handles only. In the absolute-instability view the nozzle sets amplification, not frequency (Nogueira 2021, absolute instability, p. 22); ω0i falls from about 0.44 to 0.07 as δ grows from 0.15 to 0.22 (p. 16) and rises with M_j like the measured amplitude (Nogueira 2022, p. 13), consistent with Raman's finding that amplitude plateaus while shock strength keeps rising, since the saddle is insensitive to shock amplitude. The resonance models need a fitted |R₁R₂| of 0.1–0.3 to sustain A1/A2, with a Wiener–Hopf model proposed (Mancinelli 2021, p. 19–22). LES does not yet fill the gap: the one heated LES gets the temperature trend of amplitude backwards, probably through its exit boundary layer (Gojon 2019; detail on [screech](screech.md)). The measured receptivity effects these models would have to reproduce (lip thickness, reflectors, tabs, rig walls) are also on [screech](screech.md).

## LES and linear models against experiment

LES now matches screech frequency and amplitude (Edgington-Mitchell 2023, p. 2): a blind twin-rectangular LES matches spectra to St ≈ 1, tones included ([Brès 2021](../Notes/bres-2021-les-twin-rectangular-jets-plasma.md)), and LES resolves intermittent twin-jet coupling ([Jeun 2024](../Notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md)) and flapping-axis precession ([Nogueira 2026](../Notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md)). Heated rectangular LES matches St to 0.01 at six conditions, and the mean flow and directivity, but not the heating trend (Gojon 2019, p. 13–24). An early high-order round-jet LES (M_j 1.56) reproduced two helical C tones and an axisymmetric u tone, with in-jet upstream waves on k ≈ −ω/a₀ at the tones (Gojon 2018, p. 2920–2923), but at Re_j 6 × 10⁴ from a 2 mm thin-lip pipe its St are not directly comparable with lab jets (Gojon 2018 note). Spatial simulations can self-excite through outflow-to-inflow pressure feedback, a computational-box resonance (Huerre 1990, p. 498, Fig. 8). An early Euler simulation already got 5043 Hz and 156.4 dB against 5248 Hz and 157 dB (Raman 1998, p. 98). No LES of round-jet A1/A2 staging is in the library yet; the PyFR continuation LES targets it ([screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md)). [Edgington-Mitchell 2014](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2014-underexpanded-jet-mach-disk-shear-layer.md) (p. 16–17) gives a validation target for strongly underexpanded LES: no recirculation behind the Mach disk in 200,000 PIV samples.

Linear models match frequencies to 1–10 % and G-JM shapes very closely (Edgington-Mitchell 2018, p. 9–10; [Nogueira 2021, twin jets](../Notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md) p. 19–21, "every peak, node and decay rate"; Ivelja 2024, p. 21–22), and on a fitted 3-D PIV mean the predicted rectangular tones are usually slightly low and placed near the POD-mode peak (Nogueira 2025, p. 4219). But KH shapes come out too narrow without spreading or eddy viscosity (Nogueira 2021, twin jets, p. 19–20), growth rates are not quantitative (Nogueira 2021, absolute instability, p. 11–12), as Tam & Hu already found for KH waves (p. 467), and the resolvent–POD inner product is 0.89 (Farghadan 2026, p. 17). G-JM bands are sensitive to the mean flow: RANS shear layers narrow them (Ivelja 2024, p. 23), R/θ falls from about 18 to 6 along the loop although one value is used (Mancinelli 2021, p. 22–23), the LES C tone lies 10–14 % below the thin-sheet (1, 1) edge (Gojon 2018 note), and a constant-profile base flow predicts the rectangular k_sh1 loop only up to NPR 2.6 (Nogueira 2025, p. 4220). Parabolised models cannot carry the upstream wave at all ([Nogueira 2022, wavepacket modulation](../Notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md)). So the models are best at frequency and mode shape, adequate at band edges once the shear layer is right, and weakest on growth and amplitude, the quantities a staging or amplitude prediction would need.

## Disagreements and open questions

**What selects the frequency.** Phase-criterion resonance (Mancinelli 2019, 2021), the weakest link (Tam 1986), the absolute-instability saddle (Nogueira 2021, 2022) and global modes (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves; Farghadan 2026) all hit the tone but differ on nozzle reflection: the saddle view ignores it, the resonance models need R₁R₂, and [Ponton 1992](../Notes/ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md)'s small but systematic lip-induced frequency shifts (p. 538) sit between, as does Panda's finding that covering the nozzle flange changes both amplitude and frequency (Panda 1999, p. 73). Running all four on one mean flow, and changing only the nozzle exterior in an LES, would settle it. Where inside an absolute pocket linear theory puts the frequency is itself disputed (see [linear-models](linear-models.md)).

**Which loop a prediction belongs to.** When loops coexist, frequency alone cannot pair a prediction with a tone. The AR 2 k_sh2 predictions offered as the second flapping mode (Nogueira 2025, p. 4219–4220) sit on the varicose tone at NPR 3.3 and between the two at higher NPR (the note's reading of Fig. 17). Projecting predicted mode shapes onto the SPOD mode at each tone would settle it.

**Convection speed: method or geometry?** Vorticity centroids give a constant 0.5U_j in an AR 4 rectangular jet (Alkislar 2003, p. 143–144), phase slopes and wavenumber peaks give a rise from about 0.55 to 0.7U_j in round jets (Mercier 2017, p. 214, 224–226), and the KH peak of an AR 2 flapping mode gives 0.73–0.75U_j (Nogueira 2025 note). One method across rigs, such as sliding-window k_x peaks of sign-split SPOD modes, would separate the two causes. Panda's L_sw ≈ 1.3L in two rectangular jets and an excited round jet (Table 2, p. 86; the note's ratio) is the opposite of his own jets and is unexplained, since rectangular jets have no suboptimal peak to close on (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 18–19, via the Panda 1999 note).

**Self-excited or forced.** The model-base-flow saddle is absolutely unstable (Nogueira 2021, absolute instability), but that is necessary, not sufficient, for a global mode (Huerre 1990, p. 494), and the measured mean's least-damped global mode is slightly stable (Farghadan 2026, p. 14). The base flows also differ: a model profile against a measured long-time mean. Global stability on each loop's short-time mean would test whether each state is marginally stable on its own mean ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) p. 19). Huerre & Monkewitz rank the direct tests, weakest first (p. 516–518): single-point spectra; lock-in under forcing; response against forcing amplitude, flat for an oscillator and slope 1 for an amplifier (Fig. 21); saturated amplitude growing as √(R − R_Gc) with the control parameter (eq. 58); and, conclusively, a transient after a step that fits one Landau equation (eq. 47) at every point. An NPR step in the lab or in the PyFR driver is such a transient. The operator-side version of this question is on [linear-operator-methods](linear-operator-methods.md).

**Amplitude.** No predictive model exists: |R₁R₂| is fitted, and the link between ω0i and saturated amplitude is only suggested (Nogueira 2022, p. 13). Gojon 2019's wrong heating trend (p. 18–19) means LES loop gains and staging limits need checking against the exit boundary-layer state, for instance with tripped and untripped exits.

## Methods and data

- **Model inputs.** A mean flow with its shear-layer thickness, which varies along the loop, so one R/θ is not enough (Mancinelli 2021, p. 22–23); a shock-free tanh fit to the measured mean keeps the spreading and is what a 3-D local model needs (Nogueira 2025, eq. 7, p. 4212). PIV means limit global and resolvent analysis to weak shocks and m = 0 (table above). Shock wavenumbers need a long field of view ([shock-cell-models](shock-cell-models.md)). Powell-type and convected-wave checks need the source position and a U_c averaged to it (Mercier 2017, p. 214, 224–226), from a fit over at least one cell or a k_x peak, not a local phase gradient (Panda 1999, p. 84–87).
- **Band overlays.** The closed-form lower edge of the (0, m) bands (Tam & Hu 1989, eq. 6.3, p. 476) is a one-line overlay on St–NPR maps; evaluate the bands with finite thickness before calling a tone outside one (Gojon 2018 note).
- **Validation.** Compare predicted and measured St (`Data/screech_tones.csv`), and mode shapes by projection, as with the resolvent–POD inner product (Farghadan 2026, p. 17), or by k_x-split POD for the G-JM (Edgington-Mitchell 2018; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves). In PIV, shocks inside the interrogation window smear over ≈ 0.2D, which limits strongly underexpanded validation data (Edgington-Mitchell 2014, p. 16–17).
- **Self-excitation tests** need time-resolved data through a forcing sweep or a step in NPR (Huerre & Monkewitz's list above); block-averaged spectra are the weakest test.

## Relevance to our work

**Lab data.** The PIV means already used for global analysis (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves; Farghadan 2026) are the natural common base flow for running all four frequency frameworks side by side, the test proposed above, and the group's A1 and A2 PIV would extend it across staging. The AR 2 rectangular SPOD and PIV (Nogueira 2025) are the case for the loop-assignment test.

**PyFR staging LES** (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`). Each NPR step is Huerre & Monkewitz's conclusive transient: fit the Landau equation to Hilbert envelopes at several points and check that the coefficients agree (details on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md)). Converged A1 and A2 states give mean flows for local, Floquet and global analysis at one NPR, and the fitted-mean weakest link of Nogueira 2025 is cheap enough to run on every state. Both tones should sit in the lower half of the vortex-sheet (0, 2) band, which spans St 0.634–0.794 at M_j 1.09 and 0.592–0.741 at 1.14 for cold jets, where the group's A1 (0.67) and A2 (0.65) lie (Tam & Hu 1989 note; [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) Table 1), and the near-field crest spacing should jump from k_s1 to k_s2 across A1 → A2 (Panda 1999 note). Check that tones and switch times do not depend on the outflow treatment, since box resonances can self-excite spatial simulations (Huerre 1990, p. 498), and test sensitivity to the exit boundary layer, the likely reason an LES can get frequencies right and amplitudes wrong (Gojon 2019, p. 8–10).

**SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** SPOD modes are the comparison set for resolvent and global modes, and HPOD amplitudes after an NPR step give the transient that a Landau fit needs. Split by the sign of k_x before reading a convection speed from a Hilbert phase along x, or Panda's standing-wave modulation will contaminate it (Panda 1999 note).

**Rocket noise.** Whether the G-JM band misses hot, strongly off-design plumes is a model question with full-scale consequences; it is discussed with the full-scale evidence on [screech](screech.md).
