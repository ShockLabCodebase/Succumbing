# Linear models: local stability and wave models

_Last updated: 2026-10-24 · 48 papers_

## Scope

Linear models that treat a jet's coherent structures as waves on its mean flow: why linearising about the turbulent mean works and what it leaves out; eddy viscosity; local stability, vortex sheets, absolute instability and receptivity; Floquet and cross-plane models of non-round and twin jets; guided-jet waves and the resonance models built on them; wavepackets as sources of Mach-wave radiation and BBSAN; and the ray limit of the KH–shock interaction. The page records where they match experiment or LES and where they fail.

Global stability, resolvent analysis, PSE/PFE/OWNS and the harmonic resolvent are on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md); shock cells and wavepacket–shock interaction on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md). Applications are only linked: [screech](screech.md); [screech frequency and global modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md); [screech staging and mode switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md); [non-axisymmetric jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md); [twin-jet coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md); [jet-noise mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md); [impinging jets and edge tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md); [modal decomposition](modal-decomposition.md). Entry reviews: local stability ([Michalke 1984](../Notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md)), absolute instability ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../Notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md)), guided waves ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md); planar, [Tam & Norum 1992](../Notes/tam-1992-impingement-tones-large-aspect-ratio-rectangular-jets.md)), wavepackets ([Jordan & Colonius 2013](../Notes/jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise.md); [Cavalieri 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md)) and the resonance loop ([Edgington-Mitchell 2019](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2019-aeroacoustic-resonance-review.md)).

## Current understanding

1. **Linearising about the turbulent mean works, but only statistically.** PSE on measured mean flows reproduce the averaged phase and envelope of wavepackets for St ≈ 0.2–0.9, m = 0–2, up to the end of the potential core, with one free amplitude per (m, ω) ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../Notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md), p. 109–113; [Cavalieri 2013](../Notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), p. 574–585). The support is empirical, with no scale-separation proof ([Jordan & Colonius 2013](../Notes/jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise.md), p. 181). Single realisations need not follow the model (Gudmundsson & Colonius, p. 122–123), and it works where the m = 0 wavepacket stays coherent over up to ≈ 1.9D, about four times the eddies' scale ([Jaunet 2017](../Notes/jaunet-2017-two-point-coherence-wave-packets-turbulent-jets.md), p. 14–17). Jet divergence alone caps the linear gain ([Michalke 1984](../Notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md), p. 190–191).
2. **Shock-free jets are convectively unstable.** Only low-density jets with thin laminar layers at low M go absolutely unstable (round below ρ_j/ρ∞ = 0.72, planar below 0.90; [Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../Notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 511–512), so heated turbulent jets stay convective ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../Notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md), p. 111). Global operators agree ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)).
3. **The jet is also an acoustic waveguide.** Besides KH, a jet carries trapped subsonic waves; in supersonic jets some travel upstream, neutral, in narrow bands along ω/k ≈ −a∞ ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 462–463, 475–476; planar: [Tam & Norum 1992](../Notes/tam-1992-impingement-tones-large-aspect-ratio-rectangular-jets.md), p. 308–310). They are near-neutral modes of vortex-sheet, finite-thickness and global operators ([Towne 2017](../Notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md), p. 14–19; [Schmidt 2017](../Notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 9–11): in-jet sound totally reflected by the shear layer ([Nogueira 2024](../Notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md), p. 19–22). Their bands bracket the observed screech, edge, impingement and near-nozzle tones ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), p. 10; [Gojon 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md), p. 2919–2920; [Jordan 2018](../Notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md), p. 10–14; [Jaunet 2019](../Notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md), p. 7; Towne 2017, p. 19; Tam & Norum, p. 310), and LES spectra show them inside supersonic cores ([Bogey & Gojon 2017](../Notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 583–585; Gojon 2018, p. 2921). The bands move more with shear-layer thickness, temperature and cross-section shape than with shock strength ([Mancinelli 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), p. 15–17; [Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4220; [Lu 2026](../Notes/lu-2026-rectangular-jet-screech-dimensional-reduction.md), p. 17–19), and reduced models get the upper edge (saddle) more reliably than the lower (branch point) (Lu 2026, p. 17). Tones sit mostly near the lower edge, where the wave travels at about a∞, so frequencies alone barely separate guided from free-stream closure (Gojon 2018, p. 2919–2920; Bogey & Gojon, p. 582).
4. **Shocks turn the jet into a resonator.** KH–shock interaction puts energy at k_kh ± k_s, and global analysis on a mean flow that contains the shocks recovers the screech frequency to about 1% ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 11–17; [Farghadan 2026](../Notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 11). A Floquet model shows that the shocks create a KH–guided-mode saddle (absolute instability) within 2–6% of the screech frequency ([Nogueira 2021](../Notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 13–15). Such a local pocket is necessary but not sufficient for a self-excited global mode ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../Notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 492–495). The shock-cell side is on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md).
5. **Weak azimuthal non-uniformity selects flapping.** A second jet, an elliptic cross-section or weak |m| = 2 streaks split the degenerate m = ±1 modes into flapping modes ([Rodríguez 2023](../Notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 10–12; [Nogueira 2026](../Notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 9–11; [Ivelja 2024](../Notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 17–18).
6. **Wavepackets are good sound sources.** Mean-flow instability waves matched to an outer acoustic field gave the first computed Mach-wave fields ([Tam & Burton 1984](../Notes/tam-1984-sound-instability-waves-supersonic-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 287–293) and, with white-noise lip forcing, Mach 2 directivities within about 2.5 dB ([Tam & Chen 1994](../Notes/tam-1994-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1778–1779); one coherent wavepacket per frequency dominates supersonic Mach-wave radiation ([Cavalieri 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 16, 26). For BBSAN (wavepacket × shock cells), jitter lowers and broadens the peak instead of amplifying the sound as in subsonic jets ([Wong 2019](../Notes/wong-2019-coherence-decay-wavepacket-bbsan.md), p. 978; [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md)).

## Why mean-flow models work at all

Local theory came first. [Michalke 1984](../Notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md) built inviscid spatial LSA of round jets with Crocco–Busemann temperature and the tanh "profile 2" that the group's LSA still uses (p. 161–164). As θ/R grows from 0.05 to 0.4 the most amplified St falls from about 0.75 to 0.21 (m = 0), and St < 0.35 stays unstable over the whole core (p. 169–170); Mach number stabilises m = 0 more than m = 1 in thick layers, and heating destabilises (p. 174–177). Slowly diverging theory caps the gain (about 90, 57 and 26 for m = 0, 1, 2 at St 0.5), a saturation once blamed on nonlinearity (p. 190–191). [Jordan & Colonius 2013](../Notes/jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise.md) made this the case for linearity: the turbulent mean spreads so fast that the total gain stays small and the nonlinearity is already in the mean, whereas initially laminar or strongly forced jets saturate and pair (p. 181–182). For the same reason, laminar or transitional simulations in which nonlinear terms matter may not carry over to turbulent jets (p. 183–184). Apparent wavenumbers and growth rates depend on the variable and position measured (Michalke, p. 189–190; Jordan & Colonius, p. 183).

The models then climbed a hierarchy: locally parallel LSA, multiple-scales extensions, PSE, global problems (Jordan & Colonius, p. 182–183). [Tam & Burton 1984](../Notes/tam-1984-sound-instability-waves-supersonic-axisymmetric-jets.md) is the library's multiple-scales stage. At O(1) it solves the local inviscid eigenproblem on a measured mean fitted with Gaussian profiles splined smoothly in x (p. 283–284); at O(ε), matching to an outer Fourier–Hankel field gives an amplitude equation that supplies the non-parallel amplitude and phase (eqs. 4.16–4.18, p. 282). The matching answers the uniform-validity objection Michalke records (p. 192), and PSE later made the dispute over local phase speeds moot in practice (our reading).

The empirical case followed. PSE matched a POD-filtered near-field array ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../Notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md)) and time-resolved cross-plane PIV, with |β| ≥ 0.9 from x/D ≈ 2 to the end of the core ([Cavalieri 2013](../Notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), p. 583). Both fail below St ≈ 0.2, blamed on non-parallel effects (Gudmundsson & Colonius, p. 110) or left open (Cavalieri, p. 575). Tam & Burton's forced M 2.1 jet shows the same symptom despite its non-parallel correction: the computed wave peaks about 4R_j upstream of the data at St 0.2, though correctly at St 0.4 (p. 287–288), which points beyond weak non-parallelism (note's reading; the mean flow was measured under forcing). The answer, the Orr mechanism, came from resolvent analysis ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)).

**Two-point statistics.** A linear mode has unit coherence by construction ([Jaunet 2017](../Notes/jaunet-2017-two-point-coherence-wave-packets-turbulent-jets.md), p. 1, 17). In Cavalieri 2013's M 0.4 jet, dual-plane time-resolved stereo PIV shows the m = 0 wavepacket coherent over up to ≈ 1.9D (≈ 12δω, against ≈ 3δω for the eddies), most clearly near x/D ≈ 2, St ≈ 0.4–0.45, inside the region where PSE matched PIV; past x/D ≈ 5 it decoheres on the eddies' scale, which the authors read as nonlinear interaction (p. 14–17). That settles Cavalieri's open choice (p. 587 there) in favour of downstream decoherence. The peak coincides with the resolvent's preferred mode (St ≈ 0.43–0.46, [Garnaud 2013](../Notes/garnaud-2013-preferred-mode-incompressible-jets.md)) and the region's lower edge with the m = 0 KH → Orr transition ([Schmidt 2018](../Notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md); [Pickering 2020](../Notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md)), so coherence, linear success and gain separation mark one region (Jaunet note's inference). The coherence is asymmetric, 1.5–2.2 times shorter downstream (p. 17–18), and near the nozzle the low-St phase speed reaches about U_j, as linear theory predicts (p. 20–21).

[McKeon & Sharma 2010](../Notes/mckeon-2010-critical-layer-framework-turbulent-pipe-flow.md) gave the formal basis: the nonlinear terms become forcing of the operator linearised about the mean (p. 8), whose response is low rank (σ₁ ≈ 10σ₂, p. 13); Jordan & Colonius's mode equations (eqs. 1–3, p. 182) are the same set-up. [Oberleithner 2011](../Notes/oberleithner-2011-swirling-jet-vortex-breakdown-stability.md) showed that mean-flow LSA also reproduces a self-excited global mode, a swirling jet's St = 0.49 precessing helix, except at the wavemaker (p. 26–29).

**Eddy viscosity.** Most models stand in for the turbulence with a uniform Reynolds number, and its value matters: subsonic trapped acoustic modes vanish at Re = 10³ and converge only above about 5 × 10⁴ ([Schmidt 2017](../Notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md), p. 25–27), yet the screech global analyses used Re = 10³ and still found guided and duct waves ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md); [Farghadan 2026](../Notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md)). [Tam & Chen 1994](../Notes/tam-1994-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md) used ν_t only as a regulariser for damped waves, raising R_t = u_jb/ν_t until results converged (≥ 2000, p. 1778–1779). A RANS eddy viscosity beats uniform damping in the jet resolvent ([Pickering 2021](../Notes/pickering-2021-optimal-eddy-viscosity-resolvent-jets.md), p. 28) because its equivalent forcing roughly reproduces the true forcing's projections, not because it models the physics ([Morra 2021](../Notes/morra-2021-colour-of-forcing-resolvent-channel.md), p. 11–24). For inviscid models the open choice is the base flow, above all the shear-layer thickness.

## Local stability, vortex sheets and absolute instability

**Vortex sheets** are the cheapest model. They give guided-mode bands (round: [Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md); [Tam & Ahuja 1990](../Notes/tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md); [Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md); [Gojon 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md); [Mancinelli 2019](../Notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md); [Jordan 2018](../Notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md); [Jaunet 2019](../Notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md); planar: [Tam & Norum 1992](../Notes/tam-1992-impingement-tones-large-aspect-ratio-rectangular-jets.md); [Gojon 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md)), twin-jet coupling ([Stavropoulos 2023](../Notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md); [Rodríguez 2023](../Notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md)) and the convective-Mach-number collapse of compressible shear-layer growth ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../Notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md), p. 14–15). Their known faults:
- KH phase speeds and wavefront angles come out well, growth rates poorly (Tam & Hu, p. 467).
- The vortex sheet stabilises completely above M_c ≈ 1.5–2.1, whereas a finite-thickness layer never does (Papamoschou & Roshko, p. 14–15, 22–23). Finite-thickness 2-D theory has the opposite gap: its growth tends to zero as M_c rises, while measured growth levels off near 20% of the incompressible value. Oblique modes (most amplified above M_c ≈ 0.6), duct modes in confined experiments (Tam & Hu's JFM 203 paper, not the JFM 201 one here) and shocks are the candidate reasons, and helical modes raise a round jet's theoretical asymptote to about 50% ([Gutmark 1995](../Notes/gutmark-1995-mixing-enhancement-supersonic-free-shear-flows.md), p. 379–381). Above a critical M_j that falls with jet temperature (≈ 2.8 isothermal, ≈ 2.0 at T_j/T∞ = 3) the KH zero merges into the supersonic instability waves, which then dominate (Tam & Hu, p. 456–459, 480–481).
- Finite thickness lowers the KH phase speed and makes it more dispersive, so the fitted reflection phase changes ([Mancinelli 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), p. 9–11).
- Cooling the jet raises the guided-mode band and thickening the shear layer lowers it; the isothermal vortex sheet of Mancinelli 2019 fitted A1/A2 only because the two cancel (Mancinelli 2021, p. 15–16, 25–26). Thickness matters below R/θ ≈ 30 (p. 16–17), and so does which θ: Michalke calls the density-weighted θ_c unsuitable (p. 162), yet Mancinelli 2021 used it (p. 22–23); for a cold M 1.2 jet the two differ by about 16% (Michalke note's calculation).

**Which azimuthal mode leads.** Local growth and the most unstable frequency fall with M_j, heating and thickness; m = 0 leads at low supersonic M_j and m = ±1 at higher M_j, crossing between M_j 1.0 and 1.5 in Morris's curves ([Edgington-Mitchell 2019](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2019-aeroacoustic-resonance-review.md), p. 154–155; read from figures). In thick layers (R/θ ≤ 6) the helical mode dominates above M ≈ 0.8, and heating stabilises supersonic jets although it destabilises subsonic ones ([Gutmark 1995](../Notes/gutmark-1995-mixing-enhancement-supersonic-free-shear-flows.md), p. 381, 383).

**Local LSA on shock-containing means.** [Karami 2020](../Notes/karami-2020-acoustic-hydrodynamic-waves-underexpanded-impinging-jets.md) gives the library's only one: inviscid m = 0 on LES means inside the first shock cell of an NPR 3.4 impinging jet (p. 7–8, 21–23). The most unstable fd/a∞ falls from ≈ 4 to ≈ 0.5 over x = 0.1–0.9d, Michalke's θ/R trend compressed into one diameter, and identically for plate distances 2d and 5d whose tones differ twofold: the loop, not local growth, picks the tone (p. 18, 22–23; note's reading). The LES follows the trend but not the values (p. 24–26). The profile is far from tanh, with an off-axis overshoot, and the authors warn against tanh and vortex-sheet base flows there (p. 22, 26). The note flags garbled printed coefficients and an unseparated KH branch.

**Receptivity** closes the loop at the lip and suits linear theory, but the library holds little of it. As reviewed by [Edgington-Mitchell 2019](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2019-aeroacoustic-resonance-review.md) (p. 173–174): receptivity is highest where the layer is thinnest; Tam 1986 requires matched frequency and phase velocity, which narrow beams arriving 50–80° from upstream supply best (Tam 1978); and an adjoint of a splitter-plate mixing layer finds plate thickness significant (Barone & Lele 2005). Michalke anticipated the nozzle's role: a lip can close a feedback loop that puts the natural shear-layer frequency below the most amplified one (p. 194–196), as resolvent analyses with the nozzle later confirmed ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)). Stochastic models need a closure too: [Tam & Chen 1994](../Notes/tam-1994-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md) assume white-noise excitation, equal lip pressure for every (n, ω), since the near-lip layer has no intrinsic scale (eqs. 9–12, p. 1776). Reflection coefficients are still fitted rather than computed ([Mancinelli 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md); EM 2019 note).

**Absolute instability** is the local criterion that the Floquet and guided-wave models test. A parallel profile is absolutely unstable if ω₀,ᵢ > 0 at a saddle that pinches a k⁺ and a k⁻ branch; a saddle of two branches from the same half-plane does not count ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../Notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 482–484). A purely spatial calculation sees the transition as k⁺ and k⁻ swapping (p. 486), which is how the review reads Michalke's hot-jet "mode mixing" (p. 177; Table 4, p. 530). Counterflow suction around a supersonic jet was an early application: a convective-to-absolute transition, reached at lower suction for lighter jets (Strykowski & Krothapalli 1993, in [Gutmark 1995](../Notes/gutmark-1995-mixing-enhancement-supersonic-free-shear-flows.md), p. 392–394). The WKB turning points of [Towne 2017](../Notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) (p. 25–28) and the Floquet saddle of [Nogueira 2021](../Notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) (p. 14) use this machinery; whether a local pocket yields a global mode is on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md).

## Floquet models

**Periodic in x.** Locally parallel analysis cannot include shocks, and in 2019 "no such theoretical model exists" for their modulation of wavepackets ([Edgington-Mitchell 2019](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2019-aeroacoustic-resonance-review.md), p. 156–157, 177). The shock train is periodic and KH × shock puts energy at k ± k_s ([Tam & Tanna 1982](../Notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md), not tagged); a Floquet model in x then finds a KH–guided-mode saddle, an absolute instability, near the screech frequency ([Nogueira 2021](../Notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md)), and with the two measured shock wavenumbers saddles near A1 and A2 ([Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../Notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md)). These models, PFE and the shock-cell solutions are on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md). A reading from the Tam & Hu note: at ω → 0 each (0, m) subsonic vortex-sheet branch ends at the Pack shock-cell wavenumber, so one branch runs from the stationary shock cell to the upstream guided wave, which is why k_kh − k_s lands near the saddle ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), note's derivation).

**Floquet in azimuth** and cross-plane models cover cross-sections that are not round:
- *Twin jets.* The four m = 1 KH symmetries have nearly equal growth and phase speed, so the upstream guided-mode bands decide the coupling (1SS); the upstream wavenumber matches PIV within 1%, KH within 5.6% ([Nogueira & Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../Notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md), p. 15–21).
- *Elliptic jets.* KH predictions hold on PIV-fitted or RANS base flows. The guided band is narrow (ΔSt ≤ 0.09), moves with thickness and is neutral at the screech frequency only over a short x range, where the measured upstream-wave peak sits ([Ivelja 2024](../Notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 18, 22–24).
- *Rectangular jets.* A shock-free four-tanh fit to PIV reproduces axis switching ([Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4216). Flapping is the most unstable KH mode, and a weakest-link condition, α_GJM(x) = α_KH − k_sh with k_sh measured, predicts the flapping tone within a few per cent at NPR 2.5–4.5, but only on a spreading base flow (p. 4219–4220). Varicose and wagging tones remain unmodelled (p. 4219).
- *Dimensional reduction.* A Cartesian cross-plane LSA of rectangular jets (AR 1–8, inviscid, D2-symmetric quarter domain, α² terms dropped; p. 4, 21–22) tests the planar shortcut ([Lu 2026](../Notes/lu-2026-rectangular-jet-screech-dimensional-reduction.md)). For KH it holds: the planar wavenumber is within 3.3% for AR ≥ 4 and growth within about 10% from AR 2, while an axisymmetric m = 1 model is worse even for a square jet (p. 12, 14). The most amplified St_h does not depend on AR (≈ 0.41 at NPR 2.1, ≈ 0.31 at 5.0; p. 8), and doubling δ halves the unstable band (p. 13). At AR 4–6 a second flapping KH branch, with three spanwise pressure nodes, runs within 0.03% in α_r and 1–2% in α_i of the leading one (p. 11), which a planar model cannot separate.
- *Twin jets without shocks.* Preferred-mode maps show stripes at constant St·s/D, favouring SA1 flapping ([Rodríguez 2023](../Notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md), p. 12–13). The SA guided band moves with spacing and the SS band hardly does ([Stavropoulos 2023](../Notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 11, 14–15); merged jets carry a higher-order mode of an equivalent AR ≈ 2 jet ([Stavropoulos 2024](../Notes/stavropoulos-2024-upstream-waves-merging-twin-jets.md), p. 6–13).
- *Streaks in round jets.* Steady |m| = 2 streaks couple m = ±1 ([Nogueira 2026](../Notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 7–8) and split them into flapping and wagging modes (p. 9–11).

All of these are locally parallel: they give wavenumbers, shapes and bands, not growth integrated in x, receptivity or loop selection (Ivelja 2024, p. 24–26; Nogueira 2025, p. 4220).

## Guided-jet waves and trapped acoustic modes

*Labels.* This page writes (m, n_r) for (azimuthal, radial order), as [Towne 2017](../Notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) and [Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) do; Tam & Ahuja write (n, m). The A1–A4/H1–H3 of [Bogey & Gojon 2017](../Notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md) and S1–S3/H1–H3 of [Gojon 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md) are radial orders, and the planar S1, A1, … of Tam & Norum and Gojon 2019 are first symmetric and antisymmetric bands; none is an A1/A2 screech stage or a Towne S1/S2 saddle. The Pprime group uses (0, 1) for impinging tones ([Jaunet 2019](../Notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md), p. 6) and (0, 2) for A1/A2 screech ([Mancinelli 2019](../Notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md)); [Edgington-Mitchell 2019](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2019-aeroacoustic-resonance-review.md) writes k_TH⁻ for Tam & Hu's upstream wave, with k_p⁻, k_d⁻ and k_T⁺ after Jordan 2018 (p. 168–170). Each upstream band runs from a *branch point* on the sonic line k = −ω/a∞ (lower St; Mancinelli's B, the "cut-on" of EM 2018) to a *saddle* or double zero (upper St; S(m, n_r)).

**Origin.** [Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md) solved the cylindrical vortex sheet and a half-Gaussian Rayleigh model and found three families: KH; supersonic instability waves (p. 456); and subsonic waves, trapped, neutral on a vortex sheet and unstable only with finite thickness (p. 478–479), which in supersonic jets include neutral *upstream* waves (p. 462–463). Each band starts at a sonic-line branch point, closed form for m = 0 (eqs. 6.1–6.3, p. 476) and impedance-type for m ≠ 0 (eq. 6.5), and ends at a double zero (p. 475–476), which is the *upper* edge, as the note's recomputation confirms. These are the hard-duct (m = 0) and mixed-impedance (m = 1) branch points that [Nogueira 2024](../Notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) re-derived (p. 30). Tam & Hu argued that the upstream wave must live mainly outside the jet (p. 476), which Nogueira 2024's energy flux confirms (p. 26–27), and left its role in screech or impingement open (p. 463, 480). Michalke's non-KH "irregular" roots (p. 166, 177) we read as early sightings.

[Tam & Ahuja 1990](../Notes/tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md) made the first application: KH waves hitting a plate excite the upstream neutral modes, which close the loop at the lip (p. 73). The cut-offs are the hard-duct condition for m = 0 and an impedance condition for m ≥ 1 (eqs. 19–22, p. 80), and the (0, 1) branch passes through the origin (p. 80–81). Only (0, 1) lies in the KH band of a cold subsonic jet, so subsonic impingement resonance must be axisymmetric, while (1, 1) enters at supersonic speeds (p. 81–82). The least-dispersive (0, 1) wave matches five tone data sets for M_j 0.7–0.95 (eq. 25, p. 83), and the criterion foreshadows the saddles: two zero-group-velocity points split off near M_j 0.81, at St 0.369 and 0.436 at M_j 0.9 against 0.37 and 0.43 for Towne's vortex sheet (note's calculation).

[Tam & Norum 1992](../Notes/tam-1992-impingement-tones-large-aspect-ratio-rectangular-jets.md) carried the idea to a planar jet between two vortex sheets, for AR 4.24 impinging jets. Its antisymmetric (+tan) and symmetric (−cot) dispersion relations have closed-form lower limits on the sonic line, the tangent argument being (n − ½)π or (n − 1)π (eqs. 19–23, p. 308–310): the hard-wall duct conditions, i.e. the planar branch points later used by Gojon 2019 (eqs. 15–16) and re-derived by Nogueira 2024 (eqs. 4.7–4.8). The upper limits are the branch maxima (saddles). The note reproduces the figures within about 5% (cold M_j 1.29: S1 0–0.877, A1 1.260–1.984 in ωH/u_j) and finds Fig. 10's ω labels misprinted. Norum's coexisting varicose and flapping tones, whose sums and differences give 20+ further tones, fall within or near the first symmetric and antisymmetric bands at seven M_j (p. 305–310). Two general points (note's checks): the first symmetric band starts at ω = 0, as the round (0, 1) band does, so band tests of symmetric tones constrain only the saddle; and the A1 lower limit is 1.38–1.44 times the S1 upper limit over M_j 1.0–1.8, a normalisation-free test for two first-band tones, which one of Norum's own pairs (a/s = 1.26) fails. "Only the first bands meet the KH waves" is asserted without a KH calculation (p. 310).

Two companion papers on an M_j = 0.9 LES made the subsonic case concrete.
- [Towne 2017](../Notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md): linearised Euler, vortex-sheet and soft-duct models give duct-like k± modes and an upstream "discrete free-stream" k⁻ mode (p. 14–15). Two neutral saddles, S1 and S2, bound the band where k⁺ and k⁻ coexist, which brackets the near-nozzle tones (p. 16–19); the models agree on S1 within about 4%. As WKB turning points the saddles give five resonance loops (p. 25–28), and tones exist only for 0.82 < M < 1 in isothermal jets (p. 31–33), where Tam & Hu's claim that every subsonic wave travels upstream (p. 473) fails (Towne, p. 33).
- [Schmidt 2017](../Notes/schmidt-2017-wavepackets-trapped-acoustic-modes-turbulent-jet.md) found the same waves as lightly damped global modes (least damped (0, 1, 1) at St ≈ 0.37, p. 8), which reproduce the LES frequency–wavenumber bands (p. 16–17) and explain tones once blamed on the rig (p. 12–13).

The same family closes other loops:
- *Jet–edge tones.* KH plus k_TH⁻ (M ≤ 0.82) or k_p⁻ (M > 0.82) reproduce the "frequency squeezing" of the tones, and trapping sets the high-St cut-off; the free-stream loop fits neither spacing nor Mach trend ([Jordan 2018](../Notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md), p. 4–5, 9–14). Above M ≈ 0.82, loops through k⁻_d and k⁻_p compete ([Stavropoulos 2026](../Notes/stavropoulos-2026-jet-edge-interaction.md), not tagged, p. 15–18).
- *Impinging tones.* In a cold M_j 1.5 LES the bands order the tones by shape, axisymmetric in (0, 1) and (0, 2), helical near (1, 1) and (1, 2), and an axis k–ω spectrum shows upstream waves in the supersonic core along (0, 1)–(0, 3), peaking on k = −ω/a∞ at the tones ([Bogey & Gojon 2017](../Notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 577–579, 583–585). The (0, 2) node at r ≈ 0.54–0.58r0 (note's calculation) matches the LES minimum at ≈ 0.5r0 (p. 583). There the whole upstream branch travels at 0.93–1.00a∞, so the guided loop condition reduces to the free-sound one (note's checks; p. 582). In experiment, tones over M_j 0.7–1.5 die out along S(0, 1), an "almost perfect" match ([Jaunet 2019](../Notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md), p. 7, 9), although that band is isothermal and the cold jet's sits 2–12% higher (note's reproduction). Rectangular impingement is Tam & Norum's case; detail is on [impinging jets and edge tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md).
- *Round-jet screech.* The PIV upstream wave matches the (0, 2) vortex-sheet eigenfunction inside the core but decays too slowly outside, and every A1/A2 tone lies inside its band; the band test is decisive because free-stream sound has similar eigenfunctions ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), p. 9–10). [Gojon 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md) mapped all stages: A1, A2 and u near the (0, 2) lower limit, C and c on the (1, 1) lower limit, B and D outside every band (p. 2919–2920), with LES f–k peaks near those limits (p. 2921); its thin-sheet (1, 1) band misses the dominant C tone (open questions). Prediction models are on [screech frequency and global modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md).
- *Rectangular-jet screech.* [Gojon 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md) applied the planar lower limits to AR 2 LES; heating lowers them (p. 25). Overexpanded tones sit 16–24% above the A1 limit, but hot underexpanded tones fall 8% and 31% below it, as do, by eye, cold experiments above M_j ≈ 1.45 (p. 26; note's arithmetic), and the planar model cannot tell minor-axis from diagonal flapping (p. 21–24). [Lu 2026](../Notes/lu-2026-rectangular-jet-screech-dimensional-reduction.md) shows why planar bands are a weak test. The planar GJM band is several times too wide: the saddle (GJM–duct merger) agrees within 3.4% for AR ≥ 4, but the branch point is far too low even at AR 8, and for AR 2 the tanh-based cross-plane band does not overlap the planar one (p. 16–17); its higher-order GJMs are Nogueira 2024's oblique waves. On a PIV-fitted AR 2 cross-section ("a square with rounded corners", with a much thicker layer) the band stays narrow but drops in frequency (p. 18–19). With a weakest-link closure both models follow the measured flapping tones, including a second band on k_s2 from NPR ≈ 3.5, but the planar model predicts many tones that do not occur and cannot give stage cut-on and cut-off (p. 20–21); by the note's reading of Fig. 24 the cross-plane model misses the dominant tone at NPR 3.3–3.7. PIV gives direct evidence for guided closure: the flapping mode's upstream peak is subsonic and near k_KH − k_sh1 ([Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4214, 4218).

[Nogueira 2024](../Notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) explained what the wave is. In-jet waves are totally reflected by the shear layer, and a standing wave between the layers recovers the double-vortex-sheet dispersion relation exactly (p. 19–22); sound from outside cannot excite it (p. 14); the net energy flux sets its upstream direction and roughly locates the saddles (p. 23–28); and for m = 1 the branch point does not follow the hard-duct rule (p. 30). Screech always needs the guided mode, impingement only when the upstream source sits in the core (p. 31–33). The total reflection is Snell's law across any parallel layer, leaving no wave outside when |a∞k̄ₓ| > 1 ([Suzuki & Lele 2003](../Notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md), p. 146), so Suzuki & Lele's "reflected" rays are the ray limit of the in-jet sound that forms the guided wave (Suzuki note).

Phase-only resonance models over-predict: with finite thickness they give a tone for every integer p from 1 to 7 ([Mancinelli 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), p. 17), as the planar rectangular model does (Lu 2026, p. 20). A complex-ω model with fitted |R₁R₂|(St, M_j) removes most spurious tones, but the fit uses the data it predicts (Mancinelli 2021, p. 17–21); the complex model of Jordan 2018 is fragile near saddles (p. 20–24); and bands plus a standing-wave condition allow crossings with no tone (Bogey & Gojon note). Jaunet 2019 concede that the end phase "technically allows to tune the model" (p. 10), and their dispersive vortex-sheet KH wave travels at ≈ 0.88–1.0U_j (note's reading), well above measured convection speeds (≈ 0.5–0.6U_j; p. 5). Operator models err the other way, amplifying trapped waves more than SPOD shows ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)).

## Wavepacket models

Linear wavepackets also serve as sound sources. The far field samples each wavepacket's axial wavenumber spectrum at the acoustic wavenumber times cos θ, so only supersonic-phase-speed components radiate. [Tam & Burton 1984](../Notes/tam-1984-sound-instability-waves-supersonic-axisymmetric-jets.md) first computed this for a round jet (D(χ) = 2|g(η)|², eq. 5.8, p. 283): on a forced cold M 2.1 jet the near-field SPL lobes come out right after one amplitude fit, "excellent" at St 0.4 and at St 0.2 only after shifting the field downstream, with no far-field test (p. 274, 287–293). [Tam & Chen 1994](../Notes/tam-1994-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md) made it a broadband model of the dominant downstream Mach-wave noise: random amplitudes for every (n, ω), white-noise lip forcing, eddy-viscous local stability, no non-parallel correction (p. 1776–1778). Mach 2 directivities at T_0 = 500 and 1114 K agree within about 2.5 dB at most St, with only n = ±1 radiating in the isothermal jet but n = 0 to ±5 broadening the hot one (p. 1778–1779). The fitted constant differs by a factor of 2.1 between the jets, and St 0.067 at 500 K is about 9 dB low (note's readings), a low-St miss like those above. This structure, one wavepacket per frequency from an inflow amplitude, is what [Cavalieri 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md) find sufficient for supersonic jets (p. 16, 26). The subsonic side, where envelope modulation and intermittency make the sound, is reviewed by [Jordan & Colonius 2013](../Notes/jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise.md) (p. 177, 186–188) and on [jet-noise mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md).

For BBSAN the wavepacket multiplies the shock cells, so envelope length, radial shape and coherence matter, not only wavenumber ([Ray 2005](../Notes/ray-2005-instability-wave-shock-cell-interaction-sound.md); [Wong 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md)); shock harmonics lengthen the envelope ([Nogueira 2022, PFE](../Notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md)). In a kinematic line-source model, coherence decay lowers the BBSAN peak by 2–5 dB and broadens it, and with ten shock-cell modes fills the missing high-frequency upstream sound ([Wong 2019](../Notes/wong-2019-coherence-decay-wavepacket-bbsan.md), p. 978, 984–987). By the note's reading the radiating difference wave k_h − k_s plays the part of the wavepacket wavenumber: jitter attenuates where it is supersonic and amplifies where it is subsonic. Citing Sasaki 2017, the authors hold that linear theory works to St = 4, so the upstream deficit is not its failure (p. 980), and they ask dynamic BBSAN models to be forced stochastically and carry several shock-cell modes (p. 988); Wong 2021 met the second and imposed LES coherence on PSE. Details are on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md); PSE and PFE on [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md).

**Ray models.** [Suzuki & Lele 2003](../Notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md) treat the KH–shock interaction locally: a steep weak shock obeys the eikonal of high-frequency sound, and a Blokhintzev-type invariant carries its strength along ray tubes (eq. 2.14, p. 142–145). A standing shock is evanescent outside a steady layer, so the critical angle lies below the Mach angle and leakage needs unsteady vortices (p. 145–147); the margin is about 29° at M₁ = 1.2 and 7° at 2.5 (note's calculation). The rays run through the unsteady DNS field, not the mean (p. 153), as the POD-reconstructed fields of [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) do (p. 26–28). Fronts match the DNS well, amplitude directivity only qualitatively (p. 159, 163–164). It is the sharp-front counterpart of the product waves of [Tam & Tanna 1982](../Notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md) and Ray 2005 (note's comparison); the leakage physics is on [screech](screech.md).

## Disagreements and open questions

**What selects the screech frequency?** Four views compete (see also [screech frequency and global modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md)): a phase-and-gain loop with reflection coefficients at nozzle and shock ([Mancinelli 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md)); an absolute instability that needs no nozzle, which sets only the amplitude ([Nogueira 2021](../Notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 22); the "weakest link" of lip receptivity ([Tam 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md), not tagged, p. 9–10), essentially the saddle condition ([Nogueira 2022, A1/A2](../Notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 14–15), which also predicts rectangular flapping tones ([Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4219; [Lu 2026](../Notes/lu-2026-rectangular-jet-screech-dimensional-reduction.md), p. 20); and the least-stable global mode ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md); [linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md) asks whether the jet is an oscillator at all). Even linear theory disputes where in an absolute pocket the frequency is set: at the maximum ω₀,ᵢ, at the absolute/convective boundary, or at the complex-X saddle; a wake simulation favours the boundary, which "appears to contradict" the review's saddle ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../Notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md), p. 493–495, 502). *To settle:* vary lip thickness at a fixed mean flow and check whether frequency or only amplitude moves, and measure R₁ and R₂ by projecting LES fields onto k± eigenfunctions (Mancinelli 2021, p. 22).

**Shock-cell questions.** Which shock wavenumber closes each stage, and whether shocks modulate the KH envelope or only superpose separate waves, are on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md) and [screech staging and mode switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md).

**Do guided-mode bands survive heating, thick layers and real cross-sections?** The evidence is mixed.
- [Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md) found "little or no" effect of thickness on the upstream waves, which stay neutral (p. 479); that concerns neutrality more than band edges, so it does not strictly clash with [Mancinelli 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md), where thickening below R/θ ≈ 30 lowers the (0, 2) branch point and saddle (p. 16–17).
- [Tam & Ahuja 1990](../Notes/tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md): at R_j/θ ≈ 6 the (0, 1) branch moves by under 5%, but the (0, 2) branch loses its zero-group-velocity hump and drops by up to about 10% (p. 84; read from figure), so thickening can erase a higher mode's band. The temporal mixing layers of [Bogey & Gojon 2017](../Notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md) (R/δ_θ ≈ 8) keep the bands, slightly above the vortex-sheet curves at large |k| (p. 586–588).
- The thin sheet misses helical tones even in cold jets: [Gojon 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md)'s dominant C tone lies 10–14% below the (1, 1) band, the Monash C tone below the cold limit at M_j 1.45, and six of Bogey & Gojon's seven helical tones 11–26% below theirs (notes' calculations); [Nogueira 2021](../Notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md) needed δ = 0.25 for m = 1 (p. 7).
- Cross-section shape may matter as much as thickness: in the rectangular cross-plane model δ/h = 0.1–0.2 within the tanh family barely moves the band edges, yet a PIV-fitted profile moves them strongly, and the tanh family cannot reach realistic thicknesses without a subsonic centreline ([Lu 2026](../Notes/lu-2026-rectangular-jet-screech-dimensional-reduction.md), p. 5, 18–19, 25). Its branch point also depends on the dimensional reduction (p. 17), and the GJM convergence test varied resolution but not the 40D domain, though the branch-point mode reaches the far field (p. 17, 24).
- Heating: the vortex-sheet band misses hot underexpanded rectangular tones ([Gojon 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), p. 26), and a spreading base flow rescues a rectangular prediction a constant profile misses ([Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4220). But no finite-thickness model has been run for hot jets, cooling and thickening cancel in round jets so a fit can be right for the wrong reason (Mancinelli 2021, p. 15–16), and isothermal against cold moves the (0, 1) edge by 2–12% ([Jaunet 2019](../Notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md) note).

*To settle:* finite-thickness or Floquet LSA on cold and hot LES or PIV means, with the velocity θ (not θ_c; Michalke, p. 162) and the measured cross-section at the resonant station, a varied far-field domain, and the m = 1 branch point, which nobody has computed with finite thickness.

**Can tone data tell guided from free-stream closure?** Near the lower band edge the guided wave travels at about a∞, so loop frequencies, and even f–k peaks on k = −ω/a∞, fit either closure ([Gojon 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md), p. 2919–2921; [Bogey & Gojon 2017](../Notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 582; [Jaunet 2019](../Notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md), p. 7; planar: [Tam & Norum 1992](../Notes/tam-1992-impingement-tones-large-aspect-ratio-rectangular-jets.md) note). An impinging LES could not separate the two ([Karami 2020](../Notes/karami-2020-acoustic-hydrodynamic-waves-underexpanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 16), nor can lip-line k–ω spectra at ΔkD ≈ 1.26 (Bogey & Gojon note). In 2019 this was the open challenge of the upstream process ([Edgington-Mitchell 2019](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2019-aeroacoustic-resonance-review.md), p. 176). What discriminates is band edges (tones dying at S(0, 1)), radial structure (a node inside the core, evanescent decay outside) and k–ω energy on the axis ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), p. 10). *To settle:* axis k–ω spectra with longer, tapered windows, and a test of the outer decay, which no study has made.

**Can linear models reproduce two-point statistics?** Linear modes are fully coherent, yet measured wavepackets decohere exponentially, asymmetrically and, past the core, on eddy scales ([Jaunet 2017](../Notes/jaunet-2017-two-point-coherence-wave-packets-turbulent-jets.md), p. 14–19). Kinematic models impose coherence instead, Gaussian, symmetric and in [Wong 2019](../Notes/wong-2019-coherence-decay-wavepacket-bbsan.md) a constant 1.0D against 0.2–1.9D measured (Jaunet note), and Tam & Chen's white-noise lip forcing has never been checked against near-lip spectra (Tam & Chen note). A stochastically forced operator, P_qq = RP_ffR^H, could predict the coherence, its asymmetry and the phase speeds, and would be the dynamic BBSAN model Wong 2019 asks for (p. 988); no library paper has run one. Whether jet-noise bursts are deterministic nonlinear dynamics or stochastically forced linear ones is also open; two waves of similar frequency make bursts even in a linear system ([Jordan & Colonius 2013](../Notes/jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise.md), p. 188). *To settle:* resolvent or Floquet models with coloured forcing against two-plane coherence maps, and eigenfunction-normalised near-lip spectra to test white lip forcing.

**Flapping axis and helicity.** The LSA makes flapping the more unstable KH mode, but the LES matches the wagging orientation, which the authors attribute to closure; whether energy flows from screech to the streaks or the reverse is open ([Nogueira 2026](../Notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md), p. 21, 25). Likewise, KH growth maps favour SA1 near s/D = 3 ([Rodríguez 2023](../Notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md)), yet 1SS is observed there ([Nogueira & Edgington-Mitchell 2021](../Notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md)). *To settle:* forced LES and cross-plane measurements that tell the symmetries apart.

## Methods and data

- **Inputs.** Only a mean flow is needed, so non-time-resolved PIV is enough: Gaussian or tanh (Michalke profile 2) fits with Crocco–Busemann temperature, splined smoothly in x ([Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../Notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md); [Tam & Burton 1984](../Notes/tam-1984-sound-instability-waves-supersonic-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 283–284), or for rectangular jets a four-tanh product fitted to both PIV planes ([Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md), p. 4212–4213). Near an underexpanded nozzle use the measured or LES profile ([Karami 2020](../Notes/karami-2020-acoustic-hydrodynamic-waves-underexpanded-impinging-jets.md), p. 22, 26). Measure the velocity θ rather than θ_c: it controls guided bands and growth rates more than shock strength does ([Mancinelli 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md); [Ivelja 2024](../Notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md); [Nogueira 2021](../Notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md)). For rectangular jets a planar model suffices for KH-only questions at AR ≥ 4 (usable from AR 2), but guided bands need the cross-plane model on the measured cross-section at the station where the screech SPOD mode peaks ([Lu 2026](../Notes/lu-2026-rectangular-jet-screech-dimensional-reduction.md), p. 12, 18–21).
- **Quick overlays.** Vortex-sheet branch points are closed form (m = 0: [Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), eq. 6.3; m ≥ 1: [Tam & Ahuja 1990](../Notes/tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md), eq. 19; planar: [Tam & Norum 1992](../Notes/tam-1992-impingement-tones-large-aspect-ratio-rectangular-jets.md), eqs. 21–23); upper edges need a root solve. State the temperature ratio: cold and isothermal bands differ by 2–12% ([Jaunet 2019](../Notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md) note).
- **Numerics.** Inviscid local solvers shoot from Hankel starting values outside and the bounded Frobenius solution at the axis, detour around the critical layer in complex r for damped waves, and take the second Riemann sheet for damped supersonic waves (Tam & Burton, p. 286); a weak eddy viscosity, raised until results converge, avoids the detour ([Tam & Chen 1994](../Notes/tam-1994-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1778–1779). SP-LSA takes about 3 min on a laptop (Nogueira 2021, p. 6).
- **Validation data.** *Time-resolved* (schlieren, microphones, LES): SPOD modes and k–St maps ([Towne 2017](../Notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md)); k–ω spectra on the axis and lip line, split into n = 0 and 1 ([Bogey & Gojon 2017](../Notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md); [Gojon 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md)), though 4.5–5D lines give ΔkD ≈ 1.26–1.4, too coarse to tell C = −0.93 from −1 (notes' arithmetic). Two-point coherence and phase speeds need simultaneous time-resolved data at both points (two cross-planes or azimuthal rings), with a convective time shift before Welch averaging at long separations ([Jaunet 2017](../Notes/jaunet-2017-two-point-coherence-wave-packets-turbulent-jets.md), p. 8–9). *Snapshot PIV*: a POD-pair complex mode, split by the sign of k_x, at a tone fixed by a microphone ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md); Ivelja 2024). Negative k_x is not upstream: duct-like waves have negative phase speed but positive group velocity (Gojon 2018 note; Towne 2017, p. 8, 15). A radial-node check is cheap: the (0, 2) node sits at r ≈ 0.54–0.58r0 at M_j 1.5 (Bogey & Gojon note). The β projection compares model and measured wavepackets ([Cavalieri 2013](../Notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md), eq. 4.11).

## Relevance to our work

- **Lab screech, impinging and non-axisymmetric data.** Overlay finite-thickness guided-mode bands, built from measured R/θ and temperature, on St–NPR maps. For a first look, the cold thin-sheet (0, 2) branch point puts the Monash A1 and A2 tones (St 0.67, 0.65 at M_j 1.09, 1.14) 6% and 10% above it, and the Monash C tone below the (1, 1) band ([Gojon 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md) note; [Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md) note). For the AR 2 rectangular jets, planar limits (Gojon 2019, eq. 16) are only a quick look; stage cut-on and cut-off need the cross-plane model on the measured cross-section ([Lu 2026](../Notes/lu-2026-rectangular-jet-screech-dimensional-reduction.md), p. 20–21). Split SPOD or POD-pair modes by the sign of k_x and check the upstream peak against the band, which exists only over a short x range ([Ivelja 2024](../Notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md)). Bicoherence or BMD would test which impinging tones are sum and difference products of two loops, which Tam & Norum matched by hand (note's suggestion). Record lip thickness: fitted reflection phases differ between rigs with 15% and 3% lips ([Stavropoulos 2023](../Notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md), p. 16).
- **SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Every model here is stationary and single-frequency, so none speaks to intermittency or switching. Testable links to HPOD amplitudes near a staging boundary come from Floquet growth rates ([shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md)) and global modes ([linear operator methods](linear-operator-methods.md)); instantaneous tone frequencies can be checked against band edges. A reconstruction from SPOD mode 1 alone has unit coherence, like a linear mode; one from several modes keeps the measured decoherence, and HPOD of it gives a time-domain version of Jaunet's statistics (Jaunet note's suggestion).
- **PyFR staging LES.** The LES mean can supply θ_R(x), shock positions and the k_s spectrum for local and SP-LSA models, and k± projections could estimate the R₁R₂ that [Mancinelli 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) had to fit (p. 22). The weakest-link recipe of [Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md) (p. 4213, 4219), with the guided band computed at the station where the screech SPOD mode peaks (Lu 2026), is cheap enough for every continuation step and would test whether A1 closes on k_s1 and A2 on k_s2 (our inference). n = 0 k–ω spectra at r = 0.1–1r0 should peak near the cold (0, 2) branch point, St 0.634 at M_j 1.09 and 0.592 at 1.14 (note's arithmetic). Azimuthal probe rings every 0.5D would give coherence lengths at the tone and its neighbours, a measure of how loop locking changes across A1/A2 (Jaunet note's suggestion). Comparing ω₀,ᵢ or the loop ω_i with the measured 1/t_switch is our inference, untested.
- **Rocket noise.** Rocket plumes are hotter and faster than the KH merging limit (M_j ≈ 2 at T_j/T∞ ≈ 3), so by the vortex sheet the supersonic family, not one KH wavepacket, should dominate ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), p. 459, 480; note's inference). In the stochastic Mach-wave model heating already spreads the sound over n = 0 to ±5, and the calibration constant changed twofold between two lab temperatures, so a rocket application needs its own calibration or a lip-receptivity estimate ([Tam & Chen 1994](../Notes/tam-1994-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), p. 1779; note's inference). See [rocket-noise models](rocket-noise-models.md); the heated BBSAN case is on [shock-cell models](shock-cell-models.md).
