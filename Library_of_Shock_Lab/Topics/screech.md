# Screech

_Last updated: 2026-10-22 · 43 papers_

## Scope

Screech in free, shock-containing jets: how the feedback loop closes, where shock leakage radiates the tone and where the source sits, amplitude and nozzle receptivity, and triadic interactions and mean-flow deformation. Frequency prediction, absolute instability and global modes, and the comparison of LES and linear models with experiment are on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md); staging, mode switching and multimodal loops on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md). Rectangular and elliptical jets ([non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md)) and twin-jet coupling ([twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md)) appear here only where they test the mechanism. Local and guided-jet-wave models are in [linear-models](linear-models.md), shock-cell models and Floquet-in-x analysis in [shock-cell-models](shock-cell-models.md), global stability and resolvent analysis in [linear-operator-methods](linear-operator-methods.md), BBSAN in [jet-noise-mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md), impinging and edge tones in [impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md), and SPOD/BMD/HPOD practice in [modal-decomposition](modal-decomposition.md) and [time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md).

## Current understanding

1. **The loop has four links**: KH growth from the lip, interaction with the shock cells, an upstream wave, and receptivity at the lip ([Raman 1998](../Notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) p. 47–49; [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) p. 2). Apart from the KH–shock interaction, the frequency-selecting links appear linear (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves, p. 24; [Mancinelli 2019](../Notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) p. 8), and an integer number of periods spans the lip to one dominant source ([Mercier 2017](../Notes/mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) p. 219–224).
2. **The upstream wave is the guided jet mode (G-JM), not free-stream sound.** Its core support and propagative band match A1/A2 PIV ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) p. 9–11), in-jet LES spectra of a helical C jet ([Gojon 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md) p. 2921–2923) and cold rectangular-jet tones ([Gojon 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md) p. 26); free-stream closure predicts tones where none exist (Mancinelli 2019, p. 6; [Mancinelli 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) p. 15); and in-jet sound is totally reflected by the shear layer, while outside sound cannot enter the core travelling upstream ([Nogueira 2024](../Notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) p. 14–17, 32).
3. **A KH–shock triad at the Tam & Tanna difference wavenumber k_kh − k_s energises the G-JM** ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) p. 32–34; [Tam & Tanna 1982](../Notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md)), as PIV (with a duct-like k_kh + k_s wave; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves, p. 14–17), schlieren, LES and global models confirm ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) p. 12–13; [Jeun 2024](../Notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md) p. 13–14; [Farghadan 2026](../Notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) p. 15–17), also in the resonant PIV mode of a rectangular jet ([Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md) p. 4218).
4. **Stages close on different shock wavenumbers and exclude each other near a single-jet staging point** ([Nogueira 2022](../Notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) p. 7–13; Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 11–13; Mancinelli 2019, p. 6–7).
5. **Frequency is predictable to a few per cent; amplitude is not** (see "Frequency prediction and global modes" below).
6. **The nozzle and its surroundings set amplitude and mode ranges, by tens of dB, far more than frequency** ([Ponton 1992](../Notes/ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) p. 537–548; [Powell et al. 1992](../Notes/powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md) p. 2835; Raman 1998, p. 75–78; [André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md) p. 666–668).
7. **Sound leaves near the 3rd–4th shock, at the saddles between vortices, not at the wavepacket peak.** Phase-averaged near-field pressure ([Panda 1999](../Notes/panda-1999-experimental-investigation-screech-noise-generation.md) p. 89–93), microphone phase (Mercier 2017, p. 219–222), PIV statistics and coherent vorticity ([Edgington-Mitchell 2014, helical mode](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2014-coherent-structure-helical-screech-mode.md) p. 840–844; [Alkislar 2003](../Notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md) p. 142–144) and ray tracing ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) p. 19–30) agree to about one cell, on the mechanism computed by [Suzuki & Lele 2003](../Notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md) (p. 145–149, 153–155). This contradicts [Tam 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md) (p. 4).
8. **The far-field tone is not the loop.** Round and low-AR jets radiate through acoustically matched wavenumbers and through shock leakage, now seen directly (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 13–19), both by-products of the triad (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 20–22; Nogueira 2024, p. 32). Of four loops in an AR 2 rectangular jet only the flapping one reaches the far field (Nogueira 2025, p. 4217, 4220).
9. **Screech reshapes its own jet**: it damps the mean shock train (André 2013, p. 667–668), speeds spreading ([Krothapalli 1986](../Notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md) p. 123) and gives each loop its own mean ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) p. 3, 16–19).

## Closure: from Powell's loop to the guided jet mode

Powell's model (Raman 1998, p. 48–49) treats the shock cells as a phased array of monopoles, giving f = U_c/[s(1 + M_c)]. [Tam 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md) (p. 7–10) made screech the θ → 180° limit of BBSAN: the inverse BBSAN peak frequency, linear in cos θ, extrapolates onto the tone, and only the band radiated straight at the lip sustains the loop (the "weakest link"), so the loop phase does not choose the frequency. [Tam 1988](../Notes/tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) (p. 10–12) extended this to rectangular jets, with spacing set by the minor dimension. Panda 1999 made the near-field standing wave the observable: r.m.s. crests at f_s, 20 dB above the troughs, sit at L_sw from 1/L_sw = 1/λ_s + 1/λ_h, not at the shock spacing (0.66D against 0.77D at M_j 1.19, 1.03D against 1.28D at 1.42; Table 1, p. 79–81). His L_sw form of Powell's formula is an identity (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 3) whose content, L_sw ≈ 0.8L, is Tam's empirical factor (p. 82–83). He could not say why it needs no shock spacing (p. 94). Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 5, 11) reconciled the two models: k_sw follows the shock peak that closes the loop, k_s1 in A1 and B and k_s2 in A2 and C, and both of Panda's jets (A2 and helical) have k_sw above the mean shock wavenumber (Panda note). All of these models close the loop with free-stream sound (Tam 1995, p. 37).

The guided-mode picture came from subsonic jets, whose trapped duct-like waves and upstream "discrete free-stream" mode propagate only between neutral saddle points, bounding the near-nozzle tones ([Towne 2017](../Notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) p. 14–19). Edgington-Mitchell 2018 (p. 6–11) split the leading POD pair of A1 and A2 jets (NPR 2.10, 2.25) by the sign of k_x and found an upstream wave at about a fifth of the KH amplitude, which only the band test could separate from the acoustic continuum (p. 10). In the same year Gojon 2018 found it in LES of an underexpanded M_j 1.56 jet (NPR 4.03, Re_j 6 × 10⁴; two helical C tones and an axisymmetric u tone): in-jet n = 0 and n = 1 density f–k spectra peak at the tones on k ≈ −ω/a₀, at the lower limits of the S2 and H1 vortex-sheet bands, with eigenfunction-like profiles (p. 2921–2923), and a band map puts A1, A2 and u near the S2 limit, C on H1, and B and D outside (p. 2919–2920). Near a lower limit the wave moves at a₀ in phase and group velocity (p. 2919–2920), which is why free-stream loop models work as well as they do and why Mercier's ±10 % return speed cannot decide (Gojon 2018 note). By that note the evidence is weaker than claimed. There the guided mode has the sound-line wavenumber (kD_j −2.27 against −2.25), so only the profiles discriminate (Edgington-Mitchell 2018's caveat, p. 10), and they stop at r/D_j 0.45; a 4.5D_j line (Δk D_j ≈ 1.4) can explain the spread blamed on scattering; and the dominant tone lies 10–14 % below the thin-sheet H1 limit.

Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves (p. 14–23) added the downstream duct-like wave, seen earlier on positive-group-velocity branches at negative k (Gojon 2018, p. 2921), and traced the standing wave and core modulation mostly to G-JM–KH interference, not to shocks modulating KH growth. That interference beats at 2π/k_s, so the earlier C-mode coherent-stress peaks just before each reflection point (Edgington-Mitchell 2014, helical mode, p. 836) cannot tell the two pictures apart (that note). Panda took the upstream wave to be free-stream sound, but his traverse (from r/D 0.6–0.7) sees both the KH + G-JM and the KH + sound standing waves (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 9–11). His shear-layer "vortex speed modulation" at L_sw (p. 84–86) is most simply interference in a line-of-sight phase, needing an upstream-to-KH ratio of 0.25–0.35, near Edgington-Mitchell 2018's 1:5 (Fig. 5, p. 8; Panda note). Nogueira 2024 (p. 19–33) supplies the physics: the G-JM is in-jet sound in a duct whose wall impedance runs from hard at the sonic line to soft at large |α|, and the evanescent outer field carries net energy upstream. Because the triad sits inside the shear layer, screech always needs the G-JM; impingement tones need it only when their source is in the core (p. 31–33; contrast the wall-jet shocklet in [Weightman 2019](../Notes/weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md) and [Edgington-Mitchell 2025](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md), both not tagged).

The closure generalises. An AR 2 elliptical jet's se₁ G-JM is neutral at the tone only over a short x band, where its measured amplitude peaks ([Ivelja 2024](../Notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) p. 22–24). In twin round jets the G-JM band, not KH growth, selects the coupling symmetry ([Nogueira 2021, twin jets](../Notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md) p. 16–19; [Stavropoulos 2023](../Notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) p. 18–19). In twin rectangular LES the G-JM gain (10.1) dwarfs that of free-stream sound (1.16), and its points of return sit at peaks of shock–KH interaction (Jeun 2024, p. 20–21). Single AR 2 rectangular LES (TR 1–3) put the NPR 3 tones only in the first antisymmetric planar band, with in-jet spectra peaking on k = −ω/a₀ (Gojon 2019, p. 24–31; branch points as in Nogueira 2024, p. 23). That is band membership only, with no eigenfunction match, and a 10h window (Δkh_j ≈ 0.6) cannot separate in-jet sound from a nearby A1 wave (Gojon 2019 note). Nogueira 2025 gives the stronger rectangular test. In a sharp-throat AR 2 C-D jet, the k_x spectrum of the flapping PIV POD mode has a subsonic upstream peak near k_KH − k_sh1 (about 0.85c∞ at NPR 4.5 by the note's reading), so the dominant loop closes through the G-JM on the first shock peak (p. 4218). Negative k_x is not upstream group velocity: the k_kh − k_s2 wave in an A1 jet is duct-like and cannot close the loop (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 13; Towne 2017, p. 8).

## Shock leakage and source location

**Where the sound leaves.** Raman's "feedback shock" from one dominant source in AR ≳ 9 rectangular jets was unexplained (p. 70–73), and his localisation (3rd shock at M_j 1.45; p. 71–73) assumes one source. So does Mercier 2017: near-field phase reversal across ten round jets puts the source at the 4th shock tip for A1 and A2 and the 3rd or 4th for flapping B, to one cell (p. 215, 219–222, Table 5), where Mancinelli fixed his reflection (Mancinelli 2019, p. 4–5; Mancinelli 2021, p. 7). Its return wave, known only to c₀ ± 10 %, cannot tell the G-JM from sound (p. 213, 226). Panda 1999 saw the wave born in phase-averaged near-field pressure at M_j 1.19 (A2 by Mercier's Table 1, p. 204). The edge of a passing hydrodynamic compression or rarefaction bulges radially between the 3rd and 4th shocks (2.37 < x/D < 3.07) and splits; its outer part runs upstream in "pause-and-go" fashion, gaining strength over same-sign regions (p. 89–93). This matches Mercier's 4th-shock A2 source, the A2 G-JM peak (Edgington-Mitchell 2018, p. 6) and the 4th-cell A2 leak below (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 23–27): it is the near-field pressure picture of leakage, without k_x < 0 filtering (Panda note). Powell et al. 1992's centroids at 5–6D, estimated from spark photos (p. 2833; Powell note), are the outlier Mercier lists (Table 1, p. 204); their instability-wave peak moves from 3–5D (A) to 8–10D (D) (p. 2827, 2833–2834).

**The leakage mechanism.** Suzuki & Lele 2003 supply the theory the group's leakage work builds on. A weak shock obeys the eikonal of high-frequency sound, and a steady layer always totally reflects it, since the critical angle θ₁^cr = arccos[−1/(a₂/a₁ + M₁)] lies below the Mach angle for any temperature ratio (p. 142–147). In a 2-D DNS of a forced M₁ = 1.2 mixing layer the shock leaks near the saddles between vortices, where the vorticity that rotates the wavefront normal back into the jet is weak; the saddle's strain, turning leaking rays outward by −60° to −97°, matters as much (p. 147–149, 153–156). The leaking front kinks into a compression and an expansion branch and spreads almost circularly; fronts match the DNS, amplitudes only qualitatively (p. 158–164). Leaks grow as vortices develop (4–8 of 251 rays at the first saddle, 9–12 at the second; p. 155) and fade once small eddies blur the saddles, their explanation of 2nd–4th-cell sources (p. 164).

**Vorticity proxies and direct observation.** In an AR 4 C-D rectangular jet (M_j 1.69) the circulation of phase-locked stereo-PIV structures peaks at the ends of the 3rd and 5th cells, highest at the 3rd (Alkislar 2003, p. 142–144), where Krothapalli 1986 put the source (p. 123). In the group's NPR 3.4 C-mode PIV, u′ kurtosis (≈ 7 at the 3rd reflection point, ≈ 1.5 at the 1st, read) and high-speed-side coherent vorticity fluctuation peak over cells 2–4, near x/D ≈ 4.35, while coherent energy grows to the 6th cell (Edgington-Mitchell 2014, helical mode, p. 837, 840–844). The authors put the sources where vortices most weaken the vorticity at the sonic line, Suzuki & Lele's criterion, not at the wavepacket peak (p. 843–845), though kurtosis marks any shock motion (that note). Flapping coherent vorticity in an AR 2 ellipse likewise peaks just before the 3rd shock ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) p. 2747). Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, then saw leakage in k_x < 0-filtered 1 Mfps schlieren: the shock tip overshoots the sonic line as a KH vortex passes and sheds a front that is sharp from birth (p. 3–4, 13–19). Ray tracing on phase-reconstructed PIV, the 2014 data included, puts the escape at the saddle between vortices (p. 26–27), first at the 3rd cell for A1 and C (x/D ≈ 4.3 at NPR 3.4, as in Raman and the 2014 statistics) and the 4th for A2. The site moves between the 6th and 3rd with vortex strength, close to the 3rd–5th scatter in the schlieren (p. 19–30): Mercier's cell for A2 and one upstream for A1, within both methods' resolution. The 2014 "largest vorticity fluctuation" rule holds at NPR 3.4 but fails at 2.25, where incidence angle matters as much (p. 26–28), so a circulation or vorticity peak marks where leakage can start, not the emission point. None of this supports a source at the instability-wave peak (Tam 1986, p. 4; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 26). One shock-like, upstream-directed source explains the feedback shock (p. 21–23), though several cells emit (p. 28), as Panda's wave gathers strength from "multiple sources". Closure was not tested in any of these; Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 20–22) treats leakage as a correlated sound source that may close the loop only at high AR.

## Frequency prediction and global modes

Linear models predict the tone to 1–10 % and LES to about 0.01 in St. The successful models, G-JM resonance loops (Mancinelli 2019, 2021), the Floquet saddle ([Nogueira 2021, absolute instability](../Notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md)), local stability on PIV means (Nogueira 2022; Nogueira 2025) and global or resolvent analysis (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves; Farghadan 2026), largely coincide, and all are more sensitive to shear-layer thickness than to shock strength. A model base flow is absolutely unstable while the measured mean's least-damped global mode is slightly stable, so whether screech is a self-excited global mode is open. U_c rises along the jet, so Powell-type formulas need the source position (Mercier 2017). LES matches frequency but not yet the heating trend of amplitude (Gojon 2019). The prediction table, convection speed, the global-mode question, what the models say about amplitude and how LES and linear models compare with experiment are on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md).

## Staging and mode switching

Screech jumps between Merle's A1 and A2 (axisymmetric), B (flapping), C (helical) and D stages (Raman 1998, p. 49, 52, 101). Powell et al. 1992 mapped them by azimuthal phase on a thick-lip nozzle: A1 and A2 varicose, B and D one sinuous family joined by a secondary tone, C helical, with hysteresis only at C ↔ D (p. 2825–2830, 2833–2834). A1 and B close on the primary shock wavenumber k_s1, A2 and C on a suboptimal k_s2 (Nogueira 2022, p. 7–13; Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 11–13), seen in Mercier's data as one extra loop period at a fixed 4th-shock source (Mercier 2017, p. 222–224). Near a single-jet staging point the tones exclude each other, each on its own short-time mean (Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 11–13). In an AR 2 rectangular jet, by contrast, weaker varicose, second-flapping and wagging loops appear beside the dominant flapping one (Nogueira 2025, p. 4214–4215). Selection, hysteresis, rig dependence, multimodal and twin-jet loops, and the PyFR continuation LES that traces the A1/A2 limits are on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md).

## Amplitude and receptivity

Raman (1998, p. 49) writes the limit cycle as growth × shock interaction × feedback efficiency × receptivity = 1. Shock strength keeps rising with M_j while amplitude plateaus at 154–155 dB over M_j 1.3–1.65 and then collapses (p. 64). Powell et al. 1992 saw the same saturation: near-field OASPL rises from ≈ 100 to ≈ 128 dB through A into B, then stays at 125–130 dB through B, C and D, "some sort of limit cycle" (Fig. 17, p. 2832, 2834). Alkislar 2003 (p. 143–144, 153) credits coherent vorticity plus shock strength, from a higher 3rd-cell circulation at the loud M_j 1.69 than at 1.74. That is one phase and two conditions, M_j 1.74 is only about 1–3 dB quieter, and its circulation is higher near the nozzle (Alkislar note). It is one of few measured flow-side handles, but Raman's plateau shows that one local strength cannot fix the amplitude.

The lip and surroundings matter most. Thickening the lip from t/D 0.015 to 0.625 keeps screech going to M_j ≈ 1.95 rather than 1.6–1.7, revives the B mode above M_j 1.7 and shifts B and C wavelengths slightly in opposite directions (Ponton 1992, p. 537–541). A thicker lip gives more loop gain (Powell et al. 1992, p. 2835), and on Powell's t/D 0.8 lip the sinuous D takes over from M_j ≈ 1.62–1.67 (p. 2826), probably the family Ponton calls a re-emerging B (Ponton 1992, p. 537; Powell note's inference). A thick lip raised screech by 10 dB, a flapping plane rotated about 90° between nominally identical tests, and heating makes jets quieter (Tam 1995, p. 23, 36, 39–40). Reflectors change amplitude and frequency by detuning the loop phase (Raman 1998, p. 77–78); Panda's 305 mm nozzle flange was the main reflector, and covering it changed both (Panda 1999, p. 73). The Monash upstream plate produced an unusual dominant m = 0 mode at NPR 3.3 (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 16). Identical twin nozzles on two Monash rigs, one with hard enclosure walls and a flush plenum face, shift the onset of the lower tone, while OSU's facility changes the staging most at NPR 3.0–4.0 ([Bell 2021](../Notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md) p. 6, 17–18, 35). A small tab removes the tones, lowering peak narrowband levels by about 16–21 dB ([Norum 1982](../Notes/norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md) p. 149, 162), and asymmetric trailing-edge cutouts suppress overexpanded rectangular-jet screech while symmetric ones amplify it ([Kerechanin 2001](../Notes/kerechanin-2001-trailing-edge-rectangular-jet-acoustics.md) p. 4–5). Twenty-four shallow lip notches (1 mm × 4 mm, t/D 0.013) remove the tones at M_j 1.15–1.50 and weaken them at 1.10 (about 8 dB, shifted to Sr ≈ 0.69, read) while, unlike tabs, leaving the first cells' spacing and strength unchanged (André 2013, p. 666–668); the authors suggest disrupted near-lip receptivity, perhaps with slot ejection blocking the upstream waves, "to be verified" (p. 668). A sharp-throat C-D nozzle is never shock-free and still screeches, more weakly, at its design NPR 3.7 (Nogueira 2025, p. 4214), as does the OSU nozzle of the same design ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../Notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md) p. 4, 19). An untripped rectangular LES with every tone frequency right has screech rising from 151 to 177 dB/St over TR 1 → 3 where the experiment falls (Gojon 2019, p. 18–19, 21–22), probably because of its weakly turbulent exit boundary layer (1.75 % peak, p. 8–10) and the lower Re of the hot cases (Gojon 2019 note).

What the absolute-instability and resonance models can say about amplitude is on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md). Screech also changes the jet. At peak screech an AR 16.7 rectangular jet spreads at 36° rather than 20–25° (Krothapalli 1986, p. 123), and an AR 4 jet's minor-axis spreading rate rises from 0.065 (ideally expanded) to 0.11 and then 0.33 (Alkislar 2003, p. 135–136). Strong screech adds about 3 dB to mixing noise ([Tam 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), not tagged), consistent with the 2–4 dB lower broadband level below Sr ≈ 0.6 once André's notches remove the tones (André note's reading of Fig. 2).

## Triads, harmonics and mean-flow deformation

Beyond the KH–shock triad, three kinds of interaction matter. **Between tones:** A1–A2 modulation at St₂ − St₁ during switching (Mancinelli 2019, p. 7); and above M_j ≈ 1.25 the B tone appears in the m = 0 spectrum as a quadratic m = ±1 product (cross-bicoherence ≈ 1 at St 0.34), not as an axisymmetric loop (Mancinelli 2021, p. 24–25). **Harmonics:** a bilinear harmonic resolvent on the PIV mean plus the screech mode reproduces the St = 0 shock-cell correction and the 2St_s and 3St_s sideline beams; the harmonics radiate from k_kh − k_s1 although the loop closes on k_s2, and forcing with the screech self-interaction matches the optimal harmonic responses (inner products > 0.95 per frequency) without appeal to turbulence (Farghadan 2026, p. 20–25). Panda's near field agrees: the 2f_s harmonic is generated in the flow, with beams at 90° and about 120° (Panda 1999, p. 83). In a screeching jet the BBSAN peak at 90° follows 2St_s through every stage jump (André 2013, p. 669); by the André note's reading the harmonic's sideline radiation (k_kh(2St_s) − k_s1 ≈ 0; Farghadan 2026, p. 23) may pull the hump, which BMD at (St_s, St_s) would test. **Mean flow:** the St_s − St_s = 0 self-interaction distorts the mean, so each loop has its own short-time mean and shock spacing both selects the loop and is changed by it (Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 3, 16–19), as Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 12) inferred from jumps in the shock spectrum. Krothapalli 1986 is the long-time-average precursor. André 2013 measures it most cleanly, against a notched, screech-free twin nozzle: at M_j 1.35 the screeching jet shows about 6 shock cells against 11 (note's count), and its centreline pressure oscillation collapses just downstream of x/D ≈ 5.3, where strong tones first appear, while the screech-free train lasts to ≈ 11 (Fig. 5, p. 667–668, read). High-speed schlieren points to screech-driven oscillation and mixing (p. 668), the effect Farghadan's St = 0 correction models. **Flapping and helicity:** in |m| = 1 round-jet screech the (±1, St_s) ↔ (2, 0) triad lets weak |m| = 2 streaks split the m = ±1 eigenvalues into flapping and wagging modes and fix the axis, which in LES precesses with the streaks by about π/2 over about 100 cycles ([Nogueira 2026](../Notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) p. 7–12, 20). Powell et al. 1992 reported the facts behind this: B keeps a preferred plane with spasmodic flips and a noise-like envelope; D's plane dwells, vibrates or rotates (the equivalent of ≈ 13 to ≈ 90 Hz), modulating the tone almost 100 % at a fixed point; and C is very stable, of either sense with about equal probability, possibly changing sense on restart (Figs 10, 12–16, p. 2829–2832). His linear superposition (a sinuous mode is two counter-rotating helices) is the degenerate case Nogueira 2026 starts from, and his view that nonlinear sinuous and helical modes are "essentially different" (p. 2833–2834) anticipates the streak-induced splitting. The helical C mode reverses its helicity often ([Edgington-Mitchell 2023](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2023-screeching-jet-seen-from-all-sides.md) p. 4). The group's early "single helix" from centreline shock motion (Edgington-Mitchell 2014, helical mode, p. 838) excludes m = 2 but not flapping; Edgington-Mitchell 2023 (p. 3–4) treats helicity as unproven until Léon et al. 2022.

## Disagreements and open questions

Questions on staging (labelling A2, hysteresis, which instability screeches) are on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md), and those on what selects the frequency, self-excitation and amplitude prediction on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md).

**Leakage as closure.** Round-jet evidence is against it. Leaked fronts are born outside the jet, which cannot feed the G-JM (Nogueira 2024, p. 14, 32), so Panda's "fully formed acoustic wave approaching the nozzle" (p. 93) is radiation, not necessarily the return path (Panda note). At NPR 2.10 the first leak (x/D ≈ 1.8; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 23) lies upstream of the G-JM peak (≈ 3.3; Edgington-Mitchell 2018, p. 6), though at NPR 2.25 both sit near x/D ≈ 3. Suzuki & Lele do not address closure. But their totally reflected rays obey the same Snell's-law condition as the G-JM band of Nogueira 2024 (p. 15–17), so one shock–vortex event might feed both the G-JM and the tone (Suzuki & Lele note's reading). Correlating each cycle's leakage with G-JM amplitude at the lip would separate them; at high AR (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 21–22), tones outside the G-JM band would show leakage closure.

**Directivity.** In the ray model one leakage source already radiates along Norum's 160° peak (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 21–23), but from ray streaks without amplitude. Suzuki & Lele's DNS computes the opposite for one 2-D event: mainly sideline radiation, integrated intensity at about 74–84° from the downstream axis, and amplitude falling towards zero beyond roughly 120–130° (Figs 24–27, p. 162–163, read). That favours the "superposition of many sources" view, and the authors say superposition changes the pattern "considerably" (p. 163–164), though their case is one planar layer in the near field (R ≈ 14 δ_V). Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 20) gets the upstream/downstream balance from a distributed Mach-wave-type source. All are qualitative. For the harmonic, the leakage paper's microphones stop at 120° but show the C-mode first harmonic peaking at 90° (p. 6), as in Tam 1995 (p. 21–23), and an AR 4 rectangular jet's first harmonic (≈ 132 dB) slightly exceeds its fundamental (≈ 129 dB) at the sideline (Alkislar 2003, Fig. 6, read). Both fit Powell's antisymmetric-array directivity, with its harmonic lobe at 90° (Raman 1998, p. 49), and Panda's flow-generated 2f_s beams lie at 90° and about 120° (p. 83). Krothapalli 1986, by contrast, found the fundamental dominating at 90° in an AR 16.7 convergent jet (p. 125). Far-field angles to 160° and LES with amplitude would decide.

**Flapping, helical and precession.** Whether streaks anchor screech or screech drives streaks is open (Nogueira 2026, p. 25), as is whether stage C corresponds to weak streaks. Powell's candidate causes, unsteady initial swirl and irregular downstream motion (p. 2832, 2834), are alternatives to streaks. Forced LES, a swirl-controlled or streak-forced test, or multi-view time-resolved schlieren would separate them.

**Full scale.** Screech is absent from rocket and afterburning-engine data ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 9, 16; [Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 6). Heating weakens it (Tam 1995, p. 39–40), though not in the one heated LES (above). Suzuki & Lele's mixing layer is ambiguous too: the critical-angle margin shrinks in hot jets (p. 147) and heating tilts the leaked sound about 10° downstream, but the DNS total amplitude falls (p. 163). Their point that turbulence scatters thin shock fronts into broadband shock noise (p. 164) is consistent with screech vanishing at full scale. In a planar vortex sheet heating lowers the G-JM bands (Gojon 2019, Fig. 20, p. 25), and the hot underexpanded tones (TR 2 and 3 at NPR 4) already fall 8–31 % below the A1 band (Gojon 2019 note's arithmetic), which the authors blame on the thin-shear-layer idealisation (p. 26). A cold helical LES tone falls below the thin-sheet H1 band as well (Gojon 2018 note). A finite-thickness or LES-mean-flow band calculation for hot, strongly off-design jets would show whether the band misses hot plumes.

## Methods and data

Staging-specific methods (shock-spectrum peaks, switch tracking, loop counts) are on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md), and imaging and PIV practice on [schlieren-and-density-imaging](schlieren-and-density-imaging.md) and [diagnostics-and-facilities](diagnostics-and-facilities.md).
- **Non-time-resolved PIV**: POD of transverse velocity, ring check on the pair, complex mode, FFT in x, split by the sign of k_x (Edgington-Mitchell 2018; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves, p. 9–17; Ivelja 2024; Nogueira 2025, p. 4217–4218). The POD-pair triple decomposition and higher-order statistics began with the 2014 C-mode data (Edgington-Mitchell 2014, helical mode, p. 833–841); microphone-triggered phase locking is the older alternative, needing a trackable tone and, with a mirrored half-cycle, exact antisymmetry (Alkislar 2003, p. 128–131). The reconstructed cycle feeds leakage ray tracing (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 11–13), and a POD-pair rebuild gives coherent vorticity at the shock tips as a source proxy (Edgington-Mitchell 2015, p. 2747). The route needs a single steady tone and hides helicity sign (Edgington-Mitchell 2023).
- **Time-resolved schlieren**: SPOD at the tone and the standing-wave wavenumber (Edgington-Mitchell 2022), Hilbert phase (Jeun 2024), and k_x < 0 filtering for single, intermittent leakage events (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 10, 19). SPOD waterfalls in two orthogonal views find every loop and its symmetry, and an empirical k–ω dispersion relation shows the KH, guided and duct branches (Nogueira 2025, p. 4213–4215).
- **LES**: azimuthally split f–k spectra inside the jet test for guided modes (Gojon 2018, p. 2921–2923), given a line long enough to resolve them (Gojon 2018 note).
- **Microphones**: phase-averaged axial surveys against a fixed reference locate the dominant source; radial surveys cannot, and beyond about 5D reflections take over even in an anechoic room (Mercier 2017, p. 216–221). Phase-averaged near-field maps show sound inception (Panda 1999, p. 87–93); two-microphone azimuthal phase over restarts identifies the mode type (Powell et al. 1992, p. 2828–2830); and a notched twin nozzle with unchanged first cells gives a screech-free reference (André 2013, p. 666–668).
- **Pitfalls**: phase and group velocity can differ in sign; precession splits a tone across SPOD modes 1 and 2 (Nogueira 2026, p. 22); shocks inside a PIV window smear over ≈ 0.2D ([Edgington-Mitchell 2014](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2014-underexpanded-jet-mach-disk-shear-layer.md) p. 16–17). Panda could not separate same-frequency hydrodynamic and acoustic parts (p. 89), which k_x splitting now does, and any local phase speed from a field holding counter-propagating waves swings at L_sw (Figs 7–8, p. 84–87). His "order of magnitude" hydrodynamic-to-acoustic ratio (p. 81) cannot give his 20 dB crest–trough contrast (p. 79), which needs a ratio near 0.8 (Panda note). Model inputs and validation data are discussed on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md).
- **Data tables**: tones and shock spacings in `Data/screech_tones.csv` and `Data/shock_cell_spacing.csv`.

## Relevance to our work

**SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Take the Hilbert transform in time, or along −x for the G-JM, since along +x it discards the k_x < 0 G-JM (Edgington-Mitchell 2022 note). Split by the sign of k_x before reading an instantaneous wavenumber or convection speed, or Panda's L_sw modulation appears (Panda note). Conditioning the k_x < 0 field on each cycle's KH amplitude would replace the static r̄ ± 2σ rescaling of the leakage source (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 28–30) and test Alkislar's amplitude claim at the radiating cell (Alkislar note); a precessing flapping plane should appear as a slow phase drift between the two leading HPOD modes (Powell note).

**PyFR staging LES** (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`). Its tests of staging limits, hysteresis, receptivity, leakage location and loop count are on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md). It also offers the conclusive self-excitation test, an NPR step ([screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md)), and a check of how amplitude depends on the exit boundary layer (Gojon 2019). Cheap checks on converged A1 and A2 states: Panda's crest spacing should jump from k_s1 to k_s2 (Panda note), n = 0 f–k spectra should peak at the S2 lower limit (Gojon 2018 note), and the louder state should have the higher coherent circulation at its radiating cell (Alkislar note).

**Rocket noise.** Lab screech tones do not carry over to launch vehicles (Lubert 2022), but the Tam & Tanna triad behind the G-JM is the same machinery used for BBSAN ([Wong 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), not tagged). Screech lowers BBSAN by up to about 4 dB and shifts its peak by damping the downstream cells (André 2013, p. 669–671), so screech-free lab data are the better calibration for full-scale BBSAN models (André note).
