# Screech

_Last updated: 2026-10-24 · 50 papers_

## Scope

Screech in free, shock-containing jets: how the feedback loop closes, where the tone is radiated and where its source sits, amplitude, receptivity and suppression, and triadic interactions and mean-flow deformation. Frequency prediction, absolute instability, global modes and the comparison of LES and linear models with experiment are on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md); staging, mode switching, multimodal loops and how the lip and reflectors select a stage on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md). Rectangular and elliptical jets ([non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md)) and twin-jet coupling ([twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md)) appear here only where they test the mechanism. Local and guided-jet-wave models are in [linear-models](linear-models.md), shock-cell models in [shock-cell-models](shock-cell-models.md), global stability and resolvent analysis in [linear-operator-methods](linear-operator-methods.md), BBSAN in [jet-noise-mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md), impinging and edge tones in [impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md), and SPOD/BMD/HPOD practice in [modal-decomposition](modal-decomposition.md) and [time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md).

## Current understanding

1. **The loop has four links**: KH growth from the lip, interaction with the shock cells, an upstream wave, and receptivity at the lip, which must satisfy both a phase and a gain condition ([Raman 1998](../Notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) p. 47–49; [Raman 1999](../Notes/raman-1999-supersonic-jet-screech-half-century-powell-present.md) p. 545–546; [Edgington-Mitchell 2019](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2019-aeroacoustic-resonance-review.md) p. 120–122; [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) p. 2). Apart from the KH–shock interaction, the frequency-selecting links appear linear (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves, p. 24; [Mancinelli 2019](../Notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) p. 8), and an integer number of periods spans the lip to one dominant source ([Mercier 2017](../Notes/mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) p. 219–224).
2. **The upstream wave is the guided jet mode (G-JM), not free-stream sound.** First proposed for A2 and C ([Shen & Tam 2002](../Notes/shen-2002-three-dimensional-numerical-simulation-jet-screech.md) p. 38–39), it is seen in A1 and A2 PIV ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) p. 9–11), in-jet LES spectra of a helical C jet ([Gojon 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md) p. 2921–2923) and cold rectangular-jet tones ([Gojon 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md) p. 26). Free-stream closure predicts tones where none exist (Mancinelli 2019, p. 6; [Mancinelli 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md) p. 15), and in-jet sound is totally reflected by the shear layer, while outside sound cannot enter the core travelling upstream ([Nogueira 2024](../Notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) p. 14–17, 32).
3. **A KH–shock triad at the Tam & Tanna difference wavenumber k_kh − k_s energises the G-JM** ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) p. 32–34; [Tam & Tanna 1982](../Notes/tam-1982-shock-associated-noise-cd-nozzles.md)), as PIV (with a duct-like k_kh + k_s wave; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves, p. 14–17), schlieren, LES and global models confirm ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) p. 12–13; [Jeun 2024](../Notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md) p. 13–14; [Farghadan 2026](../Notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) p. 15–17), also in a rectangular jet ([Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md) p. 4218).
4. **Frequency is predictable to a few per cent and each stage closes on its own shock wavenumber; amplitude is not predictable.** A1 and B close on k_s1, A2 and C on k_s2, and near a single-jet staging point the tones exclude each other ([Nogueira 2022](../Notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) p. 7–13; Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 11–13). Both threads have their own pages.
5. **The nozzle and its surroundings set amplitude and mode ranges, by tens of dB and stage by stage, far more than frequency** ([Ponton 1992](../Notes/ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) p. 537–548; [Powell et al. 1992](../Notes/powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md) p. 2835; [Norum 1983](../Notes/norum-1983-screech-suppression-supersonic-jets.md) p. 239–240; Raman 1998, p. 75–78; [André et al. 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md) p. 666–668).
6. **Sound leaves near the 3rd–4th shock, at the saddles between vortices, not at the wavepacket peak.** Single-source fits (Raman 1999, p. 555–556; Mercier 2017, p. 219–222), phase-averaged near-field pressure and video ([Panda 1999](../Notes/panda-1999-experimental-investigation-screech-noise-generation.md) p. 89–93; [Seiner et al. 1988](../Notes/seiner-1988-dynamic-pressure-loads-twin-supersonic-plume-resonance.md) p. 956), PIV vorticity statistics ([Edgington-Mitchell 2014, helical mode](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2014-coherent-structure-helical-screech-mode.md) p. 840–844; [Alkislar 2003](../Notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md) p. 142–144) and ray tracing ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) p. 19–30) agree to about one cell, on the mechanism computed by [Suzuki & Lele 2003](../Notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md) (p. 145–149, 153–155). This contradicts [Tam 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md) (p. 4), and core-emission models keep the question formally open (Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 139–140).
7. **The far-field tone is not the loop.** Round and low-AR jets radiate through acoustically matched wavenumbers and through shock leakage, now seen directly (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 13–19), both by-products of the triad (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 20–22; Nogueira 2024, p. 32). Of four loops in an AR 2 rectangular jet only the flapping one reaches the far field (Nogueira 2025, p. 4217, 4220).
8. **Screech reshapes its own jet**: it damps the mean shock train (André 2013, p. 667–668; Norum 1983, p. 236), speeds spreading ([Krothapalli 1986](../Notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md) p. 123) and gives each loop its own mean ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) p. 3, 16–19).

## Closure: from Powell's loop to the guided jet mode

Powell's model treats the shock cells as a phased array of monopoles, giving f = U_c/[s(1 + M_c)] (Raman 1998, p. 48–49). It has two conditions meant to hold together, a phase criterion and upstream-peaked directivity; with one dominant source the second drops out and screech becomes an edge tone with h = (N + p)s (Raman 1999, p. 545–546, 555). The formula also follows from other premises, so its accuracy validates none of them (Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 125, 128–129; frequency page). [Tam 1986](../Notes/tam-1986-bbsan-screech-relationship.md) (p. 7–10) made screech the θ → 180° limit of BBSAN, the band radiated straight at the lip sustaining the loop (the "weakest link"), and [Tam 1988](../Notes/tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) (p. 10–12) extended this to rectangular jets. Panda's near-field standing wave, its crests at L_sw rather than the shock spacing (p. 79–81), is now read as the closing shock peak (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 5, 11; numbers on the staging page). All these models closed the loop with free-stream sound (Tam 1995, p. 37), and in 2019 jet modes were "not always the closure mechanism" (Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 176).

The guided-mode picture has two roots. In [Shen & Tam 2002](../Notes/shen-2002-three-dimensional-numerical-simulation-jet-screech.md)'s URANS simulations the weakest link fits A1 and B to about 4 % but misses A2 and C by about 12 %, so they closed A2 and C through the upstream acoustic mode "guided by the jet", whose lowest (0, 2) and (1, 1) vortex-sheet frequencies follow the A2 and C wavelengths (p. 38–39). The split did not survive: the G-JM is present in A1 and A2 alike (Edgington-Mitchell 2018, p. 10–11; Gojon 2018, p. 2918–2920; Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 132), and Shen & Tam's own A1/B residuals imply a subsonic return at 0.91–0.93a∞ (Shen & Tam note). The other root is subsonic jets, whose trapped duct-like waves and upstream "discrete free-stream" mode propagate only between neutral saddle points ([Towne 2017](../Notes/towne-2017-acoustic-resonance-potential-core-subsonic-jets.md) p. 14–19).

Edgington-Mitchell 2018 (p. 6–11) split the leading POD pair of A1 and A2 jets (NPR 2.10, 2.25) by the sign of k_x and found an upstream wave at about a fifth of the KH amplitude, separable from the acoustic continuum only by the band test (p. 10). Gojon 2018 found in-jet n = 0 and n = 1 f–k spectra of an M_j 1.56 LES peaking at the tones on k ≈ −ω/a₀, at the lower band limits (p. 2919–2923), though by its note only profiles stopping at r/D_j 0.45 discriminate there and a 4.5D_j line resolves kD_j only to about 1.4. Near a lower limit the wave moves at about a₀, which is why free-stream loop models work as well as they do and why Mercier's ±10 % return speed cannot decide (Mercier 2017, p. 213, 226). Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves (p. 14–23) added the downstream duct-like wave and traced the standing wave and core modulation mostly to G-JM–KH interference, not to shocks modulating KH growth; Panda's traverse sees both the KH + G-JM and KH + sound standing waves (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 9–11). Nogueira 2024 (p. 19–33) supplies the physics: the G-JM is in-jet sound in a duct whose wall impedance runs from hard at the sonic line to soft at large |α|, and the evanescent outer field carries net energy upstream. Because the triad sits inside the shear layer, screech always needs the G-JM; impingement tones need it only when their source is in the core (p. 31–33; contrast the wall-jet shocklet in [Weightman 2019](../Notes/weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md) and [Edgington-Mitchell 2025](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md), both not tagged).

The closure generalises: an AR 2 elliptical jet's se₁ G-JM is neutral at the tone only over a short x band, where its measured amplitude peaks ([Ivelja 2024](../Notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) p. 22–24); the G-JM band selects twin round-jet coupling symmetry ([Nogueira 2021, twin jets](../Notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md) p. 16–19; [Stavropoulos 2023](../Notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) p. 18–19); its gain dwarfs that of free-stream sound in twin rectangular LES (10.1 against 1.16; Jeun 2024, p. 20–21); and the flapping PIV POD mode of an AR 2 C-D jet has a subsonic upstream peak near k_KH − k_sh1 (Nogueira 2025, p. 4218), a stronger test than band membership of rectangular LES tones (Gojon 2019, p. 24–31; Gojon 2019 note). Negative k_x is not upstream group velocity: the k_kh − k_s2 wave in an A1 jet is duct-like and cannot close the loop (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 13; Towne 2017, p. 8).

## Shock leakage and source location

**Where the sound leaves.** Every method that assumes one dominant source puts it at the 3rd or 4th shock. One equivalent shock-tip monopole fits a rectangular jet's exit-plane phase best at the 3rd shock at M_j 1.45 and the 4th at 1.75, as weaker amplification moves the wave peak downstream (Raman 1999, Fig. 6, p. 555–556; Raman 1998, p. 71–73). Near-field phase reversal across ten round jets puts it at the 4th shock tip for A1 and A2 and the 3rd or 4th for B (Mercier 2017, p. 215, 219–222, Table 5), where Mancinelli fixed his reflection (Mancinelli 2019, p. 4–5). A coupled twin B mode sends upstream waves from "near the fourth shock cell" (Seiner et al. 1988, p. 956), Shen & Tam's simulated instability wave meets the shocks around the 4th or 5th cell (p. 37–38), and Norum 1983 inferred downstream sources because the first four cells do not change with stage (p. 236–237). In Panda's A2 jet (M_j 1.19) a passing compression or rarefaction bulges radially between the 3rd and 4th shocks and splits, its outer part running upstream "pause-and-go" (p. 89–93), the near-field picture of the 4th-cell A2 leak below. Powell et al. 1992's spark-photo centroids at 5–6D are the outlier (p. 2833). The shocks themselves move, phase-locked and mostly vortex-driven (axial in A, transverse in flapping, circular in helical), and split at the 4th cell in A1 (NPR 2.15) and the 3rd in C (NPR 3.4), so a stationary shock pattern is "at best a convenient fiction" (Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 136–138).

**The leakage mechanism.** Manning & Lele 1998 had a forced supersonic shear layer struck by one oblique compression radiate a short compression and a longer expansion (Raman 1999, p. 563). Suzuki & Lele 2003 supply the theory the group's leakage work builds on. A weak shock obeys the eikonal of high-frequency sound, and a steady layer always totally reflects it (p. 142–147). In a 2-D DNS of a forced M₁ = 1.2 mixing layer the shock leaks near the saddles between vortices, where the vorticity that rotates the wavefront normal back into the jet is weak, and the saddle's strain turns leaking rays outward (p. 147–149, 153–156). Leaks grow as vortices develop (4–8 of 251 rays at the first saddle, 9–12 at the second; p. 155) and fade once small eddies blur the saddles, their explanation of 2nd–4th-cell sources (p. 164).

**Vorticity proxies and direct observation.** In an AR 4 C-D rectangular jet phase-locked circulation peaks at the ends of the 3rd and 5th cells, highest at the 3rd (Alkislar 2003, p. 142–144), where Krothapalli 1986 put the source (p. 123). In the group's NPR 3.4 C-mode PIV, u′ kurtosis and coherent vorticity fluctuation peak over cells 2–4 while coherent energy grows to the 6th (Edgington-Mitchell 2014, helical mode, p. 837, 840–844), and flapping coherent vorticity in an AR 2 ellipse peaks just before the 3rd shock ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) p. 2747). Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, then saw leakage in k_x < 0-filtered 1 Mfps schlieren: the shock tip overshoots the sonic line as a KH vortex passes and sheds a front that is sharp from birth (p. 3–4, 13–19). Ray tracing on phase-reconstructed PIV puts the escape at the saddle between vortices, first at the 3rd cell for A1 and C and the 4th for A2, moving between the 6th and 3rd with vortex strength (p. 19–30). The 2014 "largest vorticity fluctuation" rule fails at NPR 2.25, where incidence angle matters as much (p. 26–28), so a vorticity peak marks where leakage can start, not the emission point. One shock-like, upstream-directed source explains Raman's "feedback shock" in high-AR rectangular jets (p. 21–23; Raman 1998, p. 70–73), though several cells emit (p. 28). In 2019 leakage was "widely, though not universally accepted" (Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 164–165). None of this tested closure; Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 20–22) treats leakage as a correlated sound source that may close the loop only at high AR.

## Frequency and staging

Linear models predict the tone to 1–10 % and LES to about 0.01 in St, the successful models largely coincide, and whether screech is a self-excited global mode is open ([screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md)). Screech jumps between Merle's A1 and A2 (axisymmetric), B (flapping), C (helical) and D stages (Raman 1998, p. 49, 52, 101; Powell et al. 1992, p. 2825–2830), with limits that belong to the rig: a reflecting baffle moves one tube's B/C limit over NPR 2.42–2.90 (Norum 1983, Fig. 4, p. 237), and one radial tab moves every tone and turns B flapping into a "punctuated toroid" ([Johnston & Edgington-Mitchell 2016](../Notes/johnston-2016-underexpanded-jet-intrusive-tab-visualisation.md) p. 53–58). The rest is on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md).

## Amplitude, receptivity and suppression

**Saturation.** Raman (1998, p. 49) writes the limit cycle as growth × shock interaction × feedback efficiency × receptivity = 1. Shock strength keeps rising with M_j while amplitude plateaus at 154–155 dB over M_j 1.3–1.65 and then collapses (p. 64), and near-field OASPL holds at 125–130 dB through B, C and D, "some sort of limit cycle" (Powell et al. 1992, Fig. 17, p. 2832, 2834). Two-way coupling, the standing wave modulating the downstream gain that sets the upstream efficiency, makes amplitude models "exceedingly difficult" (Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 126). Alkislar 2003 (p. 143–144, 153) credits coherent vorticity plus shock strength from a higher 3rd-cell circulation at the louder of two conditions, but they are one phase and only about 1–3 dB apart (Alkislar note).

**Lip and reflectors.** Amplitude scales with "the amount of solid surface area near the jet exit" (Norum 1983, p. 240). Removing a 0.27D lip lowers screech by about 10 dB with the directivity shape unchanged, but cuts A and C by 6–14 dB while barely changing B (Norum 1983, Figs 8, 10, p. 239). A thick lip keeps screech going to M_j ≈ 1.95 rather than 1.6–1.7 and revives the B family (Ponton 1992, p. 537–541), gives more loop gain (Powell et al. 1992, p. 2835), adds 23 dB to a rectangular jet at M_j 1.75 (Raman 1999, Fig. 7, p. 557–558), and brings a weak tone into an AR 3 jet that otherwise did not screech ([Samimy 1998](../Notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md) p. 8). It acts acoustically, not through entrainment: at high NPR the expanding boundary blocks the return path to a thin lip, and a thick lip intercepts it (Raman 1997, via Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 134–135). A baffle flush with the exit adds about 5 dB and turns the main lobe downstream (Norum 1983, Fig. 11, p. 240), and a lift plate flush with a near-design C-D exit turns its BBSAN hump into a tone (St ≈ 0.30 at NPR 3.7) without shifting St above NPR ≈ 3.3 ([Krothapalli et al. 1999](../Notes/krothapalli-1999-flow-field-noise-supersonic-impinging-jet.md) Figs 17–18, p. 174–175). Reflectors also detune the loop phase (Raman 1998, p. 77–78): covering Panda's flange changed amplitude and frequency (Panda 1999, p. 73), and enclosure walls move tone onsets between rigs with identical nozzles ([Bell 2021](../Notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md) p. 6, 17–18, 35).

**Suppression.** Norum 1983 (p. 239) separates two kinds: lip changes that leave the first cells unchanged act on receptivity or reflection, while long internal slots or a bevel that destroy the axisymmetric shock train cut 13–29 dB (external grooves even enhance screech below the C range). André 2013's 24 shallow lip notches are the modern first kind, removing the tones at M_j 1.15–1.50 with the first cells unchanged, which the authors put down to disrupted near-lip receptivity, "to be verified" (p. 666–668). Tabs act inconsistently. A small Langley tab removes the tones, by about 16–21 dB at the peak ([Norum 1982](../Notes/norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md) p. 149, 162); a 1.2 % tab on one of two coupled nozzles cuts the twin B tone by 11–18 dB, to about single-jet levels, while an equal-area notch can raise it (Seiner et al. 1988, Figs 15–16, p. 959–960, read); Norum 1983's wire tab gives 5–10 dB (p. 239); and a 2 % radial tab lowers the NPR 2.6 tone by only 2–3 dB but moves it from St 0.30 to about 0.47, since it also truncates the shocks on its side and shortens the cells by about 16 % (Johnston & Edgington-Mitchell 2016, p. 53–54; note's reading). Asymmetric trailing-edge cutouts suppress overexpanded rectangular screech while symmetric ones amplify it ([Kerechanin 2001](../Notes/kerechanin-2001-trailing-edge-rectangular-jet-acoustics.md) p. 4–5), and a V-notch removes the AR 3 tone (Samimy 1998, p. 8). A sharp-throat C-D nozzle still screeches, more weakly, at its design NPR (Nogueira 2025, p. 4214; [Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../Notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md) p. 4, 19). LES does not yet capture amplitude trends: an untripped rectangular LES with every tone frequency right has screech rising over TR 1 → 3 where the experiment falls (Gojon 2019, p. 18–19, 21–22), probably through its weakly turbulent exit boundary layer (Gojon 2019 note).

**Coupled jets.** A coupled twin B mode 1.9D apart reaches 159–162.5 dB between the nozzles, more than twice the single-jet amplitude over M_j 1.15–1.5 and above a 155 dB sonic-fatigue line, the cause the authors give for F-15 and B-1B nozzle-flap fatigue (Seiner et al. 1988, p. 954–960). Coupling is on [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md), and what the stability and resonance models say about amplitude on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md).

## Triads, harmonics and mean-flow deformation

Beyond the KH–shock triad, tones interact with each other (A1–A2 modulation at St₂ − St₁, and a B tone that appears at m = 0 as a quadratic m = ±1 product; Mancinelli 2019, p. 7; Mancinelli 2021, p. 24–25; staging page), with their harmonics and with the mean flow. **Harmonics:** a bilinear harmonic resolvent on the PIV mean plus the screech mode reproduces the St = 0 shock-cell correction and the 2St_s and 3St_s sideline beams; the harmonics radiate from k_kh − k_s1 although the loop closes on k_s2, and forcing with the screech self-interaction matches the optimal harmonic responses (inner products > 0.95) without appeal to turbulence (Farghadan 2026, p. 20–25). Panda's near field agrees: the 2f_s harmonic is generated in the flow, with beams at 90° and about 120° (Panda 1999, p. 83). The harmonic's sideline radiation may also be what locks the 90° BBSAN peak to 2St_s through every stage jump (André 2013, p. 669; André note; see the staging page).

**Mean flow.** The St_s − St_s = 0 self-interaction distorts the mean, so each loop has its own short-time mean and shock spacing both selects the loop and is changed by it (Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 3, 16–19), as Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 12) inferred from jumps in the shock spectrum. André 2013 measures it most cleanly against a notched, screech-free twin nozzle: at M_j 1.35 the screeching jet shows about 6 shock cells against 11, its centreline oscillation collapsing just downstream of x/D ≈ 5.3 (Fig. 5, p. 667–668, read), and Norum's shadowgraphs already showed the visible cell count changing with stage (about 5 in B, 8 in C, 9–11 in A; Norum 1983, p. 236). Screech also spreads the jet: an AR 16.7 rectangular jet spreads at 36° rather than 20–25° at peak screech (Krothapalli 1986, p. 123), an AR 4 jet's minor-axis spreading rate rises from 0.065 to 0.33 (Alkislar 2003, p. 135–136), and suppressing the coupled B mode in one of two plumes slows the spreading of both (Seiner et al. 1988, p. 960, qualitative). Strong screech adds about 3 dB to mixing noise ([Tam 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), not tagged), consistent with the 2–4 dB lower broadband level once André's notches remove the tones (André note).

**Flapping and helicity.** In |m| = 1 round-jet screech the (±1, St_s) ↔ (2, 0) triad lets weak |m| = 2 streaks split the m = ±1 eigenvalues into flapping and wagging modes and fix the axis, which in LES precesses with the streaks by about π/2 over about 100 cycles ([Nogueira 2026](../Notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) p. 7–12, 20). Powell et al. 1992 observed the wandering B and D planes and C of either sense (p. 2829–2832), and held that nonlinear sinuous and helical modes are "essentially different" (p. 2833–2834). The helical C mode reverses its helicity often ([Edgington-Mitchell 2023](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2023-screeching-jet-seen-from-all-sides.md) p. 4); the group's early "single helix" (Edgington-Mitchell 2014, helical mode, p. 838) excludes m = 2 but not flapping.

## Disagreements and open questions

Questions on staging (labelling A2, hysteresis, which instability screeches, helical sense) are on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md), and those on what selects the frequency, self-excitation and amplitude prediction on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md).

**Leakage as closure.** The idea comes from the pre-G-JM framing, in which shock–vortex interaction made both the upstream wave and the tone (Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 120, 162–165). Round-jet evidence is now against it. Leaked fronts are born outside the jet, which cannot feed the G-JM (Nogueira 2024, p. 14, 32), so Panda's "fully formed acoustic wave approaching the nozzle" (p. 93) is radiation, not necessarily the return path. At NPR 2.10 the first leak (x/D ≈ 1.8; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 23) lies upstream of the G-JM peak (≈ 3.3; Edgington-Mitchell 2018, p. 6), though at NPR 2.25 both sit near x/D ≈ 3. Suzuki & Lele's totally reflected rays obey the same Snell's-law condition as the G-JM band (Nogueira 2024, p. 15–17), so one shock–vortex event might feed both the G-JM and the tone (Suzuki & Lele note's reading). Correlating each cycle's leakage with G-JM amplitude at the lip would separate them; at high AR (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 21–22), tones outside the G-JM band would show leakage closure.

**Source and directivity.** Raman (1999, p. 555) asked how one dominant source can direct screech so strongly upstream; G-JM closure makes this irrelevant to the loop (Nogueira 2024, p. 14–17) but not to the far field. Norum's are the primary data: the fundamental of both B and C peaks about 20° from the inlet axis (160° from the jet axis) and the second harmonic dominates at 90°, and Powell's array gets the lobe angles but misses levels off the main lobe by about 10 dB and the B fundamental entirely (Norum 1983, Figs 5–6, p. 237–238; Norum 1983 note). One leakage source in the ray model already radiates along the 160° peak (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 21–23), but without amplitude, while Suzuki & Lele's DNS gives one 2-D event mainly sideline radiation, falling towards zero beyond roughly 120–130° (Figs 24–27, p. 162–163, read), favouring "superposition of many sources" (p. 163–164). Other views remain: Umeda & Ishii's source orbiting the sonic line puts the helical peak 30° off upstream but needed several sources, Tam, Parrish & Viswanathan 2014 put emission in the core, and Lele 2005 showed such wave models can, under some conditions, equal distributed sources; the review found no consensus and called helical directivity "currently irreconcilable" (Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 139–140). Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 20) gets the upstream/downstream balance from a distributed Mach-wave-type source. C-mode, AR 4 and Panda's data put the first harmonic at the sideline, at or above the fundamental there (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 6; Alkislar 2003, Fig. 6, read; Panda 1999, p. 83), but the fundamental dominates at 90° in an AR 16.7 jet (Krothapalli 1986, p. 125), and a coupled flapping twin radiates most in its flapping plane (Seiner et al. 1988, Fig. 7, p. 957). All of this is qualitative. Norum's 92D directivities, with M_c and λ/L stated, have not been tested against the leakage, wave-model or harmonic-resolvent explanations (Norum 1983 note); far-field angles to 160° and LES with amplitude would decide.

**Flapping, helical and precession.** Whether streaks anchor screech or screech drives streaks is open (Nogueira 2026, p. 25), as is whether stage C corresponds to weak streaks; Powell's unsteady initial swirl (p. 2832, 2834) is the alternative. Forced LES, a swirl-controlled or streak-forced test, or multi-view time-resolved schlieren would separate them.

**Full scale.** Screech is absent from rocket and afterburning-engine data ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 9, 16; [Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 6). Heating weakens it (Tam 1995, p. 39–40), as Soviet preheating tests already found (Anufriev et al., via Raman 1999, p. 548), though not in the one heated LES (above). Suzuki & Lele's mixing layer is ambiguous: the critical-angle margin shrinks in hot jets (p. 147) and the DNS total amplitude falls (p. 163), and turbulence scattering thin shock fronts into broadband shock noise (p. 164) fits screech vanishing at full scale. Heating lowers the planar vortex-sheet G-JM bands (Gojon 2019, Fig. 20, p. 25), and the hot underexpanded LES tones already fall 8–31 % below the A1 band (Gojon 2019 note), blamed on the thin-shear-layer idealisation (p. 26). A finite-thickness or LES-mean-flow band calculation for hot, strongly off-design jets would show whether the band misses hot plumes.

## Methods and data

Staging-specific methods are on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md), model inputs and validation on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md), and imaging and PIV practice on [schlieren-and-density-imaging](schlieren-and-density-imaging.md) and [diagnostics-and-facilities](diagnostics-and-facilities.md).
- **Non-time-resolved PIV**: POD of transverse velocity, complex mode, FFT in x, split by the sign of k_x (Edgington-Mitchell 2018; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves, p. 9–17; Ivelja 2024; Nogueira 2025, p. 4217–4218). The POD-pair triple decomposition began with the 2014 C-mode data (Edgington-Mitchell 2014, helical mode, p. 833–841), microphone-triggered phase locking is the older alternative (Alkislar 2003, p. 128–131), and the reconstructed cycle feeds leakage ray tracing (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 11–13). It needs one steady tone, smooths shocks and fronts, and hides helicity sign and cycle-to-cycle change (Edgington-Mitchell 2023; Edgington-Mitchell 2019 note).
- **Time-resolved schlieren**: SPOD at the tone and the standing-wave wavenumber (Edgington-Mitchell 2022), Hilbert phase (Jeun 2024), and k_x < 0 filtering for single leakage events (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 10, 19). SPOD in two orthogonal views finds every loop and its symmetry, and an empirical k–ω dispersion relation shows the KH, guided and duct branches (Nogueira 2025, p. 4213–4215); without time resolution, two-point correlations in two views still give the symmetry (Johnston & Edgington-Mitchell 2016, p. 56–58).
- **LES and URANS**: azimuthally split f–k spectra inside the jet, on a line long enough to resolve them, test for guided modes (Gojon 2018, p. 2921–2923; Gojon 2018 note). One snapshot's spatial spectrum cannot show that two tones coexist (Shen & Tam 2002 note).
- **Microphones**: phase-averaged axial surveys against a fixed reference locate the dominant source; radial surveys cannot, and beyond about 5D reflections take over (Mercier 2017, p. 216–221). Phase-averaged near-field maps show sound inception (Panda 1999, p. 87–93); a notched twin nozzle with unchanged first cells gives a screech-free reference (André 2013, p. 666–668); and suppression should be quoted stage by stage, as Norum did (Norum 1983, p. 239).
- **Pitfalls**: phase and group velocity can differ in sign; precession splits a tone across SPOD modes 1 and 2 (Nogueira 2026, p. 22); shocks inside a PIV window smear over ≈ 0.2D ([Edgington-Mitchell 2014](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2014-underexpanded-jet-mach-disk-shear-layer.md) p. 16–17); local phase speeds swing at L_sw where waves counter-propagate (Panda 1999, p. 84–87); and a tab or slot that changes the shock cells is not a receptivity test (Norum 1983, p. 239; Johnston & Edgington-Mitchell 2016 note).
- **Data tables**: tones and shock spacings in `Data/screech_tones.csv` and `Data/shock_cell_spacing.csv`.

## Relevance to our work

**SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Take the Hilbert transform in time, or along −x for the G-JM, since along +x it discards the k_x < 0 G-JM (Edgington-Mitchell 2022 note), and split by the sign of k_x before reading an instantaneous wavenumber or convection speed (Panda note). Conditioning the k_x < 0 field on each cycle's KH amplitude would replace the static r̄ ± 2σ rescaling of the leakage source (Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 28–30) and test Alkislar's amplitude claim at the radiating cell; a precessing flapping plane should appear as a slow phase drift between the two leading HPOD modes (Powell note).

**PyFR staging LES** (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`). Its staging, receptivity, leakage and loop-count tests are on [screech-staging-and-mode-switching](screech-staging-and-mode-switching.md), and the self-excitation test on [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md). Converged A1 and A2 states should also show whether the louder state has the higher coherent circulation at its radiating cell (Alkislar note). Amplitudes need the exit boundary layer (Gojon 2019) and the nozzle exterior as built, since a flush baffle or plate adds about 5 dB or turns BBSAN into a tone (Norum 1983, p. 240; Krothapalli et al. 1999, p. 174–175).

**Rocket noise.** Lab screech tones do not carry over to launch vehicles (Lubert 2022), but the Tam & Tanna triad behind the G-JM is the same machinery used for BBSAN ([Wong 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), not tagged). Screech lowers BBSAN by up to about 4 dB and shifts its peak by damping the downstream cells (André 2013, p. 669–671), so screech-free lab data are the better calibration for full-scale BBSAN models (André note). Cold subscale tests of closely spaced nozzles can screech and couple above sonic-fatigue levels (Seiner et al. 1988, p. 958–960), so tones in model-scale load data should be checked before scaling (this page's inference).
