# Screech staging and mode switching

_Last updated: 2026-10-22 · 32 papers_

## Scope

Staging in free, shock-containing jets: Merle's A1, A2, B, C and D stages, which shock wavenumber each loop closes on (k_s1 against k_s2), how a jet moves between stages (mutual exclusion, dwell times, intermittency, hysteresis), how the lip, nozzle exterior and reflectors select a loop, multimodal loops at higher NPR, and how staging limits might be predicted or computed. The loop itself and amplitude are in [screech](screech.md), and frequency prediction and the absolute-instability and global-mode view in [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md). Staging in rectangular and elliptical jets is treated in [non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md) and coupling-state switching in [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md); here they appear where they test the single-jet picture, as do impinging stages ([impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md)). Switch-tracking tools are in [time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md), local and Floquet models in [linear-models](linear-models.md), global modes in [linear-operator-methods](linear-operator-methods.md), and BBSAN in [jet-noise-mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md).

## Current understanding

1. **The stages are mapped and their shapes explained; the jumps were long unexplained.** A1 and A2 are varicose, B and D sinuous and C helical ([Powell 1992](../Notes/powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md) p. 2825, 2829–2830; [Raman 1998](../Notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) p. 49, 52, 101). Guided-mode bands give each stage's azimuthal form ([Gojon 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md) p. 2919–2920) and instability growth the toroidal-to-helical switch ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) p. 39), but neither separates A1 from A2, which share m = 0 and the guided jet mode (G-JM) ([Nogueira 2022](../Notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) p. 2).
2. **Stages close on different peaks of the shock spectrum.** Spacing shrinks downstream, giving a primary peak k_s1 and suboptimal k_s2, k_s3: A1 and B close on k_s1, A2 and C on k_s2 (Nogueira 2022, p. 7–13; [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) p. 11–13). Older data carry the same signature: Mercier's loop count, one extra period at a fixed 4th-shock source ([Mercier 2017](../Notes/mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) p. 214, 222–224), Panda's near-field standing wavenumber at 1.17–1.24 times the mean shock wavenumber in A2 and C jets ([Panda 1999](../Notes/panda-1999-experimental-investigation-screech-noise-generation.md) p. 81, note's arithmetic), and Powell's directivity angle (p. 2834–2835, note's derivation). An AR 2 rectangular jet has a k_s2 flapping loop too ([Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md) p. 4219–4220).
3. **Near a single-jet staging point the tones are mutually exclusive.** Powell's jet "switches rapidly" between A2 and B (p. 2825, 2835), A1/A2 dwell times are seconds ([Mancinelli 2019](../Notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) p. 6–7), and each loop has its own short-time mean flow ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) p. 11–13), which the far-field BBSAN peak tracks through every stage jump ([André 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md) p. 669). Impinging jets also switch loops at random ([Henderson 1993](../Notes/henderson-1993-tones-choked-jet-impinging-flat-plates.md) p. 318). Twin-jet coupling states instead coexist, uncorrelated and intermittent ([Bell 2021](../Notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md) p. 16, 29).
4. **The staging NPR belongs to the rig as much as to the jet.** A1 → A2 falls between NPR 2.17 and 2.27 in five facilities but near 2.3 behind Powell's t/D 0.8 lip (p. 2826, 2832), and identical nozzles in two rigs stage at different NPRs (Bell 2021, p. 17–18, 35). Lip thickness, reflectors and upstream plates add or remove modes ([Ponton 1992](../Notes/ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) p. 537–548; Raman 1998, p. 75–78), and in an impinging jet the nozzle exterior alone decides which loop wins while the tone frequencies stay put ([Weightman 2019](../Notes/weightman-2019-nozzle-external-geometry-azimuthal-mode-impinging-jet.md) p. 430–442).
5. **Linear models allow both loops over a band, so selection is nonlinear** (Nogueira 2022, p. 13; [Mancinelli 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md)). No model predicts the staging limits. Hysteresis has been found only at C ↔ D (about NPR 4.34–4.71); in up and down sweeps the A1/A2, A2/B and B/C boundaries coincide at the ≈ 0.1 NPR point spacing (Powell 1992, p. 2826, 2832; note's reading of Fig. 17).
6. **At higher NPR several loops share one block-averaged spectrum**, each on its own shock wavenumber (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 15–17; [Mazharmanesh 2025](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) p. 11–17; [Farghadan 2026](../Notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) p. 11–17; Nogueira 2025, p. 4214–4215). Whether they coexist or alternate is unresolved.

## Stages and early explanations

Powell 1992 (D = 10 mm, t/D 0.8, NPR 2.0–5.84) is the round-jet staging map later papers cite. Every jump goes up in frequency except A → B and C → D, and a secondary tone links B to D, so the authors treat them as one sinuous mode interrupted by C (p. 2825, 2833–2834); Ponton 1992 likewise groups the old "D" with a re-emerging B (p. 537). Neither sinuous mode holds its plane: B flips spasmodically from a preferred plane, and D's plane rotates at the equivalent of 13–90 Hz (p. 2829–2832). Powell attributed each jump to the loop count N + p changing by one, so C/B should be 6/5 or 7/6; it is 1.14–1.23 (p. 2833–2834). Raman (1998, p. 49, 52, 101) still called the jumps unexplained.

Tam 1995 (p. 39) picks the azimuthal mode with the largest total instability growth, which cannot separate A1 from A2: both are m = 0 and both close on the same G-JM (Nogueira 2022, p. 2), not on separate closures as [Shen & Tam 2002](../Notes/shen-2002-three-dimensional-numerical-simulation-jet-screech.md) proposed ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) p. 10–11; Gojon 2018, p. 2918). Gojon 2018's vortex-sheet band map (M_j 1–2, with Powell's and Ponton's tones) puts A1, A2 and Powell's secondary u near the lower limit of the (0, 2) band and C on the lower limit of the first helical band, matching their shapes; B and D fall outside every band, which the authors put down to more complex flapping (p. 2919–2920). The bands explain shape, not staging, since A1 and A2 share one. The u assignment fits the weak U tone above C (Mancinelli 2021, p. 15), which Powell et al. left unidentified (p. 2825). The B/D explanation is doubtful: flapping and helical waves share the |m| = 1 vortex-sheet relation, and streak-induced splitting moves the flapping band up, not down ([Nogueira 2026](../Notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) p. 11), so thin-sheet band edges are the likelier cause (Gojon 2018 note).

The absolute-instability saddle fixes the frequency but gives no reason for B–C–D switching ([Nogueira 2021, absolute instability](../Notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md)). Nor does convection speed: U_c/U_j stays at 0.57–0.60 across all coupling modes of twin jets, while the ratio of standing wavelength to shock spacing changes from mode to mode ([Knast 2018](../Notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) p. 10–11), and Powell's U_con/U_j averages 0.61 over all stages (p. 2827–2828). The U_c inferred from BBSAN directivity does vary with the stage (0.42–0.65U_j, against 0.65 for a screech-free twin; André 2013, Table 1, p. 670), but it weights the shock-noise sources, not the screech phase.

Not every tone change is a stage. Above M_j ≈ 1.25 the B tone appears in the m = 0 spectrum as a quadratic m = ±1 product (cross-bicoherence ≈ 1 at St 0.34), not as an axisymmetric loop (Mancinelli 2021, p. 24–25). Some impinging tones may likewise be sum and difference products (Henderson 1993, p. 324), and with two impinging loops the difference tone is louder than one parent (Weightman 2019, p. 430, 433; note's reading of Fig. 5). In heated rectangular LES the tone shape changes from diagonal (TR 1) to minor-axis flapping (TR ≥ 2), which the authors link to Raman's mode II → I jump ([Gojon 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md) p. 21–24); four TR points cannot show whether that change is discrete.

## Which shock wavenumber closes each loop

The present explanation is the multi-peak shock spectrum. Nogueira 2022 (p. 7–13) fitted the centreline mean pressure as a chirp, placed A1 in the near-nozzle region and A2 downstream where spacing shrinks, and found both loops absolutely unstable at M_j 1.12, with ω0i 0.223 (A1) against 0.057 (A2) at δ = 0.2, matching the stronger measured A1. Edgington-Mitchell 2022 extended this to B, C and k_s3. The lipline standing wavenumber follows k_s1 or k_s2 by stage, even at rapidly switching conditions (p. 11); k_s2 is a local quasi-periodicity starting at the 5th–8th shock cell (p. 17); energy at k_kh − k_s2 grows before the A1 → A2 switch (Tsinghua NPR 2.15–2.2, switch at 2.25; p. 13); and jets of AR ≥ 4 do not stage, because flapping breaks the shocks down before a suboptimal peak forms (p. 18–19).

Other methods agree. Farghadan 2026 (p. 11–17) found separate lightly damped global modes at St 0.66, 0.72 and 0.77, each tied to a different shock wavenumber, with the observed tone on k_s2 hidden inside the broad primary peak. Mazharmanesh 2026 (p. 19–22) measured a loop-specific k_S from BMD interaction maps that matches k_kh − k_GJM as well as or better than k_s1, k_s2; in an AR 2 elliptical jet a single loop on k_s1 (NPR 2.3–3.3) gives way to multimodal loops on k_s2 (NPR 3.4–4.9). [Stavropoulos 2023](../Notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) (p. 17) reaches the same A1/k_s1, A2/k_s2 split for twin jets through the p/s ratios of its resonance fits.

Mercier 2017 gives the loop view of the same jump. Between M_j 1.13 and 1.15 (St 0.59 → 0.64) the loop gains one period while the source stays at the 4th shock (z_N 2.7D → 2.8D; p. 222–224, Tables 2, 5). Its test of Tam's k_h − k_sc = −k_s gives (k_h + k_s)/k̄_sc = 1.01–1.05, except 1.10 at M_j 1.13 and 1.28 at A2 (Table 4, p. 214). The integer-loop criterion makes this ratio m/N, so 1.28 ≈ 5/4 is the k_sw ≈ k_s2 signature of Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 11), seen five years earlier, and the 1.10 just below the jump may be the growing k_kh − k_s2 energy (Mercier 2017 note's derivation and speculation).

Panda 1999 measured the same ratio in the near field. The r.m.s. crests along the jet boundary sit at the standing wavelength L_sw (0.66D, 1.03D), shorter than the mean shock spacing (0.77D, 1.28D) (Table 1, p. 81), which he could not explain (p. 94). With the G-JM at k_kh − k_s, the closing shock wavenumber is k_kh + k_up ≈ k_sw, Mercier's k_h + k_s. Both jets are k_s2 stages, M_j 1.19 axisymmetric (A2; Mercier 2017, Table 1, p. 204) and M_j 1.42 helical (C), and k_sw D = 9.5 against 2π/L = 8.2, and 6.1 against 4.9, give 1.17 and 1.24 (Panda 1999 note's arithmetic), near k_s2/k_s1 ≈ 1.2–1.3 (Nogueira 2022, p. 8; Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 11, which states the reconciliation, p. 5). The match is approximate: Panda's L averages 5–6 shock terminations, and the slightly subsonic G-JM puts the closure k_s a little above k_sw. He has no A1 case to show the jump, and three other jets with L_sw ≈ 1.3L (Table 2, p. 86; two rectangular, one excited) do not fit. Powell's directivity angle is a further check: with the second-cell spacing, B obeys Powell's formula to about 2 % and C sits about 24 % above it (Fig. 21, p. 2834–2835; Powell 1992 note's derivation), as B on k_s1 and C on k_s2 requires. Powell's "N changes by one" is the loop-count form of the same statement, like Mercier's m/N.

At Pprime, M_j ≤ 1.115 gives only A1, 1.12 jumps between A1 and A2, and ≥ 1.13 only A2, with no coexistence, dwell times of 1–4 s and an A1 envelope modulated at St ≈ 0.069 ≈ St₂ − St₁ only where switching occurs (Mancinelli 2019, p. 6–7). At NPR 2.20 the A1 (St 0.606) and A2 (0.694) amplitudes have ρ = −0.82, and the St = 0 SPOD coefficient correlates at 0.96 and −0.82 with them, so the long-time mean is a state the jet never occupies (Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 8–13). How joint statistics separate switching from coexistence is set out in [time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md).

Powell 1992 is the library's only sweep test of hysteresis. With NPR swept up and down without restarting, only C ↔ D moved: at NPR 4.48 the tone is C at 11.00 kHz with pressure rising and D at 9.125 kHz with pressure falling (Fig. 3, p. 2826), over about NPR 4.34–4.71 (M_j ≈ 1.62–1.67, read from Figs 2 and 17). The A1/A2 (≈ 2.3), A2/B and B/C boundaries coincide at the ≈ 0.1 NPR point spacing (Fig. 17, p. 2832; note's reading), so any A1/A2 bistable band is narrower than ΔM_j ≈ 0.03, consistent with Mancinelli's window but unresolved. At NPR 2.45 A2 and B are both dominant and the jet "switches rapidly between them" (p. 2825, 2835), an early report of the exclusive switching Mancinelli 2019 and Mazharmanesh 2026 quantified.

Impinging jets switch the same way. At NPR 3.04 Henderson 1993 found two tones in 32-average spectra but one or the other in 2-average spectra, at random intervals, near an L1 stage jump (p. 318), where the jet seemed most receptive to other modes (p. 316); like [Ho 1981](../Notes/ho-1981-dynamics-impinging-jet-feedback-phenomenon.md) (p. 134–135) it gives no dwell times. Its impinging stage increments depend on NPR (L1 jumps 4 → 5 cycles at NPR 2.36, 4 → 6 at 2.70), which the authors attribute to a change in the preferred free-jet screech mode (p. 312–313).

The far field sees the mean flow change with the stage. Over a fine M_j 1.0–1.55 traverse with Mercier's lab and nozzle, the BBSAN peak at 90° of the screeching jet follows 2St_s through every stage jump, while a notched, screech-free twin varies smoothly; the authors attribute this to stage-dependent shock spacing or convection velocity (André 2013, Fig. 8, p. 669). This is a time-averaged counterpart of the per-loop means of Mazharmanesh 2026 (p. 11–13, 16–19), though harmonic radiation pulling the hump would also lock it (André 2013 note). Where two tones coexist in the averaged spectrum (M_j ≈ 1.25, 1.40) the hump sits between them (p. 669), as an average over two exclusive states would give (note's reading, untested).

Older evidence is ambiguous on coexistence. The B → C switch is abrupt with a thin lip, but the tones overlap over M_j 1.38–1.41 with a thick lip, which averaged spectra cannot separate from alternation (Ponton 1992, p. 538). Raman (1998, p. 55–56) cites both switching and genuinely coexisting tones in rectangular jets, and Mercier could hold a single A2 tone only at M_j 1.15, finding it shared with another mode on either side (p. 205). Powell's secondary tones, about 25 dB below the dominant ones, continue each branch on both sides of every jump (Figs 2, 18–19, p. 2825, 2833). Averaged spectra cannot tell such weak continuous loops from rare episodes of the other loop; they are the ancestor of Bell's coexisting "processes" and Edgington-Mitchell 2022's multimodal peaks (Powell 1992 note).

## Multimodal loops and non-round jets

At higher NPR several loops appear in one block-averaged SPOD: four non-harmonic tones at NPR 3.3, none on k_s1 (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 15–17), and three loops at NPR 4.0 in an AR 2 rectangular jet, including a bi-axial flapping mode (Mazharmanesh 2025, p. 11–17). An M_j 1.56 LES has two helical C tones (St 0.28, 0.305) and an axisymmetric u (0.38) in one 500 D_j/u_j record, without saying whether they coexist or alternate (Gojon 2018, p. 2920–2921). Henderson's rig shows a "symmetrical" and a "helical" screech line at the same NPR 2.36 (St ≈ 0.59, 0.44 by the note's estimate; Fig. 4, p. 312), though their source runs are not stated.

Non-round jets stage early and nozzle by nozzle. AR 2 rectangular and elliptical jets jump from varicose to flapping near NPR 2.2 (Mazharmanesh 2025, p. 7), but another Monash ellipse, with a thicker, azimuthally varying lip (Edgington-Mitchell 2015 note), already flaps at St 0.52 at NPR 2.2 with no varicose tone ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) p. 2743), falling to 0.38 and 0.27 at NPR 2.6 and 3.4 ([Ivelja 2024](../Notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) p. 23–24). In an AR 2 C-D rectangular jet a second minor-axis flapping tone from NPR ≈ 3.5 is assigned to k_sh2 (Nogueira 2025, p. 4219–4220), with k_sh2/k_sh1 ≈ 1.24–1.26 (Fig. 14, p. 4218, note's reading; cf. Mercier's 1.28 for A2). Here the k_sh1 and k_sh2 loops coexist in block-averaged SPOD rather than replacing each other (p. 4214–4215), as in the multimodal AR 2 ellipse of Mazharmanesh 2026. The assignment rests on frequency alone: the predicted k_sh2 points lie on the varicose tone at NPR 3.3 and between the varicose and second flapping tones above it (Fig. 17, p. 4220; note's reading). Some stage changes may not show in spectra: Mercier's flapping cases split into N = m = 3 and N = m = 4 with no tone jump (St 0.31 → 0.30 at M_j 1.35 → 1.37; p. 224, Table 2), though that rests on N alone and is tentative.

A second instability need not screech: the Monash ellipse at NPR 2.2 carries a slower, silent antisymmetric pair (see "Which instability screeches" below). Staging maps of non-round nozzles are on [non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md).

## Lip, exterior and reflectors

In free jets, thicker lips widen the helical C range and bring B back above M_j ≈ 1.7 (Ponton 1992, p. 537, 547); Powell's t/D 0.8 jet turns sinuous (D) from M_j ≈ 1.62–1.67, probably the same family (Powell 1992 note's inference). Covering Panda's nozzle flange changes screech amplitude and frequency (Panda 1999, p. 73), and an upstream plate gave m = 0 multimodality in one Monash rig (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 6, 16). One rig has no screech above NPR 3.79 (Henderson 1993, p. 316).

The cleanest test that the jet sets the frequencies and the boundary picks the loop is impinging. In a group NPR 3.4 jet with a plate at 5D, changing only the nozzle exterior gives helical St 0.255 (t/D 0.1), toroidal 0.385 plus helical 0.241 (15D flat cap), or toroidal 0.383 plus helical 0.253 (t/D 0.33) (Weightman 2019, p. 430–438), yet across nine configurations the tones stay near St 0.25 and 0.39 (Fig. 18, p. 441–442). Foam on the outer nozzle base, about 3D upstream of the exit (note's reading), removes the thin-lip helical loop and foam on the inner base does not, "for this facility" (p. 440–441). A reflector a chosen fraction of a wavelength behind the exit likewise suppresses one impinging tone and lets another take over (28.51 → 19.96 kHz; Henderson 1993, p. 318–319). The direction differs: a thick lip brings in a toroidal loop when impinging, but favours helical C and B in free jets (Weightman 2019, p. 433). No free-jet test has changed only the exterior and tracked the A1/A2 limits.

## Twin jets

Twin jets add symmetry competition, most intermittent at the A1 → A2 staging (Wong 2023, p. 9–12). Bell 2021 (p. 9) lists three untested staging mechanisms: a change of azimuthal mode or coupling, of the number of structures in the loop, or from free-stream to guided return. At s/D = 3, coherence reveals weaker continuous "processes" (one loop each, with fixed azimuthal modes, coupling and source) that coexist across the acoustic jumps at NPR 3.4 and 4.4 (p. 7–9). Between antisymmetric and symmetric coupling the jets couple either way or one oscillates alone; the coupled states never occur together, and the tones are uncorrelated rather than exclusive (p. 16, 23–29), which the authors read as competition between two global modes (p. 29). The loudest process is a clean limit cycle (σ ≈ 1 dB and 2–6°, about 170 dB) only at NPR 2.9–3.4 and noise-like just after its onset at NPR 2.3 (Fig. 27, p. 34; Fig. 6b, read), like Wong's intermittency near a stage boundary. Coupling can also change with no frequency jump (Knast 2018, p. 5, 11). LES resolves intermittent coupling in twin rectangular jets ([Jeun 2024](../Notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md)), and some apparent intermittency is only a beat between detuned tones ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../Notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md) p. 10–11; Wong 2023, p. 32–38). The coupling physics is on [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md).

## Disagreements and open questions

**One reflection point or two shock wavenumbers.** Mancinelli's models produce A1/A2 by changing p at a fixed 4th-cell reflection; Nogueira 2022 and Edgington-Mitchell 2022 attribute A2 to k_s2 from downstream cells. Mercier's data, the basis of the first view, fit both: the source stays at the 4th shock while the loop gains a period, and the same jump appears as (k_h + k_s)/k̄_sc = 1.28 ≈ m/N = 5/4 (p. 214, 222–224), so over the first four cells the two descriptions are one statement (Mercier 2017 note; cf. Mancinelli 2021, p. 24). Panda's A2 jet is a second case: sound is born between the 3rd and 4th shocks (2.37 < x/D < 3.07; p. 90), yet k_sw is 1.17 times the mean shock wavenumber (p. 81, note's arithmetic). Still open is where k_s2 comes from if the source does not move, since Nogueira 2022 places A2 downstream. [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) matched its own A2 case (NPR 2.25) with the leading k_s, though with a larger mismatch than for A1 (Nogueira 2022 note), and Farghadan 2026 finds the k_s2 peak buried in the primary one. Nogueira 2025 supports k_s2 labelling in a rectangular jet but measures no source position or loop count. Mercier's counts also assume a return at c₀; a slower G-JM return would push its +0.5π cases towards half-integers (Mercier 2017 note), though tones sit near the lower band edge, where the G-JM moves at about a₀ (Gojon 2018, p. 2919–2920). Long-field (> 10D) mean shock spectra, BMD k_S and the loop count (N, m) of A1 and A2 on one rig would settle the labelling.

**Selection, hysteresis and switching.** Linear models allow both A1 and A2 over a band (Nogueira 2022, p. 13; Mancinelli 2021), yet experiments switch over only ΔM_j ≈ 0.01–0.015. Screech has one hysteretic jump, C ↔ D, but the only sweep test bounds any A1/A2 band below ΔNPR ≈ 0.1 (Powell 1992, p. 2826, 2832). Open: whether A1/A2 is bistable at finer resolution, whether switching is stochastic between attractors or driven by supply-pressure drift (Wong 2023, p. 39–41), and whether multimodal tones coexist or alternate (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 7). On the last, a difference tone louder than one parent suggests two impinging loops run together (Weightman 2019 note's inference), and the rectangular k_sh1 and k_sh2 loops share a block-averaged spectrum (Nogueira 2025), while Gojon 2018's two C tones and André's hump between two tones are untested. The coexisting, uncorrelated twin-jet tones (Bell 2021, p. 16, 29) have envelope spreads of 5–6 dB and ≈ 20 % "interruptions", as a narrow-band Gaussian (Rayleigh) envelope would, so they may be the fading of damped, noise-driven resonances rather than switching between limit cycles (Bell 2021 note). Classical global-mode theory treats only supercritical Hopf bifurcations; subcritical ones were much more complicated, with no known example ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../Notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md) p. 517), so C ↔ D or A1/A2 bistability lies outside it. Three of its results still bear on competition: global modes come in families O(ε) apart in frequency (eq. 46, p. 496); a self-excited hot jet carries two modes with separate onsets (Fig. 18b, p. 513); and feedback that suppresses one global mode tends to destabilise another (p. 518–519). Global stability on each loop's short-time mean would test whether each state damps the other loop (Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 19). Other resonators add no precedent for hysteresis: jet–edge switches between two guided-wave loops repeat as M_j rises and falls ([Stavropoulos 2026](../Notes/stavropoulos-2026-jet-edge-interaction.md) p. 14–18), and impinging-jet "hysteresis" is intermittent switching at one stand-off (Ho 1981, p. 134–135; Henderson 1993, p. 318). Long time-resolved records with HPOD and JPDFs, checked against a Rayleigh null, and LES continuation are the tests.

**Helical sense and flapping plane.** On Powell's rig the C helix takes either sense about equally often, fixed while the jet runs but able to change on restart (p. 2829), and D's plane rotates at a variable rate (p. 2831–2832). On Henderson's rig the helical spin reversed at random during continuous running (p. 324). A two-microphone phase averages over reversals: an 80:20 mix gives about 46° instead of 60° (Henderson 1993 note's calculation). Weak |m| = 2 streaks that set and slowly rotate a flapping axis (Nogueira 2026, p. 11, 14) and unsteady initial swirl (Powell 1992, p. 2832) are candidate causes of the wandering plane. An azimuthal ring decomposed into m = +1 and −1 per block would measure reversal rates; swirl or streak control would separate the causes.

**Which instability screeches.** At NPR 2.2 the Monash ellipse carries a slower antisymmetric, helical-like POD pair (λ ≈ 2.4D, uncorrelated with the flapping pair) that makes no tone (Edgington-Mitchell 2015, p. 2745–2748). The authors argue that it grows only after the shock train has ended, so it cannot leak (p. 2747–2748). The paper predates G-JM closure, though, and the alternative is that no upstream wave exists for that pair near St 0.27: in this nozzle even the flapping (se₁) G-JM is neutral only over a short x band (Ivelja 2024, p. 22–24, NPR 2.6 and 3.4), and no band has been computed for the helical-like family. Quasi-helical tones do appear at higher NPR in another ellipse (St 0.28 at NPR 3.6, Mazharmanesh 2025, p. 16; 0.273, weak and intermittent, Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 13, 15), which fits the first view without excluding the second. Filtering that pair's PIV POD modes by the sign of k_x would separate the two.

## Methods and data

- **Acoustics**: fine NPR steps (ΔM_j = 0.005), swept both ways without restarts to test hysteresis (Powell 1992, p. 2826, 2832), azimuthal arrays to see m (Mancinelli 2019, p. 5), and records covering many switches (seconds). A microphone pair stepped round the jet gives m only on average (Powell 1992, p. 2828–2830); a ring decomposed into m = 0, ±1 per block also resolves spin reversals (Henderson 1993 note). Wavelets show switching in time (Mancinelli 2019; Wong 2023); coherence at all frequencies finds weak "processes", and band-pass Hilbert envelopes give per-process JPDFs and dropout statistics (Bell 2021, p. 8–14). The BBSAN peak against 2St_s is a time-averaged far-field sign of the stage's mean flow (André 2013, p. 669).
- **Mean fields** (any schlieren, BOS, PIV or probe mean): centreline FFT for k_s1, k_s2, k_s3, with a field of view of 8–10D or more and a sliding window to locate each peak (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 7, 17; Nogueira 2022, p. 7–8). Near-field r.m.s. crest spacing gives k_sw from acoustics alone (Panda 1999, p. 79–81).
- **Time-resolved schlieren**: SPOD frequency–time maps, JPDFs of tone amplitudes and BMD interaction maps for k_S (Mazharmanesh 2026); rotated views and sub-domain SPOD for non-round modes (Mazharmanesh 2025; Nogueira 2025); lip-line phase from a thin strip (16–24 px at 220–430 kHz) gives the loop count and U_c(x) (Mercier 2017, p. 206–208, 222–225).
- **LES**: f–k spectra at radii inside the jet, split into m = 0 and 1, place the upstream wave on its guided-mode band (Gojon 2018, p. 2921–2923); use a line much longer than 4.5D_j, which resolves kD_j only to about 1.4 (Gojon 2018 note).
- **Non-time-resolved PIV**: conditional POD on each jet's pair amplitude separates coupled and single-jet states (Bell 2021, p. 23–27); otherwise the POD-pair route needs one steady tone and hides switching, and two coexisting loops give a filled-disc phase portrait (Weightman 2019, p. 431).
- **Pitfalls**: beating mimics coupling (Wong 2023, p. 32–38); envelope-threshold dropouts need a Rayleigh null, a stated filter and a beat test (Bell 2021 note); check bicoherence before counting an m = 0 tone as a stage (Mancinelli 2021); block-averaged spectra cannot tell coexistence from alternation; a microphone-pair phase averages over spin reversals and a rotating plane (Henderson 1993, p. 324; Powell 1992, p. 2829–2832).
- **Data tables**: tone frequencies by stage are in `Data/screech_tones.csv`.

## Relevance to our work

**Lab experiments.** Record lip thickness, outer nozzle shape, upstream surfaces and enclosure walls in every campaign (Bell 2021, p. 6, 17–18; Weightman 2019, p. 440–441), and sweep NPR up and down in steps of 0.01 or finer around 2.15–2.30, to resolve an A1/A2 hysteresis band below Powell's 0.1. The existing Monash A1 (NPR 2.10) and A2 (2.25) PIV could give Mercier's loop count from the POD-pair phase along the lip line, testing m = 4 → 5 at fixed N on a thick-lip nozzle (Mercier 2017 note). The 2.5 s acoustic records of the group's impinging lip study could test whether its two loops coexist, from band-pass envelopes and difference-tone bicoherence (Weightman 2019 note).

**SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** Rebuild the A1 and A2 bands and the St = 0 mode separately and track their instantaneous amplitudes: mutual exclusion should appear as anti-correlation, and the lag between tone amplitude and the St = 0 mode tests two-way coupling (Mazharmanesh 2026). Test dropouts against a Rayleigh null and a beat before calling them switches. Conditioning short-time far-field spectra on the reconstructed amplitudes would show whether the BBSAN hump jumps with the tone (André 2013 note).

**The PyFR staging-continuation LES** (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`; 16 mm converging nozzle, A1 seeded at NPR 2.10, A2 at 2.25) cannot wait out 1–4 s dwell times (about 10⁴–10⁵ D/U_j). It steps NPR up from converged A1 and down from converged A2, and extrapolates each limit from a saddle-node fit of 1/t_switch: an assumption about the bifurcation, in territory classical theory leaves out (Huerre 1990 note's inference). What it could settle:
1. *Bistability.* The A1 up-branch and A2 down-branch limits bound any hysteresis band. If they coincide, staging is a sharp exchange; either way the experimental 50 % point should lie between them. C ↔ D (NPR ≈ 4.3–4.8) is a positive control, where two distinct limits should appear, though it needs a domain long enough for D's instability peak at 8–10D (Powell 1992, p. 2827; note's suggestion).
2. *Mean-flow-mediated selection.* Converged A1 and A2 states at one NPR give two mean flows. Their shock spectra test whether the loop changes k_s2 strength (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 12), and local, Floquet or global analysis tests whether each state damps the other loop; Farghadan 2026 already finds an St 0.66 mode beside the 0.72 tone at M_j 1.12. Near-field crest spacing should jump from 2π/k_s1 to 2π/k_s2 across the switch, which Panda's two k_s2 jets could not show (Panda 1999 note), and n = 0 f–k spectra of the two states would show whether the guided-mode band moves or only the shock wavenumber it pairs with (Gojon 2018 note).
3. *Rates.* Compare 1/t_switch and post-step growth or decay with ω0i (0.223 against 0.057; Nogueira 2022, p. 13) and the ω_i of Mancinelli 2021. Each NPR step is Huerre & Monkewitz's conclusive transient test ([screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md)): Landau fits to Hilbert envelopes at several points should agree (Huerre 1990, p. 497, 518). Their theory is supercritical only (p. 517).
4. *Precursors.* Check whether energy at k_kh − k_s2 grows before a forced switch (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 13), and use BMD to test whether the St₂ − St₁ modulation (Mancinelli 2019, p. 7) is a driving triad.
5. *Receptivity.* Changing only the lip or outer geometry tests whether limits shift while frequencies do not (Ponton 1992; Nogueira 2021, absolute instability, p. 22), the free-jet version of Weightman 2019's impinging result. Projecting the near-lip field onto KH and G-JM eigenfunctions would estimate the R₁ and R₂ that Mancinelli 2021 (p. 22) needs.
6. *Leakage.* Check whether the first leak moves from the 3rd to the 4th cell on staging (Panda's A2 sound starts between them, p. 90), and test leakage as closure ([screech](screech.md)) with the sound speed the ray model lacked ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) p. 12, 19–23).
7. *Loop count.* Mercier's lip-line phase analysis (y/D ≈ 0.55) should give N = 4 in both states and m = 4 → 5 (p. 222–224), a discrete state label; the core field then shows whether the upstream wave near −ω/c₀ is the G-JM or sound (p. 213).
8. *Far field and chirality.* Check whether the BBSAN peak near 90° sits at 2f_s and jumps between the A1 and A2 states (André 2013, p. 669); in helical states keep m = +1 and m = −1 separate, since the sense can reverse within a record (Henderson 1993, p. 324).

The limits and switch times must not depend on domain length, sponge or outflow treatment, since outflow-to-inflow feedback can self-excite spatial simulations (Huerre 1990, p. 498). Their sensitivity to the exit boundary layer also needs testing: it is the likely reason an LES can get frequencies right and amplitudes wrong (Gojon 2019, p. 8–10, 18–19). The README's 1 mm flat lip differs from the 16 mm nozzle of Mazharmanesh 2026 (l/D_e = 0.31), the leakage nozzle (t/D = 0.33; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 4) and Powell's t/D 0.8, which stages near NPR 2.3. Model the exterior and any flange as built, with no sponge where the experiment has a reflecting surface (Weightman 2019 note; Panda 1999, p. 73).
