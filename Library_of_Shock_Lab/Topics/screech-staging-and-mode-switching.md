# Screech staging and mode switching

_Last updated: 2026-10-24 · 41 papers_

## Scope

Staging in free, shock-containing jets: Merle's A1, A2, B, C and D stages, which shock wavenumber each loop closes on (k_s1 against k_s2), how a jet moves between stages (mutual exclusion, dwell times, intermittency, hysteresis), how the lip, nozzle exterior and reflectors select a loop, multimodal loops at higher NPR, and how staging limits might be predicted or computed. The loop itself and amplitude are in [screech](screech.md), and frequency prediction and the absolute-instability and global-mode view in [screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md). Staging in rectangular and elliptical jets is treated in [non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md) and coupling-state switching in [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md); here they appear where they test the single-jet picture, as do impinging stages ([impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md)). Switch-tracking tools are in [time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md), local and Floquet models in [linear-models](linear-models.md), global modes in [linear-operator-methods](linear-operator-methods.md), and BBSAN in [jet-noise-mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md).

## Current understanding

1. **The stages are mapped and their shapes explained; the jumps were long unexplained.** A1 and A2 are varicose, B and D sinuous and C helical ([Powell 1992](../Notes/powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md) p. 2825, 2829–2830; [Raman 1998](../Notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) p. 49, 52, 101). Reviews called the jumps unexplained in 1999 ([Raman 1999](../Notes/raman-1999-supersonic-jet-screech-half-century-powell-present.md) p. 548) and still read them through Powell's loop count in 2019 ([Edgington-Mitchell 2019](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2019-aeroacoustic-resonance-review.md) p. 133). Guided-mode bands give each stage's azimuthal form ([Gojon 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md) p. 2919–2920) and instability growth the toroidal-to-helical switch ([Tam 1995](../Notes/tam-1995-supersonic-jet-noise.md) p. 39), but neither separates A1 from A2, which share m = 0 and the guided jet mode (G-JM) ([Nogueira 2022](../Notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md) p. 2).
2. **Stages close on different peaks of the shock spectrum.** Spacing shrinks downstream, giving a primary peak k_s1 and suboptimal k_s2, k_s3: A1 and B close on k_s1, A2 and C on k_s2 (Nogueira 2022, p. 7–13; [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) p. 11–13). Older data carry the same signature, by the notes' arithmetic: Mercier's loop count, one extra period at a fixed 4th-shock source ([Mercier 2017](../Notes/mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) p. 214, 222–224), Panda's near-field standing wavenumber at 1.17–1.24 times the mean shock wavenumber in A2 and C jets ([Panda 1999](../Notes/panda-1999-experimental-investigation-screech-noise-generation.md) p. 81), Powell's directivity angle (p. 2834–2835), Shen & Tam's weakest-link residuals ([Shen & Tam 2002](../Notes/shen-2002-three-dimensional-numerical-simulation-jet-screech.md) p. 39) and an elliptic flapping stage whose standing-wave and shock spacings part ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015, staging](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-staging-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) p. 1020–1021). AR 2 rectangular jets have a k_s2 flapping loop too ([Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md) p. 4219–4220; [Lu 2026](../Notes/lu-2026-rectangular-jet-screech-dimensional-reduction.md) p. 20).
3. **Near a single-jet staging point one loop dominates at a time.** Norum's tube jet alternated between B and C "every few seconds" ([Norum 1983](../Notes/norum-1983-screech-suppression-supersonic-jets.md) p. 236), Powell's "switches rapidly" between A2 and B (p. 2825, 2835), A1/A2 dwell times are 1–4 s at Pprime ([Mancinelli 2019](../Notes/mancinelli-2019-screech-prediction-upstream-modes.md) p. 6–7, note's reading), and each loop has its own short-time mean flow ([Mazharmanesh 2026](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md) p. 11–13), which the far-field BBSAN peak tracks through every stage jump ([André 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md) p. 669). In the group's own schlieren record at NPR 2.199, dwells are only 19–30 ms and the weaker tone persists about 9 dB down (`Codebase/HPOD_Investigation/README.md`, unpublished), so "exclusive" means switching dominance, not on and off. Impinging jets also switch loops at random ([Henderson 1993](../Notes/henderson-1993-tones-choked-jet-impinging-flat-plates.md) p. 318); twin-jet coupling states instead coexist, uncorrelated and intermittent ([Bell 2021](../Notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md) p. 16, 29).
4. **The staging NPR belongs to the rig as much as to the jet.** A1 → A2 falls between NPR 2.17 and 2.27 in five facilities but near 2.3 behind Powell's t/D 0.8 lip (p. 2826, 2832), and identical nozzles in two rigs stage at different NPRs (Bell 2021, p. 17–18, 35). A baffle moves a tube jet's B/C limit anywhere over NPR 2.42–2.90 (Norum 1983, p. 237), one radial tab moves every stage ([Johnston 2016](../Notes/johnston-2016-underexpanded-jet-intrusive-tab-visualisation.md) p. 53–55), and lip thickness, reflectors and upstream plates add or remove modes ([Ponton 1992](../Notes/ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) p. 537–548; Raman 1998, p. 75–78). In an impinging jet the nozzle exterior alone decides which loop wins while the tone frequencies stay put ([Weightman 2019](../Notes/weightman-2019-nozzle-external-geometry-azimuthal-mode-impinging-jet.md) p. 430–442).
5. **Linear models allow both loops over a band, so selection is nonlinear** (Nogueira 2022, p. 13; [Mancinelli 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md)). No model predicts the staging limits, and reduced (planar) models cannot even bound a stage, because their G-JM bands are too wide (Lu 2026, p. 20–21). Hysteresis has been found only at C ↔ D (about NPR 4.34–4.71); the other boundaries coincide in up and down sweeps at the ≈ 0.1 NPR point spacing (Powell 1992, p. 2826, 2832; note's reading of Fig. 17).
6. **At higher NPR several loops share one block-averaged spectrum**, each on its own shock wavenumber (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 15–17; [Mazharmanesh 2025](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) p. 11–17; [Farghadan 2026](../Notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md) p. 11–17; Nogueira 2025, p. 4214–4215). Whether they coexist or alternate is unresolved.

## Stages and early explanations

Powell 1992 (D = 10 mm, t/D 0.8, NPR 2.0–5.84) is the round-jet staging map later papers cite. Every jump goes up in frequency except A → B and C → D, and a secondary tone links B to D, so the authors treat them as one sinuous mode interrupted by C (p. 2825, 2833–2834). Ponton 1992 likewise groups the old "D" with a re-emerging B (p. 537). Neither sinuous mode holds its plane: B flips spasmodically from a preferred plane, and D's plane rotates at the equivalent of 13–90 Hz (p. 2829–2832). Merle had already graded the stages by steadiness: A1 unstable, A2 stable, B very unstable, C very stable, D unstable (Raman 1999, p. 547).

Tube maps agree on the branches but not on which one wins. Norum's straight tube (D 6.22 mm, 0.27D lip, pipe flow) has A1, A2, B and C branches matching earlier data, yet B dominates from NPR ≈ 2.0 to 2.5 and C above (Norum 1983, Figs 2–3, 9, p. 236, 239), far below Powell's B/C near NPR 3.2 (Powell 1992 note's reading of Fig. 17). Seiner's choked tube, beside a blocked twin 1.9D away, has one dominant mode at each M_j (A1, then B from ≈ 1.13, C from 1.38, D from 1.72) and an unidentified "E" branch ([Seiner 1988](../Notes/seiner-1988-dynamic-pressure-loads-twin-supersonic-plume-resonance.md) Figs 8–9, p. 957–958; note's readings). Both name stages from wavelength alone.

Powell attributed each jump to the loop count N + p changing by one, so C/B should be 6/5 or 7/6; it is 1.14–1.23 (p. 2833–2834). Since h = (N + p)s holds for any N + p, it cannot say when a jump occurs (Raman 1999, p. 555; note's check), yet in 2019 staging was still judged "best understood in terms of Powell's original description", citing Gao & Li's a-posteriori integer-N fits (Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 132–133).

Tam 1995 (p. 39) picks the azimuthal mode with the largest total instability growth, "a major component of staging" (Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 154) that cannot separate A1 from A2: both are m = 0 and close on the same G-JM (Nogueira 2022, p. 2), not on separate free-sound (A1, B) and guided-mode (A2, C) closures as Shen & Tam 2002 proposed (p. 38–39; [Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md) p. 10–11; Gojon 2018, p. 2918; Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 132). Gojon 2018's vortex-sheet bands put A1, A2 and Powell's secondary u near the lower limit of the (0, 2) band and C on the first helical band, matching their shapes, but B and D outside every band (p. 2919–2920). The bands explain shape, not staging, since A1 and A2 share one. Gojon's explanation for B and D, more complex flapping, is doubtful: flapping and helical waves share the |m| = 1 relation, and streak-induced splitting moves the flapping band up, not down ([Nogueira 2026](../Notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md) p. 11), so thin-sheet band edges are the likelier cause (Gojon 2018 note).

The absolute-instability saddle fixes the frequency but gives no reason for B–C–D switching ([Nogueira 2021, absolute instability](../Notes/nogueira-2021-absolute-instability-shock-containing-jets.md)). Nor does convection speed: U_c/U_j stays at 0.57–0.60 across all coupling modes of twin jets, while the ratio of standing wavelength to shock spacing changes from mode to mode ([Knast 2018](../Notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) p. 10–11), and Powell's U_con/U_j averages 0.61 over all stages (p. 2827–2828). The U_c inferred from BBSAN directivity varies with the stage (0.42–0.65U_j; André 2013, p. 670) but weights the shock-noise sources, not the screech phase.

Not every tone change is a stage. Above M_j ≈ 1.25 the B tone appears in the m = 0 spectrum as a quadratic m = ±1 product (cross-bicoherence ≈ 1 at St 0.34), not as an axisymmetric loop (Mancinelli 2021, p. 24–25), and the "tilted toroid" A2 of early schlieren is probably m = 0 plus a weak m = ±1 (Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 130). Some impinging tones may likewise be sum and difference products (Henderson 1993, p. 324), and with two impinging loops the difference tone is louder than one parent (Weightman 2019, p. 430, 433; note's reading of Fig. 5). In heated rectangular LES the tone changes from diagonal (TR 1) to minor-axis flapping (TR ≥ 2), linked to Raman's mode II → I jump, though four TR points cannot show a discrete change ([Gojon 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md) p. 21–24).

## Which shock wavenumber closes each loop

The present explanation is the multi-peak shock spectrum. Nogueira 2022 (p. 7–13) fitted the centreline mean pressure as a chirp, placed A1 in the near-nozzle region and A2 downstream where spacing shrinks, and found both loops absolutely unstable at M_j 1.12, with ω0i 0.223 (A1) against 0.057 (A2) at δ = 0.2, matching the stronger measured A1. Edgington-Mitchell 2022 extended this to B, C and k_s3. The lipline standing wavenumber follows k_s1 or k_s2 by stage, even at rapidly switching conditions (p. 11); k_s2 is a local quasi-periodicity starting at the 5th–8th shock cell (p. 17); energy at k_kh − k_s2 grows before the A1 → A2 switch (Tsinghua NPR 2.15–2.2, switch at 2.25; p. 13); and jets of AR ≥ 4 do not stage, because flapping breaks the shocks down before a suboptimal peak forms (p. 18–19).

Other methods agree. Farghadan 2026 (p. 11–17) found separate lightly damped global modes at St 0.66, 0.72 and 0.77, each tied to a different shock wavenumber, with the observed tone on k_s2 hidden inside the broad primary peak. Mazharmanesh 2026 (p. 19–22) measured a loop-specific k_S from BMD interaction maps that matches k_kh − k_GJM as well as or better than k_s1, k_s2; in an AR 2 elliptical jet a single loop on k_s1 (NPR 2.3–3.3) gives way to multimodal loops on k_s2 (NPR 3.4–4.9). [Stavropoulos 2023](../Notes/stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa.md) (p. 17) reaches the same A1/k_s1, A2/k_s2 split for twin jets through the p/s ratios of its resonance fits.

The idea had forerunners. Singh & Chatterjee's simulations had A1 obey Powell's formula with the shock spacing and A2 Panda's with the standing-wave spacing (Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 133). In the group's AR 2 ellipse the two spacings agree in flapping stages II and IV but part in stage III, a ×1.20 jump with no change of symmetry: k_sw/k_s1 = 1.14 against 0.95 and 1.00, once Table 3's apparently swapped NPR 3.2 entries are corrected (Edgington-Mitchell 2015, staging, Tables 3–4, p. 1020–1021; note's check), the signature Edgington-Mitchell 2022 (p. 18) later read as k_s2. Its explanation, a switch to Shen & Tam's waveguide path, is superseded, since both stages close on one G-JM. Shen & Tam's Table 1 points the same way: with the 0.91–0.93a∞ return implied by its A1 and B residuals, A2 and C need 1.17–1.18 times the simulated shock wavenumber (p. 39; note's derivation). Norum saw the first four cells stay fixed from stage to stage while downstream cells changed (p. 236–237), as a k_s2 formed downstream would require (note's inference).

Mercier 2017 gives the loop view of the same jump. Between M_j 1.13 and 1.15 (St 0.59 → 0.64) the loop gains one period while the source stays at the 4th shock (z_N 2.7D → 2.8D; p. 222–224, Tables 2, 5). Its test of Tam's k_h − k_sc = −k_s gives (k_h + k_s)/k̄_sc = 1.01–1.05, except 1.10 at M_j 1.13 and 1.28 at A2 (Table 4, p. 214). As the integer-loop criterion makes this ratio m/N, 1.28 ≈ 5/4 is the k_sw ≈ k_s2 signature (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 11) seen five years earlier, and the 1.10 may be the growing k_kh − k_s2 energy (Mercier 2017 note's derivation and speculation).

Panda 1999 measured the same ratio in the near field: r.m.s. crests along the jet boundary at a standing wavelength (0.66D, 1.03D) shorter than the mean shock spacing (0.77D, 1.28D) (Table 1, p. 81), which he could not explain (p. 94). The closing shock wavenumber is k_kh + k_up ≈ k_sw, Mercier's k_h + k_s, and both jets are k_s2 stages (A2 at M_j 1.19, Mercier 2017, Table 1, p. 204; C at 1.42), so their ratios of 1.17 and 1.24 (note's arithmetic) sit near k_s2/k_s1 ≈ 1.2–1.3 (Nogueira 2022, p. 8; Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 5, 11). The match is approximate: Panda's L averages 5–6 shock terminations, the slightly subsonic G-JM puts the closure k_s a little above k_sw, he has no A1 case, and three other jets with L_sw ≈ 1.3L (Table 2, p. 86) do not fit. Powell's directivity angle agrees: with the second-cell spacing, B obeys Powell's formula to about 2 % and C sits about 24 % above it (Fig. 21, p. 2834–2835; Powell 1992 note's derivation), as B on k_s1 and C on k_s2 requires. Powell's "N changes by one" is the loop-count form of the same statement.

At Pprime, M_j ≤ 1.115 gives only A1, 1.12 jumps between A1 and A2, and ≥ 1.13 only A2, with no coexistence, dwell times of 1–4 s and an A1 envelope modulated at St ≈ 0.069 ≈ St₂ − St₁ only where switching occurs (Mancinelli 2019, p. 6–7). At NPR 2.20 the A1 (St 0.606) and A2 (0.694) amplitudes have ρ = −0.82, and the St = 0 SPOD coefficient correlates at 0.96 and −0.82 with them, so the long-time mean is a state the jet never occupies (Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 8–13).

The group's 400k-frame record at NPR 2.199 (16 mm, "medium lip"; `Codebase/HPOD_Investigation/README.md`, unpublished) refines both. HPOD amplitudes give ρ = −0.89 and 48 switches in 2.6 s, A2 dominant 64 % of the time, with median dwells of 19 ms (A1) and 30 ms (A2), about a hundred times shorter than Pprime's. Switches take a median 4.9 ms (≈ 64 A1 cycles), well above the method's resolution, so they are gradual. During long dwells the weaker tone stays about 9 dB down and phase-coherent, and the A2 − A1 line is phase-locked to the product of the two tones (triad coherence 0.88); its BMD mode is the beat scattered by the shock cells (k₂ − k₁ − k_s), not the cells breathing. Joint statistics that separate switching from coexistence are in [time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md).

Norum 1983 is the earliest report of the same behaviour. At the B → C transition (β 0.7, NPR ≈ 2.5) the shadowgraph shock-cell pattern switched "every few seconds", audibly, and a hand near the exit held the jet in either state (p. 236). The visible cell count changed with the stage (about 5 in B, 8 in C, 9–11 in A; p. 236–237), a qualitative forerunner of the per-loop mean flows of Mazharmanesh 2026.

Powell 1992 is the library's only sweep test of hysteresis. Swept up and down without restarting, only C ↔ D moved: at NPR 4.48 the tone is C (11.00 kHz) with pressure rising and D (9.125 kHz) with it falling (Fig. 3, p. 2826), over about NPR 4.34–4.71 (read from Figs 2 and 17). The A1/A2, A2/B and B/C boundaries coincide at the ≈ 0.1 NPR point spacing (Fig. 17, p. 2832; note's reading), so any A1/A2 bistable band is narrower than ΔM_j ≈ 0.03. At NPR 2.45 A2 and B are both dominant and the jet "switches rapidly between them" (p. 2825, 2835).

Impinging jets switch the same way: at NPR 3.04 Henderson 1993 found two tones in 32-average spectra but one or the other in 2-average spectra, at random intervals, near a stage jump (p. 318); like [Ho 1981](../Notes/ho-1981-dynamics-impinging-jet-feedback-phenomenon.md) (p. 134–135) it gives no dwell times.

The far field sees the mean flow change with the stage. Over a fine M_j 1.0–1.55 traverse, the BBSAN peak at 90° follows 2St_s through every stage jump while a notched, screech-free twin varies smoothly (André 2013, Fig. 8, p. 669), a time-averaged counterpart of Mazharmanesh 2026's per-loop means, though harmonic radiation pulling the hump would also lock it (André 2013 note). Where two tones share the averaged spectrum (M_j ≈ 1.25, 1.40) the hump sits between them (p. 669), as an average over two exclusive states would give (note's reading, untested).

Averaged spectra cannot settle coexistence. The B → C switch is abrupt with a thin lip but overlaps over M_j 1.38–1.41 with a thick lip (Ponton 1992, p. 538); Norum and Seiner see two or more stages in most long spectra (p. 236; p. 957); Raman (1998, p. 55–56; 1999, p. 549) cites both switching and coexisting tones in rectangular jets; and Mercier could hold a single A2 tone only at M_j 1.15 (p. 205). Powell's secondary tones, about 25 dB down, continue each branch on both sides of every jump (Figs 2, 18–19, p. 2825, 2833), and the group record's −9 dB partner shows that such a weak loop can run beneath the dominant one; they are the ancestor of Bell's coexisting "processes" and Edgington-Mitchell 2022's multimodal peaks (Powell 1992 note). The explicit coexistence claim came from simulation (see Disagreements).

## Multimodal loops and non-round jets

At higher NPR several loops appear in one block-averaged SPOD: four non-harmonic tones at NPR 3.3, none on k_s1 (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 15–17), and three loops at NPR 4.0 in an AR 2 rectangular jet, including a bi-axial flapping mode (Mazharmanesh 2025, p. 11–17). An M_j 1.56 LES has two helical C tones (St 0.28, 0.305) and an axisymmetric u (0.38) in one 500 D_j/u_j record, without saying whether they coexist or alternate (Gojon 2018, p. 2920–2921). Henderson's rig shows "symmetrical" and "helical" screech lines at the same NPR 2.36 (Fig. 4, p. 312), from unstated runs.

Non-round jets stage early and nozzle by nozzle. The group's AR 2 converging ellipse (D_e 14.14 mm, thick, azimuthally varying lip) has five dominant-tone stages over NPR 1.95–4.3: varicose I to NPR 2.05 (St ≈ 1), flapping II (2.10–3.10), III (3.15–3.35) and IV (3.40–3.90), and a weakly antisymmetric "helical" V (3.95–4.3) (Edgington-Mitchell 2015, staging, Fig. 2, p. 1009–1017; note's digitisation). Its varicose → flapping jump comes about 0.1 NPR before the ≈ 2.2 of thin-lip AR 2 rectangular and elliptical jets, at similar St (Mazharmanesh 2025, p. 7); the flapping tone falls from St 0.52 at NPR 2.2 ([Edgington-Mitchell 2015, multimodal](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) p. 2743) to 0.38 and 0.27 at 2.6 and 3.4 ([Ivelja 2024](../Notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) p. 23–24). Its k_s2 stage III is bounded (ΔNPR ≈ 0.2), after which the tone returns to the II/IV trendline (p. 1010, 1020), whereas k_s2 loops in Mazharmanesh 2026's thinner-lip ellipse persist from NPR 3.4 to 4.9 (p. 21–22). Only dominant tones are plotted, so whether III coexists with a weaker II/IV loop is unknown.

In an AR 2 C-D rectangular jet a second minor-axis flapping tone from NPR ≈ 3.5 is assigned to k_sh2 (Nogueira 2025, p. 4219–4220), with k_sh2/k_sh1 ≈ 1.24–1.26 (Fig. 14, p. 4218, note's reading; cf. Mercier's 1.28 for A2), and a planar model on k_s2 follows the same second band in Mazharmanesh 2025's AR 2 data (Lu 2026, p. 20). Lu calls this "mode staging", but the k_sh1 and k_sh2 loops coexist in block-averaged SPOD rather than replacing each other (Nogueira 2025, p. 4214–4215), as in the multimodal AR 2 ellipse of Mazharmanesh 2026. The assignment rests on frequency alone: the predicted k_s2 tones lie on or between other observed tones (Fig. 17, p. 4220; Nogueira 2025 and Lu 2026 notes). Some stage changes may not show in spectra: Mercier's flapping cases split into N = m = 3 and N = m = 4 with no tone jump (St 0.31 → 0.30 at M_j 1.35 → 1.37; p. 224, Table 2), though that rests on N alone and is tentative.

A second instability need not screech: the Monash ellipse at NPR 2.2 carries a slower, silent antisymmetric pair (see "Which instability screeches" below). Staging maps of non-round nozzles are on [non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md).

## Lip, exterior and reflectors

In free jets, thicker lips widen the helical C range and bring B back above M_j ≈ 1.7 (Ponton 1992, p. 537, 547); Powell's t/D 0.8 jet turns sinuous (D) from M_j ≈ 1.62–1.67, probably the same family (Powell 1992 note's inference). Which shapes appear at all is facility-specific: Lyon jets flap but never go helical, other rigs show helical modes but no flapping, Monash shows both, and lip thickness may decide whether the helical mode appears (Edgington-Mitchell 2019, p. 130–131). Covering Panda's nozzle flange changes screech amplitude and frequency (Panda 1999, p. 73), an upstream plate gave m = 0 multimodality in one Monash rig (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 6, 16), and one rig has no screech above NPR 3.79 (Henderson 1993, p. 316).

A reflector near a free jet selects the stage by loop phase. As a baffle was moved along Norum's tube from the exit to 6D upstream (β 0.7), the dominant stage changed 13 times, each stage holding for 0.2–0.8D of travel while its tone fell 2–4 % ("a longer feedback cycle") before the jet "locked onto" another (p. 236–237). Fixed baffles moved the B/C limit over NPR 2.42–2.90 and could remove B (Fig. 4, p. 237; note's conversion): the library's clearest evidence that the phase of a reflected path picks the winning loop (Norum 1983 note), though the stages are named from wavelength only.

A single lip tab acts differently. One radial tab (≈ 2 % blockage) on a 15 mm nozzle raises St at every NPR, delays the first jump by 0.1 NPR, and removes the upward B → C jump and the C → D drop (Johnston 2016, Fig. 2, p. 53–55; note's digitisation); at NPR 2.6 B flapping becomes a "punctuated toroid", symmetric in two orthogonal views (p. 56–58). This is no clean receptivity test: the tab shortens the cells by about 16 % and every tone moves, the first and third branches by about what the shorter cells alone give (×1.18–1.20) and the second by ×1.5, where the shape change was imaged (note's reading and arithmetic).

The cleanest test that the jet sets the frequencies and the boundary picks the loop is impinging. In a group NPR 3.4 jet with a plate at 5D, changing only the nozzle exterior gives helical St 0.255 (t/D 0.1), toroidal 0.385 plus helical 0.241 (15D flat cap), or toroidal 0.383 plus helical 0.253 (t/D 0.33) (Weightman 2019, p. 430–438), yet across nine configurations the tones stay near St 0.25 and 0.39 (Fig. 18, p. 441–442). Foam on the outer nozzle base, about 3D upstream of the exit (note's reading), removes the thin-lip helical loop, "for this facility" (p. 440–441), and a reflector a chosen fraction of a wavelength behind the exit swaps impinging tones (Henderson 1993, p. 318–319). The direction differs: a thick lip brings in a toroidal loop when impinging, but favours helical C and B in free jets (Weightman 2019, p. 433). No free-jet test has changed only the exterior and tracked the A1/A2 limits; Norum's baffle and Johnston's tab were followed only through dominant tones and snapshot statistics.

## Twin jets

Twin jets add symmetry competition, most intermittent at the A1 → A2 staging (Wong 2023, p. 9–12). Close spacing also moves staging: at s/D 1.9, coupling brings A → B forward from M_j ≈ 1.13 to 1.09 and widens B to about 1.09–1.6 (Seiner 1988, Figs 9–11; note's readings), whereas Wong finds A1/A2 NPRs nearly unchanged for s/D ≥ 2. Its twin B and C tones give λ_h/L ≈ 2.0 and 1.7, as k_s1 and k_s2 closure would (note's arithmetic, assumed U_c). Bell 2021 (p. 9) lists three untested staging mechanisms: a change of azimuthal mode or coupling, of the number of structures in the loop, or from free-stream to guided return. At s/D = 3, coherence reveals weaker continuous "processes" (one loop each, with fixed azimuthal modes, coupling and source) that coexist across the acoustic jumps at NPR 3.4 and 4.4 (p. 7–9). Between antisymmetric and symmetric coupling the jets couple either way or one oscillates alone; the coupled states never occur together, and the tones are uncorrelated rather than exclusive (p. 16, 23–29), which the authors read as competition between two global modes (p. 29); the loudest process is noise-like just after its onset at NPR 2.3 (Fig. 6b, read), like Wong's intermittency near a stage boundary. [Bell 2018](../Notes/bell-2018-coupled-underexpanded-supersonic-twin-jets.md) had listed switching among four explanations for a standing wave missing from the PIV statistics of a loud NPR 4.6 state (p. 15), which Bell 2021 later found (Bell 2018 note). Coupling can also change with no frequency jump (Knast 2018, p. 5, 11). LES resolves intermittent coupling in twin rectangular jets ([Jeun 2024](../Notes/jeun-2024-screech-coupling-closure-rectangular-twin-jets.md)), and some apparent intermittency is only a beat between detuned tones ([Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../Notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md) p. 10–11; Wong 2023, p. 32–38). The coupling physics is on [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md).

## Disagreements and open questions

**One reflection point or two shock wavenumbers.** Mancinelli's models produce A1/A2 by changing p at a fixed 4th-cell reflection; Nogueira 2022 and Edgington-Mitchell 2022 attribute A2 to k_s2 from downstream cells. Mercier's data, the basis of the first view, fit both: the source stays at the 4th shock while the loop gains a period, and the same jump appears as (k_h + k_s)/k̄_sc = 1.28 ≈ m/N = 5/4 (p. 214, 222–224), so over the first four cells the two descriptions are one statement (Mercier 2017 note; cf. Mancinelli 2021, p. 24). Panda's A2 jet is a second case: sound is born between the 3rd and 4th shocks (2.37 < x/D < 3.07; p. 90), yet k_sw is 1.17 times the mean shock wavenumber (p. 81, note's arithmetic). Still open is where k_s2 comes from if the source does not move, since Nogueira 2022 places A2 downstream; Norum's unchanged first four cells (p. 236–237) suggest that a near-fixed source can still meet a downstream k_s2 (note's inference). [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) matched its own A2 case (NPR 2.25) with the leading k_s, though with a larger mismatch than for A1 (Nogueira 2022 note), and Farghadan 2026 finds the k_s2 peak buried in the primary one. The rectangular-jet k_s2 labels (Nogueira 2025; Lu 2026) rest on frequency alone. Mercier's counts assume a return at c₀, which a G-JM near its lower band edge roughly satisfies (Mercier 2017 note; Gojon 2018, p. 2919–2920). Long-field (> 10D) mean shock spectra, BMD k_S and the loop count (N, m) of A1 and A2 on one rig would settle the labelling.

**Selection, hysteresis and bistability.** Linear models allow both A1 and A2 over a band (Nogueira 2022, p. 13; Mancinelli 2021), yet experiments switch over only ΔM_j ≈ 0.01–0.015, and models with over-wide G-JM bands cannot bound a stage (Lu 2026, p. 20–21). Powell's sweep bounds any A1/A2 hysteresis band below ΔNPR ≈ 0.1, and Norum's hand held either the B or the C state at one NPR (p. 236), which fits bistability but also changes the reflecting boundary. Open: whether A1/A2 is bistable at finer resolution, whether switching is stochastic between attractors or driven by supply-pressure drift (Wong 2023, p. 39–41), and why dwell times differ a hundredfold between Pprime and the group's rig. Classical global-mode theory treats only supercritical Hopf bifurcations, with no known subcritical example ([Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../Notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md) p. 517), so C ↔ D or A1/A2 bistability lies outside it. Three of its results still bear on competition: global modes come in families O(ε) apart in frequency (eq. 46, p. 496); a self-excited hot jet carries two modes with separate onsets (Fig. 18b, p. 513); and feedback that suppresses one global mode tends to destabilise another (p. 518–519). Global stability on each loop's short-time mean would test whether each state damps the other loop (Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 19). Other resonators add no precedent for hysteresis: jet–edge switches between two guided-wave loops repeat as M_j rises and falls ([Stavropoulos 2026](../Notes/stavropoulos-2026-jet-edge-interaction.md) p. 14–18), and impinging-jet "hysteresis" is intermittent switching at one stand-off (Ho 1981, p. 134–135; Henderson 1993, p. 318).

**Coexistence or alternation.** The coexistence view comes from Shen & Tam's URANS two-tone states at M_j 1.2 (A1 + A2) and 1.5 (B + C), which they said "repudiate" switching (p. 35–39). But a noise-free URANS cannot hop between attractors, its spectral blocks are about one real switch long, no run sits at the A1/A2 point, and its sign-blind snapshot spectra are not unique (Shen & Tam 2002 note). Its intermodulation lines (A2 − A1, 2A1 − A2, 2A2 − A1) are better evidence that both tones run together, and they match the group record's −9 dB partner and quadratic A2 − A1 line. A reconciling hypothesis (Shen & Tam 2002 note's, untested): a deterministic two-tone state with one dominant loop, which turbulence makes hop between A1-dominant and A2-dominant versions. For multimodal tones at higher NPR (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 7), a difference tone louder than one parent suggests two impinging loops run together (Weightman 2019 note's inference), and the rectangular k_sh1 and k_sh2 loops share a block-averaged spectrum (Nogueira 2025); Gojon 2018's two C tones, André's hump between two tones and the ellipse's bounded stage III are untested. The coexisting, uncorrelated twin-jet tones (Bell 2021, p. 16, 29) have envelope spreads of 5–6 dB and ≈ 20 % "interruptions", as a narrow-band Gaussian (Rayleigh) envelope would, so they may be the fading of damped, noise-driven resonances rather than switching between limit cycles (Bell 2021 note). Long time-resolved records with HPOD and JPDFs, checked against a Rayleigh null, and LES continuation are the tests.

**Helical sense and flapping plane.** On Powell's rig the C helix takes either sense about equally often, fixed while the jet runs but able to change on restart (p. 2829), and D's plane rotates at a variable rate (p. 2831–2832); Powell had already seen flapping modes precess "rather unpredictably" (Raman 1999, p. 547). On Henderson's rig the helical spin reversed at random during continuous running (p. 324). A two-microphone phase averages over reversals: an 80:20 mix gives about 46° instead of 60° (Henderson 1993 note's calculation). Weak |m| = 2 streaks that set and slowly rotate a flapping axis (Nogueira 2026, p. 11, 14) and unsteady initial swirl (Powell 1992, p. 2832) are candidate causes of the wandering plane. An azimuthal ring decomposed into m = +1 and −1 per block would measure reversal rates; swirl or streak control would separate the causes.

**Which instability screeches.** At NPR 2.2 the Monash ellipse carries a slower antisymmetric, helical-like POD pair (λ ≈ 2.4D, uncorrelated with the flapping pair) that makes no tone (Edgington-Mitchell 2015, multimodal, p. 2745–2748). The authors argue that it grows only after the shock train has ended, so it cannot leak (p. 2747–2748). The paper predates G-JM closure, though, and the alternative is that no upstream wave exists for that pair near St 0.27: in this nozzle even the flapping (se₁) G-JM is neutral only over a short x band (Ivelja 2024, p. 22–24, NPR 2.6 and 3.4), and no band has been computed for the helical-like family. Quasi-helical tones do appear at higher NPR, in another ellipse (St 0.28 at NPR 3.6, Mazharmanesh 2025, p. 16; 0.273, weak and intermittent, Mazharmanesh 2026, p. 13, 15) and perhaps in this one (mode V above NPR 3.95; Edgington-Mitchell 2015, staging, p. 1017), which fits the first view without excluding the second. Mode V is weak evidence: its helical label comes from two separately acquired views, which cannot test a helix's phase lock, and a figure-read k_sw/k_s ≈ 1.03–1.08 at NPR 4.2 points to a change of azimuthal family rather than the authors' k_s2 (note's estimate). Filtering the NPR 2.2 pair's PIV POD modes by the sign of k_x would separate the two views.

## Methods and data

- **Acoustics**: fine NPR steps (ΔM_j = 0.005), swept both ways without restarts to test hysteresis (Powell 1992, p. 2826, 2832), azimuthal arrays to see m (Mancinelli 2019, p. 5), and records covering many switches, since dwells range from tens of milliseconds (group record) to seconds (Mancinelli 2019). A microphone pair stepped round the jet gives m only on average (Powell 1992, p. 2828–2830); a ring decomposed into m = 0, ±1 per block also resolves spin reversals (Henderson 1993 note). Wavelets show switching in time (Mancinelli 2019; Wong 2023); coherence at all frequencies finds weak "processes", and band-pass Hilbert envelopes give per-process JPDFs and dropout statistics (Bell 2021, p. 8–14). The BBSAN peak against 2St_s is a time-averaged far-field sign of the stage's mean flow (André 2013, p. 669).
- **Mean fields** (any schlieren, BOS, PIV or probe mean): centreline FFT for k_s1, k_s2, k_s3, with a field of view of 8–10D or more and a sliding window to locate each peak (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 7, 17; Nogueira 2022, p. 7–8). Near-field r.m.s. crest spacing gives k_sw without time resolution, and its ratio to the shock spacing labels the stage (Panda 1999, p. 79–81; Edgington-Mitchell 2015, staging, p. 1018–1021).
- **Time-resolved schlieren**: SPOD frequency–time maps, JPDFs of tone amplitudes and BMD interaction maps for k_S (Mazharmanesh 2026); rotated views and sub-domain SPOD for non-round modes (Mazharmanesh 2025; Nogueira 2025); lip-line phase from a thin strip (16–24 px at 220–430 kHz) gives the loop count and U_c(x) (Mercier 2017, p. 206–208, 222–225). For HPOD, a band width of 0.5Δf resolves switches of a few beat periods, and one band holding both tones shows coexistence as an IF ripple at Δf (`Codebase/HPOD_Investigation/README.md`).
- **LES**: f–k spectra at radii inside the jet, split into m = 0 and 1, place the upstream wave on its guided-mode band (Gojon 2018, p. 2921–2923); use a line much longer than 4.5D_j, which resolves kD_j only to about 1.4 (Gojon 2018 note).
- **Non-time-resolved PIV and schlieren**: conditional POD on each jet's pair amplitude separates coupled and single-jet states (Bell 2021, p. 23–27); otherwise the POD-pair route needs one steady tone and hides switching, and two coexisting loops give a filled-disc phase portrait (Weightman 2019, p. 431). Two-point correlations in two orthogonal views classify a stage's shape (Edgington-Mitchell 2015, staging; Johnston 2016) but cannot test a helix or alternation; a standing wave missing from the averaged r.m.s. of a loud jet hints at switching (Bell 2018, p. 15).
- **Pitfalls**: beating mimics coupling (Wong 2023, p. 32–38); envelope-threshold dropouts need a Rayleigh null, a stated filter and a beat test (Bell 2021 note); check bicoherence before counting an m = 0 tone as a stage (Mancinelli 2021); stage names from wavelength branches alone need an azimuthal check (Norum 1983; Seiner 1988); block-averaged spectra cannot tell coexistence from alternation; a microphone-pair phase averages over spin reversals and a rotating plane (Henderson 1993, p. 324; Powell 1992, p. 2829–2832).
- **Data tables**: tone frequencies by stage are in `Data/screech_tones.csv`.

## Relevance to our work

**Lab experiments.** Record lip thickness, outer nozzle shape, upstream surfaces and enclosure walls in every campaign (Bell 2021, p. 6, 17–18; Weightman 2019, p. 440–441; Norum 1983, p. 237), and sweep NPR up and down in steps of 0.01 or finer around 2.15–2.30, to resolve an A1/A2 hysteresis band below Powell's 0.1. Long schlieren records at several NPRs across the switch would give dwell time and residence fraction against NPR, which a sharp exchange and a bistable band predict differently; the group's ellipse offers a second jump at fixed symmetry (II → III near NPR 3.15) for the same test. The existing Monash A1 (NPR 2.10) and A2 (2.25) PIV could give Mercier's loop count from the POD-pair phase along the lip line, testing m = 4 → 5 at fixed N on a thick-lip nozzle (Mercier 2017 note). The 2.5 s acoustic records of the group's impinging lip study could test whether its two loops coexist, from band-pass envelopes and difference-tone bicoherence (Weightman 2019 note).

**SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD.** The pipeline has been run on the 400k-frame NPR 2.199 record (`Codebase/HPOD_Investigation/`): the reconstruction route makes no difference, the band width does (B = 0.5Δf resolves the 4.9 ms switches), and the St ≈ 0 mode tracks the state (−0.92), reproducing Mazharmanesh 2026. Still to do: the lag between tone amplitude and the St = 0 mode as a test of two-way coupling (Mazharmanesh 2026); Rayleigh-null and beat tests before calling a dropout a switch (Bell 2021 note); conditioning short-time far-field spectra on the state, to see whether the BBSAN hump jumps with the tone (André 2013 note); and records at other NPRs and lips, since one record cannot say whether 19–30 ms dwells are general.

**The PyFR staging-continuation LES** (`Simulations/PyFR_Staging_Continuation/`; 16 mm converging nozzle, A1 seeded at NPR 2.10, A2 at 2.25) steps NPR up from converged A1 and down from converged A2, and extrapolates each limit from a saddle-node fit of 1/t_switch: an assumption about the bifurcation, in territory classical theory leaves out (Huerre 1990 note's inference). The design assumes Pprime's 1–4 s dwells (about 10⁴–10⁵ D/U_j), which no LES can wait out, but the group's rig switches far faster: median dwells of 19–30 ms are about 400–650 D/U_j, and a switch about 100 D/U_j (page's conversion, U_j ≈ 345 m/s). If the LES matches that rate, a few thousand D/U_j near NPR 2.2 would hold several spontaneous switches to compare with the record (check first that the record's "medium lip" nozzle is the one meshed). What the runs could settle:
1. *Bistability.* The A1 up-branch and A2 down-branch limits bound any hysteresis band. If they coincide, staging is a sharp exchange; either way the experimental 50 % point should lie between them. C ↔ D (NPR ≈ 4.3–4.8) is a positive control, where two distinct limits should appear, though it needs a domain long enough for D's instability peak at 8–10D (Powell 1992, p. 2827; note's suggestion). The driver's r(t) = ln(A_in/A_out) assumes one tone dies; a partner held at −9 dB, as in the record and in Shen & Tam's two-tone states, would read as "persisted" or "trending", so a third, "coexisting" outcome (both envelopes steady, A2 − A1 phase-locked to a₁a₂*) is worth adding (Shen & Tam 2002 note).
2. *Mean-flow-mediated selection.* Converged A1 and A2 states at one NPR give two mean flows. Their shock spectra test whether the loop changes k_s2 strength (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 12), local, Floquet or global analysis whether each state damps the other loop (Farghadan 2026 already finds an St 0.66 mode beside the 0.72 tone at M_j 1.12), and G-JM bands on each mean are the only reduced route to a stage's cut-on and cut-off (Lu 2026, p. 20–21; note's suggestion). Near-field crest spacing should jump from 2π/k_s1 to 2π/k_s2 across the switch (Panda 1999 note), and n = 0 f–k spectra of the two states would show whether the guided-mode band moves or only the shock wavenumber it pairs with (Gojon 2018 note).
3. *Rates.* Compare 1/t_switch and post-step growth or decay with ω0i (0.223 against 0.057; Nogueira 2022, p. 13) and the ω_i of Mancinelli 2021. Each NPR step is Huerre & Monkewitz's conclusive transient test ([screech-frequency-and-global-modes](screech-frequency-and-global-modes.md)): Landau fits to Hilbert envelopes at several points should agree (Huerre 1990, p. 497, 518).
4. *Precursors.* Check whether energy at k_kh − k_s2 grows before a forced switch (Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 13), and use BMD to test whether the St₂ − St₁ modulation (Mancinelli 2019, p. 7) is a driving triad. Resolvent forcing or switching one tone off would show which product makes the record's shock-scattered A2 − A1 component, which the record cannot tell (`Codebase/HPOD_Investigation/README.md`, Sect. 6).
5. *Receptivity.* Changing only the lip or outer geometry tests whether limits shift while frequencies do not (Ponton 1992; Nogueira 2021, absolute instability, p. 22), the free-jet version of Weightman 2019's impinging result. Projecting the near-lip field onto KH and G-JM eigenfunctions would estimate the R₁ and R₂ that Mancinelli 2021 (p. 22) needs.
6. *Leakage.* Check whether the first leak moves from the 3rd to the 4th cell on staging (Panda's A2 sound starts between them, p. 90), and test leakage as closure ([screech](screech.md)) with the sound speed the ray model lacked ([Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) p. 12, 19–23).
7. *Loop count.* Mercier's lip-line phase analysis (y/D ≈ 0.55) should give N = 4 in both states and m = 4 → 5 (p. 222–224), a discrete state label; the core field then shows whether the upstream wave near −ω/c₀ is the G-JM or sound (p. 213).
8. *Far field and chirality.* Check whether the BBSAN peak near 90° sits at 2f_s and jumps between the A1 and A2 states (André 2013, p. 669); in helical states keep m = +1 and m = −1 separate, since the sense can reverse within a record (Henderson 1993, p. 324).

The limits and switch times must not depend on domain length, sponge or outflow treatment, since outflow-to-inflow feedback can self-excite spatial simulations (Huerre 1990, p. 498), and their sensitivity to the exit boundary layer needs testing, the likely reason an LES gets frequencies right and amplitudes wrong (Gojon 2019, p. 8–10, 18–19). The README's 1 mm flat lip differs from the 16 mm nozzle of Mazharmanesh 2026 (l/D_e = 0.31), the leakage nozzle (t/D = 0.33; Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage, p. 4) and Powell's t/D 0.8, which stages near NPR 2.3. Model the exterior and any flange as built, with no sponge where the experiment has a reflecting surface, since a baffle near the exit moved Norum's B/C limit by ΔNPR ≈ 0.4 (Norum 1983, p. 237; Weightman 2019 note; Panda 1999, p. 73).
