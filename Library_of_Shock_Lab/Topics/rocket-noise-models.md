# Rocket noise models

_Last updated: 2026-10-25 · 31 papers_

## Scope
How launch-vehicle and rocket-motor noise is predicted: the NASA SP-8072 chain (Eldred 1971) and its distributed source methods; the corrections added to it (core length, source location, directivity, acoustic efficiency, clusters, deflectors and pads); the maximum-OASPL formula and the SATURN model that are replacing it; the instability-wave theory behind the Mach-wave source; the propagation stage; ignition overpressure; and hybrid CAA and LES prediction. Each model is set against the measurements that test it. The measurements themselves are on [rocket-noise-measurements](rocket-noise-measurements.md), lab and aircraft jet-noise physics on [jet-noise-mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md), and lab impinging-jet feedback on [impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md). SP-8072 itself is not in the library. It is known through the notes that restate it, mainly [Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), [Haynes & Kenny 2009](../Notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md), [Casalino 2009](../Notes/casalino-2009-hybrid-empirical-caa-rocket-noise.md) and [Ranow 2021](../Notes/ranow-2021-acoustic-prediction-rocket-flame-deflector.md), and through the first revisions of its free-jet inputs, [McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md) (launch far field) and [Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md) (static near field).

## Current understanding
1. **Every engineering model is still the SP-8072 chain:** acoustic power = η × mechanical power, distributed along the plume, given a spectrum and a directivity, spread spherically and summed incoherently ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 25–26; [Ranow 2021](../Notes/ranow-2021-acoustic-prediction-rocket-flame-deflector.md) p. 9–15). SATURN keeps the chain and replaces its inputs with modern full-scale data ([Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 68–71).
2. **Acoustic efficiency of modern rockets is about 0.3–0.4%,** below SP-8072's 1% and the 0.5% of the 1996 formula: RSRM 0.43%, Falcon 9 0.34% and then 0.30%, Atlas V 0.33% ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 8; [Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md) p. 627–628; [Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md) p. 14; [Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md) p. 375). Whether η depends on motor size or clustering is open.
3. **The dominant source sits about 17–22 D_e downstream,** not in the subsonic plume where SP-8072 puts it. The SP-8072 curve inherits a plotting error from Potter (1968) ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 12–14).
4. **Peak directivity is about 64–72° from the plume axis,** set by Mach wave radiation and a convective Mach number; vertical launches already gave 70–80° in 1957 ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md) p. 512). SP-8072's ~50° is an artefact of nozzle-centred arcs at 80 D_e, which are not in the geometric far field ([James 2012](../Notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md) p. 8, 11; [Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md) p. 625; [Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 82).
5. **With these inputs, far-field maximum levels are predicted to about 1–2 dB within 20 km,** against about 4 dB rms for the original 1996 formula ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md) p. 375–377; [Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 53, 91, 95). Beyond that, the atmosphere dominates and errors reach about 10 dB in either direction.
6. **Linear propagation gets OASPL right, but high-frequency levels need shocks.** OASPL is set by the low-frequency peak and barely changes with nonlinearity, while launch waveforms keep their shocks to 15 km ([McInerny & Ölçmen 2005](../Notes/mcinerny-2005-high-intensity-rocket-noise-nonlinear-propagation.md) p. 588–590; [Gee 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md) p. 9–10, 12; [Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md) p. 25–27).
7. **The near-pad environment is not solved.** Empirical pad corrections that fix one vehicle break another ([Haynes & Kenny 2009](../Notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md) vs [Ranow 2021](../Notes/ranow-2021-acoustic-prediction-rocket-flame-deflector.md)). Hybrid CAA and LES find the right mechanisms (pad-cavity confinement, ground-reflected Mach waves) but overpredict pad cases by 2–4 dB ([Casalino 2009](../Notes/casalino-2009-hybrid-empirical-caa-rocket-noise.md) p. 15; [Tsutsumi 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md) p. 12–13), while a free-jet LES/CAA now matches OASPL within about 1 dB ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md) p. 16, 28). Ignition overpressure has only a 1985 on-vehicle model ([Ikawa & Laspesa 1985](../Notes/ikawa-1985-ignition-duct-overpressure-space-shuttle-srm.md)).
8. **No model has a tonal or shock-associated term.** This fits the full-scale data, where screech, impingement tones and BBSAN are not seen ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 9, 16–17; [Tsutsumi 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md) p. 13), although BBSAN appears in one hot 1/40-scale jet ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md) p. 19).

## SP-8072 and its distributed source methods
SP-8072 offers extrapolation from similar pads (Method 1), a single-point source per frequency band (Method 2, SAM) and the distributed source method (Method 3) ([Ranow 2021](../Notes/ranow-2021-acoustic-prediction-rocket-flame-deflector.md) p. 9). DSM-1 assigns each Strouhal band to one plume slice using the Potter curve. DSM-2 lets every slice radiate a full spectrum, with source power peaking at 1.5 core lengths ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 25–26; [Casalino 2009](../Notes/casalino-2009-hybrid-empirical-caa-rocket-noise.md) p. 4).

The two variants do not agree. Users found DSM-2 5–10 dB below DSM-1 and below data ([Haynes & Kenny 2009](../Notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md) p. 15). DSM-2's power allocation also had to be shifted by about 2 dB to conserve energy, an undocumented flaw. The two methods agree in the far field but differ strongly in the near field, where launch loads matter ([James 2012](../Notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md) p. 8, 11).

Against modern data, SP-8072 gets unweighted overall levels roughly right but spectra and timing wrong. Its Ares I OASPL is within 0–2 dB of the data ([Ranow 2021](../Notes/ranow-2021-acoustic-prediction-rocket-flame-deflector.md) p. 41–44). Within 20 km, its L_Z,max RMSE is 2.2 dB (Firefly Alpha) and 2.7 dB (Starship). But its spectral peak is an octave too high, its peak noise arrives late and its A-weighted RMSE is 8.8 dB for Alpha ([Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 91–98). Lubert et al. cite OASPL errors above 10 dB at some angles, and conclude that basing predictions on SP-8072 is "no longer appropriate" ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 4, 27).

## Core length and source location
Eldred scaled the source distribution on a core length L_c = 3.45 D_e(1 + 0.38M_e)². Power per unit length peaks at 1.5 L_c and is effective out to about 5 L_c ([Casalino 2009](../Notes/casalino-2009-hybrid-empirical-caa-rocket-noise.md) p. 4). McInerny's launch analysis took a laminar core of 16–20 D_e and a supersonic core of 25–35 D_e ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md) p. 511–512).

For ONERA's M_e 3.3 static rockets, Eldred's length put the power peak at about 26D and needed η ≈ 3–4% to match near-field levels. [Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md) replaced it with L_C* = 1.75 D_j(1 + 0.38M_j)² in fully expanded variables, moving the peak to about 11D (eq. 12, p. 1855). The calibration is thin: four similar small rockets plus Rita II, rocket F and MARTEL Jet IV; apparent peaks of 6–20D inferred by fitting near-field SPL inside 20D with the model's own source shape (Fig. 11, p. 1856); ±20% on the 1.75 (eq. 14); and octave peaks near fD/U_e ≈ 0.1 (note's arithmetic), several times the full-scale Sr. Varnier proposed the supersonic length L_S = D_j(5M_j^1.8 + 0.8) as the true reference (eqs. 18–19, p. 1857), a law fitted to two jets.

[Haynes & Kenny 2009](../Notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md) adopted the halved constant but kept exit variables, a hybrid that gives about 13D rather than 11D for the ONERA rockets (Varnier note's arithmetic). Their support was Shirie's (1967) observation that shocks shorten both cores (p. 2), an RSRM RANS that puts the peak source at 36 m rather than Eldred's 75 m (p. 9), and static-test agreement within 0–3 dB against 5–7 dB with Eldred's length (slides, p. 25).

Measurements of where the sound comes from tell a different story. RSRM firings trace peak OASPL back to 17–18D ([Gee 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md) p. 4). GEM-60 near-field intensity puts the 50 Hz source at 17–32D, moving to 10–20D above 600 Hz ([Gee 2016](../Notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md) p. 1, 5). Rockets of all sizes radiate peak OASPL from 17–20 D_e, with the source about 22 D_e at Sr ≈ 0.025 ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 12–14). For the 3.88 m RSRM, the 36 m peak is about 9 D_e and Eldred's 75 m about 19 D_e, so the correction that improves vehicle levels moves the source away from where it is measured. Lubert suspects Varnier's length is biased by limited data and the Potter error (p. 27). Its calibration at high Strouhal number, where measured sources also sit further upstream, may explain part of the gap; that is a reading of the notes, not a published test.

The apparent source also depends on observation angle. For a lab M 1.9 jet, the sideline (fine-scale) source peaks at x/D ≈ 17 and the aft large-structure source at x/D ≈ 11 cold to ≈ 14 at T_r/T_a 2.2 ([Tam et al. 2008](../Notes/tam-2008-sources-of-jet-noise-experimental-evidence.md) p. 286–287); no location law has this. A parallax method locates a source without re-centring: in an inclined-plate LES, impingement-region waves keep the same peak angle on arcs of 20D, 50D and 100D about the impingement point, fixing their source there, while the wall-jet Mach-wave angle grows with radius ([Nonomura et al. 2011](../Notes/nonomura-2011-supersonic-jet-impinging-inclined-plate-acoustic-waves.md) p. 412–413).

The frequency-dependent location laws show the same factor of two. Casalino's fit to Eldred's undeflected curve is s/D_j ≈ 17.4 St^−0.24 ([Casalino 2009](../Notes/casalino-2009-hybrid-empirical-caa-rocket-noise.md) p. 6), and SATURN's fit to the GEM-60 intensity data is s = 9.85 Sr^−0.21 D_eff ([Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 80). At Sr = 0.025 they give about 42 and 21 diameters (my calculation; the diameter definitions differ), matching the thesis's statement that SP-8072 sits about 2× further downstream at low Sr (p. 80). SATURN rejected James's RSRM curve ([James 2012](../Notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md) p. 3–4) because that plume hit a hillside (thesis p. 79), which does strip high-frequency energy and skewness ([Gee 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md) p. 10–11). SATURN's own law therefore rests on a single horizontal GEM-60 firing. The compact-source-per-frequency idea holds only beyond about 100–200 D_eff, because the dominant region is 10–20 D_eff long (thesis p. 78).

## Directivity and the convective Mach number
Static RSRM arcs centred on the nozzle put the peak at about 50° ([Kenny 2009](../Notes/kenny-2009-space-shuttle-srm-plume-acoustics.md) p. 16) or 56° ([Gee 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md) p. 4), close to SP-8072. The RSRM indices that [Haynes & Kenny 2009](../Notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md) put into DSM-2 peak near 42° at 10 Hz and near 55–62° above 50 Hz (p. 12–13).

Three compatible explanations of the low static angle have been offered:
- **Arcs too close.** An 80 D_e arc is not in the far field at low frequency, where the source sits about 31 D_e downstream. Re-referencing each microphone to a frequency-dependent source rotates the OASPL peak from 51° to 65°, matching θ = cos⁻¹(1/M_c) with M_c ≈ 2.3; DSM-1 and DSM-2 only agree beyond 200–300 D_e, about 1 km for the RSRM ([James 2012](../Notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md) p. 5–8, 11). Varnier's earlier re-centring moved the peak only about 5°, because it assumed the SP-8072 curves already hold at 10 L_P ([Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md) p. 1858).
- **Ground effects in horizontal firings.** Cole et al. (1957) found 50–60° for horizontal firings and 70–80° for vertical launches, and McInerny's opinion is that the horizontal data are shaped by the ground. On Titan IV, horizontal factors (5 dB at 50–55°) put the predicted OASPL peak 3–4 s late at 0.5 km, while vertical factors (8 dB at 70–75°) fixed timing and trend at all four stations ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md) p. 512, 514–515), a timing test that anticipates Kellison's trajectory-based directivity.
- **Terrain.** The RSRM plume hit a hillside ([Gee 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md) p. 10–11). For the narrowing above 500 Hz, Haynes & Kenny also suspect terrain (p. 13), where James suggests nonlinear propagation (p. 6).

Which convective Mach number to use is the live question:
- Lubert et al. favour Oertel's M_CO. The equivalent empirical κ is about 0.31–0.32 for rockets, against 0.6–0.85 for lab jets ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 14–15).
- For Falcon 9, the static M_CO gives 69°, 5° high; subtracting the vehicle Mach number (0.55) gives the measured 64° ([Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md) p. 625–627). [Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md) measured the same 64° (1 dB-down band 58–72°) 240–750 m from the pad with no flight correction; the "supersonic" M_c (66°) came closest and Oertel's gave about 70–72° (p. 11–12).
- That speed is eq. 5.1 of [Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md), c/u_j = 1/(1 + a_j/a_o) (p. 472), the same form as the Papamoschou–Roshko M_c ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../Notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md) p. 1, 16), from which the Papamoschou note derives κ ≈ 0.27 for Falcon 9 (a note-writer's calculation). The other speeds Kellison lists (M_c′, M_c″) are Oertel's empirical W′ and W″, not Tam & Hu results; Tam & Hu's subsonic waves have |c| < a_o and no near field, so they cannot radiate Mach waves (p. 456, 465).
- Tam & Hu also suggest why the supersonic speed should win: the Mach number at which the Kelvin–Helmholtz and supersonic families merge falls to about 2 for T_j/T_∞ ≳ 3, beyond which the supersonic waves dominate (Fig. 9, p. 459, 480–481). Rocket plumes lie beyond this, but the result is for a vortex sheet with equal γ and stops at T_j/T_∞ = 3, so applying it to rockets is an inference.
- In a 1/40-scale hot jet LES the most likely lipline u_c/u_j is 0.43, between the rocket and lab κ, and u_c falls along x from about the supersonic-wave value (66–70°) before the core end to the KH value (40°) beyond it ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md) p. 18).
- Against all this, [Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md) revise the Falcon 9 angle to 71° after a 3-D geometry fix and use θ ≈ cos⁻¹√(c₀/U_e) (p. 375, footnote 2 p. 378). SATURN's Atlas V directivity settles at about 72° for Sr > 0.03, with SP-8072's indices 15–20° further aft ([Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 82).

A single M_c is in any case a simplification. Peak angle rises with frequency, from 10–30° below Sr 0.004 to 60–75° above Sr 0.0125 ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md) p. 13). Instability-wave theory gives the same trend: in Tam & Chen's hot Mach 2 jet the peak is near 25–30° at St 0.06 and 50–60° at St 0.2–0.4 (note's readings), and heating brings in azimuthal modes up to n = ±5 and broadens the directivity ([Tam & Chen 1994](../Notes/tam-1994-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md) p. 1779). Near-field intensity vectors reach 80–85° at high frequency, beyond every model's directivity index ([Gee 2016](../Notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md) p. 5). The measured lobe is narrow, about 30° wide at 3 dB down against 56–57° in the Sutherland and SP-8072 models ([Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md) p. 627), with a peak index of about 5 dB ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md) p. 13).

## Sound power, efficiency and maximum OASPL
Rocket sound power scales as U_e³, i.e. W = ηW_m with W_m = ½TU_e ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 7–8). The η used in models has drifted down:
- SP-8072 used 1% and the 1996 formula 0.5% ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md) p. 513–514). Varnier measured about 0.7% on static rockets of every size, after showing that an apparent rise with size was a ground-reflection artefact. He endorsed Sutherland's law (0.53% ± 0.24%), although it keeps V_e while his mechanical power is fully expanded, and it gives 0.2% for the slow Jet IV against 0.6% measured ([Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md) p. 1854, 1856–1857).
- Sutherland gives 0.53% for Vega ([Casalino 2009](../Notes/casalino-2009-hybrid-empirical-caa-rocket-noise.md) p. 2), and 0.60% against the Requena-Plens variant's 0.17% for Ranow's reference engine; moving from 1% to 0.60% lowers levels by about 2 dB ([Ranow 2021](../Notes/ranow-2021-acoustic-prediction-rocket-flame-deflector.md) p. 18–19, 36).
- Full-scale fits give lower values still. Atlas V is 0.25% if 6 dB is removed for ground doubling ([Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 52–53), or 0.33% with a ground factor δ = 1.9 ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md) p. 375). Near-pad Falcon 9 data give 0.30% ± 0.05%, and the earlier 196 dB (0.34%) would fall to 193–194 dB without ground reflection ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md) p. 14–15).

How the ground is treated thus moves η by 1–3 dB. Kellison et al. also find that level stops rising with Oertel M_CO at rocket conditions, Falcon 9 sitting well below the extrapolated Greska fit, which they take as η saturating (p. 17).

The maximum-OASPL formula comes from [McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md): OAPWL (η = 0.5% of ½TV_e) − 10 log(4πR²) + 8 dB, with R = d/sin 70° (Table 1, p. 513–514).
- On five launches of Scout, Delta, Peacekeeper and Titan IV at 0.3–16 km, its errors run from +6.4 to −4.7 dB, rms 4.1 dB over 19 stations (note's arithmetic), the statistics the SATURN thesis quotes (p. 53). The fixed η is too high for Scout and Delta and too low for Titan IV, which McInerny reads as η rising with individual motor thrust (p. 513–514, 516).
- McInerny suggested that the 8 dB already contains the ground reflection (p. 513). [Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md) make this explicit as Q_max = 5 dB plus a ground factor 20 log δ, with OAPWL = 115.9 + 10 log(ηρ_eU_e³D_e²) in exit-flow variables (p. 372, 375). The RMSE is then 1.8 dB for Atlas V, 1.7 dB for Vulcan once η is halved and below 1.7 dB for an F404 (p. 375–377); SATURN's Atlas V fit has an rms of 1.6 dB (thesis p. 53). For Falcon 9 the formula gave 144 dB at 100 D_e,equiv against 143 dB measured ([Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md) p. 628).
- It overpredicts at long range: by 4–6 dB at 12–16 km in McInerny's own data (Table 1, Fig. 3, p. 514–515), and beyond about 4–5 km for Falcon 9, which Kellison et al. put down to the missing absorption ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md) p. 7–8). McInerny blamed nonlinear transfer of energy out of the low-frequency peak (p. 514–515); since nonlinearity barely changes OASPL (see the propagation section), that attribution is doubtful.

Regulatory environmental-assessment (EA) models have done far worse. SLS was about 20 dB louder than its EA prediction ([Gee 2023](../Notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md) p. 3–4). For Starship, the RNOISE-based EA underpredicted L_Z,max by up to 9 dB but overpredicted SEL_A by up to 18 dB ([Gee 2024](../Notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md) p. 5).

## Spectrum and frequency scaling
Rocket spectra peak at Sr = fD_e/U_e ≈ 0.02–0.025, about ten times lower than lab supersonic jets, for reasons still unclear. Eldred's throat-based Sr* collapses lab, turbojet and rocket spectra best ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 10–11). For Falcon 9 (Sr ≈ 0.019 on the equivalent diameter), the Cole and Potter & Crocker scalings do better than the classic Strouhal number ([Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md) p. 628–629).

The 1990s launch PSDs peak at fD_e/V_e ≈ 0.015–0.02 and match SP-8072's peak frequency and level once η is corrected, but are "deficient in energy" at high frequency relative to it; whether SP-8072 was corrected for absorption is unknown ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md) p. 516). Park (2019) found the opposite, that SP-8072 underestimates high frequencies ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 4). The same 1990s records show the normalisation trap: their one-third-octave peaks sit 1.5–2× higher in Strouhal number than their narrowband peaks (McInerny note's arithmetic, p. 513). SATURN's Atlas V power spectrum peaks near Sr ≈ 0.01 on D_eff = D_e√N, roughly an octave below SP-8072 ([Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 76–77). Falcon 9 gives 0.010 measured and 0.011 from SATURN, against 0.015 for SLS and 0.02 for SP-8072 ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md) p. 15–16).

The two-source picture (directional large-structure noise plus broadband sideline fine-scale noise) rests on lab spectra, correlations and mirror maps that stop at M_j 2.0 and T_r/T_a 3.2 ([Tam et al. 2008](../Notes/tam-2008-sources-of-jet-noise-experimental-evidence.md); [Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 9–12, 27–28). Tam's similarity spectra were fitted to lab jets up to T_r/T_∞ ≈ 4.9 ([Tam 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md) p. 5–7). Rocket slopes are shallower, and several groups find these spectra unsuitable (Lubert p. 10). Mathews & Gee use a modified large-scale spectrum (f^2.5, f^−2.2) only to compute the ground factor (p. 377).

The first-principles alternative is the stochastic instability-wave model of [Tam & Chen 1994](../Notes/tam-1994-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md), with white-noise forcing at the lip and one fitted constant; it reproduces Mach 2 directivities at T_0 = 500 and 1114 K within about 2.5 dB (p. 1778–1779). It is far from rocket use. The constant differs 2.1× between the two temperatures (note's arithmetic); the model is already about 9 dB low at St 0.067, the lowest tested (p. 1779), whereas rocket peaks sit near 0.01–0.025; and the authors warn that full-scale exit non-uniformity, swirl and core turbulence may break the lip-forcing assumption (p. 1776). A rocket version would need heated Mach 3 mean flows and Tam & Hu's supersonic wave family.

Scaling on D_eff = D_e√N carried the Atlas V model (2 nozzles) to Alpha (4 engines) and Starship (33) within about 1 dB inside 20 km ([Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 98–99).

## Clustered engines
SP-8072 replaces a cluster with one equivalent nozzle. Tedrick's (1964) Saturn S-1 eight-engine cluster supports this: it matched a single F-1 of equal thrust ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 11).

The Eldred two-zone model, as implemented by [Kandula 2005](../Notes/kandula-2005-near-field-acoustics-clustered-rocket-engines.md) and [Kandula 2008](../Notes/kandula-2008-near-field-acoustics-clustered-rocket-engines.md), covers intermediate spacing. Isolated jets near the nozzles are added to a merged equivalent jet downstream, giving a double-peaked near-field spectrum. The equivalent-diameter approach is said to carry 10–15 dB uncertainty there (2008, p. 853). The validation is one four-nozzle case with no vehicle, spacing or microphone details and no error metric (2008, p. 856). The 2008 paper corrects the merging-length equation (p. 854–855).

Full-scale data give a mixed picture:
- Atlas V's RD-180 (S/D ≈ 1.14) shows a low-frequency bump when viewed broadside, not a double peak. Its peak frequency depends on azimuth (32 vs 43 Hz) and collapses with a view-dependent diameter. The two-zone model has no azimuthal dependence ([Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 54–55).
- Neither Atlas V (±1.9 dB) nor Falcon 9 shows the 2–3 dB shielding asymmetry found for lab and LES twin jets ([Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 53; [Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md) p. 8; [Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 11–12).
- Clustering may instead act through η. Vulcan's separated plumes need η halved ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md) p. 375), and Delta's six 380 kN strap-ons behaved like small motors, with an implied η well below 0.5% ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md) p. 513–514). The clustered Saturn V made 2 dB more power than SLS despite less thrust ([Gee 2024](../Notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md) p. 7). Starship's 33 tightly packed engines were run in SATURN with 0.4% ([Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 94).

## Deflectors, ducts, pads and impingement
SP-8072 treats impingement ad hoc. DSM-1 has open-scoop and closed-bucket source curves, while DSM-2 merely turns the plume axis, so the two methods disagree for vertical launch ([Haynes & Kenny 2009](../Notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md) p. 10).

Haynes & Kenny ended the core at the deflector. Against lift-off spectra from five Saturn V flights, the terminated core matches level and shape (about 145 against 143–144 dB at 85 m), while the full core is about 7 dB low and peaks at too low a frequency (p. 11–12). For Ares I it raised the predictions by 5–7 dB, with no Ares I data to compare (p. 15, 32–33). [Ranow 2021](../Notes/ranow-2021-acoustic-prediction-rocket-flame-deflector.md) then compared Ares I data with the full set of modifications (p. 41–44). With the terminated core, OASPL is overpredicted by 7–10 dB (146.1 against 139.1 dB at 45 m; 144.4 against 134.6 dB at 78 m); with an intact core by 2–4.4 dB; plain SP-8072 is within 0–2 dB. Ranow blames deluge water and shielding by the launch structure (p. 42). He also adds the Plotkin–Sutherland directivity (−3 dB), cylinder diffraction (+2.7 dB) and ray-traced flame-duct reflections (up to +3.2 dB), so that lengthening a duct can backfire (p. 39–47). The image-source ground reflection of [Kandula 2008](../Notes/kandula-2008-near-field-acoustics-clustered-rocket-engines.md) adds up to 5 dB (p. 857).

Pad measurements show sources that no plume model contains. In the Antares static fire, water quenched the plume and the flame-duct exit dominated at all frequencies ([Panda 2013](../Notes/panda-2013-noise-sources-rocket-test-firings-launch-phased-array.md) p. 17, 23). At lift-off the dominant source moved from the launch mount to the duct, then the deck, then the free plume (p. 19–20). SATURN comparisons start at T+5 s because it omits the deflected plume ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md) p. 5, 9).

Ignition overpressure (IOP), aimed by the trench (Kellison p. 5), is in no far-field model. The one model in the library is [Ikawa & Laspesa 1985](../Notes/ikawa-1985-ignition-duct-overpressure-space-shuttle-srm.md), for the Shuttle SRBs. The wall jet formed on the deflector acts as the piston of a shock tube; its velocity follows from a Prandtl–Meyer expansion of the post-shock flow, so the compression follows the chamber pressure rise rate ṗ_c (eqs. 1–5, p. 482–483). A plane-source integral over the duct opening carries the wave to the vehicle, so the radiated pressure goes as p̈_c and arrives as an N-wave (eq. 8). The wall-jet front moving down the duct is a moving source (speed C + u_wj cos α, eq. 12) that radiates a later, lower-frequency duct overpressure (DOP) from the exits (p. 483–484), consistent with Panda's duct-exit sources. The model matches the STS-1 IOP waveform and timing, but the amplitude comparison is at a different location (Fig. 3, p. 483), only the "salient features" of STS-3 are captured (p. 486), and its multiplying factors are not reported, so the amplitude calibration is not shown. A deflector that sends the plume under a covered tunnel cuts IOP (p. 485), retarding the wall jet should cut DOP (p. 487), and the suppression adopted from STS-2 was water-trough covers plus water injection (p. 481). The far-field models cannot see this phase anyway: McInerny calls 1 s one-third-octave levels during ignition "misleading" ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md) p. 514–515).

Mitigation data are pad-specific. Duct extensions cut the 31.5 Hz octave by 4–8 dB, and Shuttle water injection cut OASPL by 4 dB at 200% and 8 dB at 500% of propellant flow ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 21–23).

On mechanisms, lab plates and LES identify five impingement wave types ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 17–18). Their earliest form in the library is [Nonomura et al. 2011](../Notes/nonomura-2011-supersonic-jet-impinging-inclined-plate-acoustic-waves.md), a seventh-order WCNS implicit LES of an ideally expanded M_j 2 jet on a 45° plate (L/D 5 and 10, temperature ratio 1 and 5.4, Kirchhoff far field), with no experimental validation or grid study (p. 403–409). Every case shows free-jet Mach waves, wall-jet Mach waves and broadband impingement-region waves, the last absent from SP-8072 (p. 411–414, 422). Impinging inside the core (L/D 5) is about 2–2.5 dB louder at 20D than L/D 10, which the authors read as higher efficiency (p. 418); that leans towards a terminated core, but it is one arc in one plane. Heating adds about 7 dB at the Mach-wave peak, against 3.7 dB from ρu³ (note's arithmetic; p. 420), and turns the waves upstream (p. 421). The hot wall-jet Mach waves leave at about 60° from the plate in the far field, consistent with the ~50° from the wall-jet axis at Epsilon ([Tsutsumi 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md) p. 10–11). The spectra peak at lab values (St 0.1–0.6), and the plates sit at most 10 diameters away.

At intermediate heights, three JAXA studies agree that free-jet Mach waves reflected from the ground dominate the vehicle load: the worst cases are h ≈ 15 D_e (plate study) and h/D_e ≈ 16 (H-IIA) ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 19–22), and H/D_e = 14 for Epsilon at 149.1 dB ([Tsutsumi 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md) p. 15–16).

## Propagation and nonlinearity
Every model on this page propagates linearly: spherical spreading, plus in SATURN an empirical propagation-loss filter ([Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 68–71, 86). For OASPL this is adequate. OASPL is set by the low-frequency peak and barely changes with nonlinearity ([McInerny & Ölçmen 2005](../Notes/mcinerny-2005-high-intensity-rocket-noise-nonlinear-propagation.md) p. 588; [Gee 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md) p. 9–10, 12), and linear FW-H from 11.2D gives the same OASPL as nonlinear Euler propagation in the LES of [Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md), though it misses the waveform statistics (Fig. 28, Table III, p. 25–27).

High-frequency levels are another matter. Above about 120 dB, the 1990s launch spectra overlay at high frequency whatever the distance, falling more slowly than absorption predicts ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md) p. 516). Titan IV and Scout waveforms obey the weak-shock rise-time law out to 15 km, so a propagation stage must carry shocks ([McInerny & Ölçmen 2005](../Notes/mcinerny-2005-high-intensity-rocket-noise-nonlinear-propagation.md) p. 589–590). That paper supplies the theory (Pierce's steady-shock rise time with a diffusivity that steps at the N₂ and O₂ relaxation frequencies, eqs. 1–4, p. 582–583; the Morfey–Howell spectral equation, eq. 8, p. 587–588) but computes no propagation. Its inputs are the initial shock thickness, measured with more than 20 kHz bandwidth, and temperature and humidity: the effective diffusivity differs 11–19× between relaxation regimes and falls 5–9× from 30% to 70% RH (Table III, p. 586). Eq. 8 as printed appears to lack a factor ω and doubles the absorption (note's check). Weather changes the decay too: Atlas V followed r⁻¹ on a clear launch and about r^−1.1 in fog at 88% RH, which [Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md) suggest is lower absorption keeping waveforms shock-like (p. 375).

[Baars et al. 2014](../Notes/baars-2014-cumulative-nonlinear-waveform-distortions-high-speed-jets.md) give a criterion for when the stage must be nonlinear. With an emission point r₀ = 2.5 D_j, the shock formation distance is r̄ = r₀ exp(x̄/r₀) and the effective Gol'dberg number Λ = (l_a/x̄) exp(−(π/2)x̄/r₀) (eqs. 4.3–4.10, p. 344–347), with x̄ ∝ D_j/(M_j⁵T_j^2.5 St) (eqs. 4.11–4.13, p. 348). A cold Mach 3 lab jet has Λ ≈ 0.15 and r̄ ≈ 18 m, so a lab chamber shows no cumulative steepening, although single-point indicators (Q/S) are non-zero and mislead (p. 357–360). Transferred to full scale, the model gives Λ about 5× too high from the Mach 3 jet and 7–14× too low from a heated jet, against 764 from F-35A data (Tables 4–5, p. 353–354); it is sensitive to the emission-point size, the Strouhal mapping and the intensity law (p. 349, 353–354). Matching M_j, T_j and St preserves r̄/D_j but not Λ (eq. 4.17, p. 350), so the authors propose matching ΛD_j (p. 351, 363).

## Hybrid and high-fidelity prediction
[Casalino 2009](../Notes/casalino-2009-hybrid-empirical-caa-rocket-noise.md) keeps Eldred's source strengths but replaces spherical spreading and the directivity index with Green's functions of the real Vega pad, from a frequency-domain solution of Pierce's convected wave equation on a RANS mean flow with immersed boundaries. It reproduces the measured peak frequency and the fairing-versus-free-field level difference through low-frequency confinement in the pad cavity, which the empirical variants miss, but still overestimates by 2–3 dB (p. 12–15). The Pierce model is valid only for St ≳ 0.1 (p. 5–6); a mean flow with shock cells made the solution "stiff", so an equivalent fully expanded plume was used (p. 12); and the 60 uncorrelated point sources cause interference ripples (p. 15).

[Tsutsumi 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md) computes generation and propagation together, with a zonal RANS/LES (sixth-order compact differencing, SLAU at shocks), an FW-H surface, about 100 M points and two months on 1280 cores (p. 12). It overpredicts the 1/42-scale data by up to 4 dB and puts the 150° spectral peak at St 0.023 against 0.045 measured, which the authors blame on grid resolution and linear FW-H propagation (p. 12–13). Its value was diagnosis: the reflected Mach waves, and a front cover worth −3 dB (p. 16).

[Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md) is the two-way-coupled LES/CAA that Lubert et al. name as in development (Lubert p. 31). An unstructured finite-volume LES, with the nozzle meshed and a geometric trip, exchanges data every time step with a fourth-order DG full-Euler solver, for a 1/40-scale MARTEL hydrogen–air motor jet (M_e 3.1, T_i 1900 K, p_e/p_∞ 0.6) (p. 3–5). Band-limited OASPL (0.01 ≤ St ≤ 0.22) is within about 1 dB at most angles on a 41.6D arc (p. 16, 28), better than the pad hybrids but for a free jet. The group's earlier run, with the same FW-H or Euler propagation, was 4–6 dB high, and the trip with 3× azimuthal lip resolution fixed it (Fig. 16, p. 6). The nozzle-exit state, not the propagation, dominated that error, which qualifies Tsutsumi's explanation. The authors judge their lip separation spurious (p. 8), and the separation criteria of [Stark 2013](../Notes/stark-2013-flow-separation-rocket-nozzles-overview.md) agree, the jet being far from both thresholds (note's arithmetic; p. 3, 5).

Subscale tests have their own limits. Tsutsumi et al. keep U_e and Re > 10⁵, which keeps SPL and scales frequency with size; a 1/42 model then carries about 1.8 dB of extra absorption and about 3 dB of refraction uncertainty (p. 5–8). Steepening does not scale, since Λ is not preserved ([Baars et al. 2014](../Notes/baars-2014-cumulative-nonlinear-waveform-distortions-high-speed-jets.md) p. 350–351). For ignition the motor transient must scale too: the 6.4% Shuttle model's transient was roughly 1.7–2.5× too slow (note's estimate) with a stronger second pressure-rate peak, so IOP and DOP overlapped ([Ikawa & Laspesa 1985](../Notes/ikawa-1985-ignition-duct-overpressure-space-shuttle-srm.md) p. 486).

Lubert et al. list LES of seven launch systems, using WENO, compact and DG schemes, mostly with one-way coupling to nonlinear Euler CAA. Water injection is simulated in only one study, and wavepacket, SPOD or resolvent ROMs have not yet been applied to rocket plumes ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 29–32). The emerging division of labour is CFD/CAA for the near-field pad environment and empirical models for the far field ([Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 69–70).

## Disagreements and open questions
- **Core termination at the deflector.** It fixes Saturn V ([Haynes & Kenny 2009](../Notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md)) but causes a 7–10 dB overprediction for Ares I ([Ranow 2021](../Notes/ranow-2021-acoustic-prediction-rocket-flame-deflector.md)). The only mechanism-level evidence, a plate LES louder when impinging inside the core, is one arc ([Nonomura et al. 2011](../Notes/nonomura-2011-supersonic-jet-impinging-inclined-plate-acoustic-waves.md) p. 418). Settling it needs a source map of a deflected plume (near-pad intensity, beamforming or LES) with full pad and engine parameters.
- **Is the halved core physical?** Varnier's length improves vehicle levels but puts the RSRM peak source at about 9 D_e, while measurements put it at 17–22 D_e. It was calibrated on small static rockets, with peaks inferred through the model's own source shape at high Strouhal number ([Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md) p. 1855–1856), and vehicle users apply a hybrid with exit variables. Near-field intensity on a vertical launch, and mean-flow core lengths of shock-containing jets, would separate the two.
- **Falcon 9 peak angle and its cause.** The candidates are 64° explained by flight effects ([Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md)), 64° explained by the supersonic-wave M_c ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md)), and 71–72° ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md); SATURN). Vortex-sheet theory favours the supersonic family in hot fast plumes ([Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md)), but convection speed changes along the plume ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md)) and peak angle with frequency, so a single M_c may be the wrong target. Needed: one source-origin and 3-D angle convention applied to all data sets, ray-traced propagation, and the same engine measured static and in flight.
- **Does η depend on motor size or clustering?** McInerny reads the 1990s launches as η rising with individual motor thrust ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md) p. 513–514), while Varnier found it size-independent and traced an apparent size trend to ground reflection ([Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md) p. 1854). Separated plumes (Vulcan, 0.17%; Delta) sit below tightly clustered ones (Atlas V 0.33%, Starship 0.4%), and ground handling shifts each value. A near-pad, ground-corrected sound-power survey across vehicles, done the Kellison way, would test this.
- **Peak Strouhal number and its normalisation.** SP-8072 gives 0.02 and SATURN about 0.01, with D_e versus D_eff and narrowband versus band spectra used inconsistently; band peaks sit 1.5–2× above narrowband ones in the same records (McInerny note). Whether SP-8072 has too much high-frequency energy ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md) p. 516) or too little (Park 2019, [Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 4) is unresolved, as is why rockets peak ten times lower than lab jets (p. 10).
- **Long-range levels.** The 1996 formula is 4–6 dB high at 12–16 km, SATURN overpredicts Starship Flight 6 by 12 dB at 35.5 km, and the EA model underpredicted Flight 5 by 9 dB at the same range ([McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md) p. 514–515; [Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) p. 95; [Gee 2024](../Notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md) p. 5). Ray tracing with launch-day meteorology and nonlinear propagation is needed (thesis p. 105). Predicting Λ for rockets from exit conditions alone needs a validated mapping of emission-point size and peak Strouhal number with M_j, T_j and nozzle state ([Baars et al. 2014](../Notes/baars-2014-cumulative-nonlinear-waveform-distortions-high-speed-jets.md) p. 363).
- **Missing physics.** Ignition overpressure has only the 1985 on-vehicle model, without published amplitude factors ([Ikawa & Laspesa 1985](../Notes/ikawa-1985-ignition-duct-overpressure-space-shuttle-srm.md)). No model covers the deflected-plume phase, water, impingement-region waves ([Nonomura et al. 2011](../Notes/nonomura-2011-supersonic-jet-impinging-inclined-plate-acoustic-waves.md)), the sideline near-field radiation seen by [Gee 2016](../Notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), or the start-up nozzle state. Free or restricted shock separation sets the exit conditions every model takes as input, and side loads show start-up/shut-down hysteresis ([Stark 2013](../Notes/stark-2013-flow-separation-rocket-nozzles-overview.md) p. 3–4); whether separation-shock unsteadiness is a start-up noise source is untested. Lubert et al. call for a physics-based source model built from wavepacket, SPOD and resolvent tools (p. 31–33).
- **No tones at full scale.** Lubert et al. propose that broadband masking and pad geometry suppress them (p. 16–17); this is untested. BBSAN is clear, following Tam & Tanna with the lipline shock spacing, in the overexpanded 1/40 MARTEL jet ([Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md) p. 19), but absent from the hotter 1/42 Epsilon free jet ([Tsutsumi 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md) p. 13). Whether temperature, nozzle state or masking decides it rests on this one pair.

## Methods and data
The models are built and validated with time-resolved microphone records reduced to block statistics: 1 s running OASPL, maximum one-third-octave spectra and sound power integrated over a moving source's trajectory ([Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md); [Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md)). Source location comes from near-field vector intensity, the only array method applied to full rocket plumes ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 13). Beamforming has mapped pad sources, but SEM-type deconvolution breaks distributed plume sources into spurious points ([Panda 2013](../Notes/panda-2013-noise-sources-rocket-test-firings-launch-phased-array.md) p. 16). Engine inputs come from thermochemistry codes ([Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md) p. 378) and assume a nozzle flowing full. Stark's turbulent criterion p_sep/p_a ≈ 1/Ma_sep (above Ma_sep ≈ 2.4) and the Schmucker criterion bound about 90 data series and give a quick check ([Stark 2013](../Notes/stark-2013-flow-separation-rocket-nozzles-overview.md) p. 3, 5–7). By the note's arithmetic, Varnier's Rita II (M_e 3.8, p_e/p_a 0.3) sits at incipient separation, which fits its "unstable" emission ([Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md) p. 1856).

Four cautions recur: polar arcs must be centred on the source ([James 2012](../Notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md)); static firings near the ground include impingement and ground effects; ground reflection must be removed consistently; and metrics disagree, since a model can be right on L_Z,max and wrong on SEL_A.

No model uses modal decomposition or anything time-resolved beyond slow-time levels. SPOD and DMD (which need time-resolved data or LES) and resolvent analysis (which needs a mean flow) are proposed but not yet applied. PIV is impractical in real plumes, so POD of PIV is limited to model scale ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) p. 12, 31–32). Values are being collected in `Library_of_Shock_Lab/Data/rocket_noise.csv`.

## Relevance to our work
- **Source location and convection speed from lab data.** Time-resolved schlieren with SPOD gives what the models assume: source location against frequency (mode envelopes) and convection speed (phase speed from a streamwise FFT of the mode). Measured along x, the phase speed tests which M_c sets the directivity and whether it changes past the core, as the Langenais LES suggests. The M_c families separate only when a_j/a_∞ is large ([Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../Notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md) note), so heated jets are needed.
- **Core length.** Mean schlieren or PIV of the group's under-expanded jets can test the 1.75 against 3.45 core coefficient, and Varnier's L_S law, at lab NPRs. This is the basic-statistics step of the workflow.
- **Impingement.** Inclined-plate impingement with SPOD and BMD would show where sources sit after impingement, which is the core-termination dispute; Nonomura's parallax method is a cross-check from acoustics alone. Why the lab feedback loop ([impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md)) does not close at full scale is a question the group is placed to answer.
- **Coherent source models.** A spatially coherent SPOD wavepacket source could replace Casalino's uncorrelated points. For clusters, twin-jet SPOD split into pre- and post-merging zones would test the two-zone model.
- **Non-stationarity.** Lift-off changes the nozzle height continuously, and ignition and start-up are transients that 1 s block statistics miss. SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD, or wavelets, could track the reflected Mach-wave band (St ≈ 0.02–0.07) as height changes.
- **Lab acoustics are linear.** By Baars et al.'s criterion a lab chamber shows no cumulative steepening, so the group's acoustic data can test sources but not rocket-like propagation.
- **PyFR LES.** If the staging LES moves to hot or impinging cases, the multi-species and shock-capturing choices of the pad LES apply. Langenais et al. found the nozzle-exit boundary layer, not the propagation method, dominated their error, which bears on the nozzle-lip treatment of the staging LES.
- **Synchronised flow and acoustics.** The SATURN thesis lists synchronised schlieren and acoustics as future work (p. 104–105), which is the group's core capability.

## Papers
- [Ikawa & Laspesa 1985](../Notes/ikawa-1985-ignition-duct-overpressure-space-shuttle-srm.md): Shuttle SRB ignition and duct overpressure model (wall jet as shock-tube piston, N-wave ∝ p̈_c); the only IOP model in the library.
- [Papamoschou & Roshko 1988](../Notes/papamoschou-1988-compressible-turbulent-shear-layer.md): origin of the convective Mach number; its M_c is the "supersonic" family closest to the Falcon 9 peak angle.
- [Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md): source of the "supersonic" convection speed; KH and supersonic families merge in hot fast jets, which favours the supersonic speed for rockets.
- [Tam & Chen 1994](../Notes/tam-1994-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md): one-constant stochastic instability-wave model of Mach-wave noise; St-dependent peak angle; fails at low St.
- [McInerny 1996](../Notes/mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra.md): origin of the η 0.5% + 8 dB OASPL_max formula (rms 4 dB); 70° launch directivity; η against motor size; HF deficit against SP-8072.
- [Tam 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md): lab large- and fine-scale similarity spectra; found unsuitable for rockets, but a modified form is used for ground factors.
- [Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md): source of the halved core (1.75 with D_j, M_j); peak ≈11D on small static rockets; η ≈ 0.7% size-independent; L_S law.
- [Kandula 2005](../Notes/kandula-2005-near-field-acoustics-clustered-rocket-engines.md): two-zone clustered-nozzle model on SP-8072 with ground reflection; RAPT tool.
- [McInerny & Ölçmen 2005](../Notes/mcinerny-2005-high-intensity-rocket-noise-nonlinear-propagation.md): launch waveforms keep shocks to 15 km, so propagation must be nonlinear for HF; weak-shock theory and its inputs.
- [Kandula 2008](../Notes/kandula-2008-near-field-acoustics-clustered-rocket-engines.md): journal version with the corrected merging-length equation; the citable source.
- [Tam et al. 2008](../Notes/tam-2008-sources-of-jet-noise-experimental-evidence.md): lab evidence for the two-source spectral split; apparent source position depends on angle.
- [Casalino 2009](../Notes/casalino-2009-hybrid-empirical-caa-rocket-noise.md): Eldred and Varnier sources with CAA Green's functions of the Vega pad; captures pad confinement, still 2–3 dB high.
- [Haynes & Kenny 2009](../Notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md): DSM-2 with halved core, core terminated at the deflector and RSRM directivity; checked against Saturn V.
- [Kenny 2009](../Notes/kenny-2009-space-shuttle-srm-plume-acoustics.md) (not tagged): RSRM static-firing data behind the Haynes–Kenny directivity indices.
- [Nonomura et al. 2011](../Notes/nonomura-2011-supersonic-jet-impinging-inclined-plate-acoustic-waves.md): inclined-plate LES with three wave types, including impingement-region waves SP-8072 omits; parallax source location.
- [Gee 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md) (not tagged): RSRM source at 17–18D; hillside impingement that later disqualified the RSRM curve.
- [James 2012](../Notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md): 80 D_e arcs are not far field; recentring moves the SP-8072 OASPL peak from 51° to 65°.
- [Panda 2013](../Notes/panda-2013-noise-sources-rocket-test-firings-launch-phased-array.md) (not tagged): phased array shows flame-duct and deck sources that SP-8072 omits.
- [Stark 2013](../Notes/stark-2013-flow-separation-rocket-nozzles-overview.md): nozzle flow separation and its criteria; the exit state the models assume, and start-up hysteresis no model covers.
- [Baars et al. 2014](../Notes/baars-2014-cumulative-nonlinear-waveform-distortions-high-speed-jets.md): effective Gol'dberg number as a criterion for nonlinear propagation; lab jets are linear; ΛD_j matching for subscale tests.
- [Tsutsumi 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md): subscale similarity rules, 1/42 tests and RANS/LES + FW-H; ground-reflected Mach waves; LES up to 4 dB high.
- [Gee 2016](../Notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md) (not tagged): GEM-60 near-field intensity source locations, the basis of SATURN's source law; radiation more sideline than all model directivity indices.
- [Langenais et al. 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md): two-way-coupled LES/Euler CAA of a 1/40 hot motor jet, OASPL within ~1 dB; nozzle-exit state dominated the error; BBSAN at subscale.
- [Mathews 2021](../Notes/mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise.md): Falcon 9 test of the directivity, η, OASPL_max and Strouhal models; flight-reduced Oertel M_c.
- [Ranow 2021](../Notes/ranow-2021-acoustic-prediction-rocket-flame-deflector.md): modular SP-8072 with efficiency, core, directivity, diffraction and duct-reflection changes; 7–10 dB high against Ares I.
- [Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md): review and entry point; Potter plotting error, η, source location, M_CO directivity, pad mitigation, LES and ROM outlook.
- [Gee 2023](../Notes/gee-2023-sls-artemis-i-far-field-noise.md) (not tagged): SLS about 20 dB above its environmental-assessment prediction.
- [Gee 2024](../Notes/gee-2024-starship-super-heavy-far-field-noise.md) (not tagged): Starship against the RNOISE-based EA model (L_Z,max low, SEL_A high); clustering and η.
- [Mathews & Gee 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets.md): OAPWL and OASPL_max from exit-flow variables with a ground factor; RMSE < 2 dB; Falcon 9 angle revised to 71°.
- [Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md): SATURN, an open SP-8072 replacement built on Atlas V data; about 1 dB RMSE within 20 km on Alpha and Starship.
- [Kellison 2026](../Notes/kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.md): near-pad Falcon 9 sound power (η 0.30%), 64° directivity, SATURN validation, η saturation.
