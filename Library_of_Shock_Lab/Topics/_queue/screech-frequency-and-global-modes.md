## rockwell-1979-self-sustained-oscillations-impinging-shear-layers (queued 2026-10-24)

Two points matter for this page. First, "frequency yes, amplitude rarely" was already the consensus in 1979: frequency predicted "with reasonable success" by linear stability or semi-empirical methods, amplitude only in limited cases (p. 89), matching item 1 (Raman 1998). Second, the review divides frequency models into those with an explicit feedback phase condition (Curle 1953; Powell 1961, with |T| = 1 and arg T = 2πn; King et al. 1958; Martin et al. 1975; Rockwell 1977b) and those without one, which pick the frequency of maximum integrated amplification (Woolley & Karamcheti 1973, 1974; Sarohia 1975) (p. 89–91). Both gave "reasonably good" frequencies, but the amplification models still needed a prescribed separation–impingement phase (p. 90–91), and no critical comparison had been made. This is the earliest form of the page's finding that the loop phase criterion and the most-amplified or saddle-point conditions largely coincide (item 3). [Huerre 1990](../Notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md) cites this review as the archetype of feedback-driven global oscillations (p. 498). Bilanin & Covert's (1973) vortex-sheet cavity model, with downstream waves only (p. 91), is an ancestor of the vortex-sheet phase models now used for screech and impinging tones. The page should cite the paper as a precursor; it does not change the current understanding.
