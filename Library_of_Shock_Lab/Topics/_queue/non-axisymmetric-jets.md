## lu-2026-rectangular-jet-screech-dimensional-reduction (queued 2026-10-22)

The paper adds the first systematic test in the library of how far a rectangular jet can be reduced for linear modelling. It compares a 2D cross-plane LSA (AR 1–8, quarter domain with D2 symmetry conditions) with planar and axisymmetric models, on a shock-free tanh base flow, at NPR 2.1–5.0 (p. 4–7).
- **KH waves.** The planar model is good. The α_r error is 7.8 % at AR 2, 3.33 % at AR 4 and 1.7 % at AR 6, and only 0.3 % at AR 2 for St_h ≥ 0.2. The growth-rate error is 6.19 % at AR 2 and 1.18 % at AR 6, but over 30 % for a square jet (p. 9–12, 21). This extends [Tam 1988](../Notes/tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md)'s "AR ≥ 4 is planar" rule from shock cells to KH waves (p. 12).
- **Axisymmetric reduction.** An m = 1 round-jet model misses even the square jet by 27 % in α_r and 38 % in α_i (p. 14). The page's "Linear models against experiment and LES" section can say that round-jet equivalents are worse than planar ones.
- **GJM.** Planar bands of existence are several times too wide. The planar saddle (upper edge) is within 3.4 % for AR ≥ 4, but its branch point (lower edge) is badly off even at AR 8. For AR 2 the tanh-based 2D band lies entirely above the planar band (Fig. 21, p. 17).
- **Effect on Gojon 2019.** This weakens the planar band-membership argument of [Gojon 2019](../Notes/gojon-2019-antisymmetric-oscillation-modes-rectangular-screeching-jets.md), which rests on exactly the branch point. It reinforces the page's existing caveat about that paper, with a stronger reason than vortex-sheet thinness.
- **PIV base flow.** On PIV mean flows taken where the screech SPOD mode peaks (x/D 2.28–6.97, Table 3), the AR 2 cross-section is "a square with rounded corners" (p. 19). The GJM band becomes narrow (ΔSt_h ≈ 0.02–0.04) and moves down, consistent with [Ivelja 2024](../Notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) and [Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md).
- **Tone predictions.** Both reduced (planar) and 2D-PIV weakest-link models reproduce the AR 2 tones of [Mazharmanesh 2025](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), but the planar one also predicts many tones that do not exist (p. 20).
- **Verdict.** The page's "Verdict" paragraph can be sharpened: local models get KH wavenumbers right cheaply, but GJM band edges need the real cross-section.
- **Open question.** The note suggests the PIV base flow (from the sharp-throat C-D nozzle of Nogueira 2025) and the tones (convergent nozzle of Mazharmanesh 2025) may come from different nozzles (note's inference). This should be stated with care if used on the page.

## mitchell-2013-near-field-structure-underexpanded-elliptic-jets (queued 2026-10-23)

The paper gives the earliest evidence for minor-axis **flapping** in this nozzle. Centreline v_rms rises much more steeply in the minor-axis plane, with broad peaks at the shock spacing (wavelength 0.6d at NPR 2.2 to 1.3d at 3.4) just downstream of each shock. The centreline v PDF is bimodal in the minor-axis plane and Gaussian in the major-axis plane (Fig. 14, NPR 3.4) (p. 7–10). This is the shock-displacement test later used by [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md). Flapping grows from NPR 2.2 to 3.4 (p. 9), consistent with the POD-pair energies of 15 %, 23.6 % and 35 % on the same database (Edgington-Mitchell 2015; [Ivelja 2024](../Notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 15), so it supports claim 1.

At NPR 4.2 the signature is absent until x/d ≥ 6 (p. 9), and the authors suggest the bifurcation suppresses flapping (p. 12). There are no acoustics, though. The same ellipse stages several times in 2 ≤ NPR ≤ 5 ([Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 18), and [Mazharmanesh 2025](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) find extra quasi-helical and second-flapping branches above NPR ≈ 3.4–3.8 in another AR 2 ellipse. A stage or symmetry change at 4.2 is therefore an equally good reading, and the weak NPR 4.2 centreline v_rms is slightly larger in the major-axis plane (Fig. 13, note's reading). This is a candidate open question: which mode screeches in this nozzle at NPR 4.2?

For the "Path-integrated views" bullet, the planar analogue: centreline transverse-velocity statistics per plane classify flapping, helical and axisymmetric motion without phase data. Thin structures narrower than the depth of field (≈ 0.1–0.17d) vanish from one plane by out-of-plane averaging (p. 10).

Shock structure: the paper finds a resolvable Mach stem (centreline streak, r/d ≤ 0.12, ≈ 6 % deficit, major-axis plane only) from NPR 2.6, against only NPR 4.2 in a round jet at the same resolution (p. 9–10). Ivelja's "low-velocity streak at z = 0" is the same feature in the same data. The paper also reports stronger u_rms in the major-axis plane at all NPRs, read as more mixing there, against Kinzie & McLaughlin's ideally expanded jet (p. 6). The note flags out-of-plane flapping through the thin ellipse tips as a possible contributor.

## edgington-mitchell-2015-staging-screeching-elliptical-jets (queued 2026-10-23)

Adds the full staging map behind Current understanding item 2 for the group's AR 2 ellipse: varicose → flapping at NPR 2.05–2.10, as Morris & Bhat's elliptic-jet stability predicts (p. 1015, 1020), then three flapping stages about the major-axis plane (II, III, IV) and a "helical" mode V from NPR 3.95 (Figs 2, 6–10). The flapping-to-flapping jump II → III at fixed symmetry is the elliptical case [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) assigned to k_s2 (p. 18), so AR 2 ellipses stage by shock peak as well as by symmetry. It agrees with [Mazharmanesh 2025](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) on a varicose → flapping switch near NPR 2.1–2.2 with St ≈ 1 → 0.55–0.6, the thick-lip nozzle switching about 0.1 NPR earlier. Mode V is attributed to the boundary deforming from the ellipse close to the nozzle at high NPR, letting a mode unlocked from the axes appear (p. 1017, 1021–1022), which extends the "helical-looking motion" thread from the companion paper's silent downstream pair to a dominant tone; the evidence is weak (|R| ≲ 0.3, two non-simultaneous views). Mean schlieren show an ellipsoidal normal shock forming at lower NPR than in a round jet, with long slip lines (p. 1010), matching [Mitchell 2013](../Notes/mitchell-2013-near-field-structure-underexpanded-elliptic-jets.md)'s PIV Mach-stem streak. For the "Shock cells and frequency" paragraph: Powell's formula with the measured spacing (and U_c ≈ 0.65U_j, reconstructed) is within 8 % in II and IV but 14 % low in III (Table 4, p. 1021).

## raman-1999-supersonic-jet-screech-half-century-powell-present (queued 2026-10-23)

For single rectangular jets, Raman 1999 (p. 548–549, 552, 555–562) restates the antisymmetric (flapping) norm, symmetric modes near choking (Gutmark et al.: switch at M_j 1.15; Kaji & Nishijima, Suda et al., Nishijima & Kaji; Lin & Powell), Tam's waveguide frequency formula with U_c = 0.55U_j fitting AR 3–16.7 data better than 0.7U_j (Fig. 4b), and Raman's AR 5 bevelled C-D nozzles whose spanwise modes are symmetric (I), antisymmetric (II) or oblique (III A/B), always antisymmetric across the minor (y) direction, explained by higher-order waveguide modes (Fig. 9, p. 560–562). Rectangular-specific mechanisms quoted: Suda et al.'s travelling shock in the 3rd cell of a 2-D jet, proposed as "the actual source of screech" (Fig. 5, p. 553–554); single feedback shocks from the 3rd cell in 2-D jets, which Raman notes are not seen in round or low-AR rectangular jets (p. 557); the equivalent-source shift from the 3rd to the 4th shock between M_j 1.45 and 1.75 (Fig. 6); and a thick lip (t/h 2) restoring a large sinuous oscillation and +23 dB at M_j 1.75 (Fig. 7). Historical note: Powell's 1951 discovery was in a 0.7 × 0.118 in 2-D nozzle (AR ≈ 5.9, p. 543), and Concorde's clamshell nozzle was an early non-uniform exit (p. 559). All of this agrees with the Raman 1998 summary already on the page and with [Alkislar 2003](../Notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md) (3rd-cell source) and [Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md); it adds no new numbers beyond the thick-lip spectrum.
