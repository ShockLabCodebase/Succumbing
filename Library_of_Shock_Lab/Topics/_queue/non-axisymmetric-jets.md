## gutmark-1995-mixing-enhancement-supersonic-free-shear-flows (queued 2026-10-24)

The review is the pre-1995 China Lake summary of why non-circular supersonic jets mix faster, and it frames screech as the agent: underexpanded rectangular jets at M_j 1.4–1.8 flap at St_D ≈ 0.12 with vortices alternating on the two wide sides, which drives their high spreading (p. 395, citing Krothapalli et al. 1986 and Gutmark et al. 1990). That agrees with [Krothapalli 1986](../Notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md) (St ≈ 0.12, 36° minor-axis spreading) and is the qualitative precursor of [Alkislar 2003](../Notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md)'s measured 0.065 → 0.11/0.33 minor-axis spreading. Its Fig. 5 inset (p. 396, read) gives widths at x/D_e = 8 for 3:1 rectangular and elliptic jets peaking near M ≈ 1.5 (≈ 3.1 and ≈ 2.6 D_e) against ≈ 1.1 for a round jet. It reports that elliptic jets spread slightly faster than rectangular jets of the same AR because the rectangle's vertices reduce coherence and weaken self-induction, while their corner vorticity should give more small-scale mixing (Schadow et al. 1989, p. 395). For nozzle-shape control it adds the tapered-slot nozzle (3:1 rectangular throat to round exit, M 2), which raised minor-plane spreading 12–70 % and **never screeched** over M 1–2.5, attributed to the absence of coherent shocks (p. 403); bevelled AR 4.8 nozzles (double bevels +50 % entrainment in 10 D_e, p. 397); asymmetric exits and tabs that deflect or bifurcate the jet (Wlezien & Kibens 1988, p. 396–397); and a 3:1 collar resonator that excites strong flapping and doubles the centreline Mach decay (Schreck 1993, p. 389). It also lists Koshigoe, Tubis & Ho's (1988) three linear-stability conditions for mixing through self-induction and axis switching (localised eigenfunctions, comparable growth rates, different phase speeds; p. 381), which the Floquet LSA on the page (Ivelja 2024, Nogueira 2025, Lu 2026) could now evaluate directly. It does not change the page's current understanding but fills its thin "Passive control by nozzle shape" section with the 1985–1994 background and gives a citation for the claim that screech drives mixing in rectangular jets independent of Krothapalli.

## zaman-1999-spreading-compressible-jets-nozzle-geometries (queued 2026-10-24)

- **Passive control by shape.** At subsonic M_j, 3:1 elliptic and rectangular nozzles entrain less than 10 % more than a round nozzle near the exit, and about 7–17 % more at x/D = 14 (Figs 7a, 13, p. 204–205). This contradicts the 55 % of Ho & Gutmark 1987, which the author puts down to "unclean" initial boundary layers at M_j > 0.3 (p. 217–218).
- **Perimeter stretching is inefficient.** Rectangular orifices of AR 1–8 are indistinguishable from round; a gain appears only above AR ≈ 10, i.e. a perimeter about twice the round one (Fig. 14, p. 216–217; the printed "D_H/D" ratios are in fact D/D_H, note's check).
- **Lobed nozzle never screeches.** The six-lobed nozzle never screeches over M_j 0.3–2.0 (p. 211), a passive screech-suppression-by-shape result; it then spreads least of all at M_j 1.63 (2.65 at x/D = 14 against 2.99 round).
- **Staging at AR 3.** Both AR 3 convergent nozzles show one frequency jump, B′ → D′ near M_j 1.65 (NPR 4.61), with overlap, and both stages flap "about the major axis plane" (p. 213).
  - The jump is upward in St (≈0.16 → ≈0.20) with unchanged symmetry (Fig. 11, read). By the note's reading this is a k_s1 → k_s2 stage like the AR 2 ellipse's II → III. It matches Edgington-Mitchell 2022's count of one stage change at AR 3 (p. 18–19), and could be cited under "AR ≥ 4" in Staging as the AR 3 example.
  - The lowest-M_j AR 3 data (1.11–1.21) lie on the round A1/A2 curve and are labelled B′ without mode evidence, so a varicose stage near choking is not excluded.
- **Tabs.** Two delta tabs on the short edges of the rectangle reduce screech "drastically" and shift its frequency (≈13.9 kHz, read). They give the best spreading per thrust loss, ṁ/ṁ_e 3.12 → 5.63 at x/D = 14 for C_f 0.948, while the same tabs on the long edges interfere and resist axis switching (Table 1, p. 222, 225).
- **Elliptic against rectangular.** The ellipse beats the rectangle at the B′ peaks but not at M_j 1.63 or subsonically (Fig. 13, read). That is mixed evidence against [Gutmark 1995]'s "ellipse spreads slightly faster" (p. 395).
