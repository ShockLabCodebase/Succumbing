## mcinerny-2005-high-intensity-rocket-noise-nonlinear-propagation (queued 2026-10-21)

McInerny & Ölçmen reanalyse c. 1990 far-field recordings of a Titan IV launch (0.95–15.2 km) and a Scout launch (0.42–1.41 km) with weak-shock theory. That makes this the earliest launch-vehicle evidence for nonlinear propagation in the library, three years before the F-22A demonstration in gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.

They isolate each rising waveform segment. Its maximum rise rate follows the trend of Gaussian noise for small rises but the steady-shock law dp/dt ∝ Δp² above a transition rise, which shaped Gaussian noise with the same PSD and reflection never does (Figs. 7–9, p. 584–586). The fitted diffusivities, 1.24 × 10⁻³ and 1.49 × 10⁻² m² s⁻¹, fall in the range expected for relaxing air (Table III, p. 586).

Near-site PSDs keep sawtooth-like f² / f⁻² slopes. After spherical-spreading correction they barely change across the three nearest Titan sites within 0–20 kHz, which the authors attribute to insufficient bandwidth: the shocks rise in about 50 µs at 950 m, which is instrument-limited (p. 582, 586–587). The Morfey–Howell Q/S indicator shows energy leaving the band near the 16 Hz (Titan) peak and flowing into frequencies above 1–2 kHz, until absorption and the noise floor take over at the farthest sites (p. 588).

How this fits the other notes:
- It agrees with gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical and reichman-2018-qm2-srm-far-field-acoustics that shock content persists and that derivative-based shock metrics are bandwidth-limited. The dp/dt = 25 000 Δp ceiling makes this explicit.
- It agrees with mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise that shocks survive to tens of kilometres; here "vestiges" remain at 15 km (p. 586).
- The fall in shock importance from 0.4 to 15 km is the old-age counterpart of the near-source steepening that gee-2012 found out to 80D. It is not a contradiction.
- Table III's five- to ninefold fall in δ from 30% to 70% RH at 21 °C supports the humidity explanation that mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets gives for the foggy Atlas V launch's weak-shock decay. This link is my reading.

On the open question "shocks radiated or formed?", the paper does not decide: the nearest site is about 1 km away, and the sites see different emission times. It does name the deciding measurement: near-source shock thickness with bandwidth "well in excess of 20 kHz" and reflection-free mounting (p. 589–590). For the Methods section: microphones standing on the ground at 37 cm gave 1.5 ms reflections, with interference at 100–600 Hz, that double-count shocks and corrupt mid-frequency Q/S (p. 581, 588).

## langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation (queued 2026-10-24)

For "Nonlinear propagation and crackle":
- **Subscale waveform data.** The MARTEL test (≈1/40 H₂–air motor, 41.6D = 2.5 m arc, 50 kHz mics) gives S(ṗ) = 1.79, K(ṗ) = 9.79 and WSF = 0.68 at 40°, and 0.45/3.64/0.89 at 90° (Table III, p. 27). The derivative metrics peak near θ′ ≈ 48° (Fig. 29).
- **Simulation.** Nonlinear Euler propagation in the LES follows the trends (S(ṗ) 1.21, WSF 0.73 at 40°). Linear FW-H from 11.2D gives Gaussian waveforms (S(ṗ) ≈ 0, WSF 1.00) at the same OASPL. This is the [Gee 2008](../Notes/gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft.md) "OASPL barely affected, waveforms and spectra changed" result, reproduced by simulation.
- **Shocks in the test.** The measured S(ṗ) > 1.5 indicates shocked waveforms (Reichman et al. 2016 criterion), which the LES cannot resolve at St_c = 0.22. The Goldberg number exceeds 100 in the peak lobes at 40D (Fig. 27).
- **Scale.** The subscale values are far below the full-scale Sk{∂p/∂t} > 30 ([Gee 2012](../Notes/gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical.md), p. 7–8). They are not directly comparable without matching range in shock-formation distances and sampling bandwidth.
- It supports claim 6 without changing it. It is the only paired measurement and simulation of these metrics in the library.

## varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model (queued 2026-10-24)

The paper adds a subscale static-firing data set and one large-motor firing:
- four geometrically similar ONERA composite-propellant rockets (D 3.56–26.72 cm, M_e 3.3, U_e ≈ 2500 m/s, 1.2 s burns, horizontal, 1.05 m above concrete), with near-field arcs and a 19-microphone line array at 5–40D;
- the 84 cm Rita II missile booster;
- the MARTEL air–H₂ jet with intrusive Mach-number surveys (Tables 1–4, p. 1854–1855).

Its main lessons are about ground and instrumentation. With the nozzle fixed at 4D–30D above the ground, arc-integrated sound power gave a spurious η rising with size, from 0.4 to 0.6%. A 20D arc missed sources extending past 30D, where 160 dB was measured near the axis. Microphone protection grids inflated levels above 10 kHz, which gave rocket 5 an η* of about 0.9–1% (p. 1854–1855). This is an early case of the ground-handling problem that moves η by 1–3 dB in [kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns] and [mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets]. Spectral peaks follow Strouhal scaling with size, from 8 kHz to 1 kHz (f D/U_e ≈ 0.1 in octave bands, p. 1855). Focused beamforming puts peak-octave sources at 5–15D (Fig. 8). That is closer to the nozzle than the 17–22 D_e of [gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical], [gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings] and [lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review], consistent with the higher Strouhal number of these small motors rather than a contradiction. Rita II, very overexpanded (p_e/p_a = 0.3), showed "unstable" emission with a possible Mach disk (p. 1856), a rare report of non-stationary static-firing noise. Its directivity on nozzle-centred 11–20D arcs peaks at 40–50° (Fig. 9), so it carries the near-field bias that [james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model] describes. The MARTEL Jet IV is the rig later simulated by [langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation] at a hotter condition. Varnier's probe supersonic length (26D at T_i 1040 K) against the LES's ≈21D (T_i 1900 K) is a useful cross-check. It does not change the page's open questions, but it supports the caution list: centre arcs on the source, and remove ground reflection consistently.

## baars-2014-cumulative-nonlinear-waveform-distortions-high-speed-jets (queued 2026-10-24)

The paper adds a physical criterion to the "Nonlinear propagation and crackle" section. Computed from the F-35A run-up data of Gee et al. 2012, Λ ≈ 764 and r̄ ≈ 5.8 m, deep in the cumulative regime (Table 4, p. 353). That agrees with gee-2008-nonlinear-propagation-noise-high-power-jet-aircraft (p. 9–12), which uses the same generalized-Burgers code family (Gee 2005). Rockets have lower f₀ and larger D_j, so Λ should be larger still (my inference from eqs. 4.11–4.19). That fits shocks to km ranges (mcinerny-2005-high-intensity-rocket-noise-nonlinear-propagation p. 586; mathews-2021-falcon-9-far-field-noise p. 624; reichman-2018-qm2-srm-far-field-acoustics p. 8) and Atlas V losing far less than linear absorption predicts (mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn p. 84–85).
- It weakens the interpretation of the single-station Q/S plots in mcinerny-2005 (p. 587–589): the paper shows Q/S is non-zero at the input, before any propagation (p. 357–360). The integrated −Q_neg does grow monotonically with distance in its GBE demonstration (0.072 → 0.235, Table 7, p. 361), so a quantity like this along a line of stations is the defensible measure.
- On "Shocks: radiated or formed?" (lubert-2022, p. 10), the paper's answer is scale-dependent: formed in propagation at full scale (Λ ≫ 1), radiated or local in lab chambers. It explicitly leaves open how far pre-steepened source waveforms shorten r̄ (p. 363).
- Lab S(ṗ) of 0.2–1.7 on a 100 D_j arc (Table 8, p. 363) sits far below the ≥ 5 shock threshold of reichman-2018 (p. 8) and the > 30 of gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical (p. 8). That is a useful contrast for the page.
