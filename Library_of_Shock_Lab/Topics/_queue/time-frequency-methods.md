## jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise (queued 2026-10-22)

The review is where the jet-noise intermittency question in this library starts: far-field noise recurs at St ≈ 0.2 in bursts seen with time-local analysis and wavelets (p. 176), wavepackets form as intermittent "puffs" (Crow & Champagne 1971), and Fig. 9 (p. 187) lines up STFT envelopes of an LSE-educed LES wavepacket with wavelet bursts of the 30° far field (Kerhervé et al. 2012b). Its open question (p. 188) — deterministic nonlinear mechanism or consequence of stochastic broadband inlet forcing, noting that even a linear system makes bursts when two waves of similar frequency reinforce or cancel — maps directly onto current-understanding point 10 (two detuned tones beat; envelope period 1/Δf). It also states that frequency-domain linear models give only time-averaged wavepackets and that a time-domain operator is needed for real-time estimation and control (p. 188). The later answers in the library are the jitter model of [Cavalieri 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md) (statistical) and HPOD bursts in an M 0.9 jet LES ([Raiola 2025](../Notes/raiola-2025-hilbert-pod.md), p. 19–21; time-resolved); the beat test would separate the review's linear-beating explanation from a nonlinear one. Methods reviewed: conditional sampling on loud events (Hileman 2005), LSE (space–time and spectral), STFT, wavelets, acoustically optimised POD and ROMs (Kerhervé 2012a 20-DOF reproduces intermittent evolution). Could go in the "Tracking intermittency" table as the broadband-jet counterpart of the tonal cases.

## weightman-2018-signatures-shear-layer-unsteadiness-pod (queued 2026-10-23)

The page's "Without time resolution" paragraph cites the thesis (p. 47–49) for the STM and says it "therefore measures amplitude intermittency". The article's Fig. 18 (p. 14) shows STM energy rising with both the size (σ(δ_y) 0.04 → 0.08: ≈ 5 → 17%) and the fraction of deviating snapshots (0 → 100%: 0 → 7.4%). The authors read it as a possible indicator of intermittency (p. 14). The note's check finds that one parameter, f/(1 − f) = k Var(δ_y) with k ≈ 32, fits all 11 points within 0.35 percentage points, if the deviating snapshots in Fig. 18b draw from the same N(0.4, 0.05²), which the paper does not state. So STM energy measures the variance of the width changes and cannot tell rare large excursions from frequent small ones. The page's sentence should be softened: amplitude unsteadiness, not intermittency as such; the PDF of the STM coefficient or of r₁₂ is needed for intermittency. The STM is the snapshot (PIV) counterpart of the St = 0 SPOD coefficient that [mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech] uses as a state label (|ρ| ≥ 0.8, p. 12) and of the (f, −f, 0) BMD triad (p. 16–18). For the SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD pipeline, it says what an HPOD amplitude should be correlated with (shear-layer thickness, i.e. slow mean-flow distortion), and that a tone-only reconstruction drops it. The causal direction is open: the paper reads amplitude as setting thickness (p. 9), while [nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa] reads the same unpaired mode as streaks modulating KH (p. 11–13). This is relevant to the page's "Which way does energy flow?" question. The Stuart-vortex generator (eqs. 6–8, p. 5; fix the v typo) is a ready template for the synthetic switch benchmark the page calls for, though the paper itself uses only static, independent snapshots.

## research-question: hpod-instantaneous-frequency-uncertainty (queued 2026-10-23)

The open question "How fast can a switch be resolved?" (Disagreements and open questions) now has a synthetic answer: the lab's own benchmark in `Codebase/HPOD_Switch_Benchmark/` (test T1 of the research question; not a paper, label it as such). It used two advecting wavepackets 10% and 0.5% apart in white noise. Tracking each tone in its own band B, switch widths were mostly (0.4–1)/B, but the tones separate only for B ≲ 2Δf, so this route stops near T_b = 1/Δf: the "trade-off moves into the narrow-banding step" point (current understanding point 8) in numbers. One band holding both tones followed exclusive switches of 0.375 T_b (widths ≈ 0.05 T_b) at 0.5% spacing, but only about 1.5 T_b at 10%, where T_b is 10 carrier periods. Coexistence appeared only as the Mandel ripple, whose first harmonic is r·Δf (the research question's derivation; the benchmark measured 0.52–0.59 for r = 0.6), so the A1/A2 plateau-versus-ripple test under "Two tones in one band" works in simulation. Replace the proposal bullets under the open question with this result, keeping the real-data test (T3) and space for time (T2) open; HPOD of the raw field never separated the tones, but the aperture was envelope-limited. Pipeline checklist step 2 and the Methods table's inverse-SPOD row need changing. The checklist says to reconstruct block-wise with window-weighted stitching ([Nekkanti 2021](../../Notes/nekkanti-2021-spod-reconstruction-denoising-frequency-time.md), p. 26–27). In the benchmark that leaves an SNR-independent IF floor at the block length (1.4×10⁻⁵ f₀ at 0.5% spacing). Convolving the data with the SPOD modes (eq. 3.4, p. 18–19) cut the IF spread by up to 9×. That is the route Nekkanti recommends for time-continuous coefficients in frequency–time analysis (p. 26), so the checklist should say: for HPOD/IF, reconstruct by convolution. Precision: field HPOD reached 0.15–0.7 times the single-probe Cramér–Rao bound ([Rife 1974](../../Notes/rife-1974-single-tone-parameter-estimation-discrete-time.md)) at 0–10 dB, which supports the POD-adds-SNR point. What HPOD adds when the band comes from `tcoeffs` (research question test T8, same benchmark): POD of the one-sided convolution reconstruction gave the same results as HPOD_t, so the Hilbert step is redundant there (consistent with [Kriegseis 2021](../../Notes/kriegseis-2021-hilbert-transform-revisited-hpod.md), p. 1). Projecting a band holding two tones 10% apart onto one fixed SPOD mode cut the coexistence-ripple contrast 3–6× (worse as the field of view grew), while HPOD kept it; at 0.5% spacing they matched. The direct coefficient resolved switches only down to about its n_fft window. The page's checklist could add the practical rule: read the `tcoeffs` coefficient directly for a long steady tone; use HPOD (or POD of the one-sided reconstruction) when a band holds structures of different shape or the frequency crosses bins. This is consistent with [Horel 1984](../../Notes/horel-1984-complex-principal-component-analysis.md) (complex PCA = frequency-domain PCA averaged over frequency, p. 1661) and with Raiola's reduction to SPOD (p. 11–13). See [the research question](../../Research_Questions/hpod-instantaneous-frequency-uncertainty.md), sections 3–5.
