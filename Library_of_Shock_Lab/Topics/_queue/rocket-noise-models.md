## mcinerny-2005-high-intensity-rocket-noise-nonlinear-propagation (queued 2026-10-21)

The modelling contribution is an argument from launch data that a far-field propagation stage must carry shocks to predict high-frequency levels against distance (p. 589–590), together with the theory to do it:
- Pierce's steady-shock rise time, t_rise = 4ρ₀δ/(βΔp), with an effective diffusivity that steps down as 1/t_rise passes the N₂ and O₂ relaxation frequencies (eqs. 1–4, p. 582–583).
- The Morfey–Howell spectral transport equation derived from the generalized Burgers equation, normalised into an r-independent nonlinearity indicator (eqs. 8–10, p. 587–588).

No propagation is actually computed. Gee 2008 later ran the full GBE for the F-22A, and SATURN uses an empirical propagation-loss filter (mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn). The inputs the paper says a model needs are the initial shock thickness, measured with more than 20 kHz bandwidth, and the temperature and humidity. In Table III, δ differs 11–19× between the two relaxation regimes and falls 5–9× from 30% to 70% RH (p. 586).

The source side is SP-8072-style: L_W of 184 dB (Scout) and 199 dB (Titan IV), computed from W_m with assumed Guest (1964) efficiencies of 0.4% and 0.53% (Table I, p. 579). These are above the 0.3–0.4% now measured (topic page).

Caveats for anyone reusing the equations (my checks):
- Eq. 8 as printed appears to lack a factor ω on the nonlinear term, and its absorption term is twice the α of eq. 3.
- The line-1 diffusivity fit does not match the relaxation regime it is assigned to.

The Titan IV and Scout L_eq values fall about 24–25 dB per decade (≈ r⁻¹·²; my arithmetic). That is steeper than the r⁻¹ to r⁻¹·¹ of mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets, but these stations saw different emission times at ground level.

This complements the topic page's remark that the McInerny OASPL_max formula has no absorption (kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns). Here the same first author argues that absorption at the shock fronts controls the high-frequency levels, while OASPL is set by the sub-100 Hz peak and barely affected (p. 588).

- **"Supersonic" M_c.** The page cites kellison-2026's preference for the "supersonic" Tam & Hu M_c (66° against a measured 64°). That M_c is eq. 5.1 of this paper (p. 472), and its radiation angle cos⁻¹[(a_j + a_o)/u_j] follows from eq. 3.6 (note's derivation).

  - Oertel's W′ speed is 9% above the computed and measured KH speed at cold M_j 2.1 (0.85 against 0.78–0.79; Fig. 18, note's calculation).

  - That gives a physical reason to prefer the eq. 5.1 speed over the Oertel mean M_O for rockets. It bears on the "which convective Mach number" disagreement now on jet-noise-mechanisms.
  - Caveats: vortex sheet only, γ_j = γ_o, and Fig. 9 stops at T_j/T_∞ = 3.
- **Link to SP-8072-type models.** This is a caveat for models built on a single KH-like wavepacket (lubert-2022), but the paper says nothing about rocket conditions, so the link is an inference.

## tam-1994-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets (queued 2026-10-24)

- **Role.** This is the first-principles, one-constant model of the Mach-wave component that dominates rocket noise. The [Mathews 2025](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md) thesis cites it, with Tam 1995 and Tam et al. 1996, for the two-source picture (that note, p. 4). The F and G similarity spectra used, and found wanting, for rockets ([Tam 1996](../Notes/tam-1996-two-components-turbulent-mixing-noise-supersonic-jets.md)) are their empirical descendants.
- **Points for rocket modelling:**
  1. At M_j 2, heating brings in azimuthal modes up to n = ±5 and broadens the directivity (Fig. 8, p. 1779), so hot plumes probably radiate through many modes.
  2. The peak direction depends strongly on St (θ ≈ 25–30° at St ≈ 0.06 against ≈ 50–60° at St 0.2–0.4 in the hot jet, my readings), so a single-M_c peak-angle formula is a simplification.
  3. The calibration constant changed by a factor of 2.1 between two lab temperatures. The authors also warn that full-scale exit non-uniformity, swirl and core turbulence may break the self-similar lip assumption (p. 1776).
  4. The model already fails at the lowest St tested (0.067 at 500 K), while rocket peaks sit at Sr ≈ 0.01–0.025 ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 10).
- **What a rocket version needs.** Heated M ≈ 3 mean flows and the supersonic instability-wave family of [Tam & Hu 1989](../Notes/tam-1989-three-families-instability-waves-high-speed-jets.md).
- **Effect on the page.** The paper has no rocket data and does not change the current understanding. It supplies the theory reference for the Mach-wave directivity thread.

## tam-2008-sources-of-jet-noise-experimental-evidence (queued 2026-10-24)

The paper is the experimental basis cited, with Tam et al. 1996, for the two-source (directional large-structure plus broadband sideline) spectra used in rocket-noise modelling ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), section III B, p. 9–12; Kenny & Giacomoni's two-source fit, p. 27–28; [Mathews 2025 thesis](../Notes/mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn.md), p. 4). Its data stop at M_j 2.0 and T_r/T_a 3.2, so they do not address the poor rocket fit reported by Lubert (p. 10). Two results bear on source location in SP-8072-type models. (a) The apparent source depends on observation angle as well as frequency: for an M 1.9 jet the sideline (fine-scale) source peaks at x/D ≈ 17, but the 150° (inlet) large-structure source at x/D ≈ 11 (cold) to ≈ 14 (T_r/T_a 2.2) (Figs. 32–33, p. 286–287). (b) These lab values sit below the 17–20 D_e found for rockets ([Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md), p. 12–14; [Gee 2016](../Notes/gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings.md), p. 1, 5). The power-law exponents (n ≈ 9–10 aft) are lab-jet OASPL scalings and should not be confused with the U_e³ sound-power scaling of the Mach-wave regime (Lubert p. 7–9). The paper does not change the page's consensus; it should be listed as the experimental support for the similarity-spectrum source split, with the angle-dependent source location as a possible input to source-location laws.

## langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation (queued 2026-10-24)

[Langenais 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md) belongs in "Hybrid and high-fidelity prediction". It is the two-way-coupled LES/CAA that Lubert 2022 names as in development (Lubert note, p. 31). An unstructured FV LES with the nozzle meshed and a geometric trip is coupled at every step to a fourth-order nodal-DG full-Euler solver, for a ≈1/40 MARTEL H₂–air motor jet (M_e 3.1, T_i 1900 K, p_e/p_∞ 0.6).
- **Accuracy.** Band-limited OASPL (0.01 ≤ St ≤ 0.22) is recovered within about 1 dB at most angles on a 41.6D arc (p. 16, 28). That is better than the up-to-4 dB of [Tsutsumi 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md) and the 2–3 dB of [Casalino 2009](../Notes/casalino-2009-hybrid-empirical-caa-rocket-noise.md), but for a free jet only, cut off at St 0.22 (≈6.4 kHz at this scale).
- **Initial conditions, not propagation, dominated the error.** The group's own earlier run, with the same FW-H or Euler propagation, was 4–6 dB high. The trip and 3× azimuthal lip resolution fixed it (Fig. 16, p. 6). This nuances Tsutsumi's blaming of linear FW-H for the error.
- **Directivity.** The LES peak sits at θ′ ≈ 48° (40° from the nozzle), against 63° for M_co. The authors put this down to an arc too close to distributed sources (p. 19), which is the [James 2012](../Notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md) bias.
- **Convective Mach number.** The most likely lipline u_c/u_j is 0.43. That lies between the rocket κ ≈ 0.31 and lab 0.6–0.85 (Lubert note, p. 14–15). Along x, u_c runs from about M_c3 (66–70°) before the core end to M_c1 (40°) beyond it (p. 18). This is relevant to the "which M_c" debate.
- **Linear FW-H gets OASPL but not waveforms.** Linear FW-H from 11.2D gives the same OASPL as nonlinear Euler propagation (Fig. 28), which supports linear OASPL models. It misses the waveform statistics (Table III).
- **BBSAN at subscale.** BBSAN is clearly present here and follows Tam & Tanna with the lipline spacing. That contrasts with the 1/42 Epsilon free jet (no BBSAN at 90–120°, T₀/T_a 12) and with full scale. It is a datum for the "no tones at full scale" open question and for the temperature-saturation idea, though it is only one pair of tests.
- No change to the current-understanding list. It adds a validated free-jet LES/CAA reference to claim 6 and to the high-fidelity section.

## nonomura-2011-supersonic-jet-impinging-inclined-plate-acoustic-waves (queued 2026-10-24)

Nonomura, Goto & Fujii (2011) is the first LES in the library of the canonical pad surrogate, an ideally expanded M_j 2.0 jet on a 45° plate. It uses a seventh-order WCNS implicit LES at Re 10⁵ with L/D = 5 and 10 and TR_c = 1 and 5.4, and a Kirchhoff integral for the far field (p. 403–409). It finds three wave types in every case: free-jet Mach waves (i), wall-jet Mach waves (iii), and broadband impingement-region waves (ii). The authors stress that SP-8072 has no source of type (ii) (p. 411–414, 422). This is the earliest form of the five-type taxonomy that [Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) summarises (p. 17–18), and it strengthens the "missing physics" open question on the page.

The source of (ii) is fixed at the impingement point because its peak angle is the same on arcs of 20D, 50D and 100D about that point, while the (iii) angle grows with r (p. 412–413). This is a parallax method of source location, the complement of [James 2012](../Notes/james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model.md)'s re-centring, and worth adding to "Core length and source location". The note's own estimate puts the (iii) source about 4–5D down the plate.

Levels: impinging inside the core (L/D = 5) is about 2–2.5 dB louder at 20D than at L/D = 10, which the authors read as higher efficiency (p. 418). This is qualitatively on the side of [Haynes & Kenny 2009](../Notes/haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off.md)'s terminated core, but it comes from one arc in one plane. Heating adds about 7 dB at the Mach-wave peak, against +3.7 dB from ρ_Ju_J³, which contradicts the constant-η assumption the authors themselves invoke (p. 420; the note's arithmetic).

Directions: heating turns both waves upstream (p. 421). Hot wall-jet Mach waves leave at 40–50° from the plate at 20D and about 60° in the far field, consistent with the ~50° from the wall-jet axis in [Tsutsumi 2015](../Notes/tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics.md) (p. 10–11). The cold angles converted to the free-jet axis (≈75° for (iii), ≈120° for (ii)) match the Tsutsumi 2014 LES angles quoted by Lubert (p. 19). Spectral peaks are at St 0.1–0.6 on u_J, lab-jet values far above rocket Sr ≈ 0.01–0.025, so the paper says nothing about that scaling. It does not reach the ≈15 D_e height where ground-reflected Mach waves dominate. It does not change the current understanding; it adds a mechanism-level source to the "Deflectors, ducts, pads and impingement" section.

## varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model (queued 2026-10-24)

This is the source paper of the "Varnier core length" that the page currently knows only through Casalino 2009, Haynes & Kenny 2009, Ranow 2021 and Tsutsumi 2015. SP-8072's L_C = 3.45 D_e(1 + 0.38M_e)² put the sound power peak at ≈26D for ONERA's M_e 3.3 static rockets and needed η ≈ 3–4% to match near-field SPL. Varnier replaces it with L_C* = 1.75 D_j(1 + 0.38M_j)², written in fully expanded D_j, M_j and V_j (eqs. 12–13, p. 1855), and the peak moves to ≈11D. This confirms the page's remark that [haynes-2009-sp-8072-dsm-ii-ares-i-lift-off] halved the constant but kept exit variables, which is not Varnier's model; for the ONERA rockets that hybrid gives ≈13D against ≈11D (note-writer's arithmetic). The calibration evidence is thin, which bears on the open question "Is the halved core physical?":
- four similar small rockets, plus Rita II, rocket F (Mayes 1959) and MARTEL Jet IV;
- apparent peaks of 6–20D, inferred by fitting near-field SPL inside 20D with the model's own source shape (Fig. 11, p. 1856);
- a ±20% uncertainty on 1.75 (eq. 14);
- octave peaks at f D/U_e ≈ 0.1, several times the full-scale Sr ≈ 0.02.

That supports [lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review]'s suspicion of bias, and fits the upstream shift of sources with frequency seen in [gee-2010-near-field-vector-intensity-solid-rocket-motor] and [gee-2016-near-field-intensity-static-rocket-firings]. The paper also proposes the supersonic length as the true reference: L_S = D_j(5M_j^1.8 + 0.8) (eq. 19), which matches the 26D probe value for Jet IV, and L_S/L_P ≈ M_j^α with α ≈ 0.8–0.9 (eq. 18, p. 1857). That law has been fitted to two jets only and is untested since. On efficiency, it measures η* ≈ 0.7% for every size (W_M 0.85–72.6 MW) and endorses Sutherland's law (0.53% ± 0.24%). It defines W_M = 0.5 m_t V_j² in fully expanded variables (eq. 10), while Sutherland's η keeps V_e, so "fully expanded everywhere except η" (p. 1857). Sutherland gives 0.45–0.6% for the off-design rockets but 0.2% for the slow Jet IV, against 0.6% measured (p. 1856). Its App. B re-centring of the directivity on the peak source anticipates [james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model]. It moves the peak only ≈5°, however, because it assumes the SP-8072 curves already hold at 10 L_P. The stated model accuracy is ±2 dB OASPL and ±4 dB per octave in the near field, worst near the nozzle where shock noise is unmodelled (p. 1854). Note also that [ranow-2021-acoustic-prediction-rocket-flame-deflector] cites this paper for dismissing screech. The paper only says Mach-wave and supersonic shear noise dominate very hot, highly supersonic jets, and leaves screech unmodelled (p. 1852).

## baars-2014-cumulative-nonlinear-waveform-distortions-high-speed-jets (queued 2026-10-24)

The paper supplies a scaling law for whether a propagation stage must be nonlinear. Emission point r₀ = 2.5 D_j, Gaussian x̄ = ρ∞a∞³/(β2πf₀p_rms) and r̄ = r₀ exp(x̄/r₀) give Λ = (l_a/x̄) exp(−(π/2) x̄/r₀) (eqs. 4.3–4.10, p. 344–347). With I ∝ U_j⁸ and f ∝ St U_j/D_j, x̄ ∝ D_j/(M_j⁵ T_j^2.5 St) (eqs. 4.11–4.13, p. 348).
- Lab-to-full-scale transfer is through η = (x̄_L/r_{0L})/(x̄_F/r_{0F}) (eq. 4.15, p. 349; Fig. 15). The model predicts full-scale Λ within a factor of about 5 (Mach 3 lab case, high) and 7–14 (heated M 1.55 case, low) of the value computed from F-35A data (Tables 4–5, p. 353–354).
- Its sensitivities are the emission-point size s, the St mapping, and the intensity law for hot fast jets (p. 349, 353–354).
- It bears on subscale similarity (tsutsumi-2015-epsilon-liftoff-acoustics, p. 5–8): matching M_j, T_j and St preserves r̄/D_j (η = 1, eq. 4.17, p. 350) but not Λ. Thermoviscous α ∝ f² ∝ D_j⁻² gives Λ ∝ D_j (my derivation from eq. 4.18), so a 1/40 model has about 1/40 of the full-scale Λ.
- The authors therefore propose matching ΛD_j to preserve steepening (p. 351, 363). This adds to the existing 1.8 dB extra-absorption caveat for 1/42 models.
- It gives SATURN-type models (mathews-2025 thesis, empirical propagation-loss filter, p. 68–71) a physics-based check on when nonlinear losses apply.
- Open question to add: a validated mapping of s and peak St with M_j, T_j and nozzle state, so that Λ can be predicted for rockets from exit conditions alone.

## ikawa-1985-ignition-duct-overpressure-space-shuttle-srm (queued 2026-10-25)

- **First IOP model in the library.** This is the only ignition-overpressure model in the library and the classic engineering one. Two lines of the topic page need revising:
  - "Ignition overpressure is aimed by the trench and is not modelled at all" (Deflectors section).
  - "No model covers ignition overpressure" (open questions).
  - They should say that no modern far-field model covers it, while a 1985 on-vehicle engineering model does.
- **IOP mechanism** (p. 482–483). The thickening wall jet formed where the SRB plume hits the flame deflector acts as the piston of a shock tube. Its velocity comes from a Prandtl–Meyer expansion of the post-normal-shock flow, so the compression strength follows ṗ_c (eqs. 1–5). A Rayleigh-type plane-source integral over the duct opening carries the wave to the vehicle (eq. 8). Because the source term is the time derivative of the pulse, the radiated pressure goes as p̈_c and arrives as an N-wave.
- **DOP mechanism** (p. 483–484). The wall-jet front moving down the horizontal duct is a moving source, with convection speed v_c = C + u_wj cos α (eq. 12). It radiates a later, lower-frequency duct overpressure from the downstream exits. Crosswind enters as C + V_wind.
- **Validation and calibration.** The model matches STS-1 IOP waveform and timing, but the amplitude comparison is at a different location (Fig. 3, p. 483). It captures the "salient features" of STS-3 (Fig. 11, p. 486). Its multiplying factors K, K_f and L are not reported, so the amplitude calibration is not shown.
- **Subscale similarity.** This limits the rule of [Tsutsumi 2015] (same U_e, so the same SPL and frequency ∝ 1/L) for the ignition phase. The motor transient must also scale with size. The 6.4% Tomahawk transient was roughly 1.7–2.5× too slow (my estimate from Figs. 7–8) and had a stronger second ṗ_c peak, so IOP and DOP overlap in the model tests (p. 486). Tsutsumi's own note cites the same Dougherty & Guest 6.4% Shuttle programme (p. 7).
- **Pads and ducts** (Deflectors, ducts, pads section).
  - The paper supports [Panda 2013] (duct exits as dominant sources) and [Ranow 2021] (duct length changes the result).
  - It adds pad-design levers: a deflector that sends the plume under a covered tunnel cuts IOP (p. 485), and retarding the wall jet should cut DOP (p. 487).
  - It also gives the 1981–82 suppression baseline, water-trough covers plus exhaust water injection from STS-2 onwards (p. 481).

## mcinerny-1996-launch-vehicle-acoustics-overall-levels-spectra (queued 2026-10-25)

- **Origin of the OASPL_max formula.** Table 1 tests the formula OAPWL(η = 0.5% of ½TV_e) − 10 log 4πR² + 8 dB with R = d/sin 70°. It is reproduced to 0.1 dB with R = 1 km/sin 70°.
  - Over 19 stations the errors are +6.4/−4.7 dB, mean +2.0 dB, rms 4.1 dB (note-writer's arithmetic). This matches exactly the McInerny-fit statistics quoted in mathews-2025-thesis-supersonic-jet-rocket-noise-saturn (p. 53), against 1.6 dB for SATURN's Atlas V fit.
  - The paper itself says the 8 dB vertical-launch directivity factor may contain the ground reflection (p. 513). That is the basis of the 8 → 5 dB + ground-factor split in mathews-2025-predicting-sound-power-oaspl-heated-supersonic-jets (p. 375), which also records the 5 dB / 8 dB usage confirmed here (p. 513–514).
- **Efficiency and motor size.** The fixed 0.5% is too high for Scout and Delta (implied ≈ 0.15–0.17%) and too low for Titan IV (≈ 0.9% for the second launch), in the historical convention that includes the ground.
  - The author's reading is that η scales with individual motor thrust: Delta's 380 kN strap-ons behave like small motors (p. 513–514).
  - This adds an early data point to the open question "Does η depend on clustering?". It contradicts varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model (η size-independent, 0.6–0.7%) and agrees in direction with Vulcan's low η.
  - The note flags a possible misprint in Scout's OAPWL (185.9 dB implies V_e ≈ 3540 m/s, whereas 185.0 dB would match mcinerny-2005).
- **Directivity.** The 70° peak angle (from Cole et al.'s vertical launches, 70–80°) sits within the modern 64–72° range. Horizontal-firing factors (5 dB at 50–55°) put the predicted OASPL peak about 3–4 s late at 0.5 km, while vertical-launch factors (8 dB at 70–75°) match timing and trend at all four stations (Figs. 2–3, p. 514–515).
  - The author blames ground effects in horizontal firings (p. 512). This is a third explanation of the SP-8072 ~50° angle, beside the nozzle-centred arcs of james-2012-directivity-curves-rocket-noise-model and the hillside impingement of gee-2012-reusable-srm-noise-spectral-statistical. The three are compatible rather than competing.
  - The timing test anticipates the trajectory-based directivity of kellison-2026-falcon-9-noise-two-campaigns.
- **Far-range overprediction.** The model overpredicts by about 4–6 dB at 12–16 km (Table 1; Fig. 3, about 113 against 107–108 dB at 12.62 km). This is consistent with kellison-2026's finding that the formula holds to about 4–5 km. The paper attributes it to nonlinear energy transfer rather than absorption (p. 515); that attribution is disputed (see rocket-noise-measurements above).
- **Spectrum.** Dimensionless PSDs (eq. 2b, with 10 log Q = 5 dB and η = 0.005) peak at about 0.015–0.02 on fD_e/V_e and match SP-8072's peak frequency and level once η is corrected. They are, however, "deficient in energy" at high frequency relative to SP-8072 (p. 516).
  - This is opposite to Park (2019) as cited in lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review (p. 4), and different from the SATURN thesis finding that SP-8072's peak is an octave too high.
  - The paper's caveat is that it is not known whether SP-8072's one-third-octave-based PSD was corrected for absorption. This should go under "Peak Strouhal number and its normalisation" as an unresolved conflict.
- **Core lengths and source region.** The paper gives laminar core 16–20 D_e, supersonic core 25–35 D_e and source-region length (L_c + L_s)/2 (p. 511–512). These are the "McInerny 1996a" lengths in Lubert. The lubert-2022 note writes L_c 16–25 D_e, which should be checked.
  - The paper's far-field criteria are d ≫ 30 D_e, d ≥ 125 D_e for an undeflected plume and d ≤ 2000 D_e for a vertical trajectory (p. 512).

## stark-2013-flow-separation-rocket-nozzles-overview (queued 2026-10-25)

Stark's overview (AIAA 2013-3840) is the library's first source on what happens inside a sea-level, strongly overexpanded rocket nozzle. Whether it runs full, with free shock separation (FSS), or with restricted shock separation (RSS, reattachment via the cap-shock pattern in thrust-optimised Rao nozzles) sets the exit state (effective exit area, M_e, U_e, D_e) that SP-8072, the Mathews & Gee OAPWL formula and SATURN take as input (p. 3). His turbulent criterion p_sep/p_a = 1/Ma_sep (valid above Ma_sep ≈ 2.4, p. 5) and the Schmucker criterion (1.88Ma_sep − 1)^−0.64 (p. 3), which bound a database of about 90 data series from above and below (Fig. 2, p. 6–7), give a quick check of whether a library case is separated. In the note's arithmetic, Rita II in [Varnier 2001](../Notes/varnier-2001-rocket-engine-freejet-noise-model.md) (M_e 3.8, p_e/p_a 0.3) sits between the 1/Ma (0.26) and Schmucker (0.31) thresholds, at incipient separation, which fits its "unstable" emission and possible Mach disk (Varnier p. 1856). The MARTEL jet of [Langenais 2019](../Notes/langenais-2019-hot-supersonic-jet-noise-simulation-nonlinear-propagation.md) (M_e 3.1, p_e/p_∞ 0.6) is far from both thresholds (0.32/0.37), supporting the authors' view that their LES lip separation is spurious. The paper gives the background to the start-up separation flapping and base loads that [Lubert 2022](../Notes/lubert-2022-supersonic-jet-noise-launch-vehicles-review.md) mentions (p. 25). This covers RSS persisting longer in shut-down than start-up (Nave & Coffey 1973, p. 3), the start-up/shut-down hysteresis of Vulcain side loads (Terhardt et al. 1999, p. 3), and a maximum transient TIC side load 45% of a parabolic nozzle's (Ruf et al. 2010, p. 4). It extends the "Missing physics" open question: no rocket-noise model covers the ignition-transient nozzle state. The separation-shock unsteadiness (Lawrence's 600 Hz fan-shaped shock, p. 2; the separated region acting as a resonator, Torngren 2001, p. 4) is a candidate low-frequency near-field source during start-up that no SP-8072-type model contains. That last point is my inference, not the paper's. Nothing in the paper contradicts the existing page.
