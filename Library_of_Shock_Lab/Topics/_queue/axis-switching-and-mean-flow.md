## mitchell-2013-near-field-structure-underexpanded-elliptic-jets (queued 2026-10-23)

This is the original two-plane PIV database (NPR 2.2, 2.6, 3.4, 4.2; 1,100 samples; 0–10d) of the group's 20 × 10 mm AR 2 ellipse, which [Edgington-Mitchell 2015](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md) and [Ivelja 2024](../Notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md) later decomposed (p. 3, 5). All four jets switch axes, and the mean-field major-axis contraction is "most pronounced" at NPR 4.2 (p. 5), but no switch position is given.

The fluctuation fields show a clear near-nozzle asymmetry only at NPR 2.2 and 2.6. The major-axis shear layer pinches in at x/d ≈ 3.5 and 4.0, and the minor-axis peak-fluctuation line moves outwards from x/d ≈ 2 to 3.5–4. At 3.4 and 4.2 growth looks axisymmetric (p. 6), and shear-layer mixing is highest at NPR 2.6, then 2.2 (p. 12). The note reads this as two processes under one name: a gas-dynamic boundary switch at high NPR (Schadow's underexpanded AR 3 jet switches at x/d_e ≈ 3, cited p. 2) and a flapping-driven shear-layer switch at low NPR. The second fits claim 1 of the page ([Krothapalli 1986](../Notes/krothapalli-1986-screech-tones-mixing-underexpanded-rectangular-jet.md); [Alkislar 2003](../Notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md)).

The pinch positions sit just upstream of the 4.3d half-width crossing (Edgington-Mitchell 2015, NPR 2.2) and the AR = 1 point near 5d (Ivelja 2024, NPR 2.6). This supports, across metrics, the page's statement that the AR 2 switch moves downstream with NPR within the screech range.

New for the page: a Hussain & Husain cut-and-connect **vortex bifurcation** is proposed at NPR 4.2, previously reported only for AR > 3.5. It rests on a pair of outward-angled low-speed streaks in the minor-axis plane, with u_rms and v_rms peaks at r/d ±0.12 (x/d 2.1) moving to 0.2 (x/d 3.2) and a u_rms spike at x/d 1.8 (p. 11–12). The authors' causes are greater shear-layer curvature or stronger feedback self-excitation (p. 12). The note offers an alternative: the planar trace of the slip surface behind an elliptic Mach disc that is widest in the minor-axis plane. Fig. 15 shows a wider deficit there at x/d = 1.5, and the round-jet inner shear layer of [Edgington-Mitchell 2014, Mach disk](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2014-underexpanded-jet-mach-disk-shear-layer.md) looks the same in a planar cut. This is worth an open-question line, settled by cross-plane PIV at x/d ≈ 2–3.

## edgington-mitchell-2015-staging-screeching-elliptical-jets (queued 2026-10-23)

The paper proposes that strong underexpansion deforms the jet boundary away from an elliptical cross-section close to the nozzle, allowing an instability "not locked to a particular axis", and labels mode V at NPR 4.2 helical on that basis (p. 1017, 1021–1022). This moves the companion paper's idea (a silent helical-like pair downstream of the switch at NPR 2.2, [Edgington-Mitchell 2015, multimodal](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2015-multimodal-instability-weakly-underexpanded-elliptic-jet.md)) to the dominant tone at high NPR, and anticipates the quasi-helical tones of another ellipse ([Mazharmanesh 2025](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), St 0.28 at NPR 3.6; dominant at NPR 4.1–4.9 in [Mazharmanesh 2026](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 21–22). The evidence (weak antisymmetric correlations, |R| ≲ 0.3, in two separately acquired views; Fig. 10) carries the same caveats as the original pair: no cross-plane phase, and it fits flapping/wagging alternation, a precessing plane or [Suzuki 2026](../Notes/suzuki-2026-coherent-structures-axis-switching-elliptical-jets.md)'s SA-family structure equally. No switch location or cross-section is measured here; [Mitchell 2013](../Notes/mitchell-2013-near-field-structure-underexpanded-elliptic-jets.md) found the strongest major-axis contraction (p. 5) and no flapping (p. 9) at NPR 4.2, consistent with the proposal but not proof of a helix. The page's open question (one wavepacket or two; helix or not) is unchanged.

## gutmark-1995-mixing-enhancement-supersonic-free-shear-flows (queued 2026-10-24)

The review offers two pre-PIV mechanisms for axis switching: for triangular and square jets, flat sides spreading faster than corners plus corner-curvature vortex deformation into streamwise vortices (Gutmark et al. 1991a, p. 395); and natural streamwise vortices in underexpanded jets, located between the intercepting shock and the jet boundary and seeded by tiny nozzle imperfections (Novopashin & Perepelkin 1989, p. 401), the same source [Alkislar 2003](../Notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md) cites for its x/h = 6 vortices (p. 136). Its most useful datum for the page is Zaman's (1994) result that tabs on AR 3–8 rectangular and elliptic nozzles can control or completely suppress axis switching while raising or lowering near-field entrainment, with spreading beyond x/D = 10 unchanged or slightly lower (p. 402). That is an early interventional test of the streamwise-vortex explanation, which the page currently says is "inferred, not demonstrated"; it supports the claim that streamwise vorticity can set the switch, though from mean-flow data only. Trailing-edge devices ([Samimy 1998](../Notes/samimy-1998-rectangular-jet-trailing-edge-control.md)) and notched nozzles (Pannu & Johannesen 1976: side jets in the notch plane, p. 403) are the same family. Delta tabs make counter-rotating pairs that impose their own symmetry on the cross-section (Fig. 8, M_j 1.63, p. 402). Nothing here addresses the modes across the switch.

## zaman-1999-spreading-compressible-jets-nozzle-geometries (queued 2026-10-24)

- **Primary source for the "Zaman, cited by Raman 1998" sentence.** Zaman 1999 is the primary source for that sentence in "Where the switch sits". It should replace the second-hand citation, with the nuance that the gain depends on the baseline.
- **The stage-dependent numbers.** For the round convergent nozzle, the mass flux at x/D = 14 reads:
  - 5.64 and 5.55 in flapping B (M_j 1.32 and 1.43);
  - 3.58 to 2.92 in helical C (1.456–1.68);
  - ≈3.6 in D;
  - only 3.3–3.8 in the axisymmetric A stages (Fig. 12, p. 214, read).
- **Baselines.** A steady RANS no-screech curve falls from 3.5 at M_j 1.0 to 2.2 at 1.6. Against it, B, C and D all raise the flux, by about +50 % to +110 %, while A does not; against the measured subsonic level (≈3.8) only B does (note's arithmetic).
- **Abrupt mean-flow change at a stage jump.** The −35 % drop between M_j 1.43 (B) and 1.456 (C) on one fixed nozzle is the earliest library evidence that the time-averaged far field changes abruptly at a stage jump. It anticipates [Mazharmanesh 2026]'s per-tone short-time means.
- **Switching follows screech in the AR 3 jets.** The plain AR 3 rectangular and elliptic convergent jets have switched axes by x/D = 10 at M_j 1.63 (screeching), but show no switch over x/D 2–19 at M_j 0.3 (Figs 5–6, p. 204). This agrees with [Krothapalli 1986] (p. 133) and [Alkislar 2003] (p. 135–136).
- **Streamwise vortices switch without screech.** The tabbed rectangle, with its screech almost removed, switches by x/D ≈ 2 (Fig. 2, p. 203) through in-flow vortex pairs that regroup into out-flow pairs (Fig. 16, p. 220–221). So stationary streamwise vortices switch a jet without screech. This qualifies Alkislar's prediction that suppressing screech removes the crossover, and supports [Samimy 1998]'s off-design streamwise-vortex route.
- **Initial conditions set the switch.** Hussain & Husain's tripped ellipse moved its switch from 3.5D to 15D (p. 218). This adds weight to the open question "Where is the switch?": the switch position depends on boundary-layer state and "natural excitation", not only on the nozzle.
- **RANS check.** RANS (NPARC) reproduces the hot-wire streamwise vorticity of the tabs at x/D = 1, and predicts the same pattern at M_j 1.63 (Fig. 15, p. 219–220).
