## johnston-2016-underexpanded-jet-intrusive-tab-visualisation (queued 2026-10-23)

Johnston & Edgington-Mitchell 2016 is the library's only round-jet staging map with an azimuthally local lip change: one radial triangular tab (0.128D deep, just over 2% blockage) on a 15 mm converging nozzle, NPR 2.1–4.5 in 0.1 steps (Fig. 2, p. 53). The baseline jumps at NPR 2.4, 3.0 and 3.8 with the A → B down, B → C up, C → D down pattern of [Powell 1992](../Notes/powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md) (p. 2825); the tab raises St at every NPR, delays the first jump by 0.1 NPR, makes the jumps smaller, and removes both the upward B → C jump and the C → D drop (p. 53–55; note's digitisation). At NPR 2.6 two orthogonal views of two-point schlieren correlations show the baseline's antisymmetric B flapping replaced by a pattern symmetric in both planes, weaker on the tabbed side, the authors' "punctuated toroid" (p. 56–58): a loop change at fixed NPR set by the lip. It supports current-understanding point 4 (the staging NPR belongs to the rig) but is not the clean receptivity test the "Lip, exterior and reflectors" section asks for: unlike [Weightman 2019](../Notes/weightman-2019-nozzle-external-geometry-azimuthal-mode-impinging-jet.md) (tones fixed) and André's notches ([André 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), cells unchanged, p. 666–668), the tab truncates the shocks on its side and shortens the cells by about 16% (≈ 1.07D → 0.90D, note's reading of Figs 4–5), and every tone moves. By the note's arithmetic the tabbed first and third branches sit 1.18–1.20 times above baseline A and C, about what the shorter cells alone would give (Powell's f ∝ 1/L_s), while the second branch is about 1.5 times baseline B, exactly where the B → A-like change was imaged; this hints that the tab mostly rescales loops and replaces only B, but the spacing was measured only at NPR 2.6. The baseline A branch also falls 0.64 → 0.51 over NPR 2.1–2.3 with no upward A1 → A2 jump, below the same lab's A2 at St 0.63–0.65 at NPR 2.25 ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md)), more evidence that tip details move low-NPR staging. The open question "No free-jet test has changed only the exterior and tracked the A1/A2 limits" still stands; snapshot correlations cannot show whether the tabbed jet alternates between loops near its jumps (cf. [Knast 2018](../Notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md), p. 6).

## edgington-mitchell-2019-aeroacoustic-resonance-review (queued 2026-10-23)

This 2019 group review sits between [Raman 1998](../Notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) and the guided-mode era, and supplies the four-process vocabulary (downstream q_d, generation η_g, upstream η_u, receptivity η_r) and Powell's phase and gain criteria (eqs. 1–2, p. 120–122) that the screech page's item 1 uses.
- *Powell's formula.* It shows eq. 5 follows from three different premises: a timing argument with n = N, maximum upstream directivity of a phased array, and Tam's weakest link (p. 125, 128–129). Its success therefore validates none of them.
- *Shock motion.* Motion is phase-locked to the cycle and mostly vortex-driven: axial in A, transverse in flapping, circular in helical. Shock splitting appears at the 4th cell in A1 (NPR 2.15) and the 3rd cell in C (NPR 3.4), so a stationary shock pattern is "at best a convenient fiction" (p. 136–138).
- *Source location.* The author places the source at the shock tips via shock leakage, following Manning & Lele, Suzuki & Lele, Shariff & Manning and group PIV ray tracing (Fig. 32, A2, leak at x/D ≈ 2.5; p. 162–165). This is against Tam et al. 2014's core emission (p. 139), and the review records "no consensus" on source location or helical directivity (p. 140). Umeda & Ishii's orbiting sonic-line source and Lele 2005's proof that wave models equal distributed sources belong in the page's "Directivity" open question.
- *Framing since revised.* The review frames generation as producing both the upstream wave and the tone (p. 120), with free-stream against G-JM return still case-dependent (p. 172–173, 176). That is superseded for free-jet screech by [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) and [Nogueira 2024](../Notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md) (triad closure, leakage as radiation). It supports the page's "Leakage as closure" paragraph as the historical origin of the leakage-as-closure view.
- *Lip.* It argues the lip acts acoustically, not through entrainment: Raman 1997 found the expanding boundary blocks the path to a thin lip, and reflectors enhance, suppress or shift the tone (p. 134–135). This fits item 6.
- No change to current understanding.

## raman-1999-supersonic-jet-screech-half-century-powell-present (queued 2026-10-23)

Raman 1999 adds no new measurements to [Raman 1998](../Notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) but sharpens three arguments. First, it insists that Powell's theory contains two separate conditions, a phase criterion (convection plus return time = integer periods, with optional emission and receptivity delays) and an amplitude criterion (upstream-peaked directivity of the phased monopole array), meant to hold together (p. 545–546). Second, it shows that single-source feedback (the equivalent monopole at the 3rd shock for M_j 1.45 and the 4th for 1.75, Fig. 6, p. 555–556) removes the amplitude criterion and makes screech "analogous to the edgetone" with h = (N + p)s (p. 555), which is the integer-loop picture later measured by [Mercier 2017](../Notes/mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md) (p. 219–224) and used by [Powell 1992](../Notes/powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md) (p. 2833). Third, it poses the question of how one source can give strong upstream directivity (p. 555); [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, shock leakage](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md) (p. 21–23) and [Suzuki & Lele 2003](../Notes/suzuki-2003-shock-leakage-vortex-laden-mixing-layer-screech.md) (p. 162–163) answer it in opposite ways, and guided-mode closure ([Nogueira 2024](../Notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md), p. 14–17) makes upstream directivity irrelevant to the loop. It also summarises Manning & Lele 1998 (a forced supersonic shear layer hit by one oblique compression; radiated wave a short compression then a longer expansion, amplitude linear in the compression; p. 563), the first computation of the shock-leakage line, and Westley's "eddy whiplash" idea with the whip-crack analogy (p. 556–557). On amplitude it repeats the 1998 thesis (feedback and receptivity hold the key; p. 564), quotes +23 dB from a lip thickener at M_j 1.75 (Fig. 7, p. 557–558), Powell's 1998 keynote prediction of the screech amplitude slope from feedback efficiency (p. 555), and the Soviet finding that preheating lowers screech intensity (Anufriev et al., p. 548), an early piece of the "Full scale" heating thread. Nothing here changes the page's current understanding; it supports items 1 (four links) and 6 (nozzle and surroundings set amplitude) and gives the history behind the closure section.

## shen-2002-three-dimensional-numerical-simulation-jet-screech (queued 2026-10-23)

- **Origin of the guided-mode closure idea for free-jet screech** (p. 38–39; Fig. 6).
  - A2 and C close through Tam & Ahuja's upstream acoustic mode of the jet, "guided by the jet". The lowest frequencies of the (0, 2) and (1, 1) vortex-sheet modes on D_j follow the A2 and C wavelengths.
  - A1 and B use Tam's weakest-link free-sound return.
  - The instability-wave–shock interaction sits around the 4th–5th shock cell (p. 37–38), consistent with Mercier 2017's 4th-shock source.
- **How it fits claim 2.** Claim 2 (the G-JM, not free-stream sound) is the later resolution. Edgington-Mitchell 2018 (p. 10–11) and Gojon 2018 (p. 2918–2920) put A1 on the same (0, 2) mode. Shen & Tam's own Table 1 gives an implied A1/B return speed of 0.91–0.93a∞, subsonic like the G-JM (Mancinelli 2019, p. 3–4), so even their data do not single out free sound for A1 and B (note's derivation).
- **Band-edge caveat.** In their Fig. 6 the A1 points lie 6–14 % above the (0, 2) line, which is why they excluded A1. Band edges depend on temperature and shear-layer thickness (Mancinelli 2021, p. 15–16, 25–26), and the sheet's temperature is not stated.
- **Instability-wave–shock interaction.** The near-lip pressure at r = 0.3D in Fig. 8b, shock cells and instability waves superposed, is the Tam & Tanna interaction region of claim 3.
