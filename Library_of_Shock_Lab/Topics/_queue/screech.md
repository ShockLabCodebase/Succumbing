## gutmark-1995-mixing-enhancement-supersonic-free-shear-flows (queued 2026-10-24)

The review treats screech purely as a mixing agent, and its evidence is all mean-flow: strengthening the loop raises mixing (a cylinder within 8D, Umeda et al. 1987; paddles beside an M 1.4, 5:1 rectangular nozzle at design pressure, +48 % mixing rate, Rice & Raman 1993a; p. 385, 389), blocking it with baffles changes the mixing of underexpanded jets (p. 389), and collars, cavities and ejector ducts that resonate with the jet raise spreading (p. 385, 389, 391–392). This agrees with Glass 1968 and Rice & Raman as reported by [Raman 1998](../Notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) (p. 93–95) and with the page's claim 8 ("screech reshapes its own jet"). Two cautions for the page: the paddle case is at design pressure, so it is an induced supersonic edge tone rather than shock-cell screech (as [Raman 1999](../Notes/raman-1999-supersonic-jet-screech-half-century-powell-present.md) p. 562 describes it, with "about 30 %" more mass flux rather than 48 %); and the review never separates screech modes, whereas Zaman's B-mode-only mass-flux gain (Raman 1998, p. 94, 97) shows mixing depends on the stage. For suppression it adds tabs that raise mixing and reduce screech at once (Ahuja & Brown 1989, p. 401) and the screech-free tapered-slot nozzle (p. 403), consistent with [Norum 1983](../Notes/norum-1983-screech-suppression-supersonic-jets.md)'s shock-train-disruption category, and lip thickness changing screech far more than spreading (Ponton & Seiner 1989, p. 389–390; cf. [Ponton 1992](../Notes/ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md)). An underexpanded rectangular jet in an ejector mixes best when its screech tone is at the most unstable St_D 0.11–0.14, and tone–duct matching makes the dominant tones change "in a staged manner" (Hsia et al. 1988, p. 392): an early installation-driven tone-selection observation. No change to the page's open questions on closure or amplitude.

## zaman-1999-spreading-compressible-jets-nozzle-geometries (queued 2026-10-24)

- **Screech reshapes its own jet.** The paper is a primary source for claim 8 ("Screech reshapes its own jet … speeds spreading"). The mass flux of every screeching nozzle at M_j 1.63 exceeds the non-screeching lobed nozzle's (p. 207, 211).
- **Gains against a no-screech RANS.** On one round nozzle, flux at x/D = 14 is 50–110 % above a steady RANS no-screech computation in B, C and D (Fig. 12, read; note's arithmetic).
- **Mechanism, by analogy only.** The proposed mechanism is the analogy with acoustic excitation: screech organises and intensifies the azimuthal structures, whose breakdown near the end of the potential core entrains (Crow & Champagne +15 % at x/D 8–10; p. 212). No phase-resolved data test it here.
- **Agreement with centreline data.** It agrees with Glass 1968 and Sherman et al. 1976, whose centreline pressure drop is largest in B (p. 215). It also fits [André 2013]'s finding that screech shortens the shock train.
- **Nuance against Raman 1998's summary.** Raman 1998 reports "B about +60 %, other stages not significant". This paper says D is also "relatively high", and only the axisymmetric A stages show no gain beyond the RANS offset.
- **Tabs.** Four 2 %-blockage delta tabs remove the round jet's tone yet spread it more than any screech stage (p. 211, 216). Streamwise vortices therefore beat screech as a mixer, at a thrust cost: C_f 0.913 for ṁ/ṁ_e 5.58 against 3.12 (Table 1).
- **Lip sensitivity.** Flux amplitudes are expected to vary with lip thickness through screech amplitude, while the stage trend should hold (p. 214–215). This links to the Lip and reflectors paragraph ([Norum 1983]; [Ponton 1992]).
