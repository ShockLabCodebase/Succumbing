## bell-2018-coupled-underexpanded-supersonic-twin-jets (queued 2026-10-22)

Bell 2018 is the first paper from the group's PIV enclosure, "facility two" in [Bell 2021](../Notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md). It uses the same 10 mm nozzles at s/D 3 as [Knast 2018](../Notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md). Its NPR 4.6 and 5.0 PIV sets (about 9000 independent fields each at 0.5 Hz, p. 3) appear to be the data later reanalysed by [Nogueira 2021](../Notes/nogueira-2021-twin-jet-coupling-spatial-lsa.md) (NPR 5.0, 1SS) and Bell 2021 (conditional POD). The paper does not say so; the match is the note's inference from rig, NPRs and field counts.
- **Coupling states.** At NPR 5.0, single-reference R11 and R22 maps show steady symmetric coupling: R11 mirror-symmetric, R22 bands opposed across the midplane (Figs 20, 22, p. 12). This agrees with Nogueira's 1SS. At NPR 4.6 there is only a faint symmetric pattern, and no standing wave survives the averages. Of the four explanations offered (p. 15), temporal switching is the one Bell 2021 later confirmed, with three intermittent states.
- **Rig dependence.** In this enclosure the jump to the steady symmetric stage falls between NPR 4.7 and 4.8 (St 0.21 → 0.19, Fig. 3 read; the text says 4.85, p. 3). In the schlieren rig the symmetric process starts at NPR 4.4 (Bell 2021, Fig. 5). This is one more datum for the page's point that identical nozzles stage at different NPRs in different enclosures.
- **New flow-field results:**
  - no inward distortion of the shock cells at s/D 3, unlike s/D 1.9 (Seiner 1986; Goparaju & Gaitonde 2017) (p. 5);
  - first hydrodynamic contact at x/D ≈ 4–4.5 from rms and skewness, against 7.5–8.5 from extrapolating the shear-layer edges (p. 9–11, 17);
  - interior TKE overtakes exterior TKE only beyond x/D ≈ 10 (p. 11);
  - a stronger standing wave between the jets than outside, attributed to in-phase addition of both jets' upstream waves (p. 14).
- **Screech-based coupling.** Panda's relation closes at NPR 5.0 (λ_s,est 3.7 ± 0.17D against 3.9D, Table 2, p. 15), which the authors read as evidence for screech-based coupling. That anticipates Nogueira 2021's guided-mode closure of 1SS on the same data.
- **For "Methods and data".** This paper is the cautionary example for non-time-resolved diagnostics. Log₁₀ rms standing-wave maps (as in Esfahani 2021's schlieren) and single-reference correlation maps work on PIV, but switching between states blurs both.
- **Correction to check.** The Bell 2021 note summarises this paper as finding "clear antisymmetry with a strong near-field standing wave at the steady condition" (Bell 2021, p. 3–4). This paper calls NPR 5.0 symmetric throughout (p. 12, 15, 17), in the same sense as Bell 2021's symmetric and Nogueira's 1SS. If the naming row cites Bell 2018, map it as "symmetric = mirror-symmetric flow (R11), = 1SS".

## raman-1999-supersonic-jet-screech-half-century-powell-present (queued 2026-10-23)

Raman 1999 (p. 544, 557–560) gives the motivation (sonic fatigue on the VC-10, F-15 and B-1B; high inter-nozzle dynamic pressures; stealth skins and F-22-type vectoring nozzles at risk), names the NASA Langley, USAF and McDonnell Douglas twin-jet programmes (including Seiner, Manning & Ponton 1988, AIAA J. 26, PDF held, not yet indexed) and Umeda & Ishii's multi-jets, and summarises Raman & Taghavi 1998 (JFM 354): side-by-side rectangular jets with one movable nozzle, where slightly different M_j give completely different coupling, mode I (antisymmetric) minimising and mode II raising the inter-nozzle SPL, with phase-averaged yz near fields (Fig. 8, p. 560). Useful detail: the Fig. 8 sketch gives the exit as 34.5 × 6.9 mm, i.e. AR 5.0. The page currently takes "AR 5" for Raman & Taghavi from [Esfahani 2021, coupling modes](../Notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md) (p. 3); this note confirms it from Raman's own figure, and the [Raman 1998](../Notes/raman-1998-supersonic-jet-screech-review.md) note's remark that the 1998 review does not give the AR could now point here. No frequencies, spacing or dB values are given in this paper (those are in Raman 1998). No change to current understanding.

## seiner-1988-dynamic-pressure-loads-twin-supersonic-plume-resonance (queued 2026-10-23)

The library's earliest twin-jet coupling study: two choked round tubes at s/D 1.9 on a 1/40-scale F-15 empennage, M_j 1.02–1.81, unheated (p. 954). At M_j 1.32 (f_s 7.62 kHz, St ≈ 0.32 by the note's conversion) coherence is ≈ 1 between all three exit-plane sensors. The two outer sensors are in phase with each other and in antiphase with the internozzle sensor (Figs 4–5, p. 955–956). With the 90°-rotated schlieren showing no waves normal to the nozzle plane (Fig. 7, p. 957), this means lateral flapping in the nozzle plane, mirror-symmetric about the midplane: **1SS** in the Rodríguez/Nogueira convention, Bell 2021's "symmetric" (0°), and the 0° that Knast 2018 label "antisymmetric". The paper's own "antisymmetric" means each jet about its own axis, so the labels need mapping. At s/D 3 the loudest band of [bell-2021](../Notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md) is antisymmetric at NPR 3.0–3.5, so the selected symmetry depends on spacing. [rodriguez-2023](../Notes/rodriguez-2023-preferred-flapping-round-twin-jets.md)'s KH maps favour SA1 below s/D 3 near St 0.3 (p. 12), so this is a second case (after Nogueira's 1SS) where KH growth picks the wrong symmetry; [stavropoulos-2024](../Notes/stavropoulos-2024-upstream-waves-merging-twin-jets.md) (SA band moves with spacing, SS barely) offers a guided-mode route (the note's inference). The coupled B mode reaches 162.5 dB (0.385 psi) at the internozzle point against ≤ 152 dB for one tube with the other blocked: a factor >2 over 1.15 ≤ M_j ≤ 1.5, ≈ 4 (12 dB) at M_j 1.30 (Figs 9, 11, 12, p. 957–958). Two points qualify Current understanding item 3 ("coupling barely moves the tone"): at this close spacing the twin B tone is 3–9 % (≈ 0.01–0.027 in St) below the single-jet B tone, attributed to 10–15 % longer shock cells (p. 958–959), and the A → B switch moves from M_j ≈ 1.13 to ≈ 1.09 (figure reading). The pair has fewer modes (no E; A modes only to M_j 1.10). Decoupling: a tab of ≈ 1.2 % blockage on one nozzle drops the B tone 11–18 dB to single-jet levels; a notch of the same area fails (Figs 15–16, p. 959–960). This parallels [esfahani-2021-control-twin-rectangular-jet-coupling](../Notes/esfahani-2021-control-twin-rectangular-jet-coupling.md). The phase-locked, 32-flash schlieren assumes steady coupling, and intermittency was not examined. A coherent 5.0–5.8 kHz band with antiphase outer sensors (Fig. 5b, note's reading) suggests the opposite symmetry is present as broadband content.
