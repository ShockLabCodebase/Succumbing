## bell-2018-coupled-underexpanded-supersonic-twin-jets (queued 2026-10-22)

## lu-2026-rectangular-jet-screech-dimensional-reduction (queued 2026-10-22)

- **Staging limits.** The paper's closing message bears directly on staging-limit prediction (current understanding item 5, "No model predicts the staging limits"). Reduced (planar) models predict screech frequencies but cannot give "the cut-on and cut-off frequencies of the different stages", because their GJM bands are too wide (p. 21). The 2D model with a measured base flow gives narrow bands and fewer spurious tones but is very sensitive to the base flow (p. 20–21).

## johnston-2016-underexpanded-jet-intrusive-tab-visualisation (queued 2026-10-23)

Johnston & Edgington-Mitchell 2016 is the library's only round-jet staging map with an azimuthally local lip change: one radial triangular tab (0.128D deep, just over 2% blockage) on a 15 mm converging nozzle, NPR 2.1–4.5 in 0.1 steps (Fig. 2, p. 53). The baseline jumps at NPR 2.4, 3.0 and 3.8 with the A → B down, B → C up, C → D down pattern of [Powell 1992](../Notes/powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md) (p. 2825); the tab raises St at every NPR, delays the first jump by 0.1 NPR, makes the jumps smaller, and removes both the upward B → C jump and the C → D drop (p. 53–55; note's digitisation). At NPR 2.6 two orthogonal views of two-point schlieren correlations show the baseline's antisymmetric B flapping replaced by a pattern symmetric in both planes, weaker on the tabbed side, the authors' "punctuated toroid" (p. 56–58): a loop change at fixed NPR set by the lip. It supports current-understanding point 4 (the staging NPR belongs to the rig) but is not the clean receptivity test the "Lip, exterior and reflectors" section asks for: unlike [Weightman 2019](../Notes/weightman-2019-nozzle-external-geometry-azimuthal-mode-impinging-jet.md) (tones fixed) and André's notches ([André 2013](../Notes/andre-2013-bbsan-screeching-non-screeching-underexpanded-jets.md), cells unchanged, p. 666–668), the tab truncates the shocks on its side and shortens the cells by about 16% (≈ 1.07D → 0.90D, note's reading of Figs 4–5), and every tone moves. By the note's arithmetic the tabbed first and third branches sit 1.18–1.20 times above baseline A and C, about what the shorter cells alone would give (Powell's f ∝ 1/L_s), while the second branch is about 1.5 times baseline B, exactly where the B → A-like change was imaged; this hints that the tab mostly rescales loops and replaces only B, but the spacing was measured only at NPR 2.6. The baseline A branch also falls 0.64 → 0.51 over NPR 2.1–2.3 with no upward A1 → A2 jump, below the same lab's A2 at St 0.63–0.65 at NPR 2.25 ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md)), more evidence that tip details move low-NPR staging. The open question "No free-jet test has changed only the exterior and tracked the A1/A2 limits" still stands; snapshot correlations cannot show whether the tabbed jet alternates between loops near its jumps (cf. [Knast 2018](../Notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md), p. 6).

## edgington-mitchell-2015-staging-screeching-elliptical-jets (queued 2026-10-23)

This is the group's original staging map of its AR 2 converging ellipse (D_e 14.14 mm, thick azimuthally varying lip): five dominant-tone stages over NPR 1.95–4.3 in 0.05 steps, varicose I (St 1.03 → 0.88, to NPR 2.05), flapping II (2.10–3.10, 0.57 → 0.30), flapping III (3.15–3.35, 0.36–0.34), flapping IV (3.40–3.90, 0.27–0.24) and a weakly antisymmetric "helical" V (3.95–4.3, 0.28–0.26) (Fig. 2, p. 1009–1010; Figs 6–10, p. 1014–1018). II → III is a ×1.20 tone jump with no change of symmetry, the non-round analogue of A1 → A2, which the authors tie to the shock spacing and the near-field standing-wave spacing parting by about 15 % and frame as weakest-link → waveguide feedback after Shen & Tam (Tables 3–4, p. 1020–1021). The framing is superseded, since both stages close on the same G-JM ([Edgington-Mitchell 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md); [Gojon 2018](../Notes/gojon-2018-oscillation-modes-screeching-jets.md)), but the observation is the k_s2 signature: with Table 3's NPR 3.2 entries corrected (they appear swapped; note's check against Tables 2, 4 and Figs 3, 5), k_sw/k_s1 = 1.30/1.14 = 1.14 in III against 0.95 and 1.00 in II and IV, agreeing with the reanalysis of this very jet in [Edgington-Mitchell 2022](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md) (p. 18) and with Panda's 1.17–1.24 and Mercier's 1.28 for A2/C. Unlike round A2/C, the k_s2 stage is bounded (ΔNPR ≈ 0.2) and the tone returns to the k_s1 trendline in IV; in [Mazharmanesh 2026](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md)'s thinner-lip ellipse k_s2 loops instead persist from NPR 3.4 to 4.9 (p. 21–22). **Correction to the page:** "Multimodal loops and non-round jets" says this nozzle "already flaps at St 0.52 at NPR 2.2 with no varicose tone"; this paper shows a varicose stage in the same nozzle up to NPR 2.05, with the jump between 2.05 and 2.10, about 0.1 NPR earlier than the ≈ 2.2 of [Mazharmanesh 2025](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) (p. 7), at similar St (varicose ≈ 1 at NPR 2.0; flapping ≈ 0.4 at 2.6). For "Which instability screeches", mode V at NPR 4.2 is a helical label from two separately acquired views (untested helicity), and its waveguide/k_s2 assignment rests only on the III/V trendline; the note's estimate k_sw/k_s ≈ 1.03–1.08 at NPR 4.2 (figure-read) points instead to a change of azimuthal family. Only dominant tones are plotted (no spectra, secondary tones, sweep directions or hysteresis test), so coexistence or alternation at the jumps is untested; the page's open question on multimodal tones stands.

## raman-1999-supersonic-jet-screech-half-century-powell-present (queued 2026-10-23)

The paper gives the staging history compactly (p. 547–548): Powell's four stages A–D; Merle's split of A into A1 and A2 with her stability grading (A1 unstable, A2 stable, B very unstable, C very stable, D unstable and not always visible); Davies & Oldfield's two-microphone classification (A1, A2 axisymmetric, B sinuous, C helical, D unclassified); and Powell's observation that flapping modes precessed one way or the other "rather unpredictably", an early report of the precession modelled by [Nogueira 2026](../Notes/nogueira-2026-flapping-motion-screeching-round-jets.md). It repeats that no one had explained the mode jumps (p. 548), and it labels the high-M_j thick-lip mode change in Ponton & Seiner as C → D (p. 557), which [Ponton 1992](../Notes/ponton-1992-nozzle-exit-lip-thickness-plume-resonance.md) itself calls a re-emerging B (the page already groups the two). The edge-tone analogy h = (N + p)s (p. 555) is Powell's N + p picture of stage jumps; it holds for any N + p, so by itself it cannot say when a jump occurs, which the shock-spectrum peaks k_s1/k_s2 now do ([Nogueira 2022](../Notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 7–13). In rectangular jets, Gutmark et al.'s symmetric → antisymmetric switch at M_j 1.15 and the coexistence of both modes (Kaji & Nishijima, Suda et al.) are quoted (p. 549). Raman's remark that the shock spacing to use is unclear when spacing varies (p. 546) is the effect that later explains staging. No change to current understanding.

## shen-2002-three-dimensional-numerical-simulation-jet-screech (queued 2026-10-23)

- **Origin of the "coexistence" view.** Shen & Tam 2002 is the source of the view that two screech tones coexist rather than switch, which the page currently cites only through Mancinelli 2019 (p. 7) and Gojon/Edgington-Mitchell.
  - Their unsteady-RANS k–ε DRP simulations (one cold start at each of M_j 1.1–1.5, ΔM_j 0.1) give two-tone states at M_j 1.2 (A1 146 dB + A2 133 dB) and 1.5 (B 153 dB + C 142 dB) (Figs 2, 5, p. 35–36).
  - At M_j 1.5, two instability wavenumbers appear in single pressure snapshots (Fig. 9, p. 38). From this they claim that two tones in a spectrum always coexist and that continuous switching is "repudiate[d]" (p. 38–39).
- **The page should state the limits** (note's analysis):
  - It is a deterministic model: axisymmetric k–ε, five azimuthal modes, steady axisymmetric inflow, no noise. It cannot hop between attractors.
  - Spectral blocks are ≈ 100–150 D/a∞, about 30–60 cycles (inferred from the 134 Hz bandwidth with D = 25.4 mm). Experimental dwells are 1–4 s (Mancinelli 2019), and in the Large_Test record median dwells are 19–30 ms ≈ 400–640 D/a∞.
  - No run is at the A1/A2 staging point (M_j ≈ 1.12). At M_j 1.2 the Ponton & Seiner data the simulation is compared with show B + A2, not A1 + A2 (Figs 6–7, note's reading).
  - The sign-blind cosine transform puts instability, acoustic and shock-scattered wavenumbers within 0.35/D of each other, so the snapshot argument is not unique.
- **Stronger in-simulation evidence.** The intermodulation lines in Fig. 2 (A2 − A1 at ≈ 120 dB, 2A1 − A2, 2A2 − A1, 3A1 − 2A2; note's reading) show the two tones together more convincingly. They mirror the Large_Test result that A2 − A1 is a genuine quadratic interaction, with the weaker tone persisting at about −9 dB during dwells (`Codebase/HPOD_Investigation/README.md`).
- **Reconciling hypothesis** (note's, labelled): a deterministic two-tone state with one dominant loop, which turbulence makes hop between A1-dominant and A2-dominant versions.
- **Table 1 reread** (note's derivation).
  - The A1/B residuals imply an upstream return at 0.91–0.93a∞, like the G-JM.
  - With that return, A2 (M_j 1.2) and C (1.5) need a shock wavenumber of 1.17–1.18K₁; with a return at a∞, 1.13K₁.
  - This matches k_s2/k_s1 ≈ 1.2–1.3 (Nogueira 2022, p. 7–13; Edgington-Mitchell 2022, p. 11–13) and Panda's 1.17 and 1.24. Their "second mechanism" is plausibly k_s2 closure, seen two decades early.
- **Staging limits.** The simulated staging is offset from the experiment it is compared with: A → B between M_j 1.2 and 1.3 against ≈ 1.18, and C never dominant up to 1.5 against ≈ 1.39–1.44. The authors stress that staging Mach numbers vary between facilities and between repeats in one facility (p. 33, 36), which supports claim 4.
- **Effect on the page.** This supports rather than overturns the current understanding. It adds the original "coexist" source to "Selection, hysteresis and switching", and adds a data point to the k_s2 thread.
