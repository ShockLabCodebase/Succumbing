## rockwell-1979-self-sustained-oscillations-impinging-shear-layers (queued 2026-10-24)

The review gives the cross-configuration background for staging. Jet–edge, cavity and mixing-layer–edge flows all show a three-stage staircase in fL/U (Fig. 8, p. 79–80), and the same jumps occur in edge, hole, ring and jet–wall tones (p. 80). At a frequency jump, two peaks in time-averaged spectra "may, in reality, be due to the intermittent switching from one mode to another" (Hussain & Zaman 1977; p. 81), which anticipates the coexistence-or-alternation question for multimodal screech (item 6). Hysteresis depends on the flow class (p. 81–82). Jet–edge and hole tones are hysteretic: the note's reading of Fig. 10 gives a loop about 500 wide in Re_d at L/d = 3.0. Cavities and wedges are not, and there the energy moves gradually from one frequency to the other through the jump. That bears on item 5, where free screech shows hysteresis only at C ↔ D ([Powell 1992](../Notes/powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md), p. 2826, 2832). Fig. 11 (Stegen & Karamcheti 1970, p. 83–84) shows a stage-1 mode persisting into stage 2 with its own amplitude envelope. The authors call this persistence "essential" to explaining jumps and hysteresis; it matches Powell 1992's secondary tones and B–D link. The authors' recommendation, to follow the streamwise evolution of all spectral components (fundamental, subharmonic, harmonics) through a jump (p. 82), translates into SPOD and BMD along the shear layer and into up-and-down NPR sweeps in the PyFR A1/A2 continuation. No change to the current understanding, but it adds a classical precedent for both outcomes (gradual or abrupt) at a jump and for hysteresis being configuration dependent.
