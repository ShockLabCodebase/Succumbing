## rockwell-1979-self-sustained-oscillations-impinging-shear-layers (queued 2026-10-24)

The review gives the cross-configuration background for staging. Jet–edge, cavity and mixing-layer–edge flows all show a three-stage staircase in fL/U (Fig. 8, p. 79–80), and the same jumps occur in edge, hole, ring and jet–wall tones (p. 80). At a frequency jump, two peaks in time-averaged spectra "may, in reality, be due to the intermittent switching from one mode to another" (Hussain & Zaman 1977; p. 81), which anticipates the coexistence-or-alternation question for multimodal screech (item 6). Hysteresis depends on the flow class (p. 81–82). Jet–edge and hole tones are hysteretic: the note's reading of Fig. 10 gives a loop about 500 wide in Re_d at L/d = 3.0. Cavities and wedges are not, and there the energy moves gradually from one frequency to the other through the jump. That bears on item 5, where free screech shows hysteresis only at C ↔ D ([Powell 1992](../Notes/powell-1992-oscillation-modes-choked-circular-jets.md), p. 2826, 2832). Fig. 11 (Stegen & Karamcheti 1970, p. 83–84) shows a stage-1 mode persisting into stage 2 with its own amplitude envelope. The authors call this persistence "essential" to explaining jumps and hysteresis; it matches Powell 1992's secondary tones and B–D link. The authors' recommendation, to follow the streamwise evolution of all spectral components (fundamental, subharmonic, harmonics) through a jump (p. 82), translates into SPOD and BMD along the shear layer and into up-and-down NPR sweeps in the PyFR A1/A2 continuation. No change to the current understanding, but it adds a classical precedent for both outcomes (gradual or abrupt) at a jump and for hysteresis being configuration dependent.

## zaman-1999-spreading-compressible-jets-nozzle-geometries (queued 2026-10-24)

- **Round-jet stage map.** For the 1.27 cm clear-plastic round convergent nozzle with a slightly rounded exit edge (Fig. 12 top, read):
  - A1 from M_j ≈ 1.09 to 1.16;
  - A2 at 1.17–1.18;
  - B (flapping) from 1.17 to 1.44;
  - C (helical) from 1.45 to 1.70;
  - D (flapping) from 1.68 to 1.79 (p. 213–214).
- **Equivalent NPRs** (note's isentropic conversions): A1 → A2 at ≈2.30–2.33, B → C at ≈3.4, C → D at ≈4.8–4.9. The A1/A2 point sits at the high end of the page's 2.17–2.27 range, like Powell's thick lip; lip thickness is not given.
- **Stage-dependent spreading.** Spreading changes discontinuously with stage: mass flux at x/D = 14 is highest in B, drops 35 % at B → C and is "relatively high" again in D (p. 214–215). Flux minima sit at stage jumps (M_j 1.16, 1.68, and 1.68 for the ellipse at B′/D′). The author puts the minima down to screech amplitude falling as a jump is approached (p. 216). Time-averaged data cannot distinguish that from alternation between loops.
- **Open questions posed in 1999.** The paper poses "why A1 → A2 when both are axisymmetric" and "why B′ → D′ with similar motion" as open (p. 214). The k_s1/k_s2 closure ([Nogueira 2021, A1/A2 closure]; [Edgington-Mitchell 2022]) now answers both, the second by the note's reading.
- **B-mode switching and B′/D′ overlap.** B is cited as switching between helical and flapping motion (Ponton & Seiner 1995, p. 213). The B′/D′ overlap at M_j 1.64–1.66 is reported only from time-averaged spectra, so coexistence against alternation is untested.
- **Strouhal caveat.** The plotted St values for B, C and D appear about 20 % below f D/U_j (from the paper's own Fig. 10 tone and from Sherman et al. and Pprime data), while A1 agrees. Use Figs 11–12 for stage boundaries, not absolute St.
- **Bearing on the PyFR staging LES.** Axisymmetric (A) screech does not raise integral entrainment at x/D = 14, but B onset does (+40 % between M_j 1.16 and 1.20). This is a cheap mean-flow check for the PyFR A1/A2 LES.
