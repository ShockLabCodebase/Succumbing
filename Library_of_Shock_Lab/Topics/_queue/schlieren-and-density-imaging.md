## mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets (queued 2026-10-20)

Mercier 2017 gives two measurement recipes that transfer to the lab.

**Thin-strip TR schlieren** (Table 3, p. 207–208, 212):
- Set-up: Phantom V12 on a Z-type system (two 200 mm f/8 mirrors 2.5 m apart, focused Cree XP-L LED, knife edge perpendicular to the jet axis at about 50% cut-off).
- The strip is only 16–24 px across y/D 0.44–0.55, by 640–1024 px over 4.2–4.9D, at 220–430 kfps, chosen by Veltin et al.'s rule of at least 15U_j/D_j. That is enough for lip-line phase and wavenumber at f_s.
- Wavenumber peaks over short (2–4 wavelength) windows are refined with a Gaussian window, ×2 zero padding and a three-point Gaussian fit (Gasior & Gonzalez; error below 1% of an 80 rad m⁻¹ bin).

This complements the page's long-record SA-Z table: a much faster rate on a thin strip, at the cost of SPOD capability. It does not change the current understanding.

## powis-2016-impingement-tones-shock-instabilities-plug-nozzle (queued 2026-10-22)

The paper documents an early group use of the Shimadzu HPV-1 (102-frame bursts) at two magnifications (Table 1, p. 4):
- 250 kfps over 4.4D × 3.7D at 0.22 mm/px, for the loop;
- 500 kfps over 1.6D × 1.4D at 0.081 × 0.085 mm/px, for the standoff shock;
- a PCO4000 (1 fps, 1.6 µs LED, 0.047 mm/px) for means from 242 frames.

All use the 2032 mm Z-type rig. This adds 2016 rows to the page's camera table, consistent with the 102-frame limit noted for Weightman 2019. The first **quantitative feature extraction from time-resolved schlieren in the library** is here (p. 4, 6). It runs Gaussian filter → Sobel edges → length filter → straight-line fit, giving the shock centroid and angle per frame; then an FFT of the centroid track per burst (frequency, mean of 20 bursts) and a sinusoidal fit (mean standoff, amplitude). It tracks motion normal to the plate but fails for tilt and in-plane motion, so helical cases were excluded (p. 4, 6). Path integration makes a rotating (helical) shock look like side-to-side motion (p. 6), the same single-view limitation the page records from edgington-mitchell-2023 and mazharmanesh-2025. The knife edge was set radial (∂ρ/∂y) in the impingement region, which the authors say improved contrast near the plate despite the strong axial gradients there (p. 4). An axial edge was used for the mean shock-cell image (Fig. 12). From the note: each 500 kfps burst spans only about two tone periods, a 4.9 kHz FFT bin, so the 20-burst ensemble and fit carry the estimate and "agreement within one σ" is a loose test. The paper's σ grows as the amplitude shrinks. A long-record camera (SA-Z) would turn the centroid into a time series for Hilbert-transform frequency and amplitude tracking. For helical cases, SPOD or SPOD → reconstruction → HPOD of the shock window would replace the scalar tracker. This belongs in "Choosing frame rate, exposure and record length" (burst length vs cycles) and in a short "feature tracking" item under "What the image measures" or "Pre-processing". Compare Knast 2018's Hough-transform x–t slopes and Rezay Haghdoost 2022's front tracking. Estruch 2008 (Canny), Diebold & Elliott 2014 and André 2011 (AIAA J. 49) are cited antecedents not in the library.

## mitchell-2012-visualization-acoustic-feedback-loop-impinging-jet-schlieren (queued 2026-10-23)

This is the group's first ultra-high-speed schlieren paper and the source the later group papers cite for the 2032 mm Z-type system ([Edgington-Mitchell 2014](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2014-coherent-structure-helical-screech-mode.md), p. 826). It belongs at the start of the "Ultra-high-speed bursts" paragraph, before Weightman 2019 and Knast 2018. It used two cameras with different jobs (p. 335). A PCO Dimax (2016 × 2016 px, 1 kfps, Phlatlight CBT-120 green LED) gave independent high-resolution images. A Shimadzu HPV-1 (312 × 260 px, up to 1 Mfps, used at 250 and 500 kfps) lit by a Metz Mecablitz photographic flash gave sequences. Exposure, sequence length and image counts are not stated. Both dρ/dr and dρ/dx knife edges were used (Fig. 3). One practice transfers: intensity sampled along a line on the outer edge of the shear layer, where path integration is weakest, and normalised by mean intensity, resolves a λ ≈ 0.05d disturbance that the full image hides (Fig. 6, p. 337–340). The paper also shows the limits of a composite of independent images (Fig. 3 assumes a repeating cycle, p. 337) and of short bursts (visualisation only, no frequency or convection speed). The lip transient (St_j ≈ 10 by the note's estimate) is far above the Nyquist limit of long-record SA-Z data at 150 kfps (St_j ≈ 2.8 for d = 15 mm), so burst cameras remain necessary for receptivity. This supports the page's point that record length and frame rate trade off, without changing its current understanding.

## johnston-2016-underexpanded-jet-intrusive-tab-visualisation (queued 2026-10-23)

An early (2014–15) group example of high-resolution, non-time-resolved schlieren: pco.dimax at 2016 × 2016 px, 740 px/D, up to 1279 fps, with an overdriven LED pulsed only while the shutter is open (Willert et al. 2012) (Table 1, p. 52); pulse length and optics are not given, as the topic page warns is common. For "Knife edge", it uses both ∂ρ/∂x and ∂ρ/∂r; the radial edge is chosen specifically because streamwise vortices show as bright streaks (Settles 2001; p. 52–54), the clearest being the tab vortex pair in the ∂ρ/∂r RMS image (Fig. 5). For "Views", it rotates only the nozzle tip to get two orthogonal views without disturbing the optics (p. 55), a two-view forerunner of [Mazharmanesh 2025](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md)'s 19-view rotation, and shows why: symmetric correlation patterns in both views are what separate an A-like mode from B flapping (p. 56–57). Two-point intensity correlation (eq. 1, p. 52) gives the dominant pattern and a wavelength, but the bands extend to r/D ≈ ±2 because the images capture the acoustic near field (p. 56; note's reading of Fig. 8), and a frequency needs an assumed U_c (Table 2), so it cannot show switching, as [Knast 2018](../Notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) (p. 6) also notes. It adds a snapshot feature-tracking method: streamwise low-pass filtering, then a brightest-pixel march from the tab tip with a growing window, accumulated over 5000 images into a probability map of the vortex path (p. 58–59). Reporting gaps: the image counts for the means and correlations and the second, larger field of view of Fig. 8 are not described.

## mason-smith-2015-underexpanded-jet-impinging-cylindrical-sections (queued 2026-10-23)

This paper is an early, fully documented example of the group's non-time-resolved schlieren practice (p. 613):
- **Set-up:** PCO Dimax, 12-bit, 20 px/mm; Phlatlight CBT-120 LED with 1.28 µs integration; two 200 mm mirrors of 2 m focal length; knife edge across the axis with 70% cut-off; 1000 images per case.
- **Statistics:** mean, RMS, single-pixel two-point correlation and a region-of-interest coefficient of variation. None needs time resolution (p. 614–621).

**"Views" paragraph.** Two orthogonal views of the convex cylinder (on-axis xy, off-axis xz) identified a fixed flapping plane. A precessing flapping mode seen in one view gives a weak, washed-out correlation map (p. 617). This is the cheap two-view counterpart of mazharmanesh-2025's 19-view rotation and a concrete case of item 3 (one view misses out-of-plane symmetry). The authors note that the correlated quantity is a path-integrated gradient, so the maps are a visualisation, not a flow property (p. 617).

**"What the image measures".** RMS images are used to separate shock motion from convected structures: a sharp mean shock means little motion, and bright shocks in the RMS image mean oscillating shocks (p. 614). The σ/μ of intensity in a 1d × 1d window beside the exit (0.2 concave against 0.06 convex, about 8 × 10⁷ samples) is a new scalar use of schlieren intensity. It depends on the knife edge, the cut-off and the path, so like SPOD eigenvalues it compares only within one set-up, and the authors say it cannot be converted to flow properties (p. 621).

**Note's addition for "Methods and data".** The axial band spacing in a non-TR correlation map gives the acoustic wavelength of the tone the images carry (≈ 1.9d at NPR 3.0 → St ≈ 0.56; ≈ 2.8–3d at NPR 3.4 → St ≈ 0.40). This is a cheap check that ensemble images and a microphone spectrum describe the same oscillation; here it suggests they may not for the convex NPR 3.0 case.

## edgington-mitchell-2015-staging-screeching-elliptical-jets (queued 2026-10-23)

A methods contribution from non-time-resolved high-resolution schlieren (PCO DiMax, 1.6 µs LED, 1000–2000 independent images per view; p. 1009, 1010, 1014): (i) mode classification by zero-lag two-point correlation in two orthogonal, separately acquired views, symmetric in both = varicose, antisymmetric in the minor-axis view only = flapping, weak antisymmetric in both = "helical" (eqs. 4–5, Figs 6–10, p. 1012–1017); (ii) vortex spacing from correlation-peak spacing (Table 1, p. 1018); (iii) near-field standing-wave spacing from lobes of intensity standard deviation outside the shear layer, validated against Panda's relation within 2–3 % (Table 2, p. 1018–1019); (iv) reference-point sensitivity: X on a standing-wave lobe gives strong far-reaching correlation, X between lobes almost none, but the derived spacing is unchanged (Fig. 11, p. 1019–1020). ∂ρ/∂y is used instead of ∂ρ/∂r because it does not change sign at the centreline (p. 1009). It is the same toolkit as [Knast 2018](../Notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md) and [Johnston 2016](../Notes/johnston-2016-underexpanded-jet-intrusive-tab-visualisation.md), and the std-lobe method is the precursor of Esfahani 2021b's log-std standing-wave maps. Limits: hand-picked X, non-simultaneous views (no helix test), shock-spacing method unstated, and frequency only from the microphone.
