## powis-2016-impingement-tones-shock-instabilities-plug-nozzle (queued 2026-10-22)

Powis et al. add a nozzle type to the thread: a cold, truncated conical plug nozzle (annular throat, design M 1.94, 1 mm lip) run overexpanded at NPR 3.4 against a 6D × 7D plate. Staged tones follow Powell's equation with θ = 2–6 and p = 0.85 (Fig. 8, p. 4–5), at most four at once and multimodal at z/D 3.9–4.1 and 5.2. In the −1 ≤ p ≤ 0 convention of weightman-2017 that is N = 3–7 with p = −0.15, one more value for the phase-lag spread (+0.4 henderson-2002, 0 ho-1981, −0.24/−0.49 weightman-2017). The paper gives no u_c; the note's back-calculation (h = z, c = 340 m/s) gives u_c ≈ 0.65–0.74 of the isentropic U_j, nearer the LES-type ≈ 0.6U_j of karami-2020 and bogey-2017 than the thesis PIV's 0.44–0.48U_j. The new result is that **no tones occur for z/D < 3.2**, where round convergent (Powell 1988, NPR 3.38, z/D ≤ 1.7) and C-D (Krothapalli 1999, NPR 3.7, z/D ≤ 2.0) nozzles resonate. The authors attribute this, untested, to the annular near field with its inner shear layer and plug recirculation (p. 5). That fits item 5 of the page (the nozzle and near field are boundary conditions of the loop; weightman-2019-nozzle-external-geometry-azimuthal-mode-impinging-jet, henderson-1993). With a conventional thin lip, the cause must lie in the source or path, not in receptivity (note's reasoning). The 250 kfps sequence at z/D 3.60 shows a toroidal structure hitting the plate and a sound wave returning to the lip, as in mitchell-2012 (p. 5). The note flags that the arrows imply ≈ 3a∞ if T = 95 µs, so the panel timing needs checking. On the source question, the 500 kfps shock tracking shows the **standoff shock oscillating at the tone frequency** (toroidal cases, z/D 3.56–5.0, within σ ≤ 10–15%; Fig. 10, p. 6) and normal to the plate. Helical cases move it sideways and tilt it (p. 5–6). The mean standoff s/D (0.43–0.55) follows the equivalent free jet's shock cells, supporting Henderson et al. 2005 (p. 6). Within a cell, a larger s goes with a smaller amplitude A/D (0.03–0.14); the authors propose, untested, a local small-plate (Hartmann-type) shock–plate loop (p. 6–7). The note points out that at equal s/D 0.47 the amplitude doubles from z/D 3.64 to 4.80, so s is not the controlling variable. At z/D 4.80 the shock **splits** into a staircase and briefly vanishes each cycle (Fig. 14, p. 7), like Henderson 2002 (JASA) and Panda 1998. This is image-level support for the multi-front picture at the standoff shock in weightman-2019-sound-production (leakage) and edgington-mitchell-2025 (split fronts), though no outgoing wave is tracked. Possible additions: the "Mode selection" section (nozzle geometry), "The feedback equation and the phase lag", "Where the upstream wave is generated" (standoff-shock motion and splitting), and the rocket-scale section. The paper's motivation is aerospike launch vehicles, and it cites Kumar 2008 and Gustavsson 2010 for tones surviving in hot jets (p. 2), which bears on the open question "Why full-scale pads show no tones". It changes no consensus item, but it adds an open question: why does the annular near field suppress tones for z/D < 3.2?

## mitchell-2012-visualization-acoustic-feedback-loop-impinging-jet-schlieren (queued 2026-10-23)

Mitchell, Honnery & Soria (2012) is the earliest group paper on impinging-jet resonance and the first time-resolved imaging of the loop. A 15 mm converging nozzle at NPR 3.2 fires at a flat plate at z/d 4, imaged with Shimadzu HPV-1 schlieren at 250–500 kfps (p. 335, 337). A composite of independent images (Fig. 3, p. 336–337) shows the four accepted stages, without spectra or an azimuthal measurement. The main result is for the "Receptivity at the lip" section. The arriving upstream wave produces a short-wavelength (λ ≈ 0.05d), sinusoid-like train of density-gradient bands that convect and die out by x/d ≈ 0.3. The vortex-ring precursor appears at their trailing edge at x/d ≈ 0.25 (Figs 5–6, p. 337–340). No slow undulation at the loop period is seen, which is the first experimental evidence against Ho & Nosseir's collective interaction in a supersonic impinging jet ([Ho 1981](../Notes/ho-1981-dynamics-impinging-jet-feedback-phenomenon.md), p. 135–139). It agrees with Karami 2020's LES (no collective interaction, the most-unstable St falling from ≈ 4 to 0.5 within 1d, little energy upstream of x ≈ 0.3–0.4d; p. 21–24) and with Karami's companion impulsive-receptivity LES as summarised in Edgington-Mitchell 2025 (p. 2). **Correction needed on the page:** the current sentence "The lip responds within about 2 µs of a wave's arrival ... (Mitchell et al. 2012, as cited in Weightman 2017, p. 2)" is Weightman 2017's reading (the 500 kfps frame interval). The printed paper shows frames 10 µs apart, states no delay, and its stated timings give bands within about 8 µs of arrival (Fig. 5, p. 337; note's derivation). Both bounds are small against a ≈ 90–150 µs loop, so the conclusion that the phase lag sits at the source stands. Two smaller points. The paper assumed reflections off the nozzle's mounting flange do not affect the loop (p. 337), which Weightman et al. 2019 later contradicted for their rig (the outer base is a necessary path, p. 434–441). And it takes the source from Henderson et al. 2005 without imaging it. The paper does not change the page's current understanding, but it gives the receptivity section its first direct observation, and its unresolved question (do the high-frequency transients feed vortex genesis or only mask it, p. 339) is a candidate for the open questions.

## mason-smith-2015-underexpanded-jet-impinging-cylindrical-sections (queued 2026-10-23)

Mason-Smith et al. are the group's only systematic change of impingement-surface shape. A 15 mm converging nozzle fires at a flat plate and at convex and concave half-cylinders of radius 2.5d, at h/d 3.5 and NPR 2.2–4.2 (p. 612–614). The paper is the source of the "cylinder locks the flapping axis" choice used in weightman-2017-phase-lag-supersonic-jet-impingement (p. 3), the weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets thesis (p. 29–30) and edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance (p. 6–7). In two views, the two-point correlation of 1000 schlieren images at NPR 3.4 is antisymmetric on-axis and symmetric off-axis, so the jet flaps in the plane normal to the cylinder axis; on the flat plate the flapping plane precesses (p. 617). Weightman 2017's stationary POD pair at NPR 3.6 confirms this independently (p. 7–8), and the tones agree to 1.3% (St f d/u_e 0.407 here against 0.402 there; note's arithmetic). For "Mode selection" and the open questions, the key new result is that a concave surface silences the jet for NPR 2.9–3.9 while flat and convex plates at the same h resonate (Fig. 6, p. 614). This zone of silence therefore does not depend on where the plate sits in the free-jet shock train, unlike Henderson's (p. 614, 618). It extends item 5 of the page (the loop's boundary conditions) from the nozzle end (weightman-2019-nozzle-external-geometry-azimuthal-mode-impinging-jet; henderson-1993 reflectors; powis-2016 plug) to the impingement end. The authors favour reduced shear-layer receptivity from the recirculated wall jet. Their evidence is σ/μ of entrainment-region intensity of 0.2 against 0.06 (Table 1, p. 620–621), but it was taken at a different condition (h/d 4.5, NPR 4.0) and does not measure thickness. Their own images show a steady stand-off structure with fluctuations confined to the shocks (p. 614), like the silent case of henderson-2002-flow-structure-supersonic-impinging-jets (p. 4), so a source-side mechanism (no plate-shock collapse, leakage or shocklet) fits equally well; the note argues this. Fig. 6 is also a staging map for "Staging, switching and frequency selection": branches at St ≈ 0.28, 0.40–0.43 and 0.55–0.65. The axisymmetric (NPR 3.0) to flapping (NPR 3.4) change coincides with a branch jump. Powell counts do not land cleanly on integers (up to about 0.5 off) whether h′ = h with u_con = 0.55U_j or Henderson & Powell's constants (p = 0, M_con,u 0.73, h + 0.8d) are used, the same looseness as henderson-1993 and ho-1981 (note's check). In the note's reading, the convex 0.278 branch is exactly half the flat-plate 0.56 branch (ratio 0.495–0.497) and the NPR 3.0 correlation maps carry the ≈ 1.9d acoustic band spacing of St 0.56. Together with the n × 0.401 + 0.265 sidebands in Fig. 10, this points to a subharmonic or quadratically interacting component, a new target for the "coexistence or switching" question. The band test repeats the thesis pattern on the page: the flat axisymmetric tone at NPR 3.0 has St_j ≈ 0.46, inside the (0, 2) band, and the flapping tones at NPR 3.4 have St_j ≈ 0.32–0.34, 9–13% below (1, 1) (tam-1990 note bands). For "Why full-scale pads show no tones", a concave surface that turns the exhaust back towards the nozzle is a flame-trench analogue that removes the tones.

## weightman-2018-signatures-shear-layer-unsteadiness-pod (queued 2026-10-23)

For the thin-lip NPR 3.4, z/D 5 flat-plate case (the same case as [weightman-2019-nozzle-external-geometry-azimuthal-mode-impinging-jet]), the paper argues for a single helical mode whose amplitude varies, not switching. The evidence: one POD pair with 33.7% of the energy, with antisymmetric axial components; no symmetric pair in 20 modes; one acoustic peak; a spectrogram with a nearly constant tone frequency (p. 6, 9). This bears on the page's "Coexistence or switching of tones" question only for the thin lip; the thick-lip coexistence question remains open. The per-snapshot helical amplitude correlates with shear-layer thickness from the first shock reflection (x/D ≈ 1.4) onward, with a 0.12D (27%) spread of δ_ω between quintiles at x/D = 3.6 (p. 8). Weak-amplitude snapshots have nearly straight vorticity sheets; strong ones meander, with large vorticity packets at x/D ≈ 2.4–3.2 (p. 9). Since leakage location depends on vortex amplitude ([edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage], p. 28–30; thesis p. 112–117), STM- or r₁₂-conditioned phase averages are a ready non-time-resolved way to test how the source moves with amplitude in this case (suggestion). The paper also shows the same mode as mode 10 of the cylinder dataset of [weightman-2017-phase-lag-supersonic-jet-impingement] (Fig. 1b, p. 3), and notes that the impinging jet's turning wall flow inflates the STM's transverse energy (p. 12). Shock reflection points at x/D ≈ 1.4 and 2.8 (p. 8) agree with free-jet spacings at this NPR (1.25–1.37D in [tan-2017-broadband-velocity-fluctuations-screeching-jets] and [edgington-mitchell-2014-coherent-structure-helical-screech-mode]). The page's statement that "No paper in this thread uses SPOD, BMD or HPOD" still holds.

## edgington-mitchell-2019-aeroacoustic-resonance-review (queued 2026-10-23)

- *Classical history* (p. 140–144):
  - Powell 1988's small-plate (stand-off-shock, Hartmann-type) and large-plate classes and eq. 16 with phase lag p.
  - Henderson & Powell 1993: 3 ≤ N ≤ 8, L1 jumping 4 → 6 cycles at NPR 2.70 and 4 → 5 at 2.36; reflector cancellation proves nozzle–plate feedback.
  - Henderson & Powell 1996: small-plate tones are reflector-insensitive, a plate–stand-off-shock loop. Kuo & Dowling 1996's entropy-wave linear model.
  - Henderson 2002: stand-off-shock "collapse", zones of silence.
  - Krothapalli 1999: a phase lag is needed even in a near-design jet.
  - Henderson, Bridges & Wernet 2005: the 1.3D wall-jet source, and recirculation does not correlate with tones.
  - The page treats Powell's small-plate class and the Kuo & Dowling entropy loop only through [Henderson 1993](../Notes/henderson-1993-tones-choked-jet-impinging-flat-plates.md); the review adds them as mechanisms.
- *Group results* (p. 144–148, 165–167, 171–175):
  - UHS schlieren of a full cycle (Fig. 17, NPR 2.3, h/D 2.66, m = 0).
  - The wall-jet shocklet as the phase lag ([Weightman 2017](../Notes/weightman-2017-phase-lag-supersonic-jet-impingement.md)), but "not universal": stand-off-shock-edge sources in other cases, and Gojon et al. 2016's impingement-region source needing no phase lag. Hence "at least two different" generation mechanisms (p. 166–167).
  - Free-stream waves seen closing the loop at the lip (Fig. 38, previously unpublished, NPR 3.1, z/D 2.66), alongside Gojon/Bogey's LES k_TH⁻ evidence, "the strongest evidence to date" for guided closure (p. 171–172).
  - No evidence of Ho & Nosseir's collective interaction in group UHS schlieren or LES (Fig. 39; p. 174).
- This is the source-location-decides-closure view later formalised by [Edgington-Mitchell 2025](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md) and Nogueira 2024, so it confirms items 1, 2, 3 and 5 without changing them.
- It also adds:
  - convex cylinders lock flapping normal to their axis, and concave surfaces sometimes suppress tones (p. 147–148);
  - inclined plates give many feedback path lengths (p. 147);
  - steady microjets have more authority over helical than toroidal impinging instabilities (p. 152);
  - Gojon & Bogey 2017's intermittent tone strength in shock-containing impinging LES (p. 147), relevant to "Coexistence or switching of tones".

## raman-1999-supersonic-jet-screech-half-century-powell-present (queued 2026-10-23)

Raman 1999 links screech to edge tones explicitly (p. 555): when one shock dominates the radiation, the amplitude (directivity) criterion drops out, only the phase criterion remains, and screech becomes an edge tone with the nozzle-to-edge distance replaced by h = (N + p)s, N an integer and p < 1. The note checks that this reproduces Powell's screech formula for any N + p, the same structure as the [Henderson 1993](../Notes/henderson-1993-tones-choked-jet-impinging-flat-plates.md) and [Jordan 2018](../Notes/jordan-2018-jet-flap-interaction-tones.md) feedback equations; as in screech, later edge-tone and impinging work replaced free-stream return by guided jet modes (Jordan 2018; [Tam 1990](../Notes/tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md)). Other relevant items: Lepicovsky & Ahuja's near-field standing wave in subsonic edge tones (p. 554); Nagel et al.'s reflector at odd multiples of λ/4 cancelling screech via a pressure minimum at the exit (p. 557), which parallels the reflector tests of Henderson 1993; "induced screech", a supersonic edge tone set up by obstacles beside an on-design rectangular C-D jet, giving about 30 % more mass flux at a thrust penalty of almost 20 %, reduced by shaping the obstacles (p. 562), with NRL Euler FCT simulations matching frequency, amplitude and thrust loss (p. 563); and the distinction between the Hartmann whistle (a tone, slight heating) and the Hartmann–Sprenger tube (violent oscillation, very high temperature at the closed end), which depends on tube length (p. 562–563). This is supporting history; it does not change the page's current understanding.

## krothapalli-1999-flow-field-noise-supersonic-impinging-jet (queued 2026-10-23)

This is the ideally expanded (NPR 3.7, M_d 1.5 C-D, M_j 1.50) supersonic impinging-jet data set whose tones [Bogey 2017](../Notes/bogey-2017-feedback-loop-upwind-waves-ideally-expanded-impinging-jets.md) reproduces, and the origin of the "p = −0.4" that the page so far quotes only second-hand. The exit is baffled by a flush lift plate 10d across.
- **Phase-lag argument.** Single-snapshot PIV gives C_i/U_clm = 0.52 at h/d = 4, rising linearly to 0.56 at h/d = 10 (0.60 for the free jet), and higher with shock cells: 0.56–0.59 at NPR 2.5 and 5.0 (Fig. 10, p. 167). With these speeds, Powell's equation with p = 0 misses the staged tones, and p = −0.4 fits them at NPR 3.7 and 5.0 (Figs 20–21, p. 176–178).
  - The authors reject Henderson & Powell's stand-off-shock source, because at NPR 3.7 there is none (smooth centreline, Fig. 13a; p. 177). They attribute the lag to receptivity at a lip that is also a reflecting plate, but concede that p is a best fit that cannot be separated from convection-speed error (p. 178).
- **The note's re-reading of Fig. 20.** The fractional part of N + p for 33 dominant tones has a circular mean of −0.42, with about ±0.2-stage scatter. N + p also creeps upwards within each stage, i.e. frequency squeezing as in [Ho 1981](../Notes/ho-1981-dynamics-impinging-jet-feedback-phenomenon.md).
  - A C_i about 14 % lower (0.45–0.48U, the range of [Weightman et al. 2019](../Notes/weightman-2019-nozzle-external-geometry-azimuthal-mode-impinging-jet.md)) would make p = 0 work. Bogey 2017's LES fits the same tones with p = 0, u_c of 0.58–0.62u_j and different N. So p, N and u_c trade off.
  - This strengthens the page's "The phase lag" open question. Here p ≈ −0.4 appears with no stand-off shock, leakage or shocklet. Read as a delay (0.4 cycle = 70–175 µs), it is 2–6 times the shocklet's 29–40 µs ([Weightman 2017](../Notes/weightman-2017-phase-lag-supersonic-jet-impingement.md)), so a negative p is not by itself evidence for the shocklet.
  - A cheap test: compare Bogey's standing-wave cell count N with the N assigned here.
- **Mode selection.** Shadowgraphs show helical oscillation at h/d ≈ 8 and 3 and axisymmetric at h/d ≈ 4–6 (p. 163). The dominant-tone St values (the note's conversion, f D_j/U_j) cluster at 0.35–0.38 (axisymmetric heights), 0.25–0.30 (helical heights) and 0.14–0.20.
  - This matches Bogey 2017's alternating bands and the group's 0.25 and 0.385 families. The lift plate is in effect an infinite-lip baffle, comparable to the thesis t/D 7 cap.
- **Other points.**
  - The source is placed in the stagnation region by front curvature (Fig. 4a, p. 164), consistent with an in-core vortex–wall source ([Edgington-Mitchell 2025](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2025-vortices-shocks-nonlinear-waves-impinging-jet-resonance.md)).
  - There are no tone-free gaps over h/d_j 1.8–9.2 (no zones of silence in an ideally expanded jet, as [Edgington-Mitchell 2019](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2019-aeroacoustic-resonance-review.md) remarks), and tones persist down to h/d_j ≈ 1.8.
  - Duct-mode standing waves form between the lift plate and the ground (Fig. 4b, p. 164, 178).
  - Impingement raises near-field OASPL by about 8 dB (Fig. 22, p. 178), relevant to the launch-scale section.
- **Effect on the page.** It does not change the current understanding. It supplies first-hand page references for the −0.4 value and the tone set used to validate the LES.

## tam-1992-impingement-tones-large-aspect-ratio-rectangular-jets (queued 2026-10-23)

Tam & Norum carry the guided-mode closure of [Tam & Ahuja 1990](../Notes/tam-1990-theoretical-model-discrete-tone-impinging-jets.md) to large-aspect-ratio (AR 4.24) supersonic rectangular jets on a wall, using Norum's (1989) data at M_j 1.16–1.70. They show that the 20–28 tones in each spectrum are sums and differences of just two basic tones, a symmetric (varicose) s and a higher antisymmetric (flapping) a, plus sidebands of a 0.6 kHz facility tone, and strobe-locked schlieren shows both loops running in the same jet at the same plate distance (p. 305–308; e.g. s = 2.5 kHz, a = 6.4 kHz at M_j 1.29, L/H 11.05, both ≈ 154 dB). This is the earliest library evidence for coexisting impingement loops plus nonlinear combination tones, the mechanism the group's thesis invokes for its 0.144 = 0.385 − 0.241 difference tone ([Weightman et al. 2019](../Notes/weightman-2019-nozzle-external-geometry-azimuthal-mode-impinging-jet.md), p. 430, 433) and that Bogey 2017's sum tone also shows. The planar vortex-sheet bands of the upstream neutral modes bound the measured tones "within (or almost within)" at all seven M_j (Fig. 11, p. 310), but the note finds the test one-sided: the first symmetric band runs from zero, so only its upper (zero-group-velocity) limit constrains, and from M_j 1.49 the symmetric tones press against it (up to 5 % above, one point 11 %), the same pattern as round tones dying at S(0,1) in [Jaunet 2019](../Notes/jaunet-2019-dynamics-round-jet-impingement.md). The antisymmetric tones sit in the lower part of the first antisymmetric band, near the branch point where the upstream phase speed is −a∞, so frequencies alone do not separate guided from free-stream closure (as the Bogey 2017 note found for round jets). By the note's check, the first-band model requires a/s > ≈ 1.4; Fig. 3 (s = 5.3, a = 6.7 kHz, a/s 1.26) violates it, so at least one of its basic tones lies outside the first bands, a counterexample the paper does not discuss. The paper supports the "guided closure" side of the open question only through band membership; it has no source location or wavenumber split. The flapping tone (St = fH/u_j ≈ 0.20–0.21 at M_j 1.29) lies where free-jet flapping screech of this nozzle would be (Tam 1988 formula, the note's estimate), echoing Henderson & Powell's remark that some Norum stages sit near screech (Henderson 1993, p. 312), and paralleling the unexplored round "helical impinging tone ≈ C-mode screech" coincidence on this page.
