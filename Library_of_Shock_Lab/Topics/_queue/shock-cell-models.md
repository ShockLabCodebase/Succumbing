## edgington-mitchell-2019-aeroacoustic-resonance-review (queued 2026-10-23)

The review restates the product picture: Tam & Tanna's KH × shock weak interaction (eq. 10), its bandwidth narrowing towards upstream (eq. 11), and screech as the θ → 180° limit (p. 127–128). It cites Tam, Parrish & Viswanathan 2014 for harmonic directivity from nonlinear instability-wave × shock terms, and Lele 2005 for the equivalence of the wave model and distributed sources (p. 139–140).

Its main contribution to this thread is that real shocks are unsteady. Their motion is phase-locked and mode-dependent (axial in A, transverse in flapping, circular in helical, with tilting and core distortion), and they split at the 3rd and 4th cells (Figs 11–12). The steady spacing may therefore not be the effective spacing (p. 137–138). It shows coherent energy as monotonic growth modulated by the shocks or the standing wave (Fig. 27), and coherent axial length scales modulated much more than transverse or random-part ones (Fig. 28; [Tan 2017](../Notes/tan-2017-broadband-velocity-fluctuations-screeching-jets.md); p. 157–159). These are data for "Shock modulation of the wavepacket".

In 2019 no model of that modulation existed (p. 177). It is consistent with item 3 (screech damps the mean train), since an oscillating, splitting train averages to a weaker mean; no change.

## raman-1999-supersonic-jet-screech-half-century-powell-present (queued 2026-10-23)

Raman 1999 summarises the linear shock-cell models (p. 550–551): Prandtl–Pack, with Powell's 1995 remark that the Prandtl wavelength is shorter than the diamond pattern of choked jets; Tam, Jackson & Seiner's multiple-scales model matching Norum & Seiner ([Norum 1982](../Notes/norum-1982-mean-static-pressure-far-field-acoustics-shock-containing-jets.md)); and the rectangular vortex-sheet models of [Tam 1988](../Notes/tam-1988-shock-cell-screech-rectangular-non-axisymmetric-jets.md) (eigenfunction expansion) and Morris, Bhat & Chen 1989 (boundary elements, any cross-section), both giving a good average spacing although a linearly perturbed jet "does not have the same structure as a choked jet". Fig. 4a (p. 551) compares both with Raman & Rice's rectangular-jet spacings: by the note's reading S/h ≈ 1.3–1.63 at M_j ≈ 1.26, ≈ 2.2–2.33 at 1.44 and ≈ 2.7–3.1 at 1.56 (shocks 1–5), with the scatter across shocks growing with M_j; Tam's line passes S/h = 1 at M_j ≈ 1.14 and 4 at ≈ 1.73. Raman's call for a "second-generation model" capturing non-uniform spacing, strength and structure after Mach-disk formation (p. 551) is what the zero-frequency PSE/PFE and measured-train approaches on the page later supplied ([Nogueira 2022, PFE](../Notes/nogueira-2021-wavepacket-modulation-shock-containing-jets.md)). The hot-film trace of Fig. 3 (M_j 1.44) shows the screech-mode amplitude dipping near shocks spaced ≈ 2.2h (note's reading); Raman calls this nonlinear, but PFE reproduces shock modulation linearly except where an upstream wave forms a standing wave (that note, p. 17), and [Edgington-Mitchell 2021, waves](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md) (p. 14–23) attributes most core modulation to guided-mode–KH interference. Glaznev's Soviet observation that tone amplitude "changed abruptly at the shock locations" (p. 548) is the earliest report of the modulation. Consistent with current understanding items 1–2 and 8.

## seiner-1988-dynamic-pressure-loads-twin-supersonic-plume-resonance (queued 2026-10-23)

A twin-plume (s/D 1.9) mean shock spacing from centreline static-pressure traverses: L/D = 2.06M_j − 1.6, said to be 10–15 % longer than the single choked jet of Seiner, Dash & Wolf 1985, by "shock wave stretching caused by mutual interaction" (p. 958–959). The only twin-plume spacing correlation in the library. Fig. 13 (M_j 1.42, figure reading): about six strong cells, spacing falling from ≈ 1.4D to ≈ 1.15D, mean ≈ 1.25D, against 1.325D from Eq. 5 and 1.40D from Prandtl–Pack. This is consistent with item 2 (cells shrink downstream) and with mean spacings sitting below Pack's first-cell value. Eq. 5 is 1–7 % below Pack over M_j 1.2–1.6 (note's arithmetic), so the paper's claim that Pack-type models "severely underestimate" the twin spacing must refer to mean-spacing predictions. It doesn't change the page's understanding, but it adds a coupling effect (longer cells in close twins) that no shock-cell model on the page covers. Whether Fig. 13 is a twin or single traverse is not stated.
