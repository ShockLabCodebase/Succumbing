## jordan-2013-wave-packets-turbulent-jet-noise (queued 2026-10-22)

The review gives the cleanest statement of the mean-flow hypothesis the page opens with: Crighton & Gaster's "equivalent laminar flow profile" lacks a rigorous scale-separation proof, and the support is empirical (p. 181). Its argument for why linear works — the turbulent mean spreads much faster than a laminar layer, so the total gain at fixed frequency is small and nonlinearity is already in the mean (p. 181–182) — is the same as [Michalke 1984](../Notes/michalke-1984-survey-jet-instability-theory.md)'s divergence-capped gain (p. 190–191), and it explains why initially laminar or strongly forced jets need nonlinear models (Bradshaw 1966; Crow & Champagne 1971). It lays out the model hierarchy (locally parallel LSA with at most one unstable family per m in subsonic unheated jets, Mattingly & Chang 1974; energy-integral and multiple-scales extensions; PSE; global and boundary-value problems; p. 182–183) and restricts itself to convective instability (p. 182), consistent with [Huerre & Monkewitz 1990](../Notes/huerre-1990-local-global-instabilities-spatially-developing-flows.md). The evidence summarised (Suzuki & Colonius 2006 array vs quasi-parallel LSA; [Gudmundsson & Colonius 2011](../Notes/gudmundsson-2011-instability-wave-models-near-field-jets.md) PSE vs array POD; Cavalieri et al. 2012b = [Cavalieri 2013](../Notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md) PSE vs TR-PIV; Kerhervé et al. 2012b LSE-educed LES wavepacket vs LST at x/D 3, St 0.35; p. 183–187) confirms current-understanding point 1, and Fig. 8a shows the St 0.2, m = 0 shortfall later traced to the Orr mechanism. Its caution that laminar/transitional simulations (Mohseni 2002, M 1.92, Re ≈ 2000; Cheung & Lele 2009; Suponitsky 2010/2011, nonlinear difference modes dominating) may not transfer to turbulent jets (p. 183–184), and Crighton & Gaster's warning that apparent wavenumbers and growth rates depend on variable and position (p. 183), are worth a sentence in "Why mean-flow models work at all". It adds no guided-wave, Floquet or shock content. Suggested Papers line: "founding wavepacket review; mean-flow hypothesis unproved; linear LSA/PSE match to the core end; intermittency open."

## wong-2019-coherence-decay-wavepacket-bbsan (queued 2026-10-22)

- A kinematic wavepacket model in the Cavalieri line-source family ([cavalieri-2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), §3), extended to wavepacket–shock products.
- It is the BBSAN counterpart of the review's result that jitter matters little for supersonic Mach waves. The difference wave k_h − k_s plays the role of the wavepacket wavenumber. Where it is supersonic, jitter attenuates; where it is subsonic, it amplifies (p. 980–987; note's closed-form check).
- The conclusion (p. 988) is a stated requirement for dynamic linear models of BBSAN. They must be forced stochastically to produce jittering solutions with coherence decay, and must carry several shock-cell modes.
- [wong-2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md) met the second requirement and imposed LES coherence on PSE. A stochastically forced resolvent or Floquet operator for BBSAN is still untested, which could be added to the page's open questions.
- The paper also cites Sasaki et al. (2017a) to argue that linear theory holds to St = 4, so the high-frequency deficit is not a linear-theory failure (p. 980).

## lu-2026-rectangular-jet-screech-dimensional-reduction (queued 2026-10-22)

- **Where it goes.** The paper belongs in "Floquet in azimuth / rectangular jets" (or a new "dimensional reduction" bullet) and in "Guided-jet waves… rectangular-jet screech".
- **Method.** It is an inviscid, Cartesian 2D cross-plane LSA: Chebyshev with mapping, quarter domain with symmetry BCs, `eigs`, α² terms dropped to keep L q̂ = αF q̂ (p. 4, 21–22). It is a counterpart to the cylindrical Floquet-in-azimuth model of [Nogueira 2025](../Notes/nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles.md).
- **KH results.** The KH most-amplified St_h is independent of AR (≈ 0.41 at NPR 2.1 → 0.31 at NPR 5.0, p. 8), consistent with Morris & Bhat. Doubling δ halves the unstable bandwidth (p. 13).
- **Competing KH branch.** At AR 4–6 a second flapping KH branch with three spanwise pressure nodes runs within 0.03 % in α_r and about 1–2 % in α_i of the leading one (αD = 14.7446 − 2.9202i vs 14.7419 − 2.9583i at AR 6, NPR 4.5, St_h 0.45; p. 11). A planar model cannot separate them.
- **GJM band edges.** The saddle (GJM–duct merger) is robust to the reduction, the branch point (sonic-line detachment) is not (p. 16–17). This links to the page's branch-point/saddle labels and to [Nogueira 2024](../Notes/nogueira-2024-guided-jet-waves.md), whose oblique waves are the paper's higher-order GJMs (p. 16–17).
- **Shear-layer thickness.** Appendix C finds 2D band edges nearly insensitive to δ/h 0.1–0.2 within the tanh family (p. 25), yet strongly changed by the PIV profile (p. 19). This nuances the page's claim that shear-layer thickness controls the bands ([Mancinelli 2021](../Notes/mancinelli-2021-complex-valued-resonance-model-screech.md)): for a rectangular jet the cross-sectional shape (relaxed AR) may matter as much as thickness.
- **Open question on thick layers and heating.** This adds that the rectangular branch point depends on dimensional modelling. The tanh model also cannot reach realistic thicknesses without a subsonic centreline (p. 5, 18).
- **Methods and data.** Add that a planar model is adequate for KH-only uses for AR ≥ 2–4, but not for GJM bands.
- **Verification point.** The GJM convergence test varied resolution but not the 40D domain (p. 24), although the branch-point mode reaches the far field (p. 17). Note it as a numerical question.

## jaunet-2017-two-point-coherence-wave-packets-turbulent-jets (queued 2026-10-23)

This is the two-point companion to [Cavalieri 2013](../Notes/cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets.md) on the same M 0.4 Pprime jet. It measures what linear models assume away: by construction a linear mode has unit coherence (p. 1, 17).
- **Linear region = long coherence.** Inside the (x, St) region where PSE matched PIV (a polygon from (x/D, St) ≈ (1.2–2, 0.3–0.7) to (4.5–7, 0.3–0.7), read from Fig. 16), the m = 0 wavepacket's exponential coherence length is far longer than that of the turbulence: up to ≈ 1.9D, or ≈ 12δω, against ≈ 3δω for the eddies. The peak ΔL ≈ 1.5D is near x/D ≈ 2, St ≈ 0.4–0.45 (p. 14–17).
- **Beyond the core.** Past x/D ≈ 5, where linear theory fails, the m = 0 coherence length collapses on the same δω scaling as the energetic eddies (L/δω ≈ 3–3.7). The authors read this as nonlinear wavepacket–turbulence interaction (p. 17).
- **What this settles.** This answers the open choice in Cavalieri 2013 (p. 580–581, 587 there) in favour of loss of coherence downstream, rather than a coherent packet hidden by a low energy share.
- **Link to operator results (my inference; neither paper makes it).**
  - The ΔL peak at St ≈ 0.4–0.45 coincides with the resolvent gain-separation and preferred-mode peak ([Garnaud 2013](../Notes/garnaud-2013-preferred-mode-incompressible-jets.md), St ≈ 0.43–0.46; [Cavalieri 2019](../Notes/cavalieri-2019-wave-packet-models-jet-dynamics-sound-radiation.md), p. 22).
  - The polygon's lower edge, St ≈ 0.3, coincides with the m = 0 low-rank cut-off and KH → Orr transition of [Schmidt 2018](../Notes/schmidt-2018-spectral-analysis-jet-turbulence.md) and [Pickering 2020](../Notes/pickering-2020-lift-up-kh-orr-jets.md).
  - So long coherence, success of linear models and large gain separation mark the same region. That strengthens claim 1 on the page ("works, but only statistically") and gives it a quantitative edge.
- **Asymmetry and phase speed.** The wavepacket coherence is asymmetric, 1.5–2.2 times shorter downstream at x/D = 4, St 0.4–0.6 (p. 18). At low St near the nozzle its phase speed reaches 0.8 to just over 1.0U_j, as linear theory predicts (p. 20–21). Both are statistics a stochastic-forcing operator model, P_qq = RP_ffR^H, should reproduce. No paper in the library has tested this.
- **Suggestion for the page:** a short "two-point statistics" paragraph under "Why mean-flow models work at all" or "Wavepacket models", and a line in Papers.
