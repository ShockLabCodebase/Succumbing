## wong-2020-azimuthal-decomposition-radiated-noise-shock-containing-jets (queued 2026-10-22)

This paper is the primary description and qualification of the group's SJAF anechoic chamber (p. 2016–2018, 2023–2025).
- **The chamber and rig.** The chamber is 1.5 × 1.2 × 1.4 m wedge tip to tip, with 400 mm wedges, a 500 Hz nominal cut-on and a lined floor. It runs continuously up to NPR 5 from a 5 m³ reservoir shared with the supersonic wind tunnel. NPR is set with a Fairchild 100 regulator and held to about ±1%. The nozzle is an 8 mm convergent with a 0.5 mm lip and a contraction ratio of 10.
- **Claim 4 needs qualifying.** It says "The group's jet rigs are not anechoic". That holds for the optical rigs (edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage, p. 4), but not for SJAF.
- **Cut-on and contraction.** SJAF's cut-on (500 Hz) is well above that of mayer-2019-bristol-aeroacoustic-wind-tunnel (160 Hz) and of the Syracuse and OSU rooms (150–160 Hz). Its contraction ratio (10) belongs in the page's contraction list, which has 81, 93 and >100 for the other group rigs. The edgington-mitchell-2021 note (p. 5) already suspects a thicker exit boundary layer in this nozzle.
- **Qualification.** An ISO 3745 inverse-square test used a 45 mm speaker at 500, 750, 5000 and 12 000 Hz along 60°, 90° and 140°, at about 12.5–75D (App. A, Fig. 7, p. 2024–2025). The paper calls it within tolerance except for slight deviations near the source at 140°, blamed on upstream piping (p. 2024).
- **The note's reading of the qualification.** Fig. 7 shows points out of tolerance at 12.5D (90°, 5 and 12 kHz) and 25D (140°, 12 kHz; 500 Hz off-scale). The test stops at 12 kHz, below the BBSAN band at roughly 15–74 kHz (derived with an assumed T₀ ≈ 293 K). The midfield ring at R ≈ 11–14D is therefore not formally qualified.
- **Acquisition practice** (p. 2018, 2024): G.R.A.S. 46BE 1/4-in. microphones; 2 s at 200 kHz; 80 kHz low-pass; mean and linear trend removed; N_fft 2048 with a Hann window; 974 blocks, which implies about 80% overlap (not stated); dB/St scaled with U_j/D_j.
- **Azimuthal method** (App. C, p. 2024–2026). One microphone is fixed and one is traversed in 10° steps over ±90°. Re and Im of R_pp are fitted with sinc-type forms and Fourier transformed in azimuth. It is a cheap alternative to an array (Suzuki & Colonius 2006). It assumes stationarity, so it cannot track switching in time, and the sinc fit shapes the modal spectrum (note's caution).
- **Re and later use.** Re is 2.8–4.5 × 10⁵, in the transitional range (p. 2018). Later group papers use this facility: wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan, edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage, stavropoulos-2023-axisymmetric-screech-round-twin-jets-lsa and nogueira-2025-screech-military-style-rectangular-nozzles. The page could list it under "Anechoic facilities".

## weightman-2018-signatures-shear-layer-unsteadiness-pod (queued 2026-10-23)

This is the published source of the thesis PIV settings the page quotes: Imperx B6640, 11.8 µm/px, final window 24 px = 0.019D with 6 px (0.005D) spacing, ΔT = 884 ns, DOF 0.55 mm against a ≈ 1 mm sheet, 18 µm (1.5 px) particle images, ViCount smoke ≈ 600 nm from τ_p ≈ 2 µs (Mitchell et al. 2013; Melling 1997), 10,000 pairs, multigrid 192 → 24 px, median 3.0 / dynamic-mean 1.0 validation (p. 4–5, Table 2). This paper shows why, and its cost: the v basis suppresses the amplitude-linked STM (p. 12–14). ## mitchell-2011-particle-relaxation-piv-cross-correlation (queued 2026-10-24)

This is the source of the "noiseless cross-correlation" particle-response model that the topic page already uses second-hand. Its Table 2 predictions appear in [mitchell-2013] (p. 5) and [edgington-mitchell-2014-coherent-structure-helical-screech-mode] (p. 829), and the Mach-disk paper's "≥ 8%" also comes from it. The page currently attributes the τ_p = 2.0 µs smoke value to "Mitchell et al. 2013, not in the library"; that paper is now indexed as mitchell-2013-near-field-structure-underexpanded-elliptic-jets, and this one supplies the method behind it.

What it adds:
1. **Polydisperse seed relaxes more slowly than its median particle.** PIV weights each particle by d², so log-normal seed with the same 0.8 µm geometric mean as monodisperse seed is measured as relaxing much more slowly (Fig. 4, p. 938–939). The paper therefore calls τ_p from a nominal diameter "of little value" and urges in-situ measurement. This agrees with Ragni's fourfold PTV spread [ragni-2011-particle-tracer-response-shocks-piv] (p. 63), and Aerosil R974 silica at τ_p = 2.1 µs (p. 944) sits with Ragni's stored powders (2.4–3.3 µs) and the group's later 2.0 µs smoke.
2. **A shock inside a window creates an artificial shot-to-shot fluctuation, not just a mean bias.** Where the correlation peak lands depends on the random particle positions. With the shock mid-window the artefact grows monotonically with SR = IW/ξ_p, reaching 0.32U at M 3 and SR 10, and polydispersity lowers it (Fig. 8, p. 941–942). With the shock at the window's upstream edge it peaks at intermediate SR (≈0.07–0.15U at M 3) and goes to zero as SR → ∞, and polydispersity raises it (Fig. 12, p. 942–944).
3. **Ideal deformation helps only monodisperse seed.** An idealised image-shift deformation removes most of the monodisperse artefact but little of the polydisperse one (Fig. 13, p. 943–944).
4. **Measured artefacts.** In a jet, u′_rms ≈ 0.35U_e behind a stable Mach disc (NPR 2.5) and ≈ 0.1U_e behind oblique shocks, confirmed as not shock motion by 1 MHz schlieren (Figs 14–15, p. 944–946).

**Reconciling with Ragni.** The paper's prescription (SR ≫ 10, with the shock on a window edge) appears opposite to Ragni's no-bias criterion (window ≤ ξ_p, i.e. SR ≤ 1, p. 56). The two address different errors: Ragni the measured relaxation profile, Mitchell the spurious fluctuation, which is small at SR ≤ 1. The group's working SR ≈ 0.4–0.75 sits in the low-artefact corner, which is why the later measured artefacts are only 3–10%. This should be stated on the page, since it currently cites only Ragni's criterion.

**Open questions it bears on.** It strengthens the page's open question on shock-associated random stress (Tan 2019a anti-phase coherent/random k at reflections). The artefact is random per shot but could be phase-modulated if the shocks move with the tone. It also suggests a practical tool: the mean correlation-coefficient map locates normal and oblique shocks (Fig. 16, p. 945–946) and could generate shock masks automatically. The paper concludes that turbulence statistics in shock-containing windows are "essentially impossible" by PIV (p. 946), a stronger statement than the page's current wording ("shocks still add artificial fluctuation").

## prasad-2000-stereoscopic-piv-review (queued 2026-10-25)

- **What it adds.** Prasad 2000 is the page's first general reference on stereo PIV, and it supplies the error budget that the "Calibration, stereo and stitching" section lacks.
  - Planar PIV carries a perspective error w tan θ at off-axis angle θ: about 10% at a 5° field edge when the in- and out-of-plane displacements are similar (eq. 2, p. 104).
  - A stereo pair removes that error. Its out-of-plane to in-plane error ratio is 1/tan θ for both translation and Scheimpflug systems: 3–4 at the θ ≈ 15° typical of translation rigs, and 1 at 45° (eq. 9, p. 105, 110–111).
- **Agreement with existing notes.** The θ ≈ 45° Alkislar et al. 2003 rig (`alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv`, about 90° between the camera axes, p. 126–127) sits at the error-balanced angle. The Pprime cross-planes of `cavalieri-2013-wavepackets-velocity-field-turbulent-jets` use Willert's either-side, forward-scatter arrangement (p. 107–108).
- **Calibration methods.** The review classifies reconstruction as geometric, 2-D calibration or 3-D calibration (Soloff et al. 1997; three or more target planes, no geometry needed; p. 111–115) and states the field's trend: Scheimpflug, digital cameras and calibration-based reconstruction (p. 115).
- **The open problem it leaves.** No study of target–sheet misalignment existed. Willert (1997) found that a rotation below 0.6° gave 10 px, i.e. 1 mm, of misregistration even though the grids overlapped to better than 1 px, and Prasad calls for a "thorough error-analysis" (p. 115). This frames `shehzad-2021-laser-sheet-alignment-piv` as the later answer, along with self-calibration ([Wieneke 2005](../Notes/wieneke-2005-stereo-piv-self-calibration-particle-images.md)).
- **Mapping order** (Willert's higher-order map was no better with low-distortion lenses, p. 114) is consistent with `sun-2021-distortion-correction-large-sensor-piv` finding a bicubic map necessary for a strongly distorting large-sensor lens. The two together say the map order should follow the lens.
- **Suggested additions to the page** (my assessment, in the note's Relevance section):
  - Willert's 1 mm misregistration is 0.07–0.1D for the group's 10–15 mm nozzles, several times the 0.025D windows, and Prasad notes misregistration worsens in strong gradients (p. 111). Stereo near shocks therefore needs sheet alignment or self-calibration plus a cross-camera check.
  - For flapping and helical modes, planar PIV mixes w′ tan θ (7–14% of w′ at a 4–8° field edge) into the POD pairs.
  - A tentative open question: how large is the perspective error in the group's planar POD pairs of helical and flapping modes? A synthetic-image test from LES would settle it.
- **Wave-mates.** The page should link the companion notes now being indexed: Soloff et al. 1997 (3-D calibration details), Willert 1997 (the misalignment test, the 35° either-side rig, the projective and rational maps) and Willert & Gharib 1991 (the DPIV correlation that interrogates each stereo view). Prasad summarises the first two; their notes should be checked for agreement with his account (p. 107–108, 113–115). Prasad cites Willert & Gharib 1992 (three-pinhole defocusing), not the 1991 DPIV paper.

## soloff-1997-distortion-compensation-generalized-stereoscopic-piv (queued 2026-10-25)

- **What it adds.** This is the founding reference for calibration-based stereo-PIV reconstruction, the "Soloff polynomial" that [shehzad-2021-laser-sheet-alignment-piv] uses as its eq. 1 without derivation.
  - Each camera gets an empirical map X = F(x), fitted by least squares from target images at three x₃ planes. It is cubic in x₁ and x₂ and quadratic in x₃, with 19 vector coefficients (eq. 18, p. 1447).
  - The displacement is linearised as ΔX ≃ ∇F Δx with a 2 × 3 magnification matrix (eqs. 5–6, 10), and the two cameras' 4 × 3 system is solved by least squares / generalized inverse (eqs. 15–16, Fig. 5).
  - A user-defined grid in the fluid is mapped forward into each image plane, so the inverse map is never needed (p. 1450–1452).
- **Calibration and stereo subsection.** The page could add a short "reconstruction" paragraph: calibration-based (this paper; displacements from the raw images converted through ∇F) against image dewarping followed by geometric reconstruction (Willert 1997, as used by Shehzad et al.).
- **Perspective error belongs on the page.** For a single camera, dropping the perspective column contaminates in-plane velocity by (x_I/d_o)·w, up to about 10% of w at the edge of a 100 mm field at d_o = 500 mm (eq. 12, p. 1446).
  - This is the only notes-backed number for perspective error in planar PIV. [sun-2021-distortion-correction-large-sensor-piv] (p. 4) mentions perspective but gives only a 0.03 px example.
  - It bears on the group's wide stitched planar fields of helical or flapping modes, whose w is large. The notes do not record the group's object distances, so this is an open check for the page's "Relevance" list.
- **Agrees with [sun-2021-distortion-correction-large-sensor-piv].** Sun's P3 bicubic map is eq. 18 at x₃ = 0, and their "Method II" (PIV on raw images, then remapping) is this paper's processes A–B route.
- **Gap filled by [shehzad-2021-laser-sheet-alignment-piv].** This paper only asks that the target be aligned "as precisely as possible" with the sheet centre (p. 1448), and its bench tests have no light sheet, so the misalignment error is untested.
- **Accuracy.** Mapping residuals are 0.53 px (single camera) and 1.1–1.2 px (stereo) (p. 1452–1453). The fluctuating displacement errors are about 0.1 px in-plane, and the out-of-plane absolute error is 4 × the in-plane one (Figs. 8–13, p. 1453; px conversions are the note's arithmetic).
  - So the position residual of F matters much less than its gradient. As a fraction of the imposed displacement, the errors are 0.4–2.8% in-plane and about 5% out-of-plane (note's arithmetic), much larger than the 10⁻⁴ "relative to domain" figures the paper quotes.
  - Shehzad et al.'s modern 3-C uncertainties (0.05–0.09 px) are similar in pixels.
- **Effect on current understanding.** It does not change claim 2 ("Calibration errors are larger than random PIV error if they are ignored, and both are cheap to remove") and gives it its methodological basis. Suggest adding "Soloff et al. 1997" to the Calibration paragraph and the Papers list: "polynomial mapping functions and ∇F least-squares 3-C reconstruction; perspective error (x/d_o)·w in planar PIV".
- **Cross-links to make** once the wave-mates' keys exist: Willert 1997 (dewarping, viewing-angle reconstruction and misalignment correction), Prasad 2000 (stereo-PIV review) and Willert & Gharib 1991 (digital cross-correlation, process A). ## willert-1991-digital-particle-image-velocimetry (queued 2026-10-25)

- **What it adds.** This is the founding paper of FFT-based cross-correlation PIV, and the page so far has no note on the correlation algorithm itself, which the group's Soria multigrid processing builds on. The method (p. 182–185):
  - two single-exposure frames, which removes the directional ambiguity without image shifting;
  - N × N windows correlated by FFT (F G*, then inverse FFT);
  - integer peak plus a three-point Gaussian fit in x and y separately, preferred to centre-of-mass centroiding;
  - usable displacement about N/3 (Nyquist N/2);
  - a digital window offset for mean convection, which is the seed of multigrid offsets.
- **Error budget** (p. 185–186):
  - about 0.01 px best uncertainty with 32² windows (translation-stage test), and a 0.002 px floor from 8-bit quantisation;
  - about 1000:1 dynamic range;
  - roughly double the uncertainty with 16² windows (more truncated and unpaired particle images);
  - uncertainty falls with seeding, levelling off above about 36 particles per 32² window (Fig. 6).
- **Window as filter.** The window is a moving-average low-pass filter (Fig. 7, a sinc-like response with zeros at λ = N, 0.64 at λ = 2N; the formula is my reconstruction), and at 75% overlap only 240 of 3,500 vectors are independent (p. 187).
- **Fit with the group's numbers.**
  - The group's quoted 0.1 px minimum displacement ([Tan et al. 2017](../Notes/tan-2017-broadband-velocity-fluctuations-screeching-jets.md) p. 2324; [Weightman 2019](../Notes/weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md)) is ten times WG's ideal value, as expected for real jet images.
  - The group's 50% overlap gives 1 in 4 independent vectors, not WG's 1 in 16.
  - WG's moving-average argument agrees with the Δx/L < 0.1 criterion of Tan et al. 2017 (Table 4, p. 2332).
- **Gradients.** WG's remark that gradients broaden and split the correlation peak (p. 183, 186, 188) is formalised by [Mitchell et al. 2011](../Notes/mitchell-2011-particle-relaxation-piv-cross-correlation.md), whose eq. 3 (self plus cross terms) is the explicit form of WG eq. 5c, applied to a shock inside the window.
- **What it does not change.** The current understanding (shock-induced artefacts, tracer lag, calibration) stands.
- **Suggested additions to the page:**
  - a short "Correlation algorithm" subsection under PIV in shock-containing jets: the N/3 rule, three-point Gaussian fit, window transfer function, overlap and independence;
  - a pointer that peak locking (not discussed by WG, whose particle images are about 4–6 px across) is a risk for the group's 1.2 px images (Tan et al. 2017, p. 2324);
  - a "Relevance" line: filter LES with the window's top-hat before comparing RMS, POD or spectra with PIV.

## willert-1997-stereoscopic-dpiv-wind-tunnel-flows (queued 2026-10-25)

Willert 1997 is the origin of the general, non-symmetric stereo-PIV reconstruction (eqs. 1–5, p. 1467) that the page's stereo work already relies on: `shehzad-2021-laser-sheet-alignment-piv` uses it as its 3C reconstruction (their eq. 4, p. 12) and lists Willert's correction (0.34 px residual) among the post-hoc misalignment fixes it replaces (their Table 1, p. 4). The paper confirms the page's claim 2, that calibration errors outweigh random error: a calibration grid tilted by less than 0.6° gave a 10 px (1 mm) mismatch between the two dewarped views, a third of a 32 px window, although the grids themselves overlapped to better than 1 px (p. 1470, Fig. 4). Willert could only remove a uniform 6 px offset and called for a calibration that guarantees grid–sheet alignment, which is exactly what Shehzad et al. 2021 supply and Wieneke 2005 self-calibration (PDF held, being indexed) addresses. Its uncertainty budget from quiescent-flow images is a useful benchmark for the "Calibration, stereo and stitching" section: 6.3 µm (0.063 px) in plane and 10.3 µm (0.103 px) out of plane at a 70° opening angle, with in-plane error about 1/√2 of a single camera's and out-of-plane about 1.65 times the in-plane, more than the 1/tan 35° = 1.43 that error propagation predicts (Table 3, p. 1476, 1478). It also shows that dewarping stretched 3 px particle images to above 4 px, raising the uncertainty along the stretched direction (p. 1469, 1476), which bears on the group's small final windows in shocked jets. Its multi-pass, window-offset interrogation recovers displacements beyond the final window's Nyquist limit and cut outliers from about 100 to 0–2 per 12,000 vectors with preconditioning (Table 2, p. 1471); it is the ancestor of the multigrid schemes on the page. Compared with `alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv` (Scheimpflug, ≈90°, same-side and opposite-side modes), Willert argues for opposite-side forward-scatter viewing, which gives brighter images (at least ten times) and matching trapezoidal fields (p. 1473, 1477). It does not change an open question on the page; it adds historical grounding and a reference error budget, and pairs with Soloff et al. 1997 and Prasad 2000 from the same batch.

## parikh-2023-lego-calibration-targets-large-fov-piv (queued 2026-10-25)

Parikh et al. 2023 (UniBw München) add a hardware answer to the "Calibration, stereo and stitching" thread: a modular two-sided, two-level stereo-PIV target built from LEGO baseplates on CNC aluminium backing plates, about 380 × 1150 mm (> 0.4 m², against about 0.1 m² for a LaVision Type 31), which calibrates several stereo pairs from one reference so that stitched fields need no separate registration step (p. 4–5, 10–11). In the Atmospheric Wind Tunnel Munich (six sCMOS cameras in three pairs, 2.3 m working distance, 950 non-TR pairs at 15 Hz), the LEGO calibration's mean fit error is 0.336 px against 0.123 px for Type 31 (about 0.05 mm), and the mean out-of-plane velocity agrees to about 0.8% of U∞ (mean) and 0.5% (median) (Tables 1–2, p. 6–7). The in-plane components agree less well; the authors blame short residence times in the sheet and the 3× coarser marker pitch (p. 8), but the downstream camera's inferred stereo angle also differs by 4° between calibrations (Table 1, p. 6), which could leak the large out-of-plane component into v and w — a point the paper does not make (note's reading, using the stereo geometry of willert-1997-stereoscopic-dpiv-wind-tunnel-flows and prasad-2000-stereoscopic-piv-review). A bench study finds small (7.8 mm) markers better than large (15.8 mm), pitch of weak influence (32–64 mm), and binarised calibration images lowering fit error in most cases; scaled fit errors stay at 0.08–0.15 mm, 2–3% of a 32 px window (Fig. 10, p. 9–10). It uses pinhole mapping plus planar self-calibration (Wieneke 2005), so it complements rather than tests soloff-1997-distortion-compensation-generalized-stereoscopic-piv (polynomial mapping, which the paper itself says is preferred for stitching at aberrated edges, p. 2), sun-2021-distortion-correction-large-sensor-piv (edge distortion, not examined here) and shehzad-2021-laser-sheet-alignment-piv (sheet alignment instead of self-calibration). It does not change the topic page's current understanding or open questions; it adds practical rules (shared target for stitched cameras, binarise calibration images, quote fit error in mm and as a fraction of the window) and a caution that in-plane components are the weak point when the main velocity is out of plane, as in cross-plane stereo PIV of jets. For the group's small jet fields the LEGO pitch (minimum 16 mm) is too coarse; it suits facility-scale fields.

## stanislas-2005-second-international-piv-challenge (queued 2026-10-25)

- **What it adds.** This is the library's first benchmark of the PIV interrogation step itself. The page so far covers tracer lag, calibration, distortion and sample size, but not what the correlation code adds.
- **Real-image error.** Fifteen contributors (16 team entries) processed the same real water-jet images (case A). The rms error, read from the zero-turbulence outer region, came out at 0.05–0.18 px and grouped by window size and sub-pixel fit, as Westerweel's theory predicts (p. 176–177). That is 5–18 times the ≈ 0.01 px bench-test floor of [Willert & Gharib 1991](../Notes/willert-1991-digital-particle-image-velocimetry.md).
- **Peak locking.** With ≈ 1 px particle images, peak locking varied 25-fold between codes (coefficient 0.0007–0.018, Table 5, p. 175, 178). Deformation codes gained nothing on these images (p. 189).
  - This bears directly on the group's diffraction-limited ≈ 1.2 px smoke images ([Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2014, helical mode](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2014-coherent-structure-helical-screech-mode.md), p. 827), so fractional-displacement histograms should be checked for every campaign.
- **Synthetic DNS images (case B).** Deformation codes cut the noise floor of near-wall spectra by about two orders of magnitude and resolve 2–3× smaller scales (p. 183). Mean bias was ±0.02 px away from the wall and ±0.3 px at the first wall point (p. 180, 189). Bias and rms error were uncorrelated (p. 184). PTV reached closest to the wall but had higher rms error (p. 182).
- **High-speed water PIV (case C).** The mean error was 0.09 px (2.3%), from 0.049 px (UDN) to 0.181 px (TSI) (Table 7, p. 188). Some deformation codes smooth the field beyond the nominal window, so they "artificially" lower the spatial resolution, and some codes leave spurious vectors at image edges (p. 186).
- **Synthetic versus real.** Synthetic images were judged not representative of real ones (p. 190). This qualifies the synthetic-image error figures that group papers quote, e.g. Scarano 2002's 0.04 px in [Mitchell et al. 2013](../Notes/mitchell-2013-near-field-structure-underexpanded-elliptic-jets.md) (p. 3–4). The real-image median (≈ 0.1 px) matches the 0.1 px resolvable-displacement limit of [Weightman 2019](../Notes/weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md) (p. 159–160).
- **Noise in turbulence statistics.** The finding that PIV noise is white and additive (p. 189) supports the noise-floor caveats for residual statistics in [Tan et al. 2017](../Notes/tan-2017-broadband-velocity-fluctuations-screeching-jets.md) (SNR > 500, though defined differently).
- **Scale of the errors.** These interrogation errors are smaller than the calibration and lag errors already on the page: up to 20% stress bias from sheet misalignment (Shehzad 2021), up to 80% edge bias from distortion (Sun 2021), and shock artefacts (Ragni 2011, Mitchell 2011). None of those were present in the Challenge images.
- **Proposed new open question for the page:** how large are interrogation errors in shock-containing jet images, where particle images are about 1 px and gradients are step-like? The Challenge tested neither. Synthetic PIV images generated from the PyFR LES with modelled particle lag would separate interrogation, lag and shock artefacts.
- **Placement.** Suggested for a new "Interrogation accuracy" subsection under "PIV in shock-containing jets", and as a line in the Relevance list: report a quiescent-region noise floor and a peak-locking histogram with each dataset.

## wieneke-2005-stereo-piv-self-calibration-particle-images (queued 2026-10-25)

Wieneke 2005 is the source of the disparity-map "self-calibration" that the page's "Calibration, stereo and stitching" section knows only through `shehzad-2021-laser-sheet-alignment-piv`. The method fits a Tsai pinhole model to a 3D plate and cross-correlates the dewarped camera-1 and camera-2 particle images of the same instant, summing 5–50 correlation planes. Each disparity vector is triangulated, a plane is fitted to the points, and both mappings are transformed so that this plane is z = 0. The loop is repeated 2–3 times (p. 269–271, Fig. 3).

It puts numbers on claim 2 for stereo PIV. On a synthetic field with 10% gradients, 8–9 px of uncorrected disparity gives 0.39–0.44 px error, and 38 px or more gives no usable field. After self-calibration, residual disparities are 0.004–0.103 px (0.286 px for one view), and velocity errors are 0.023–0.046 px for initial disparities up to about 180 px (Tables 1–2, p. 273–274). On real particle images, three calibrations with 44–252 px of disparity agree to 0.055 px after correction (p. 276).

It agrees with `willert-1997-stereoscopic-dpiv-wind-tunnel-flows`: a sub-degree tilt gives about 10 px of disparity, and shifting the sample positions alone works only for small misalignments (p. 270). It closes the misregistration gap that `prasad-2000-stereoscopic-piv-review` names (Prasad p. 115). Against `soloff-1997-distortion-compensation-generalized-stereoscopic-piv`, it argues for about 22 pinhole parameters rather than 80 polynomial coefficients, except where the distortion is not perspective-like (p. 269).

It partly supports Shehzad's caution that post-hoc correction can leave bias. The paper reports a systematic 1.2 px bias at a 5 mm (100 px) out-of-plane move, "probably" from using mapping derivatives at z = 0 only, and leaves it for further study (p. 275). The 0.06 px that Shehzad's Table 1 gives for this method is probably the 0.055 px of Sect. 3.3. Shehzad's "5% error" for a 20 px offset is not in this paper, which gives a 1 px change (p. 269).

It adds two things the page lacks:
- a 3-media (air–glass–water) pinhole model, so cameras can be calibrated outside a closed channel, accurate to within 1% for glass-to-sheet distance errors up to 20 mm (Table 5, p. 278);
- the correlation-peak width w_c = d(cot α₁ + cot α₂), which measures sheet thickness, position, flatness and the overlap of the two laser pulses (eq. 8, p. 279).

It does not change the page's conclusion that planar PIV has no disparity fix, since the method needs two views. The short-Δt two-pulse overlap check it mentions does work with one camera (p. 279).

New point for the open question on particle lag: errors from residual disparity scale as disparity × velocity gradient (p. 269, 273). In shock-containing jets they therefore concentrate at the shocks, where the lag artefacts are. A spurious out-of-plane w at shocks is plausible (note's estimate). Any stereo dataset used to test lag should have its disparity map checked first.

## willert-2012-high-power-led-high-frame-rate-schlieren (queued 2026-10-25)

For the summary and facility sections, this paper documents the light source and driver of the group's supersonic jet rigs (Risborg 2008 facility, D = 5 and 15 mm converging nozzles), and is a methods paper in its own right: pulse-response tests with a PIN photodiode against a 5 ns Nd:YAG reference (p. 415), duty-cycle limits (25% in bursts, 0.5% stated for continuous 1 kHz pulsing; my arithmetic gives 0.2% for 2 µs at 1 kHz), the 20 nm blue shift of the emission above 100 A (p. 416), and hazards (low voltage, uncollimated light, p. 420). The same pulsed LEDs back-light the group's BOS (Tan et al. 2015, 532 nm LED array, 100 µs pulses) and are cited for PIV and spray shadowgraphy (p. 420), so the paper links the schlieren and PIV/BOS practice threads. Its "lag-free triggering at varying frequency" point (p. 420) is a route to phase-locked imaging of drifting tones that the page could mention alongside the near-field phase surveys. It agrees with Settles & Hargather 2017 and with Mitchell et al. 2012 (same HPV-1 / PCO Dimax / CBT-120 set-up, p. 335). No contradiction with existing notes; it adds no new open question.
