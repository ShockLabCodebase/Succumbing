# Diagnostics and facilities

_Last updated: 2026-10-25 · 49 papers_

## Scope
How to measure velocity in shock-containing jets, and the facilities the measurements are made in. The page covers:
- PIV practice in shocked flow: tracer response and seeding, the correlation step and its errors, Δt and window size, stereo reconstruction and calibration (perspective error, sheet–target misalignment, self-calibration, lens distortion, targets), sample sizes, phase recovery, and the statistics PIV can hand to models;
- facilities and their acoustic environment: anechoic design, chamber qualification and rig-noise checks, near-field and azimuthal microphone surveys, the group's jet rigs and their installation effects, and the shock-tube rigs.

Optical imaging (schlieren, TR schlieren, shadowgraph, BOS, interferometry, tomography), the mean density for base flows and image–simulation matching are on [schlieren-and-density-imaging](schlieren-and-density-imaging.md); a short summary, with the light sources and cameras, is below.

The PIV-method papers (Willert & Gharib 1991 to Parikh et al. 2023) come from outside jet research; the page reads them for what they imply for the group's shock-jet PIV. Working settings come from the setup sections of the group's screech, impinging-jet and twin-jet papers; those notes are marked "(not tagged)" under Papers.

Other topic pages cover the rest:
- the analysis algorithms: [modal-decomposition](modal-decomposition.md) and [time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md);
- the physics found with these tools: [screech](screech.md), [impinging-jets-and-edge-tones](impinging-jets-and-edge-tones.md), [non-axisymmetric-jets](non-axisymmetric-jets.md), [twin-jet-coupling](twin-jet-coupling.md), and [jet-noise-mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md) for the noise models that PIV statistics calibrate;

## Current understanding
1. **Group PIV of shock-containing jets is not time-resolved, and shocks inside a window corrupt its turbulence statistics.** Smoke with τ_p ≈ 2 µs, Δt of 0.48–1 µs, final windows of 0.02–0.03D and 8,000–10,000 snapshots ([Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2014, helical mode](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2014-coherent-structure-helical-screech-mode.md), p. 827–828; [Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2018](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2018-upstream-acoustic-modes-screech-closure.md), p. 4; [Weightman 2019](../Notes/weightman-2019-sound-production-impinging-supersonic-jets.md), p. 26; [Tan et al. 2017](../Notes/tan-2017-broadband-velocity-fluctuations-screeching-jets.md), p. 2324) sit inside Ragni's no-bias criteria ([Ragni et al. 2011](../Notes/ragni-2011-particle-tracer-response-shocks-piv.md), p. 56), and at the small-window end where a shock inside the window adds the least shot-to-shot artefact ([Mitchell et al. 2011](../Notes/mitchell-2011-particle-relaxation-piv-cross-correlation.md), Fig. 8, p. 941–942; the Mitchell 2011 note's reading). The artefact remains and grows with shock strength: 2–10% behind the first shock of elliptic jets at NPR 2.2–4.2 ([Mitchell et al. 2013](../Notes/mitchell-2013-near-field-structure-underexpanded-elliptic-jets.md), Table 2, p. 5), and 0.35U_e behind a Mach disc with a 0.056D window, which led Mitchell et al. 2011 to call turbulence statistics in shock-containing windows "essentially impossible" (p. 944–946). Dynamics therefore come from TR schlieren. PIV supplies velocity, phase-averaged structure, base flows and noise-model length scales, but no time scales; convection velocities come only indirectly, from structures in single snapshots ([Krothapalli et al. 1999](../Notes/krothapalli-1999-flow-field-noise-supersonic-impinging-jet.md), p. 166–167) or POD wavelength times the tone ([Weightman et al. 2019](../Notes/weightman-2019-nozzle-external-geometry-azimuthal-mode-impinging-jet.md), p. 432–433), and are otherwise modelled ([Tan et al. 2019b](../Notes/tan-2019-equivalent-bbsan-source-reconstruction-screeching-jets.md), p. 7, 23).
2. **Calibration errors are larger than random PIV error if they are ignored, and both are cheap to remove.**
   - Planar PIV mixes out-of-plane velocity into the in-plane components, by about 10% of w at a typical field edge ([Soloff et al. 1997](../Notes/soloff-1997-distortion-compensation-generalized-stereoscopic-piv.md), eq. 12, p. 1446; [Prasad 2000](../Notes/prasad-2000-stereoscopic-piv-review.md), eq. 2, p. 104).
   - In stereo, a target tilted by under 0.6° from the sheet misregisters the views by 10 px ([Willert 1997](../Notes/willert-1997-stereoscopic-dpiv-wind-tunnel-flows.md), p. 1470), and 8–9 px of uncorrected disparity gives about 0.4 px of velocity error in a flow with 10% gradients ([Wieneke 2005](../Notes/wieneke-2005-stereo-piv-self-calibration-particle-images.md), Tables 1–2, p. 273–274). Self-calibration or pre-alignment brings this to 0.02–0.09 px (Wieneke 2005, p. 273–276; [Shehzad et al. 2021](../Notes/shehzad-2021-laser-sheet-alignment-piv.md), p. 12–13).
   - In planar PIV, sheet–target misalignment can bias turbulent stresses by up to 20%, with no after-the-fact correction (Shehzad et al. 2021, p. 3–4).
   - Lens distortion at the edge of a large sensor biased the mean velocity by up to about 80% ([Sun et al. 2021](../Notes/sun-2021-distortion-correction-large-sensor-piv.md), p. 11).
3. **The correlation step adds about 0.05–0.2 px on real images.** FFT correlation with a Gaussian sub-pixel fit reaches about 0.01 px on bench tests ([Willert & Gharib 1991](../Notes/willert-1991-digital-particle-image-velocimetry.md), p. 185–186), but codes run on the same real jet images gave 0.05–0.18 px rms, set by window size and sub-pixel fit, and with ≈ 1 px particle images their peak locking varied 25-fold ([Stanislas et al. 2005](../Notes/stanislas-2005-second-international-piv-challenge.md), p. 175–178). The group's 0.1 px working limit (Weightman 2019, p. 159–160) fits this, and its 1.2 px smoke images are in the peak-locking range (Mitchell et al. 2013, p. 3). These errors are smaller than the calibration and lag errors but have not been measured on shock-containing images.
4. **The snapshot budget depends on the statistic and the mode.** About 30 triggered pairs per phase give phase averages to 5% of the peak velocity ([Alkislar et al. 2003](../Notes/alkislar-2003-screeching-rectangular-jet-stereo-piv.md), p. 128–129). The leading POD pair converges by 500–1,000 snapshots (Weightman 2019, p. 56–57), an unpaired mode tied to the pair's amplitude more slowly ([Weightman et al. 2018](../Notes/weightman-2018-signatures-shear-layer-unsteadiness-pod.md), Fig. 4, p. 5), and length scales of the residual after the pair is removed need at least 3,000 for 1% error (Tan et al. 2017, p. 2332).
5. **Only one group rig is anechoic.** The optical rigs, where PIV and schlieren are taken, are "in no way" anechoic ([Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2021, shock leakage](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2021-generation-screech-tones-shock-leakage.md), p. 4): tone frequency and phase are reliable but amplitudes are qualitative, and nearby plates and walls add peaks or shift staging (Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2018, p. 4; [Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2022](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2022-unifying-theory-jet-screech.md), p. 6–7; [Bell et al. 2021](../Notes/bell-2021-intermittent-modal-coupling-screeching-twin-jets.md), p. 6, 17–18). The SJAF chamber is anechoic above 500 Hz ([Wong et al. 2020](../Notes/wong-2020-azimuthal-decomposition-radiated-noise-shock-containing-jets.md), p. 2016), against 150–160 Hz for purpose-built chambers ([Mayer et al. 2019](../Notes/mayer-2019-bristol-aeroacoustic-wind-tunnel.md), p. 4), but its qualification stops at 12 kHz, below the BBSAN band (Wong 2020 note). A high contraction ratio does not by itself make a rig quiet, and overall levels hide rig noise ([Viswanathan 2004](../Notes/viswanathan-2004-aeroacoustics-hot-jets.md), p. 46–47, 68–70). Even in anechoic rooms, reflections end near-field phase surveys once the tone is 20 dB down ([Mercier et al. 2017](../Notes/mercier-2017-screech-feedback-loop-underexpanded-round-jets.md), p. 216–218).

## Schlieren and density imaging (summary)
Long-record TR schlieren is the group's main tool for dynamics: a Photron SA-Z at 100–200 kHz recording 10⁴–10⁵ frames per condition, which replaced phase-locked imaging and 102-frame bursts (Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2022, p. 6; [Mazharmanesh et al. 2026](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2026-triadic-interaction-mean-flow-deformation-screech.md), p. 4; Weightman 2019, p. 24–26). That is the trade Settles & Hargather describe: CMOS cameras give megapixel frames at up to 20 kfps, while 5–10 Mfps cameras record only a few hundred frames ([Settles & Hargather 2017](../Notes/settles-2017-recent-developments-schlieren-shadowgraph.md), p. 3). The light source is an overdriven Luminus CBT-120 LED, pulsed at 120 A or more for 0.5–2 µs; its light decays in about 350 ns, so it can be pulsed at up to 1 MHz ([Willert et al. 2012](../Notes/willert-2012-high-power-led-high-frame-rate-schlieren.md), p. 413–416). Settles & Hargather agree that LEDs pulse to about 100 ns and that the CBT-120 suits schlieren, though it is too large for shadowgraphy (p. 4–5).

The intensity is a path-integrated, knife-edge-dependent proxy for ∂ρ/∂x, so quantitative density needs BOS with Abel inversion, physics-informed BOS or tomographic interferometry. Schlieren velocimetry is no substitute for PIV: it is path-averaged (about 46% of the true centreline speed in one example), and one Δt cannot span a 2–3 decade speed range (Settles & Hargather, p. 12–14). PIV and the optical methods meet in two places. First, the mean density for stability base flows is usually derived from PIV via Crocco–Busemann or constant stagnation density along streamlines, and only two group datasets have been checked against BOS ([Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2021, waves](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2020-waves-in-screeching-jets.md), p. 10; [Wong et al. 2021](../Notes/wong-2021-wavepacket-modelling-bbsan.md), p. 11). Second, TR-schlieren SPOD wavenumbers are the lag-free check on PIV modes, though with a line-of-sight bias of their own (see the particle-lag question below). Set-ups, error budgets and open questions are on [schlieren-and-density-imaging](schlieren-and-density-imaging.md).

## PIV in shock-containing jets

### Tracer response
**Measuring τ_p.** Ragni et al. 2011 fitted the exponential slip of particles across an oblique shock:
- **No-bias criteria:** the response time is measured without bias if the window is no larger than the relaxation length ξ_p and Δt no larger than τ_p (p. 56).
- **Measured τ_p:** DEHS 1.9–2.0 µs, stored powders 2.4–3.3 µs, and dried powders through a 1 µm filter 0.56 µs (TiO₂) and 0.37 µs (SiO₂). The filter cuts seeding density (p. 61–62).
- **Spread:** PTV shows τ_p varies by a factor of four within one sample (p. 63).
- **Aero-optics:** tilt the line of sight 2–5° upstream and raise the f-number (p. 57–58).
- **Guideline:** Samimy & Lele's Stokes number ≤ 0.1 (p. 53).

**Modelling what the correlation returns.** Mitchell et al. 2011 model PIV across a normal shock as a noiseless cross-correlation of relaxing particles; the artefact predictions below all come from this model.
- **Polydisperse seed looks slower than its median particle**, because PIV weights each particle by d². τ_p from a nominal diameter is therefore "of little value" and should be measured in situ (Fig. 4, p. 938–939). Ragni's fourfold PTV spread is the same effect seen directly (the Mitchell 2011 note's link).
- **A shock inside the window adds a shot-to-shot fluctuation**, because where the peak lands depends on the random particle positions. Mid-window it grows with SR = IW/ξ_p, to 0.32U at M 3 and SR 10, and polydispersity lowers it (Fig. 8, p. 941–942). At the window's upstream edge it peaks at intermediate SR and vanishes as SR → ∞, and polydispersity raises it (Fig. 12, p. 942–944). Ideal image deformation removes most of the monodisperse artefact but little of the polydisperse one (Fig. 13, p. 943–944).
- **In a jet** (Aerosil R974, τ_p = 2.1 µs): u′_rms ≈ 0.35U_e behind a stable Mach disc and ≈ 0.1U_e behind oblique shocks, which 1 MHz schlieren showed were not moving (Figs 14–15, p. 944–946). The mean correlation-coefficient map picks out both kinds of shock (Fig. 16, p. 945–946).
- **Two criteria for two errors.** Mitchell's prescription (SR well above 10, shock on a window edge) looks opposite to Ragni's SR ≤ 1. The Mitchell 2011 note reconciles them: Ragni protects the measured relaxation profile, Mitchell the spurious fluctuation, which is also small at SR ≤ 1, where the group's SR ≈ 0.4–0.75 sits.

**The group's smoke matches Ragni's DEHS.** Mitchell et al. 2013 measured it. Vicount 1300 smoke is nominally 300 nm (0.48 µs by Melling's relation), but across the Mach disc of a strongly underexpanded round jet it relaxes in τ_p = 2.0 µs, like 600 nm particles, which the authors put down to injecting it into a mixing chamber (eq. 1, p. 4). The Stokes number on the shock spacing is ≈ 0.07 and SR ≈ 0.4 (eqs 2–3, p. 4–5). The note flags that eq. 3 is printed inverted (ξ/IW for IW/ξ) and that Fig. 3's time axis does not show the 2.0 µs. Every later group paper reuses the value unchanged: the helical-mode paper (Fig. 3, p. 828), the [Mach-disk paper](../Notes/edgington-mitchell-2014-underexpanded-jet-mach-disk-shear-layer.md) (p. 4–5), Weightman et al. 2018 (p. 4–5), Weightman 2019 (p. 26) and Tan et al. 2017 (p. 2324). Its consequences:
- **Artificial fluctuation scales with shock strength.** Mitchell 2013's Table 2 (p. 5) predicts 1/2/4/5% (monodisperse) or 1/3/7/8% (log-normal) behind the first shock of elliptic jets at NPR 2.2/2.6/3.4/4.2, against 2/2.5/7/10% measured. The model is a lower bound (noiseless, no peak broadening), and at NPR 2.2 and 4.2 the measurement exceeds both predictions. At low SR the velocity PDF across the shock has one broad peak, not two (Fig. 4). The helical-mode paper adapts the NPR 3.4 row: its round jet, with no resolvable normal shock, shows 3% (Table 2, p. 829). Across the NPR 4.2 round-jet Mach disk the prediction is at least 8% and the anisotropy is inflated, but the artificial peak extends over only about 0.2D, so lag cannot hide a recirculation bubble (Mach-disk paper, p. 4–5, 16–17; helical-mode paper, p. 830–832).
- **Stokes number at the tone.** On the flow scale τ_pU_e/D ≈ 0.04 (helical-mode paper, eq. 2.1, p. 828). At the tone, 2πfτ_p ≈ 0.1 for the C mode at about 7.7 kHz (the helical-mode note's arithmetic), 0.10–0.16 for the elliptic jets at NPR 3.4–2.2 (the Mitchell 2013 note's arithmetic) and about 0.2 for A modes near 16 kHz (the Ragni note's estimate), all at or above the guideline. Dried, filtered powders would bring the A modes below 0.1.
- **Window and Δt.** The 0.03D window of Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2018 (SR ≈ 0.75 in the Ragni note) and the 0.39 mm window of the helical-mode paper (below the ≈ 1 mm relaxation length, with Δt < τ_p, in that note) are both inside the no-bias criteria.

**Other labs' seeding:**
- [Thethy et al. 2022](../Notes/thethy-2022-shock-diffraction-non-quiescent-medium.md) used TiO₂ with τ_p = 0.84 µs in 10 kHz PIV (p. 6).
- Paraffin of 0.6–0.8 µm ([Farghadan et al. 2026](../Notes/farghadan-2026-wave-interactions-screeching-jet.md), p. 4) and Ondina oil ([Nogueira et al. 2022](../Notes/nogueira-2021-closure-mechanism-a1-a2-screech.md), p. 6).
- Opposite size choices for jet and ambient. [Henderson et al. 2002](../Notes/henderson-2002-flow-structure-supersonic-impinging-jets.md) put 0.6 µm olive oil in the jet and 0.2–0.3 µm smoke in the ambient (p. 2); Ragni cites about 7 µs for olive oil (p. 54), probably for larger droplets. Alkislar et al. 2003 did the reverse, 0.3 µm oil in the jet (τ_p ≈ 0.25 µs, the note's estimate) and 1–5 µm smoke in the ambient, arguing that the large particles stay in subsonic regions (p. 124); their centreline PIV matched pitot/static velocities through six shock cells, a direct lag check (Fig. 11, p. 134). Krothapalli et al. 1999 used ≈ 1 µm oil and ≈ 5 µm fog smoke the same way (p. 160). Lag smeared their stand-off shock (Fig. 13b, p. 172), and the note's τ_p ≈ 80 µs for the fog suggests the 8–50 m s⁻¹ entrainment may lag too.
- Seed both the jet and the ambient air (Mach-disk paper, p. 4–5; helical-mode paper, p. 827).

### Correlation and interrogation error
**The algorithm.** Willert & Gharib 1991 founded FFT cross-correlation PIV: two single-exposure frames (no directional ambiguity), N × N windows correlated by FFT, a three-point Gaussian fit to the peak, a usable displacement of about N/3, and a digital window offset for the mean convection (p. 182–185). Willert 1997 made the offset multi-pass, which recovers displacements beyond the final window's limit and, with preconditioning, cut outliers from about 100 to 0–2 per 12,000 vectors (Table 2, p. 1471). The group's Soria multigrid processing follows this line (the Willert & Gharib note's reading): 64 → 16 px with 50% overlap (Mitchell et al. 2013, p. 3) and 192 → 24 px (Weightman et al. 2018, p. 4–5). The FSU alternative is mesh-free: particle images as interrogation regions and a second-order least-squares fit to scattered displacements, with analytic gradients (Krothapalli et al. 1999, eqs 2–3, p. 161–162), for which Alkislar et al. 2003 quote 0.03 px, about ±2.5 m s⁻¹ (p. 125–126).

**Errors.**
- **Bench:** about 0.01 px with 32² windows, roughly double with 16², levelling off above about 36 particles per window (Willert & Gharib, p. 185–186, Fig. 6).
- **Real images:** in the second PIV Challenge the rms error on real water-jet images was 0.05–0.18 px, grouped by window size and sub-pixel fit as Westerweel's theory predicts (Stanislas et al. 2005, p. 176–177). With ≈ 1 px particle images the peak-locking coefficient ranged from 0.0007 to 0.018 between codes, and deformation codes gained nothing (Table 5, p. 175, 178, 189).
- **Synthetic DNS images:** deformation codes cut the near-wall spectral noise floor by about two orders of magnitude (p. 183), though some smooth beyond the nominal window (p. 186). The noise is white and additive (p. 189), which supports noise-floor corrections of residual statistics such as Tan et al. 2017's SNR > 500. Synthetic images were judged unrepresentative of real ones (p. 190).
- **The group's numbers:** the 0.1 px limit (Weightman 2019, p. 159–160; Tan et al. 2017, p. 2324) is ten times the bench value and close to the Challenge's real-image errors, so the 0.04 px that Mitchell et al. 2013 quote from synthetic tests (p. 3–4) is probably optimistic. Particle images are 1.2 px, with little peak locking reported (Mitchell et al. 2013, p. 3, 5), and 1.5 px in Weightman et al. 2018 (p. 4–5). The floor also limits dynamic range: a Δt of 0.9 µs moves particles 0.5 px in the slow near field and 30 px in the core ([Bell et al. 2018](../Notes/bell-2018-coupled-underexpanded-supersonic-twin-jets.md), p. 4).

**The window is a filter.** It acts as a moving average with zeros at λ = N, and overlapping vectors are not independent: 240 of 3,500 were at 75% overlap (Willert & Gharib, Fig. 7, p. 187), and about one in four is at the group's 50% (the note's arithmetic). Tan et al. 2017's Δx/L < 0.1 criterion (Table 4, p. 2332) follows the same logic. Gradients broaden and split the peak (p. 183, 186, 188); Mitchell 2011's model is the explicit form of this for a shock in the window (the Willert & Gharib note's link).

### Group settings
- Δt: 0.88–1 µs (Weightman 2019, p. 26; Weightman et al. 2018, p. 4–5; Bell et al. 2018, p. 4; Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2018, p. 4), and 480 ns in the 15 mm nozzle C-mode campaigns (Tan et al. 2017, p. 2324; [Tan et al. 2019a](../Notes/tan-2019-correlation-analysis-piv-screeching-jets.md), p. 15–16).
- Final windows: 0.019–0.034D (Weightman 2019, p. 26; the 0.019D is 24 px with 6 px spacing in Weightman et al. 2018, Table 2, p. 4–5), 0.025–0.026D (helical-mode paper, Table 1, p. 827; Tan et al. 2017, p. 2324) and 0.026D ([Ivelja et al. 2024](../Notes/ivelja-2024-modal-analysis-screeching-elliptical-jets.md), p. 4). Bell et al. 2018's Table 1 is inconsistent: 12 px is 0.024D, not the tabulated 0.032D, and its vector count implies 6 px spacing, not 8 px (the note's check).
- Validation: median and dynamic-mean filters (Weightman et al. 2018, p. 4–5), or a Chauvenet 3σ test on the stacked fields (Bell et al. 2018, p. 3).
- Cameras: the 15 mm nozzle campaigns stitched two PCO 4000 cameras (23 µm/px; adaptive Gaussian blending over a 7.5% overlap) into a 10D × 2.2D field, with a 1.5 mm sheet (Mitchell et al. 2013, p. 3; helical-mode paper, p. 827; Tan et al. 2017, p. 2324). Later rigs use single large sensors such as the Imperx B6640 at 6600 × 4400 px, at 11.8 µm/px with a 0.55 mm depth of field against a ≈ 1 mm sheet (Weightman et al. 2018, p. 4–5) or 19.9 µm/px (Bell et al. 2018, p. 3–4; also Weightman 2019, p. 26; Farghadan et al. 2026, p. 4).
- Twin jets: a grazing sheet is diffracted by the first jet's Mach disk, so the second jet is lit inconsistently from snapshot to snapshot, and Bell et al. 2018 analyse only the first (p. 5–6, 8–9).
- Long acquisitions: 8,000 fields at 0.5 Hz is about 4.4 h of running, with up to 1% NPR drift from the regulator, and mode stability over the run was not checked (helical-mode paper, p. 825, 827, with the note's arithmetic).
- Conventions: the NPR 3.4 C-mode dataset is quoted at St 0.30 (helical-mode paper, p. 829) and 0.26 (Tan et al. 2017, Table 2, p. 2325). The Tan 2017 note traces the 0.26 to U_j = M_j a∞ (isentropic U_j gives 0.31), and the Re of 6.8–8.6 × 10⁵ quoted for the same data also differ by definition (helical-mode note). State the definition with any St or Re.

### Stereo reconstruction and calibration
**Perspective error and viewing angle.** Off its axis, one camera adds (x/d_o)·w to the in-plane velocity, about 10% of w at the edge of a 100 mm field viewed from 500 mm (Soloff et al. 1997, eq. 12, p. 1446); Prasad 2000 gives w tan θ, about 10% at a 5° field edge when in- and out-of-plane displacements are similar (eq. 2, p. 104). Correcting lens distortion leaves it in place (Sun et al. 2021, p. 4), and the notes do not record the group's object distances. Stereo removes the term, with an out-of-plane to in-plane error ratio of 1/tan θ for translation and Scheimpflug systems alike: 3–4 at the θ ≈ 15° of translation rigs, 1 at 45° (Prasad, eq. 9, p. 105, 110–111). Willert 1997 measured 0.063 px in plane and 0.103 px out of plane at a 70° opening angle, a ratio of 1.65 against the 1.43 predicted (Table 3, p. 1476, 1478), and argued for opposite-side forward-scatter viewing, which gives images at least ten times brighter (p. 1473, 1477). Alkislar et al. 2003's motorised Scheimpflug mounts, about 90° apart and imaging cross-planes from one side (p. 126–127), sit at the error-balanced angle.

**Reconstruction.** Prasad classes the methods as geometric, 2-D calibration or 3-D calibration, and records the field's move to calibration-based reconstruction with Scheimpflug mounts and digital cameras (p. 111–115).
- **Polynomial maps (Soloff et al. 1997).** Each camera gets a map X = F(x), fitted to target images at three planes, cubic in-plane and quadratic out of plane (eq. 18, p. 1447). The displacement follows from ΔX ≃ ∇F Δx, solving the two cameras' 4 × 3 system by least squares (eqs 5–6, 15–16). Mapping residuals are 0.53–1.2 px, yet displacement errors are about 0.1 px in plane and four times that out of plane, so the gradient of F matters more than its position residual (Figs 8–13, p. 1452–1453; the note's reading).
- **Dewarping plus geometry (Willert 1997).** Projective dewarping is followed by general, non-symmetric geometric reconstruction (eqs 1–5, p. 1467), the route Shehzad et al. 2021 use (their eq. 4, p. 12). Dewarping stretched 3 px particle images to over 4 px (p. 1469, 1476).
- **Pinhole models.** Wieneke 2005 prefers about 22 pinhole parameters to 80 polynomial coefficients unless the distortion is not perspective-like (p. 269); [Parikh et al. 2023](../Notes/parikh-2023-lego-calibration-targets-large-fov-piv.md) note that polynomial maps are preferred for stitching at aberrated edges (p. 2). The map order should follow the lens: Willert's higher-order map gained nothing with low-distortion lenses (Prasad, p. 114), while Sun's large-sensor lens needed a bicubic map (below).

**Sheet–target misalignment.**
- **The problem.** In Willert 1997 a grid tilted by less than 0.6° gave a 10 px (1 mm) mismatch between the dewarped views, a third of a 32 px window, though the grids overlapped to better than 1 px; only a uniform 6 px offset could be removed (p. 1470, Fig. 4). Soloff et al. only ask for alignment "as precisely as possible" (p. 1448), and Prasad calls for an error analysis, noting that misregistration worsens in strong gradients (p. 111, 115). For the group's 10–15 mm nozzles, 1 mm is 0.07–0.1D, several times the windows (the Prasad note's arithmetic).
- **Self-calibration (Wieneke 2005).** Dewarped particle images from the two cameras at one instant are correlated (summing 5–50 planes), the disparity vectors triangulated, a plane fitted, and both mappings moved to make it z = 0, two or three times (p. 269–271, Fig. 3). In a synthetic field with 10% gradients, 38 px or more of uncorrected disparity leaves no usable field; after correction the velocity error is 0.023–0.046 px for initial disparities up to about 180 px (Tables 1–2, p. 273–274), and three real calibrations with 44–252 px of disparity agree to 0.055 px (p. 276). Errors from residual disparity scale as disparity × velocity gradient (p. 269, 273). A 1.2 px bias remained after a 5 mm out-of-plane move, "probably" from using the mapping derivatives at z = 0 only (p. 275). The paper also gives a 3-media (air–glass–water) model (Table 5, p. 278) and the correlation-peak width w_c = d(cot α₁ + cot α₂), which measures sheet thickness, position and pulse overlap (eq. 8, p. 279).
- **Pre-alignment (Shehzad et al. 2021).** The sheet is aligned to the target before calibrating, with a slit and three photodiodes whose outer readings are brought within 2% of the centre (p. 6). Stereo uncertainty is below 5.9 µm (0.09 px), comparable with self-calibration at 4.0 µm, without post-correction (p. 12–13). Wieneke's 1.2 px bias partly supports Shehzad's caution that post-hoc correction can leave bias, but Shehzad's "5% error" for a 20 px offset is not in Wieneke's paper, which gives a 1 px change (the Wieneke note's check, p. 269).
- **Planar PIV has no disparity-based fix** (Shehzad et al., p. 4); only Wieneke's two-pulse overlap check works with one camera (p. 279). The problem is real in the group's data: Ivelja et al. 2024 found their major-axis plane biased by slight sheet misalignment and enforced symmetry to remove it (p. 4).

**Lens distortion.** Sun et al. 2021 studied a 47 MPx sensor with pincushion distortion:
- Without correction, the near-wall mean velocity at the image edge was off by up to about 80%, and ⟨u′u′⟩ was 12% low (p. 11–12).
- A bicubic map plus a rotation taken from a reference geometry made the wall straight to 0.62 px (p. 8). This is Soloff's eq. 18 at x₃ = 0 (the Soloff note).
- Running PIV on raw images and then remapping only the vector positions matched full dewarping, because a window deforms by only about 0.08 px (p. 8–9, 12–13).

The notes do not record a distortion correction for the group's large-sensor jet PIV.

**Targets and stitching.** Parikh et al. 2023 built a 380 × 1150 mm two-level target from LEGO baseplates that calibrates several stereo pairs from one reference, so stitched fields need no separate registration (p. 4–5, 10–11). Its fit error was 0.336 px against 0.123 px for a commercial plate, and the mean out-of-plane velocity agreed to about 0.8% of U∞ (Tables 1–2, p. 6–7). The in-plane components agreed less well; besides the authors' reasons (p. 8), the note points out that the inferred stereo angle of one camera differs by 4° between the calibrations (Table 1, p. 6), which could leak the large out-of-plane component into them. Small markers and binarised calibration images lowered fit errors to 0.08–0.15 mm, 2–3% of a 32 px window (Fig. 10, p. 9–10); the 16 mm minimum pitch is too coarse for the group's jet fields (the note). Alkislar et al. 2003 stitched four 65 × 105 mm zones with linear weights over 30 mm overlaps (p. 126).

### Sample size and phase
**POD convergence.**
- Weightman 2019's thesis tests (p. 56–57): the leading POD pair converges by N ≈ 500 snapshots in resonant flows and modes 3–4 by about 1,000; without a dominant mode it takes about 2,500.
- Weightman et al. 2018 drew 200 random resamples at N = 100–5,000: the 95% ranges at 5,000 are below 1.7% for the pair and 2.6% for the unpaired shear thickness mode (STM), which converges more slowly at every N ≥ 250 (p. 5, Fig. 4). The note cautions that this "jackknife" is random subsampling; drawn without replacement from 10,000, it understates the spread at N = 5,000 by a factor of about 0.71.
- Bootstrap 95% intervals are at most 0.1 px on the mean (Weightman 2019, p. 159–160) and about ±2% on the near-field axial rms, largest upstream of the first shock (Bell et al. 2018, Figs 4–5, p. 4).

**Residual statistics need more.** Tan et al. 2017 removed the tone by subtracting the leading POD modes, then degraded the data and redid the chain from POD to residual length scales (p. 2332). The test case was the hardest, the precessing B mode at NPR 2.6, which needed 18 modes (38% of the energy) against two for the C mode (p. 2326–2328):
- snapshots: error below 1% for at least 3,000 (about 8% at 1,000, read from a figure);
- noise: below 1% for SNR > 500;
- resolution: 1.4% at 80% vector sampling, and above 1% once Δx/L > 0.1 (Table 4).

Both Tan et al. 2019 papers quote these budgets for their 8,500-field data (2019a, p. 18; 2019b, p. 20). Statistics of the residual thus need several times the snapshots that the pair itself needs.

**Shocks and basis in the POD.** Masking the shock-containing core left the POD modes and energies unchanged at a 0.025D window (Tan et al. 2017, p. 2324–2325), so the shocks do not drive the modes there. POD of the transverse velocity alone separates the pair more cleanly than POD of both components, with the same mode shapes (helical-mode paper, p. 834; Tan et al. 2017, p. 2325). Weightman et al. 2018 show the cost. In NPR 3.4 impinging-jet PIV, the STM's coefficient tracks the pair's per-snapshot amplitude (Pearson 0.74) and describes snapshot-to-snapshot changes of shear-layer thickness; v-only POD nearly hides it, and its energy changes with the basis (u, v, (u, v), ω_z) and the region of interest (p. 6–8, 12–14). Synthetic Stuart-vortex shear layers reproduce the STM only when the layer width varies between snapshots, not when vortices merely translate (p. 9–12).

**Phase recovery.**
- *POD-pair phase.* Weightman 2019 binned by the pair's phase in 5° bins of about 130 fields each (p. 102). The group prefers POD phase to microphone triggering because it uses the whole field and is less sensitive to turbulent jitter, but it needs a clear phase-coherent component, i.e. a self-excited or forced flow (helical-mode paper, p. 833–834).
- *Microphone tagging.* Henderson et al. 2002 tagged each PIV pair with the microphone phase and got 20–60 fields per bin from 800 pairs (p. 2, 5).
- *Microphone triggering.* Alkislar et al. 2003 band-passed a near-field microphone into a frequency tracker (400 Hz – 12 kHz), divided the trigger by up to 10,000 to suit the 30 Hz cameras, and delayed it in 0.1 µs steps to get 16 phases at 22.5° (Fig. 4, p. 128–129). Only eight were measured: the other half-cycle is their mirror image, which assumes exact antisymmetry and was checked by eye at one phase pair (Fig. 5, p. 130–131). Each phase has about 35 pairs, against Weightman's 130 per bin. The random part includes vortex-to-vortex jitter (p. 146), and with 35 samples a threshold such as R_uu > 0.6 has a standard error of about 0.1 (the note's estimate). A pulsed LED's lag-free triggering at varying frequency allows the same phase-locking for schlieren of a drifting tone (Willert et al. 2012, p. 420).
- *Other routes.* 800 snapshots at 3 Hz were enough to build a 3D helix from 2D planes ([Oberleithner et al. 2011](../Notes/oberleithner-2011-swirling-jet-vortex-breakdown-stability.md), p. 7, 21–22). Space-only Hilbert POD works on shuffled snapshots ([Raiola & Kriegseis 2025](../Notes/raiola-2025-hilbert-pod.md), p. 13, 15).

### PIV fields as model input
PIV mean fields are the usual base flows for stability analysis, with density derived as in the summary above. Weightman 2019 also ray-traced acoustics through PIV fields. It preferred POD phase reconstructions, because the real shocks in raw PIV cause spurious reflections (p. 116–117), and the results are qualitative only (p. 112–115).

PIV also calibrates statistical noise-source models (the models are on [jet-noise-mechanisms](jet-noise-mechanisms.md)). Tan et al. 2019a,b took k, ε, integral length scales and mean pressure from 2D-2C data:
- The missing component and gradients come from statistical axisymmetry, with ν at ambient conditions. Both papers say PIV resolution limits ε but do not correct for it (2019a, p. 19; 2019b, eqs. 22–23, p. 22). The 2019a note reads ε as a resolved-scale estimate, which favours the k^{1/2}D_j/V̄₁ length scale because it needs no gradients.
- Correlations are integrated only to r = 0.1, to avoid the negative lobes of screeching jets (2019a, p. 19).
- Mean static pressure comes from the mean velocity: isentropic in the core, van Oudheusden's mean-momentum integration in the shear layer, and no Mach-disk treatment (2019b, eqs. 13–20, p. 17–18). The 2019b note adds that this neglects the Reynolds-stress divergence.
- The sonic line assumes constant total temperature, and shock reflection points are taken where ∂V̄₁/∂x = 0 on the high-speed side (2019a, p. 20–21).

These statistics agree reasonably with k–ω SST RANS of the same nozzle, except near the exit, where the shear layer is thin and the PIV unreliable, and in k and ε at M_j 1.59 (2019b, p. 29–33). The integral time scale is modelled (2019b, p. 7, 11, 23). Convection velocities can be estimated without time resolution, at a price. Krothapalli et al. 1999 tracked vorticity peaks in single snapshots: C_i ≈ 0.6U_j in the free jet and 0.52 of the centreline velocity at h/d = 4 in the impinging jet, rising with h/d. The method assumes a vortex moves at its centre velocity, and no structures could be followed within 20–25 mm of the wall (p. 166–167). POD wavelength times the tone gives 0.44–0.48U_j in the group's impinging jet (Weightman et al. 2019, p. 432–433).

## Facilities and acoustic environment

### Anechoic facilities and rig-noise checks
Mayer et al. 2019 is the library's design reference:
- **Chamber:** anechoic to 160 Hz under ISO 3745 for sources up to 0.65 m (p. 4).
- **Fan noise:** the fan sits about 6 m away in an isolated room, and the silencers are sized on dynamic insertion loss (p. 2–4).
- **Contraction:** Su contractions with powers 3 and 5 joined by a cubic spline (p. 6–7). Steady RANS was used only as a design screen; there is no comparison between the CFD and the hot wire (p. 8–10).
- **Free-stream turbulence:** 0.09% and 0.12%, after high-pass filtering at U/(2L_TS) (p. 1, 8).

Data-quality checks that transfer directly to jet rigs:
- coherence below 0.05 between a microphone at the fan and one in the chamber (Mayer et al., p. 10);
- a velocity exponent N(f) > 7, which flags jet mixing noise (Mayer et al., p. 10). Heated jets genuinely fall to 5.5 at 90° (Viswanathan 2004, Fig. 32, p. 75–76), so this is a cold-jet criterion (the Viswanathan note's inference);
- spectra against raw frequency, where rig noise stays fixed while jet noise scales with Strouhal number (Viswanathan, p. 70), and collapse across nozzle sizes with SPL − 80 log(V_j/a) − 10 log A, within 1 dB up to St 4 (Fig. 6a, p. 47–48);
- never OASPL or OAPWL: classic spectra 5 dB high above 13 kHz differed in OASPL by only 1.7 dB, and overall levels "must be avoided" for validation (p. 69–70, 77).

A high contraction ratio is no guarantee: NGTE's 250 and 100 gave louder, rougher spectra than Viswanathan's 28 and 10.7, because fittings, gaps, steps and screw heads downstream of the plenum spoil the data (p. 46–47).

The supersonic-jet chambers in the library are similar to Mayer's: Syracuse Skytop at 150 Hz ([Berry et al. 2017](../Notes/berry-2017-pod-time-resolved-schlieren-multi-stream-rectangular-jet.md), p. 2–3), OSU GDTL at 160 Hz ([Esfahani et al. 2021b](../Notes/esfahani-2021-coupling-modes-twin-rectangular-jets.md), p. 5), the NASA Glenn 20 m dome (Henderson et al. 2002, p. 2) and the LMFA 10 × 8 × 8 m room, which runs a continuous 0.85 kg s⁻¹ supply up to NPR 3.9 (Mercier et al. 2017, p. 205). Mercier et al. also give a repeatability benchmark: the same M_j 1.15 jet, measured in 2011 and 2016, gave matching far-field screech and BBSAN spectra and shock spacings within 1.3% (p. 206).

**The group's SJAF chamber** (a Shock Lab group facility) is described and qualified by Wong et al. 2020:
- 1.5 × 1.2 × 1.4 m wedge tip to tip, with 400 mm wedges, a nominal 500 Hz cut-on and a lined floor (p. 2016); continuous running up to NPR 5 from a 5 m³ reservoir shared with the supersonic wind tunnel, with NPR within about ±1% (p. 2016–2017);
- an 8 mm converging nozzle with a 0.5 mm lip and a contraction ratio of 10 (p. 2017), at Re 2.8–4.5 × 10⁵ (p. 2018), which straddles Viswanathan's 4 × 10⁵ threshold for clean broadband spectra (p. 74);
- ISO 3745 inverse-square tests with a 45 mm speaker at 500 Hz – 12 kHz along 60°, 90° and 140°, at about 12.5–75D (App. A, Fig. 7, p. 2024–2025). The paper calls them within tolerance apart from slight deviations near the source at 140°, blamed on upstream piping (p. 2024). The note reads several near-source points (12.5D at 90°, 25D at 140°) as outside tolerance and points out that the test stops below the BBSAN band (roughly 15–74 kHz), so the midfield ring at R ≈ 11–14D is not formally qualified;
- G.R.A.S. 46BE ¼-in. microphones, 2 s at 200 kHz, N_fft 2048 with a Hann window (p. 2018, 2024). Later group far-field acoustics come from it (Wong et al. 2021; Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2021, shock leakage, p. 5).

### Near-field and azimuthal surveys
Mercier et al. 2017 located screech sources by traversing one microphone against a fixed reference (p. 215–221):
- **Phase averaging, not cross-spectra.** Close to the jet, hydrodynamic and acoustic pressure mix, so 2π jumps cannot be told from real phase changes. The reference is band-passed with a zero-lag FIR filter (±10% of f_s), periods are marked from its maxima, and the moving signal is averaged in 20 bins per period over two periods; one wavefront is then followed by its zero crossings (p. 218–219). This needs time-resolved signals and one steady tone.
- **Geometry.** A radial traverse in the exit plane cannot separate the 3rd–5th shock tips, while an axial traverse at y = 1.5D can (p. 216, 219–221).
- **Reflections.** Even in the anechoic room the tone falls 20 dB by y ≈ 5.5D, beyond which reflections dominate the phase (Fig. 8, p. 216–218).

For the group's non-anechoic rigs the Mercier note therefore recommends the schlieren route: cross-spectral phase along the lip line from TR schlieren, which Mercier et al. also used (p. 222).

Wong et al. 2020 decompose the far field in azimuth with two microphones instead of an array: one fixed, one traversed in 10° steps over ±90° on a ring at 11D, with the cross-correlation fitted by sinc-type forms and Fourier transformed in azimuth (App. C, p. 2024–2026). The note cautions that it assumes stationarity, so it cannot follow mode switching in time, and that the sinc fit shapes the modal spectrum.

### The group's jet rigs
**The optical rigs are not anechoic** (Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2018, p. 4; helical-mode paper, p. 829; Weightman 2019, p. 24; Wong et al. 2021, p. 8) and have many hard reflecting surfaces (Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2021, shock leakage, p. 4), so studies use tone frequency and phase, not amplitude ([Knast et al. 2018](../Notes/knast-2018-coupling-modes-underexpanded-twin-axisymmetric-jet.md), p. 3). The Gas Jet Facility's Perspex walls are 60D from the centreline (Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2021, shock leakage, p. 4–6), and the PIV enclosure is 60 × 60 × 200 D, untreated, with one microphone at 23D (Bell et al. 2018, p. 2–3, 15).

**Installation matters.**
- A plate about 2D upstream of the exit reflects upstream waves and is blamed for extra peaks at NPR ≥ 2.9 (Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2022, p. 6–7).
- Identical twin nozzles on two Monash rigs give different onset NPRs for the same coupling process, which Bell et al. 2021 link to the PIV enclosure's hard Perspex walls and a plenum face flush with its base (p. 6, 17–18).
- In a non-anechoic room, a flush 10d lift plate turned the BBSAN hump of a NPR 3.7 C-D jet into a distinct screech tone, and the gap between plate and ground plane carried a duct-like standing wave at h/d ≈ 5.5 (Krothapalli et al. 1999, p. 160, 164, 174–175).
- Weightman 2019 ran PIV and schlieren in two facilities with the same nozzle, because the PIV cage's stresses spoil schlieren (p. 23). The acoustics of the two disagreed for two foam cases, and that schlieren was discarded (p. 66). This is the cross-facility counterpart of Mercier's cross-campaign check above.

**Flow and supply.**
- Contraction ratios are 10 in SJAF (Wong et al. 2020, p. 2017), 81 (Weightman 2019, p. 29), 93 for the 15 mm nozzle, which has a 5 mm lip (Tan et al. 2019a, p. 15; helical-mode paper, p. 825), and above 100:1 ([Mazharmanesh et al. 2025](../Notes/mazharmanesh-2025-screech-modes-non-axisymmetric-jets.md), p. 4). Viswanathan's evidence is that a high ratio alone does not guarantee clean spectra.
- The regulator drifts by up to 1% (Weightman 2019, p. 23; helical-mode paper, p. 825; Wong et al. 2020, p. 2017).
- The room temperature is held to about 0.1 K ([Wong et al. 2023](../Notes/wong-2023-steady-unsteady-coupling-twin-round-jets.md), p. 5).
- For contrast, the LMFA nozzle has a 4.4:1 contraction and a 0.5 mm lip on 38 mm, M_j is held to ±0.2%, and the stagnation temperature settles at 30 ± 2 °C after half an hour (Mercier et al. 2017, p. 205).

### Shock-tube and PDC rigs
These record single events:
- TR schlieren is triggered by a PCB transducer ([Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2022](../Notes/rezay-haghdoost-2022-shock-dividers.md), p. 3–4).
- Shock speed comes from correlating frames at 80 kfps (Thethy et al. 2022, p. 13).
- The background jet is measured from 53–55 PIV pairs taken before the shock arrives (Thethy et al. 2022, p. 13).

## Disagreements and open questions
- **Does particle lag distort the modes used to find upstream waves?**
  - Evidence so far: Ragni's τ_p values come from steady shocks only, and the group's 2.0 µs comes from one round-jet Mach disc and has been reused unchanged since (Mitchell et al. 2013, p. 4); a nominal diameter says little, since polydisperse seed is weighted by d² (Mitchell et al. 2011, p. 938–939). The Mach-disk paper argues lag is small for the mean field, and Alkislar et al. 2003 matched PIV to probes through six shock cells with finer oil (p. 134). Masking the core leaves the POD modes unchanged (Tan et al. 2017, p. 2324–2325), but that does not test lag at the tone. There the Stokes number is about 0.1 for the C mode (helical-mode note) and 0.2 for A modes (Ragni note), so the A1/A2 data are the most exposed.
  - Two complications. The TR-schlieren reference has a line-of-sight bias of its own (Settles & Hargather 2017, p. 12–14), so a mismatch between PIV and schlieren phase speeds need not be all lag; the Settles note expects the bias to be smaller for tone-locked wavepackets than for broadband eddies. In stereo data, residual-disparity errors scale with disparity × gradient (Wieneke 2005, p. 269, 273), so they also concentrate at the shocks.
  - Why it matters: the k_x-filtered guided-jet mode is a weak signal next to shocks (Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2018).
  - What would settle it: an in-house oblique-shock test of the group's smoke, then a repeat of the POD-pair and k_x analysis with dried, filtered TiO₂ (τ_p < 0.6 µs), compared with TR-schlieren SPOD wavenumbers at the same NPR. Any stereo data used should have its disparity map checked first.
- **Are the random-stress peaks behind the reflection points real?**
  - Evidence so far: in the NPR 3.4 data the coherent axial stress peaks just upstream of each shock reflection point and the random stress just downstream (helical-mode paper, p. 836–837). Tan et al. 2019a find coherent and random k in anti-phase there and read it as transfer from large to small scales (p. 22). The helical-mode note points out that shock-induced artificial fluctuation is strongest at the same places, and neither paper separates the two. Mitchell et al. 2011 show that the artefact is a shot-to-shot fluctuation (p. 941–944); the Mitchell 2011 note adds that it would be phase-modulated if the shocks move with the tone, in which case it would not average out of phase-resolved statistics.
  - Why it matters: the residual k, ε and length scales that calibrate the noise models inherit any artefact.
  - What would settle it (a suggestion): locate the shocks from the mean correlation-coefficient map (Mitchell et al. 2011, Fig. 16, p. 945–946), predict the artefact at each reflection with the noiseless-correlation model and subtract it, repeat with low-τ_p seeding, or compare with the residual from an LES of the same jet.
- **What counts as "random" in a triple decomposition?**
  - The split depends on how phase is found. A triggered average puts vortex-to-vortex jitter into u″ (Alkislar et al. 2003, p. 146), and POD phase is less sensitive to jitter (helical-mode paper, p. 833). Rebuilding the pair snapshot by snapshot (Tan et al. 2017) counts cycle-to-cycle amplitude modulation as coherent, while phase binning (Weightman 2019, p. 102) puts it in the random part (the Tan 2017 note's reading). It also depends on the POD basis: the amplitude-linked STM nearly vanishes in v-only POD (Weightman et al. 2018, p. 12–14). No paper compares the choices on one dataset.
  - What would settle it: apply all three, with more than one basis, to one dataset, or measure the modulation directly with SPOD reconstruction and HPOD on TR data ([time-frequency-methods](time-frequency-methods.md)).
- **How large are the processing errors not yet measured in the group's shock-jet PIV?** (new)
  - Interrogation: the PIV Challenge tested neither step-like gradients nor shocks inside windows (the Stanislas note), and ≈ 1 px particle images are where peak locking varied most between codes (p. 175, 178).
  - Perspective (tentative): for helical and flapping modes, planar PIV may mix w′ tan θ, about 7–14% of w′ at a 4–8° field edge, into the POD pairs (the Prasad note's estimate); the group's object distances are not recorded.
  - What would settle it: synthetic PIV images rendered from the PyFR LES with modelled particle lag, to separate interrogation, lag and shock artefacts (the Stanislas note's proposal), checked against quiescent-region noise floors and peak-locking histograms from the real data, since synthetic images are not fully representative (p. 190); and one stereo plane of a helical-mode case to bound the perspective term.
- **How much does the rig shape the resonance?**
  - Evidence so far: Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2022 attribute extra peaks to the upstream plate but did not remove it. Bell et al. 2021 see coupling onsets move between two rigs with the same nozzles (p. 6, 17–18), and Krothapalli et al. 1999 a lift plate turn a broadband hump into a tone (p. 174–175). A high contraction ratio alone does not settle it (Viswanathan 2004, p. 46–47).
  - What would settle it: repeat the NPR sweep with the plate foam-covered or removed, add a Mayer-style coherence check against compressor and valve noise and Viswanathan's raw-frequency plots, and cross-check staging limits in SJAF, whose own qualification above 12 kHz is still open (Wong 2020 note).

Which mean density PIV-based base flows should use is an open question on [schlieren-and-density-imaging](schlieren-and-density-imaging.md).

## Methods and data
Which diagnostic suits which analysis, following the CLAUDE.md workflow:
- **Non-TR PIV** gives:
  - mean, RMS and higher-moment fields;
  - POD pairs, phase averages and k_x filtering (Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2018), or microphone-triggered phase averages (Alkislar et al. 2003);
  - the broadband residual after the pair is subtracted, with k, ε and spatial length scales (Tan et al. 2017; 2019a);
  - base flows and mean pressure for models (Tan et al. 2019b);
  - space-only HPOD (Raiola & Kriegseis 2025).

  It gives no time scales, and convection velocities only through structure tracking or POD wavelengths (Krothapalli et al. 1999; Weightman et al. 2019).
- **Stereo PIV** is the check wherever w is large (cross-planes, helical and flapping modes). It needs a 3-D calibration (Soloff et al. 1997; Willert 1997), sheet pre-alignment or self-calibration (Shehzad et al. 2021; Wieneke 2005), and one target for stitched cameras (Parikh et al. 2023).
- **Single events** in the shock-tube rigs need triggered TR schlieren plus pressure probes (Rezay Haghdoost et al. 2022), with PIV of the background flow before the shock arrives (Thethy et al. 2022).
- **Microphones** are the frequency reference throughout. In the non-anechoic rigs use their frequency and phase, not amplitude (Knast et al. 2018), and check coherence against supply noise (Mayer et al. 2019). Near-field phase surveys need time-resolved signals and phase averaging where hydrodynamic and acoustic pressure mix (Mercier et al. 2017). Two microphones give the azimuthal content of stationary tones (Wong et al. 2020).
- **TR schlieren, BOS and the other optical methods**: [schlieren-and-density-imaging](schlieren-and-density-imaging.md).

## Relevance to our work
- **PIV.**
  - Align the sheet before calibrating; for stereo, self-calibrate as well and check the disparity map. Check edge distortion on the large sensors and remap vector positions if needed, and record the object distance so the planar perspective error can be bounded.
  - Report a quiescent-region noise floor and a peak-locking histogram with each dataset (Stanislas note), state the overlap, and filter LES with the window's top-hat before comparing RMS, POD or spectra with PIV (Willert & Gharib note).
  - Flag shock-containing windows from the correlation-coefficient map (Mitchell et al. 2011).
  - Quote τ_p together with SR, the Stokes number and 2πfτ_p at the tone, measured in situ rather than from a nominal size. Consider dried, filtered powders where shock-adjacent modes matter, above all for A modes.
  - Use at least 1,000 snapshots for the leading pairs, more for unpaired amplitude-linked modes, 3,000 with SNR > 500 for residual statistics, and about 10⁴ for means; report the POD basis and region with every modal energy.
  - In twin jets, light the jet of interest first or check symmetry before using both (Bell et al. 2018). Log the NPR drift over long runs, and state the St and Re definitions.
- **Rigs.** Record the regulator drift, room temperature, enclosure and plenum geometry with each run. Keep the upstream plate and enclosure walls in mind when reading extra peaks or shifted staging, and treat amplitudes from the optical rigs as qualitative. Plot spectra against raw frequency and never validate with overall levels. Locate sources from TR-schlieren phase rather than near-field microphone surveys.
- **PyFR staging LES.** The group's PIV of A1 and A2 jets at NPR 2.10 and 2.25 (Edgington-Mitchell et al. 2018, p. 6–11) gives velocity means and POD pairs to set against the LES, within the particle-lag caveat above, which is largest for A modes. The LES also gives what PIV must model (time scales, the true mean pressure, 3D k and ε), so it can test the PIV pressure reconstruction and the axisymmetric closure (Tan 2019b note), and synthetic PIV images rendered from it would size the shock artefacts and interrogation errors. Check its Re against Viswanathan's 4 × 10⁵ before comparing broadband spectral shapes; the threshold concerns shape near the peak, not tone frequency. The image comparisons are on [schlieren-and-density-imaging](schlieren-and-density-imaging.md).
- **Rocket noise.** Mayer-style coherence and Viswanathan's rig-noise checks suit model-scale tests, but N(f) > 7 would wrongly flag clean heated-jet data. The PIV-calibrated source coefficients are for cold jets only (Tan et al. 2019a, p. 12).

## Papers
- [Willert & Gharib 1991](../Notes/willert-1991-digital-particle-image-velocimetry.md): founding FFT cross-correlation PIV: N/3 rule, three-point Gaussian fit, ≈ 0.01 px bench floor; the window as a moving-average filter.
- [Soloff et al. 1997](../Notes/soloff-1997-distortion-compensation-generalized-stereoscopic-piv.md): polynomial-map, ∇F least-squares stereo reconstruction; planar perspective error (x/d_o)·w.
- [Willert 1997](../Notes/willert-1997-stereoscopic-dpiv-wind-tunnel-flows.md): non-symmetric stereo reconstruction with dewarping; sub-degree target tilt gives 10 px misregistration; multi-pass offset windows; stereo error budget.
